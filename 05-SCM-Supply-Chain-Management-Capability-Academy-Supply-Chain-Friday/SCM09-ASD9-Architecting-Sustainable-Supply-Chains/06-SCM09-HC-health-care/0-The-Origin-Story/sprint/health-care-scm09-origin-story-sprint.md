## 1. Start with the story

A footprint figure for a medical consumable at a hospital network is reported as lower than last year.

**Supply chain lead:** Ravi Menon — Procurement and Clinical Asset Director (OHS-P15).  
**Counterpart:** Priya Nair — Patient Identity and Data Governance Lead (OHS-P09).  
**Operations or data owner:** Michael Chen — Group CFO (OHS-P02).

Ravi Menon asks what must be true before the team decides. Priya Nair asks which evidence and owner support the decision. Michael Chen asks for the outcome that matters to the people affected.

**Evidence reveal:** In the synthetic record, the boundary changed between periods, and one supplier figure was an estimate.

**Pause:** What must be true for this decision to stand? Which evidence is missing, and who can confirm it? Ask the counterpart one discovery question before choosing a solution.

**Case boundary:** OmniHealth Systems Ltd. is an aspirational case study (USD 34B healthcare group; 126,000 staff; 19 countries). These incidents, services and dialogue are proposed learning scenarios, not corporate history, confirmed deployments or achieved results. Preserve the enterprise persona IDs above. Country-specific permissions, contracts and operating rules require validation; this sprint does not invent them.

## 2. Trace the origin: 1970–2026

These overlapping periods are teaching lenses, not invention dates or a universal adoption sequence. Earlier practices can coexist with newer ones. Pre-1978 scenes describe the wider domain, not OmniHealth Systems Ltd. history.

- **1970s–1980s:** Environmental impact is rarely measured.
- **1980s–1990s:** Compliance reporting begins; methods differ.
- **2000s–2010s:** Carbon accounting spreads; boundaries and estimates vary.
- **2020s–2026:** Assisted data collection helps; boundaries, evidence and verification still need owners.

**Mindset shift:** start with the service promise, the evidence and who carries the risk, then choose the model or automation. Automating an unclear step can move the same error faster.

**Pause:** Which old problem survives in a newer interface? Who bears its cost?

## 3. Explore possible futures: 2030–2050

**2030 experiment:** allow assistance to draft scenarios and flag exceptions; retain the evidence gate, a named decision owner and a challenge route.

**2040 question:** if partners share signals directly, can each node's evidence be visible without exposing commercial detail?

**2050 scene:** Imagine a network that adjusts itself within agreed promises. Providers may react faster, but people must own the promise, the trade-offs and the right to stop.

**Competing future:** A simpler network with buffers, clear fallbacks and trained planners may beat an adaptive one on resilience and trust.

All future passages are design hypotheses, not forecasts or product roadmaps. Test a useful part today rather than assume the future will arrive as described.

## 4. Draw the smallest useful architecture

**Capability — OHS-SCM09-OS-CAP01:** comparable sustainability evidence. A capability is an ability; it is not an application or a sequence of steps.

**Value stages — OHS-SCM09-OS-VS01:** Define boundary → collect data → label estimates → calculate → verify → report.

**Objects to distinguish:** boundary, activity data, estimate, factor and claim.

**Proposed responsibility:** Sustainability owns the method; operations owns data; suppliers own their figures. SCM09 owns the evidence flow, not the reporting standard.

**Protected boundary:** A footprint figure is not comparable unless its boundary and estimates are stated.

Planning, manufacturing and logistics applications are optional demonstration candidates, chosen after requirements. No product is assumed installed, licensed or able to cover the whole chain. This sprint focuses on application responsibility while referencing data, integration and security owners.

## 5. Build one Decision Card

Use synthetic case **OHS-SCM09-OS-SYN001**. Synthetic footprint OHS-FP300, boundary versions OHS-BD300 and OHS-BD301, estimate flagged. Keep everything on one card or screen.

1. **Need:** the outcome, affected people and one missing fact.
2. **Evidence:** relevant object IDs, source, validity or effective time, data purpose and permitted access.
3. **Ownership:** business decision owner, authoritative application and receiving owner.
4. **Gate:** prerequisite, allowed next state, authorized actor and the message anyone affected will see.
5. **Alternative:** Compare reporting the lower figure with restating on a common boundary.
6. **Exception:** one failure state, escalation owner and a safe recovery or assisted route.
7. **Proof:** the three results below, one measure and a stop, revise or proceed decision.

**Requirement — OHS-SCM09-OS-REQ01:** link one sustainability-evidence experiment hypothesis to an owned intervention and observed follow-up, including negative evidence.

**Decision — OHS-SCM09-OS-ADR01:** record your chosen option and the alternative from item 5, one trade-off, and the evidence that would change your choice.

## 6. Run three tabletop tests

### T01 — The boundary changes between periods

**Expected:** Restate on a comparable boundary before comparing.

### T02 — A supplier figure is an estimate

**Expected:** Label it as an estimate and show the range.

### T03 — A reduction claim has no evidence

**Expected:** Do not report it until evidence and an owner exist.

For each, record **input → expected → actual paper result → evidence note → revision → retest**. These are sprint-local tests OHS-SCM09-OS-T01/T02/T03, supporting OHS-SCM09-OS-REQ01. A completed lesson is not an executed tenant test.

**Measure — OHS-SCM09-OS-KPI01:** Share of reported figures with stated boundary, evidence and estimate labels; define window and exclusions. Assign Ravi Menon as proposed measure owner, establish the baseline and exclusions, and review after the experiment. No numeric improvement is asserted.

**Acceptance:** the card identifies source and owner, preserves the protected boundary, handles all three tests after documented revisions and explains the decision in 60 seconds. Missing evidence remains visible. Record what is still unverified.

## 7. Use your 120 minutes

- **0–20:** read the origin timeline; explain what changed and what did not.
- **20–45:** role-play the story; ask the lead, counterpart and voice one useful question each.
- **45–65:** draw responsibilities and consult one resource selectively.
- **65–100:** complete the single Decision Card and compare the alternative.
- **100–115:** run the three tests; revise from observed paper behavior.
- **115–120:** explain the decision in 60 seconds and choose the next experiment.

All reading fits within these blocks. Do not complete an entire product course during the sprint.
