### Day 2 V2: Concept 1: Sense and Plan: Integrated and Demand Planning
*Duration: 12-15 min*

**Hook:** The best supply chains are decided before the first truck moves.

Did you share your notes in the previous video? [pause] If not, pause here, drop them in the comments, then come back. Sharing is how learning becomes yours.

Welcome back. Today's concept is **Sense and Plan: Integrated and Demand Planning**. It anchors two product academies: ASC1 IBP for Supply Chain and APS2 IBP for Demand. Together with the 11 GICS sectors that is 22 of the 132 courses in our meta anchor file, for example ASC1-EN, ASC1-MA, ASC1-IN ... (22 courses in this concept). [show slide: concept map]

**1. What is the concept?**
Integrated Business Planning (IBP) connects demand, supply, inventory and finance in one plan. IBP for Demand sharpens the starting point: statistical forecasting, demand sensing and consensus on what customers will actually buy. [pause]

**2. Why is it important?**
Plans built in silos create stockouts in one place and excess in another. A weak demand signal forces every downstream function to compensate with expensive buffers, expediting and write-offs.
Here is a real pattern I have seen. A consumer staples company planned in spreadsheets by region, so promotions surprised the factories every quarter. Moving to a single demand and integrated plan cut emergency shipments and released working capital within two planning cycles. [pause]

**3. Benefits of implementing it the right way**
When you get this right, here is what changes: One agreed plan across commercial, supply and finance; Better forecast accuracy and earlier demand signals; Lower inventory with higher service levels; Faster, scenario-based planning decisions. [on screen: four benefits]

**Sector lens.** Sector lens: Consumer Staples plans around promotions and seasonality; Health Care plans around shelf life and critical availability; Energy plans around outages and maintenance windows.

Together with the 11 GICS sectors that is 22 of the 132 courses in our meta anchor file, for example APN3-EN, APN3-MA, APN3-IN ... (22 courses in this concept). [show slide: concept map]

**1. What is the concept?**
IBP for Supply and S&OP balances demand against capacity, materials and inventory, and runs the monthly Sales and Operations Planning cycle that aligns the business. Business Network Supply Chain Collaboration shares forecasts, orders and inventory with suppliers and partners. [pause]

**2. Why is it important?**
Internal balance is not enough when suppliers cannot see your plan. Late signals to partners produce shortages and expedite costs, while an S&OP meeting without data turns into opinion.
Here is a real pattern I have seen. An industrials manufacturer shared a rolling forecast with its top suppliers through a network. Suppliers reserved capacity earlier, and chronic material shortages stopped appearing on the monthly escalation list. [pause]

**3. Benefits of implementing it the right way**
When you get this right, here is what changes: Executable supply plans that respect constraints; A disciplined S&OP rhythm with one set of numbers; Suppliers and partners reacting to real signals early; Fewer shortages and last-minute escalations. [on screen: four benefits]

### Day 2 V4: Concept 3: Make with Quality: Manufacturing and Quality
*Duration: 12-15 min*

**Hook:** Quality is not an inspection step. It is how you make things.

Did you share your notes in the previous video? [pause] If not, pause here, drop them in the comments, then come back. Sharing is how learning becomes yours.

Welcome back. Today's concept is **Make with Quality: Manufacturing and Quality**. It anchors two product academies: AMP4 Digital Manufacturing and AQC7 Quality Management. Together with the 11 GICS sectors that is 22 of the 132 courses in our meta anchor file, for example AMP4-EN, AMP4-MA, AMP4-IN ... (22 courses in this concept). [show slide: concept map]

**1. What is the concept?**
Digital Manufacturing connects the plan to the shop floor: work orders, execution, tracking and performance on the line. Quality Management defines inspections, controls and corrective actions so defects are prevented, caught and traced. [pause]

**2. Why is it important?**
Plans mean nothing if production cannot execute them, and output means little if quality fails. Disconnected shop floor data hides losses, while late quality findings create scrap, recalls and damaged trust.
Here is a real pattern I have seen. A materials producer saw a recurring defect only at final inspection. Linking shop floor data with quality records showed the cause in one machine setting, and the defect rate fell within weeks. [pause]

**3. Benefits of implementing it the right way**
When you get this right, here is what changes: Plan-to-execution visibility on the shop floor; Less scrap, rework and unplanned downtime; Traceability from supplier batch to customer shipment; Quality built into the process, not inspected at the end. [on screen: four benefits]

**Sector lens.** Sector lens: Health Care manufacturing carries strict traceability and regulatory quality; Industrials focuses on uptime and yield.
