**1. What is the concept?**
Integrated Business Planning (IBP) connects demand, supply, inventory and finance in one plan. IBP for Demand sharpens the starting point: statistical forecasting, demand sensing and consensus on what customers will actually buy. [pause]

**2. Why is it important?**
Plans built in silos create stockouts in one place and excess in another. A weak demand signal forces every downstream function to compensate with expensive buffers, expediting and write-offs.
Here is a real pattern I have seen. A consumer staples company planned in spreadsheets by region, so promotions surprised the factories every quarter. Moving to a single demand and integrated plan cut emergency shipments and released working capital within two planning cycles. [pause]

**3. Benefits of implementing it the right way**
When you get this right, here is what changes: One agreed plan across commercial, supply and finance; Better forecast accuracy and earlier demand signals; Lower inventory with higher service levels; Faster, scenario-based planning decisions. [on screen: four benefits]

**Sector lens.** Sector lens: Consumer Staples plans around promotions and seasonality; Health Care plans around shelf life and critical availability; Energy plans around outages and maintenance windows.
