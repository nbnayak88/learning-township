## 2. Trace the origin: 1970–2026

These overlapping periods are teaching lenses, not invention dates or a universal adoption sequence. Earlier practices can coexist with newer ones. Pre-1978 scenes describe the wider domain, not OmniTech Solutions Ltd. history.

- **1970s–1980s:** Distribution is planned by experience and paper; networks grow by habit.
- **1980s–1990s:** Planning systems and optimisation models appear; assumptions are often hidden in the model.
- **2000s–2010s:** Global sourcing and lean inventory cut cost; disruption exposes single points of failure.
- **2020s–2026:** Scenario tools and assisted analysis speed options; a named owner still decides which risk to carry.

**Mindset shift:** start with the service promise and who carries the risk, then choose the model. Optimising an unclear objective can move cost and risk to someone else.

**Pause:** Which old problem survives in a newer interface? Who bears its cost?

## 3. Explore possible futures: 2030–2050

**2030 experiment:** allow assistance to draft scenarios and surface constraints; retain the promise, a named decision owner and a challenge route.

**2040 question:** if partners share signals directly, can each node's constraints be visible without exposing commercial detail?

**2050 scene:** Imagine a network that rebalances itself within agreed promises. Providers may react faster, but people must own the promise and the trade-offs.

**Competing future:** A simpler network with buffers and clear fallback may beat a lean, adaptive one on resilience and trust.

All future passages are design hypotheses, not forecasts or product roadmaps. Test a useful part today rather than assume the future will arrive as described.

## 4. Draw the smallest useful architecture

**Capability — OTS-SCM01-OS-CAP01:** service-promise and network decisioning. A capability is an ability; it is not an application or a sequence of steps.

**Value stages — OTS-SCM01-OS-VS01:** Define the service promise → model options → decide → plan → execute → learn.

**Objects to distinguish:** service promise, network node, lane, capacity, constraint and scenario.

**Proposed responsibility:** Supply chain owns the network decision; operations owns capacity and constraints; finance owns the cost view; the customer-facing owner confirms the promise. SCM01 owns the strategy loop, not the planning system build.

**Protected boundary:** A network scenario score does not prove the service promise can be kept, or that cost savings are not simply shifted to a partner or customer.

Planning and logistics applications are optional demonstration candidates, chosen after requirements. No product is assumed installed, licensed or able to cover the whole network. This sprint focuses on application responsibility while referencing data, integration and security owners.

## 5. Build one Decision Card

Use synthetic case **OTS-SCM01-OS-SYN001**. Synthetic sourcing node IT-N300, client deployment IT-DEP300, part IT-PRT300, service promise. Keep everything on one card or screen.

1. **Need:** the outcome, affected people and one missing fact.
2. **Evidence:** relevant object IDs, source, validity or effective time, data purpose and permitted access.
3. **Ownership:** business decision owner, authoritative application and receiving owner.
4. **Gate:** prerequisite, allowed next state, authorized actor and the message anyone affected will see.
5. **Alternative:** Compare the lowest-cost network with an option that protects the service promise and names the risk owner.
6. **Exception:** one failure state, escalation owner and a safe recovery or assisted route.
7. **Proof:** the three results below, one measure and a stop, revise or proceed decision.

**Requirement — OTS-SCM01-OS-REQ01:** link one network-strategy hypothesis to an owned intervention and observed follow-up, including negative evidence.

**Decision — OTS-SCM01-OS-ADR01:** record your chosen option and the alternative from item 5, one trade-off, and the evidence that would change your choice.

## 6. Run three tabletop tests

### T01 — Deployment dates are not modelled

**Expected:** Add the client promise to the objective.

### T02 — Cost savings shift delay to clients

**Expected:** Show the client effect and record the owner.

### T03 — A key supplier is delayed

**Expected:** Use the fallback supplier and a named owner.

For each, record **input → expected → actual paper result → evidence note → revision → retest**. These are sprint-local tests OTS-SCM01-OS-T01/T02/T03, supporting OTS-SCM01-OS-REQ01. A completed lesson is not an executed tenant test.

**Measure — OTS-SCM01-OS-KPI01:** Service-promise attainment for the chosen segment, with a defined window and comparison population; record where risk is carried. Assign Ingrid Sørensen as proposed measure owner, establish the baseline and exclusions, and review after the experiment. No numeric improvement is asserted.

**Acceptance:** the card identifies source and owner, preserves the protected boundary, handles all three tests after documented revisions and explains the decision in 60 seconds. Missing evidence remains visible. Record what is still unverified.

## 7. Use your 120 minutes

- **0–20:** read the origin timeline; explain what changed and what did not.
- **20–45:** role-play the story; ask the lead, counterpart and voice one useful question each.
