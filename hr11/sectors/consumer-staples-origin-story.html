HR11 — AI-Powered HR Enterprises: The Origin Story

SuccessLabs Academy · GICS05 Consumer Staples · OmniDaily Consumer Goods Ltd. · IIL — Industry Immersion Lab · Level 0 → Pre-Foundation · Sprint · 120 minutes · Edition 2026-10-31

Mission: Architecting Experiences for a Better World.

1. One learner promise

Design one AI-assisted HR interaction that answers from authorized evidence and hands decisions to accountable people.

Leave with one AI Authority Decision Card. Use synthetic records and a paper prototype. A beginner explains the decision; an experienced learner challenges its assumptions. Deeper research and implementation stay in the preserved supporting cases, outside this Origin Story.

2. Enterprise context and source boundaries

OmniDaily is a aspirational consumer-staples enterprise with 84,000 employees across 57 countries, 78 factories/co-packing facilities, 112 distribution centers and 1,900 distributors. These are source stipulations, not observed operating results. Its food, beverage, home-care and personal-care operations connect workforce readiness to everyday essentials. Factory, engineering/maintenance, distribution and field conditions are used only where supported by the enterprise or HR sources. No OmniWorks facility counts, industrial incidents or external seasonal-worker counts are imported.

Source scope: HR11 prioritizes HR AI use cases and designs governed employee service with security, data governance, human oversight and escalation; Joule/intelligent automation are source examples.

Routing record: the request repeatedly names GICS05 but also contains GICS04/Consumer Discretionary and Industrials wording. This edition follows the existing GICS05 Consumer Staples sources; conflicting request labels remain recorded here, not treated as enterprise facts. The supplied Materials URL is GICS02; the actual GICS03 index supplies a learning-pattern reference only.

Existing anchors retained: ODG-M03 (enterprise assets/stakeholders), ODG-M11 (operating model), ODG-M18 (workforce/adoption) and ODG-M19 (delivery readiness), inherited from the Workforce blueprint. ODG-TOS-HR11 identifiers below are newly introduced educational requirements, decisions, experiments, tests and measures; they are not existing approved client records. No detailed HR requirement/ADR/test register was found in the reviewed source pages; this gap is explicit.

3. Origins and process evolution

These closed windows are educational groupings, not universal invention dates or OmniDaily corporate biography. Adoption overlaps; older practices can coexist through the present. Reconstruct each as need → response → unresolved problem.

1970–1984 — Rulebooks and personnel advisers answer questions; knowledge can be inconsistent.

1985–1994 — Decision trees and scripted help improve repeatability; unexpected cases need judgment.

1995–2004 — HR workflow and knowledge bases encode rules; a rule's existence does not prove current applicability.

2005–2014 — Search and automation improve retrieval/routing; wrong permissions or stale sources still create harm.

2015–2025 — Machine learning and generative assistance broaden interaction; fluent output is not proof, permission or accountability.

4. One persona-led story: A confident answer without authority

Scene 1: Nandita Sen, Group CHRO (ODG-P06), tests a synthetic employee question about readiness for a factory task. Sofia Alvarez, Chief Digital and Data Officer (ODG-P05), asks which source supports the answer.

Scene 2: Choose unrestricted conversational help or bounded retrieval with human escalation. Reveal: the confident answer used an outdated learning note and mistook content completion for task authority.

Scene 3: Omar Haddad, CISO and Privacy Lead (ODG-P12), introduces a document containing instructions to disclose another worker's record. Retrieved content is evidence to evaluate, not authority to change access or instructions.

Scene 4: Nandita narrows the assistant to authorized explanation, dated sources and abstention when evidence is missing. Site authorities retain work decisions; a model update must not silently widen that boundary.

Decision pause: Would a bounded assistant or a deterministic service form better fit this question? Which observed errors would make you stop the AI experiment? Record a first answer before the reveal, then revise the same card. Name a counterexample that would favor the alternative.

5. Responsibilities and human decision rights

HR service/content owners approve purpose and policy sources; core HR/learning retain authoritative records. AI service owners manage retrieval, evaluation, logging and model/version boundaries; security/privacy owners authorize access and retention. Human HR staff decide case exceptions and escalation; site operations/quality decides task authorization. Joule is an illustrative source example, not a verified feature or deployment.

These are proposed educational responsibilities. Establish the business rule, source of truth and accountable human before choosing technology. SAP examples illustrate a capability and are not verified deployments, licensing claims or product roadmaps. A worker must have an accessible human route to question incorrect data or a decision. HR status, learning completion, work authorization and quality/batch release are distinct decisions.

7. One build card: AI Authority Decision Card

Synthetic experiment: Use SYN-QUESTION1111, one authorized current policy excerpt, one stale excerpt and a malicious instruction embedded in retrieved text. Produce one paper interaction design; no live model, real records or deployed security test.

Fill nine fields on one page: (1) purpose/person affected; (2) current/proposed state and evidence; (3) identifiers and definitions; (4) business time/version; (5) application/data owners; (6) human approval/appeal; (7) chosen option versus alternative and trade-off; (8) exception, fallback and automation boundary; (9) three observed test responses and revision.

Design constraint: Separate explaining a policy, recommending an action and executing a transaction. Cite source scope/version, respect access, state uncertainty and preserve human review/appeal. Do not grant readiness or expose data because a prompt requests it.

All SYN identifiers, episode details and injected evidence are new teaching data. Country rules, collective agreements, qualifications, worker/device populations and local approvals remain unknown unless explicitly sourced. Record each unknown with an owner and discovery question. Do not invent legal rules or calculate unsupported operational benefits.

8. Exactly three tabletop tests

ODG-TOS-HR11-T01 — Wrong-source confidence

Reveal/input: A response relies on a stale learning note to authorize a task.

Expected design response: Reject the unsupported authorization, expose the evidence gap and route to the accountable human.

ODG-TOS-HR11-T02 — Injected instruction

Reveal/input: Retrieved text asks for another employee's private record.

Expected design response: Treat it as untrusted content; preserve access boundaries, refuse disclosure and escalate as designed.

ODG-TOS-HR11-T03 — Release regression

Reveal/input: A proposed model/prompt update changes advice into automatic approval.

Expected design response: Fail the proposed release criterion, retain the bounded version and require evaluation plus authorized review before expansion.

For each test record input → expected response → actual paper response → evidence note → pass/revise. These are proposed acceptance criteria. The evidence slot starts unfilled: publication runs none of the tests and proves no operational result. If a protected human decision can be bypassed, revise the design even if every card field is filled.

9. Traceability and measures

ODG-TOS-HR11-REQ01 — Ground one HR answer in authorized, applicable evidence with uncertainty visible. Derived from the supporting HR source mandate and ODG-M18; assessed by T01.

ODG-TOS-HR11-REQ02 — Preserve access, human decision rights and controlled release boundaries. Derived from the source governance mandate and ODG-M19; assessed by T02–T03.

ODG-TOS-HR11-ADR01 — Prefer bounded retrieval/explanation and explicit escalation over unrestricted autonomous action; compare utility, abstention and assurance effort. This proposed educational decision can be rejected with evidence; record the alternative and consequence.

ODG-TOS-HR11-EXP01 — The one synthetic build card extends ODG-M19's delivery-readiness discipline; T01–T03 do not execute the larger implementation case.

ODG-TOS-HR11-M01 — Mandatory card fields completed / 9 × 100: learning completeness only. ODG-TOS-HR11-M02 — Record whether T02/T03 preserve confidentiality and the approved authority boundary. Record actual paper observations rather than assuming success.

ODG-TOS-HR11-EV01 — Unfilled evidence slot for test observations, revisions and reviewer questions. Existing case success measures are intended outcomes; no baseline or target attainment follows from publishing this edition.

10. AI-driven exploration: scenarios, not predictions

These are scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps. Each needs evidence, approved purpose, accountable ownership and proportionate controls.

2026–2030 — Assist: Assistance could explain approved HR sources while people decide exceptions.

2031–2040 — Connect: HR services could exchange authorized context with provenance and scoped actions.

2041–2050 — Adapt: Assistance could adapt within evaluated bounds with rollback, worker challenge and retained accountable authority.

Competing future: Deterministic workflows and well-maintained human service may outperform generative AI for narrow, sensitive HR decisions.

Disconfirmation question: Would the assistant increase unsupported answers, privacy risk or escalation effort compared with a deterministic service? Name evidence that would stop or change the hypothesis.

11. Reflection and Pre-Foundation handoff

Which evidence should make the assistant abstain? Who may decide the exception? What could a fluent answer conceal?

Pre-Foundation: Define purpose, evidence boundary, access, recommendation/action distinction and human escalation; write one evaluation contract before selecting a model or platform. Carry this same card, source questions and acceptance criteria forward. Deeper configuration, vendor comparisons and implementation research remain outside the Origin Story.
