3. Origins and process evolution

These closed windows are educational groupings, not universal invention dates or OmniCapital corporate biography. Adoption overlaps; paper, spreadsheets, ERP and cloud practices can coexist through the present.

Teaching window Process response and persistent question
1970–1984 Annual paper appraisals depend on manager recall.
1985–1994 Rating databases improve storage but retain subjective judgments.
1995–2004 Goal cascading links business plans and reviews; comparability remains difficult.
2005–2014 Online check-ins add feedback; frequency is not quality.
2015–2025 Continuous performance and analytics combine signals; attribution and fairness still need review.

Counterexample: digitizing an incomplete record makes it easier to retrieve, not more trustworthy. Which decision was the original record intended to support?

4. One persona-led story

Priya Rao (OCF-P03), Chief Customer and Retail Officer, proposes a top rating for a synthetic relationship-manager team. Elena Fischer (OCF-P05), Chief Compliance and Conduct Officer, brings unresolved suitability complaints. Nadia Fernandes (OCF-P07), Group CHRO, must support fair calibration without turning an allegation into a proven finding.

Choose before revealing: Award the rating on sales alone, automatically penalize the team, or seek role-relevant evidence before calibration? Record your first reason on the card.

Evidence reveal: The synthetic sales target was met; complaints are unresolved and one complaint belongs to another team. Priya withdraws a sales-only recommendation. Elena separates investigation evidence from the performance record; Nadia requests corrected attribution and gives employees a response route.

Revise your decision: Balance outputs with customer and control evidence. Unresolved allegations require governed review, not automatic guilt or automatic exclusion. Preserve an accessible human route to challenge errors. Financial service continuity matters, but an urgent cut-off does not create authority. Customer KYC and employee readiness are different processes; do not copy customer identity checks into HR without purpose and policy review.

5. Application responsibilities and human authority

Frame the business need first; applications implement the chosen operating model. The following is a proposed teaching architecture. Confirm named delegates, country policy, legal basis and actual product scope before implementation.

Application responsibility Accountable data owner Human decision right
Performance and goals; SAP example HR performance owner and managers own goals and review evidence Authorized manager proposes; calibration panel decides with employee response.
Customer and conduct evidence Customer-service steward owns complaint attribution; Conduct owns investigation status Elena’s delegated reviewer resolves findings; HR cannot rewrite investigation evidence.
Rewards handoff Reward owner owns approved rating-to-reward policy Compensation committee authorizes awards separately; an unapproved score is not payroll input.

Core HR owns workforce facts; domain owners own performance, learning, rewards, time or payroll facts as applicable. IAM executes access policy; integration transports and reconciles states; analytics reads authorized data and does not become its owner. AI may summarize permitted evidence or draft a reminder, but cannot approve consequential employment, regulated duties, payments or access. An interface acknowledgment is not proof of the business outcome. SAP examples illustrate responsibilities, not guaranteed features or universal localization.

7. One Decision Build Card

Synthetic input: Team FIN-TEAM300; sales attainment 110% (synthetic); three complaint entries, one misattributed; conduct finding pending; rating proposal unapproved.

Keep all work in this one card. Capture: business problem; initial and revised choice with reason; entity/identity/date semantics; authoritative source and accountable owner; human approval boundary; one justified local variant with purpose/minimum data/viewers; known versus unknown downstream state; exception owner and approved fallback; all three test observations and evidence references. Missing evidence stays blank or unknown, never silently becomes approval.

Compare one alternative: a manual controlled handoff may be preferable to automation when exception ownership or recovery is absent. State the evidence needed to change your mind. Avoid collecting real screening, health, payroll, customer or conduct records in this learning card.

8. Exactly three tabletop tests

Test Input / reveal Expected proposed behavior Evidence to record
OCF-HR03-T01 — Misattributed evidence Reveal one complaint is linked to the wrong team. Correct attribution with lineage; do not silently delete the original reference. Source, correction reason, owner and revised assessment.
OCF-HR03-T02 — Unresolved finding Panel receives an allegation with no concluded investigation. Keep status explicit; follow approved review/defer policy; offer a human response route. Decision basis, permitted evidence and due-process gap.
OCF-HR03-T03 — Incomparable roles Apply the sales scorecard to an independent risk team. Reject the identical metric; define role-relevant control outcomes before comparison. Revised goal, accountable owner and counterexample.

For each test, record input → expected → observed → revision/evidence → Pass, Revise or Blocked. All observed results are initially unfilled. Publication does not execute any tabletop, system test, approval or operational transaction. Acceptance requires a defensible card and all three observed tabletop results with revisions; bypassing a protected human decision requires redesign.

10. Separate AI-driven exploration: 2026–2050

Scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps.

Scenario window Question to explore
2026–2030 — Assist Could approved-source assistance explain evidence gaps and draft next steps without approving the decision?
2031–2040 — Connect Could separately authorized services coordinate this handoff while keeping unknown states and local rights visible?
2041–2050 — Adapt Could reviewed feedback propose reversible process changes while people retain consequential authority?

Competing future: fragmented systems, uneven access, policy restrictions and manual review persist; a simpler controlled workflow outperforms orchestration. Falsifiable hypothesis question: Would more frequent feedback improve fairness if complaint attribution and manager capability stay weak? Define an observation that would disprove your claim—for example fewer steps but more unresolved exceptions or worse worker outcomes. Compare against a manual baseline before claiming value.

11. Reflect and move to Pre-Foundation

What evidence changed your first decision? Which authority did you initially assume? Who bears the exception burden? What would make you stop this design? Pre-Foundation handoff: Define goal, measure, evidence, behavior and rating. Build one role-specific goal with an acceptance rule before configuring review forms. Continue the same semi-fiction through Pre-Foundation (concepts/data), Foundation (small working model), Essentials (end-to-end handoffs), Advanced (exceptions and resilience), and Ultimate (architect-level value realization, troubleshooting, lessons and evidence-based ROI). These are future learning handoffs, not published deeper editions or additional Origin Story formats.

12. Architecture alignment and separate axes

TOGAF: compare architecture options and document decisions. BIZBOK: connect capability and value. BABOK: identify needs, requirements and acceptance evidence. DAMA-DMBOK: assign ownership, definitions, quality and lineage. COBIT: distinguish accountability, controls and monitoring. Relevant ISO/IEC information-security principles: confidentiality, integrity, availability, access and controlled change. Alignment is educational; no certification, conformance or compliance is claimed.

HR classification = HR03 High-Performance Organizations. Architecture classification = the 12 distinct streams (Enterprise, Business, Data, Application, Technology, AI, Security, UI-UX, Infrastructure, Integration, Domain, Industry), applied selectively. Enterprise transformation classification = Workforce. These axes relate but are not interchangeable.
