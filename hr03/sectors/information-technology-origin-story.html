More commits, less client value

One learner promise

In 120 minutes: Design one fair performance review that connects contribution to quality and client outcomes. One persona-led story, one Decision Build Card and exactly three tabletop tests. Use synthetic records. All choices, observations and revisions stay on this one card.

OmniTech is a aspirational multinational IT enterprise. Its source profile stipulates 98,000 employees, 41 countries and cloud/platform, enterprise software, cybersecurity, AI/data and managed-service businesses. These are aspirational baselines, not independently measured facts. Existing source targets and roadmaps are proposals, not achieved outcomes. Origins: the question that survives each era

These closed windows group process evolution for teaching; they are not universal invention or adoption dates. Adoption overlaps. Paper, spreadsheets and older systems can coexist through the present. 1970–1984 — Annual supervisor appraisals summarize individual work 1985–1994 — Personnel databases standardize ratings 1995–2004 — Goal systems connect organizational and individual objectives 2005–2014 — Continuous feedback adds coaching and calibration 2015–2025 — Digital telemetry and AI summaries expose attribution and fairness limits A team outcome cannot automatically be attributed to an individual; activity is not contribution.

One story: choose, reveal, revise

Maya Sterling (OTS-P03), Chief Product Officer, sees a synthetic platform team's commit count rise while customer recovery work grows. Priya Shah (OTS-P13), DevSecOps and Release Director, objects to ranking engineers by commits. Nadia Fernandes (OTS-P07), Group CHRO, initially supports a productivity leaderboard because it appears easy to compare. Choose before reading on: Reward volume, discard all measures, or use role-relevant evidence with quality and collaboration? Evidence reveal: The largest commit total includes generated code and repeated fixes. A quieter engineer prevented a security defect and coached colleagues. Nadia replaces the ranking with role-relevant evidence, quality and collaboration criteria, a manager conversation and calibration. No rating is finalized until the employee can review the evidence. Write what changed your decision and what remains unknown. An accessible explanation and a human challenge route must be available to the affected worker. An uncertain record cannot silently become an authorization.

Application responsibility and human authority

Performance workflow — HR owns review criteria and confidentiality. Manager proposes assessment; calibration panel challenges evidence. Engineering telemetry — Engineering owns contextual delivery/quality records. Release owner validates interpretation, not employee rating. Customer outcomes — Customer success owns client outcome evidence. Client-facing owner confirms attribution limits. Learning — Learning owner records development actions. Employee and manager agree practical development. Business owners remain accountable; applications store and process the records. Discover the actual delegated approvers before implementation. Restrict client, candidate, pay and health information to its purpose and permitted viewers. Minimize replicated data and unnecessary compute rather than assuming automation is sustainable. SAP examples in the preserved source are discussion options, not a technology selection or a claim that configuration has been validated. Start with the business need. AI may summarize permitted evidence or draft a reminder; it cannot approve a consequential people decision, grant privilege or declare a payment complete.

Your 120-minute sprint

0–10: Read the promise and frame the people/business need. 10–30: Reconstruct the five teaching windows and one enduring question. 30–45: Choose, reveal evidence and revise the decision. 45–60: Assign application responsibilities, data owners and human decision rights. 60–90: Complete the one build card; compare an alternative and a justified local variant. 90–110: Walk through exactly three tabletop tests and record observations on the same card. 110–120: Give a 60-second explanation, reflect and name the Pre-Foundation question. Selective resource reading fits within this time. No additional deliverable is required.

One build experiment: Decision Build Card

Synthetic inputs: Team PLATFORM-300; synthetic engineer IT-P300; commit index 120; baseline 100; incident context missing; coaching and defect-prevention evidence later revealed. Index units are illustrative, not business results. Complete these fields once: 1. People and business need: who benefits, what could go wrong and why this matters in IT services or engineering. 2. Initial choice and reason: compare the choices before seeing the reveal. 3. Evidence reveal and revised choice: state what changed, and one counterexample. 4. Identity, date and source owner: identify authoritative records, effective time, provenance and uncertain facts. 5. Application responsibility: separate record keeping, orchestration, calculation, analytics and access enforcement. 6. Human approval and protected action: name the decision role, limits and worker challenge route. 7. Minimum data and permitted viewers: state purpose, retention question, client isolation and accessible communication. 8. Alternative and local variant: defend a common core with a justified regional/client variant, or explain a different design. 9. Exception owner and recovery: define hold, escalation, retry/reconciliation and confirmation conditions. 10. Measure and missing evidence: Proposed assessments supported by role-relevant verified evidence / assessments reviewed; disputed evidence remains unresolved. 11. Pre-Foundation question: Define goal, measure, evidence, feedback and rating separately; write one role-relevant acceptance criterion. For each test below record input → expected → observed paper result → evidence → revision. Blank observations remain blank. A complete card can still contain a poor decision.

Exactly three tabletop tests

OTS-HR03-T01 — Volume high, quality uncertain Expected proposed behavior: Do not infer a high rating from commits; request contextual quality evidence. Evidence to record: Record missing denominator, work type and revised criteria. Observed result / evidence / revision: initially unfilled; learner completes during the exercise.

OTS-HR03-T02 — Unequal role comparisons Expected proposed behavior: Compare against role expectations, not raw counts across dissimilar jobs; inspect collaboration and opportunity. Evidence to record: Record normalization choice and employee challenge. Observed result / evidence / revision: initially unfilled; learner completes during the exercise.

OTS-HR03-T03 — Evidence contested Expected proposed behavior: Keep assessment provisional and route a confidential human review; preserve original evidence and correction history. Evidence to record: Record review owner, revised rationale and restricted access. Observed result / evidence / revision: initially unfilled; learner completes during the exercise. Acceptance: one complete card, three recorded tabletop observations after any revisions, and an explanation of source ownership and human authority. A breached authority or privacy boundary requires redesign. Publishing these tests does not execute them.

2026–2050: explore and challenge

2026–2030 — Assist: Summarize permitted review evidence. 2031–2040 — Connect: Connect team outcomes to contextual contribution evidence. 2041–2050 — Adapt: Adapt goals with employee participation and accountable review. These are scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps. Competing future: restrictive client contracts, unreliable data and fragmented regional systems favor smaller human-led workflows. Disproof question: would the proposed assistance still improve this decision if the trusted source is unavailable, stale or contested? Define evidence that would make you reject the hypothesis; preserve worker agency and an accountable stop route.

Reflection and the next chapter

Which evidence changed your first choice? Which record could be digitally available yet wrong? How can the affected worker challenge it? What would make your chosen alternative better? Pre-Foundation handoff: Define goal, measure, evidence, feedback and rating separately; write one role-relevant acceptance criterion. Continue the same semi-aspirational thread: Pre-Foundation defines the language and boundaries; Foundation models the process and data; Essentials connects the end-to-end flow; Advanced investigates exceptions and recovery; Ultimate concludes with measured value, costs, ROI assumptions, troubleshooting and lessons learned. These are handoff directions, not newly published deeper-level curricula. Ultimate must distinguish observed benefits from targets and unvalidated ROI.

Educational architecture alignment

TOGAF: business need, options, architecture decisions and transition evidence. BIZBOK: capabilities and value streams. BABOK: requirements, alternatives and acceptance criteria. DAMA-DMBOK: authoritative ownership, quality, lineage and definitions. COBIT: accountability, controls and monitoring. Relevant ISO/IEC security and quality principles: least privilege, controlled change, assurance evidence and continual improvement. This is educational alignment, not certification, compliance or a conformance assessment. Classification axes are separate: HR theme = HR03 High-Performance Organizations; enterprise transformation stream = Workforce; architecture views = Enterprise, Business, Data, Application, Technology, AI, Security, UI/UX, Infrastructure, Integration, Domain and Industry as relevant. Cross-references do not make these interchangeable.
