HR03 — High-Performance Organizations: The Origin Story

SuccessLabs Academy · GICS03 Industrials · OmniWorks Industrial Systems Ltd. · IIL — Industry Immersion Lab · Level 0 → Pre-Foundation · Sprint · 120 minutes · Edition 2026-10-31

Mission: Architecting Experiences for a Better World.

1. Your one promise

Design one performance review that recognizes sustainable industrial contribution and gives workers a fair way to challenge incomplete evidence.

Produce one Sustainable Contribution Build Card. Use synthetic records and a paper prototype. Beginners explain the decision in plain language; experienced learners challenge the same decision's assumptions. Configuration and implementation research remain in the supporting case outside this sprint.

2. Read the enterprise and sources accurately

OmniWorks is aspirational: 74,000 employees, 36 countries, 61 manufacturing/engineering/assembly facilities and 320 service depots. The 61 is a mixed-facility count, not a confirmed plant-only count. Engineering, maintenance, production and field service shape the workforce problem. Case targets and victory criteria are aspirations, not achieved results. This edition adds a bounded synthetic episode; it does not assert a deployment.

Source-scoped lead: Maya Sen, Vice President of Performance and Organization Development. The source's high-output plant with rising near misses, rework and fatigue supplies the dilemma. Viktor Stein, High-Output Plant Manager; Amara Johnson, Quality Engineer; Jin Park, Field Service Technician; and Fatima Al-Sayed, Employee Relations and Fairness Lead retain their case designations. Source success measures remain proposed outcomes.

Existing enterprise anchors: OW-M17 — risk/security; OW-M18 — workforce/adoption; OW-M19 — delivery readiness. The workforce blueprint also inherits OW-M03, OW-M09 and OW-M11. Keep those IDs unchanged. New IDs prefixed OW-TOS-HR03 are educational additions, not previously approved client requirements.

3. Origins: five closed historical teaching windows

These windows group teaching ideas, not universal invention dates or OmniWorks corporate biography. Adoption overlaps; older practices can coexist through the present. Each generation changes the medium without automatically resolving ownership, evidence or authority.

1970–1984 — Foreman judgments and production tallies recognize visible output; prevention, teamwork and unsafe shortcuts can be invisible.

1985–1994 — Annual appraisal forms introduce consistency; delayed ratings still lose context and worker voice.

1995–2004 — Goal systems connect individual objectives to plant results; the wrong metric can reward rework or conceal risk.

2005–2014 — Balanced goals and calibration bring quality and development into review; comparable forms do not establish comparable opportunity.

2015–2025 — Continuous feedback and digital evidence improve timeliness; context, appeal and accountable judgment remain necessary.

Recall prompt: reconstruct the five responses as problem → process/application response → unfinished problem. Which old failure could survive in the newest system?

4. One persona-led dilemma

Scene 1

Maya reviews Viktor's proposal to award the highest rating to a record-output team. She asks what counts as contribution before selecting a performance application.

Scene 2

Amara reveals that rework increased and prevention activity was unrecognized. Maya separates production facts from a manager's interpretation: output is one signal, not a complete rating.

Scene 3

Jin explains that customer-site delays and travel reduced his completed jobs while he helped another team restore service. Fatima asks whether the comparison accounts for work opportunity and whether Jin can contest the evidence.

Scene 4

Maya revises the proposed review: preserve operational evidence, discuss context with workers, calibrate against agreed goals and record a human decision with appeal. She does not convert a near-miss report into an automatic personal penalty.

Decision pause: Use output-only ranking, a balanced role-specific review, or team-only assessment. Which evidence would justify changing your choice?

5. Responsibilities, ownership and human authority

MES/quality/EAM/service applications (operating owners): production, defects, maintenance and service facts with period and context.

Performance service (performance policy owner): agreed goals, discussions, evidence references and human review decisions; SAP Performance & Goals is an example.

HR core (HR owner): role, manager and assignment context; learning owns development evidence and rewards owns separate compensation decisions.

Fatima's fairness/employee-relations function owns review/appeal procedure; managers and authorized calibration panels make rating decisions, with worker participation.

Design boundary: Analytics can expose missing context but cannot turn operational correlations or safety reporting into an automatic rating or payout. Proposed responsibilities are learning architecture, not proof that the aspirational enterprise already operates this design. Select technology only after the business rule and accountable owner are clear. SAP examples illustrate capabilities, not mandatory product choices.

6. Exactly 120 minutes

0–10: Read the promise and dilemma; state the worker and business outcome in two sentences.

10–30: Reconstruct the five historical windows; explain what each process improvement left unresolved.

30–50: Play the scenes and reveal the evidence in order; record your first choice and revised reasoning.

50–65: Draw the responsibility/ownership map; name the human decision right and exception route.

65–95: Complete the one build card; compare two options and explore the three future scenarios. Scan only the resource relevant to your uncertainty.

95–115: Perform the three tabletop tests below; record observed paper results and revise the same card.

115–120: Explain your decision in 60 seconds, reflect, and carry the card into Pre-Foundation. Total: 120 minutes.

7. One build card: Sustainable Contribution Build Card

Synthetic experiment: SYN-TEAM301 reports 110 synthetic output units against 100 planned; SYN-REWORK301 is unresolved and SYN-TECH301 spent time supporting a remote repair. These are teaching inputs, not enterprise KPIs. Draft one provisional review.

Fill these nine fields on one page: purpose and affected person; current/proposed state and source evidence; identity/object definitions; business date/time and version; application/data owners; human approval and appeal route; chosen option versus alternative with trade-off; exception/fallback and automation boundary; three test observations and revision.

Minimum design reasoning: Record period, population, goal version and evidence owner; assess controllability and opportunity. A missing quality result is unknown, not zero defects. Keep development dialogue separate from automatic financial consequences.

Do not invent country rules, safety thresholds, collective-agreement terms, live data or missing approvals. Record unknowns with an owner and discovery question. Support an accessible human route for the worker. Consider fatigue, learning opportunity, avoidable travel/rework or resource use where the dilemma provides evidence; do not infer environmental savings.

8. Exactly three tabletop tests

OW-TOS-HR03-T01 — Output counterexample

Reveal/input: The production target is exceeded, but quality evidence is incomplete.

Expected design response: Hold the final recommendation, seek the quality owner's evidence and explain uncertainty rather than assume outstanding contribution.

OW-TOS-HR03-T02 — Safety reporting

Reveal/input: A near miss was reported and a manager proposes reducing every reporter's rating.

Expected design response: Reject a blanket penalty, preserve reporting and investigate conduct/evidence under the appropriate human process.

OW-TOS-HR03-T03 — Unequal opportunity and appeal

Reveal/input: Field technician's raw job count is lower because assignments and travel differ.

Expected design response: Review relevant context, permit worker challenge, record panel rationale and route development needs without silently changing facts.

For each test, record input → expected response → actual observed paper response → evidence note → pass/revise. Expected responses are proposed acceptance criteria, not executed results. Publication executes none of these tabletop tests. A bypass of the protected decision requires redesign, even if the card is complete.

9. Traceability and honest measures

OW-TOS-HR03-REQ01 — Base a performance judgment on role-relevant evidence with period and opportunity context. Source connection: supporting HR case mandate and OW-M18. Assessed by T01; card must identify the authoritative fact and owner.

OW-TOS-HR03-REQ02 — Preserve reporting, worker voice and human review before consequential decisions. Source connection: HR case governance/scenario injection and OW-M17/OW-M19. Assessed by T02–T03; card records exception, authority and recovery condition.

OW-TOS-HR03-ADR01 — Prefer a balanced, role-specific review over output-only ranking; accept extra evidence effort to reduce misleading comparison. Compare the stated alternative and trade-off; this is a proposed educational decision that learners may reject with evidence.

OW-TOS-HR03-EXP01 — this synthetic card and T01–T03 extend OW-M19's traceable-pilot discipline. They do not complete the wider case's tests or rollout.

OW-TOS-HR03-M01 — completed mandatory card fields / 9 × 100; learning completeness only. OW-TOS-HR03-M02 — Record whether each final judgment cites contextual evidence and an appeal route; no productivity or fairness improvement is measured by publication. No baseline, target attainment or operating benefit is inferred from an empty result.

OW-TOS-HR03-EV01 — initially unfilled evidence slot for observed tabletop results, revisions and reviewer questions. Existing case success metrics remain separate planned outcomes.

10. AI-driven exploration: scenarios, not predictions

These are scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps. All require approved purpose, evidence, worker participation, accountable ownership and proportionate controls.

2026–2030 — Assist: Assistance could summarize authorized feedback and flag missing goal evidence; Maya retains judgment.

2031–2040 — Connect: Connected evidence could link service, quality and team goals with minimal personal data and clear ownership.

2041–2050 — Adapt: Adaptive goals could respond to changing industrial work after consultation; historical versions and contestability remain.

Competing future: Periodic team conversations with fewer metrics may support trust better than continuous personal scoring.

Disconfirmation question: Would the proposed balanced review increase reporting suppression or unexplained rating differences despite more data? Record the evidence that would make you stop or change the hypothesis.

11. Reflection and Pre-Foundation handoff

What changed Maya's rating recommendation? Which useful contribution was invisible to job count? What evidence may the worker challenge?

Pre-Foundation: Define goal, evidence, review period, rating rationale, calibration and appeal; map one role-specific review before configuring forms. Carry forward the same card, unresolved questions and proposed acceptance criteria. Deeper implementation, vendor comparisons and the wider capstone stay outside this Origin Story.
