The portal says done. Has the worker received help?

SuccessLabs Academy · GICS02 Materials · OmniForge Materials Ltd. · HR10 — Digital Employee Experience · Level 0 → Pre-Foundation

IIL — Industry Immersion Lab · Sprint · 120 minutes · Edition 2026-10-31 · Planning reference: HUB-ORIGIN-HR10-MAT-sprint

Mission: Architecting Experiences for a Better World. Aspirational educational case; a published lesson is not enrollment, certification or a deployed enterprise service.

1. One learner promise

Design one inclusive HR-help journey that gives a plant worker a trustworthy next step, a traceable request and a private route to a human.

One deliverable: an Employee Help Journey Build Card. Master five distinctions: journey versus screen; saved versus received; case closure versus resolution; portal visibility versus source-system authority; engagement feedback versus permission to profile a worker.

Frame the problem before technology: how can a worker find applicable guidance, ask for help and know what happens next without relying on a personal phone or exposing private information?

2. Origins and process evolution — 1970–2025

Employee experience grew from personnel counters, paper forms and notices into self-service and coordinated journeys. Its purpose is useful service in a person's real working context. A common front door can connect services, but it does not inherit all their data ownership or decision rights.

These closed windows organize teaching, not invention dates or a universal chronology. Adoption overlaps: counters, telephones, noticeboards, legacy portals and modern services coexist through the present. This is a general history, not OmniForge's corporate biography.

Teaching window Process change Materials context and remaining gap
1970–1984 Personnel counters, paper requests and physical notices. A quarry night-shift worker misses daytime HR hours; human help exists but access is uneven.
1985–1994 Telephone support and electronic office communication. Corporate messages travel faster, while plant workers without desks remain harder to reach.
1995–2004 Intranets and employee self-service. Workers can find information directly, but multiple logins and outdated local forms create friction.
2005–2014 Portals and HR case management. A request becomes traceable, yet automated case closure can conceal an unresolved need.
2015–2025 Mobile services, guided journeys, listening and assisted navigation expand. A shared cement-plant kiosk needs private sessions, accessible interaction and an alternative when connectivity fails.

Reconstruct problem → response → remaining gap for each window. Which benefit of the personnel counter should survive in a digital service? When might a polished screen make exclusion harder to notice?

3. AI-driven exploration — 2026–2050 scenarios

The following are scenarios, not predictions, product roadmaps, guaranteed capabilities or OmniForge deployments. Historical windows end in 2025; this separate AI-driven exploration begins in 2026. The current journey must work without AI.

Scenario period Possible assistance Counterexample and experiment question
2026–2030 · Assist Find and explain current, authorized guidance with source/version links. An answer uses an expired transfer policy. Can the worker see the uncertainty and reach a person?
2031–2040 · Connect Coordinate worker-chosen tasks across a site transfer or return to work. A convenient journey shares sensitive case details with a supervisor. Can purpose and access boundaries prevent disclosure?
2041–2050 · Adapt Offer accessible, worker-controlled support that learns from service outcomes. Personalization becomes surveillance or a channel disappears. Can people decline it and still receive help?

Competing future: local channels remain fragmented and personal-device access stays uneven. A service owner, minimal context, honest status and assisted completion remain useful. Choose one hypothesis and write the observation that would make you revise it.

4. Persona-led story — help at the end of a kiln shift

Source baseline: OmniForge has 46,000 employees plus contractors; contractor numbers and device access are unknown. Production-site, quarry and cement-plant counts may overlap and must not be summed. This incident and its teaching records are new fiction.

Nikhil Sen (OMF-P16), Shift Supervisor and Workforce Representative, brings a worker's concern to a design workshop: after a quarry-to-cement assignment change, the worker cannot find applicable HR guidance or a private way to ask for help. The shared kiosk is slow, and a form says “done” even though no receipt appears.

Arjun Mehta (OMF-P05), Group CHRO, asks how the service includes shift workers and contractors without assuming personal devices. Elena Petrova (OMF-P07), Cement Plant Director, asks how the journey behaves during connectivity loss and how routine communications coexist with operational channels.

Nikhil represents operator acceptance and escalation; Arjun sponsors adoption and skills; Elena retains local operational release/rollback authority. Their source IDs and designations are preserved. Titles do not prove access to private HR cases or every service-approval delegation.

First choice: call the journey successful because the page loaded, require a phone app, or provide an accessible request route with a named owner and visible receipt state. Pause and record the tradeoff before the evidence reveal.

Evidence reveal: the kiosk timed out after submission; the backend may already hold the request. The guidance page is an old site version. The worker then asks for a confidential conversation about the transfer. Repeating the request blindly could duplicate the case; copying the concern into a team feed could expose it.

Revise the design: reconcile receipt by stable request ID, show uncertainty honestly, identify current applicable guidance and offer a restricted human route. Nikhil can help someone navigate without reading the confidential narrative. An HR service must remain available through an approved assisted channel during outages.

The portal is not an emergency-control system. Routine campaigns, reminders and HR help cannot replace site-approved safety instructions or operational escalation. A worker must use a service at an appropriate safe location/time under site practice; the design does not require device interaction while operating equipment.

5. Responsibilities, data ownership and human decisions

Area / application Accountable responsibility Boundary to preserve
Experience front door / experience product owner Discover services, accessible navigation, language choice and truthful journey status. A card or chatbot response does not prove source-system acceptance or resolution.
Identity and access / identity owner Authenticate the right person, scope entitlements and isolate shared-device sessions. No shared credentials; contractor access does not imply employee benefit or payroll entitlement.
Knowledge repository / policy content owner Version, effective dates, site/workforce applicability, review and approved translations. Search or AI summaries cannot make expired guidance authoritative.
HR service / delegated case owner Receive, triage, respond privately, track next steps and resolve disputes. Sensitive matters require authorized humans; service closure is not automatically worker-confirmed resolution.
Time, payroll, learning and other source applications / domain owners Authoritative transaction states and authorized corrections. HR10 explains and routes; it does not approve leave, amend pay or authorize plant work.
Communications and operations / respective owners Govern routine targeting and maintain authoritative safety channels/fallback. Portal delivery, a view or a click is not safety understanding or operational readiness.

For the prototype, Arjun sponsors service inclusion, Nikhil challenges usability, and Elena reviews local channel/fallback boundaries. Delegated HR case owner, content steward and identity/access reviewer remain discovery roles, not newly invented canonical personas or established enterprise delegations.

Source objects: service catalog entry, worker/identity reference, policy version, applicability rule, journey attempt, request ID, case ID/status, language preference, acknowledgment and feedback. Record owner, purpose, effective/extraction dates, access and retention for each used object.

Keep confidential narrative inside the restricted case service. Use minimal non-sensitive correlation/status information in the portal. On shared devices do not retain personal drafts, cached details, downloads or previous-user state; test session expiry, sign-out, browser history/back navigation and local storage behavior.

Automation may prepare navigation, detect stale links and propose routing. People retain policy interpretation for disputed cases, access exceptions, sensitive-case handling and consequential employment decisions. An escalation path must identify the next owner and expected follow-up under a validated service agreement.

Value stream: need → discover → authenticate appropriately → request → track → resolve → learn. The capability is providing inclusive employee services; the value stream describes progress toward useful help. A portal inventory alone describes neither.

7. One build card — Employee Help Journey

Use paper or a simple table. Walk through two synthetic variants without real workers, confidential case data or live transactions. The card is a prototype of service behavior, not proof that any platform provides it out of the box.

Card field Write or simulate
Purpose and completion Worker finds applicable transfer guidance, can request private HR help, receives a reference and can inspect next steps. Receiving a request completes intake only; resolution requires the separate service outcome.
Variant A · MAT-HR10-SYN01 Worker MAT-W1001 at an illustrative cement site uses a shared kiosk after a quarry assignment change. No personal phone assumed. Request MAT-REQ1001 times out during submission; backend acknowledgment is initially unknown.
Variant B · MAT-HR10-SYN02 Worker MAT-W1002 needs language/accessibility assistance and a confidential conversation. The policy search returns expired version v1; current illustrative version v2 applies to the relevant site/workforce. These versions are teaching fixtures, not actual company policies.
Minimal steps and copy Find help → sign in or use approved assisted route → check applicable guidance → request private contact → inspect receipt/status → receive human response. Use labels, keyboard-reachable controls and text status, not color alone; give a visible way to ask for help.
State contract Draft → submitting → received (source acknowledgment with case ID) → assigned → awaiting worker/service action → resolution proposed → confirmed resolved or disputed/reopened. Network uncertainty remains “receipt not yet confirmed”; never display “resolved” from a page click.
Recovery Reconcile MAT-REQ1001 with the service before retry. Retry with the same stable request ID must not create a second case; map it to the existing case. No offline sensitive draft on a shared kiosk; offer approved private assisted intake and reconcile references after recovery.
Privacy and access Reauthenticate before personal case access; authorized case handler sees minimal necessary detail. Supervisor sees no confidential narrative by default. Contractor route uses its own validated service scope. Sign-out/expiry clears session and rendered personal content; prevent next-user recovery of it.
Decision ADR01 Compare adding more portals, replacing every backend with one system, and a governed front door with accountable service/source owners. Propose the third plus assisted access; validate costs, identity, integration and site needs before product selection.
Evidence and measure Log T01–T03 input → expected → actual paper observation → revision/rerun → reviewer. M01 = tests meeting expected behavior / 3. Leave actual values and EV01 empty until learners execute and record observations.

Accessibility and translation need evaluation with representative users and assistive technologies. A tabletop exercise can expose design gaps; it cannot demonstrate WCAG conformance, comprehension, secure browser behavior or working integration.

Proposed enterprise measure after discovery: intended journeys successfully completed / eligible journey attempts in a defined pilot window. Define a stable attempt ID, retries, abandonment and the specific intended outcome; include assisted attempts and report assisted/self-service results separately. Do not count logins, page views or chatbot deflection as resolution. The service owner defines exclusions and privacy-safe frontline/office reporting; no baseline, target or achieved improvement is claimed.

8. Three tabletop tests

Test Inject Expected behavior — planned, not executed
OMF-HR10-T01 · Uncertain receipt and shared kiosk Run A, time out after submission, retry, sign out and simulate a different worker using Back/history or reopening the service. Show receipt unknown until reconciled; recover exactly one case with the stable request ID. Give an accessible status and assisted path. The next worker cannot retrieve prior personal content or session; no sensitive local draft is retained. Record real browser/security checks still needed.
OMF-HR10-T02 · Stale guidance and competing messages Run B with expired policy v1 and an ordinary HR campaign alongside a site operational warning. Do not present v1 as current or treat AI summary confidence as authority. Show source/applicability/version, route unresolved guidance to its owner and provide usable language/accessibility assistance. Routine HR messages yield to approved operational channels; a portal view does not count as safety acknowledgment.
OMF-HR10-T03 · Private human handoff and false closure Worker requests confidential help; automation proposes copying the narrative to the supervisor and closing the case after sending an answer. Restrict context to an authorized human, show private next steps and avoid supervisor disclosure by default. A sent answer is not confirmed resolution; preserve disputed/reopened state and correction route. Optional feedback is purpose-limited and not reused as an individual performance/attrition score.

Execute all three manually and record what actually happened, including failures. Revise the same card and rerun affected steps. Acceptance requires honest status, accessible/assisted access, traceable ownership and bounded disclosure. Publication verifies saved lesson content; it does not execute these tests or populate OMF-HR10-EV01.

9. Reflection and Pre-Foundation handoff

What evidence lets the interface say “received”? Who owns the worker outcome when a backend fails? What would the next kiosk user see? Which decision changed after the evidence reveal? What human quality of the original personnel counter should the future service retain?

Pre-Foundation: choose the one HR-help journey and validate worker context, service scope, identity, policy applicability, translations, accessibility, receipt/retry contract, privacy, retention and assisted fallback. Agree the completion criterion with workers and service owners before configuring cards, cases or integrations.

HR07 owns payroll correction; HR08 owns time/readiness handoffs; HR09 governs workforce measures. HR10 makes help and authoritative status understandable without taking those responsibilities. HR11 can later explore bounded AI assistance against these same evidence and human-handoff requirements.
