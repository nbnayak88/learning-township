HR09 — Workforce Intelligence: The Origin Story

SuccessLabs Academy · GICS03 Industrials · OmniWorks Industrial Systems Ltd. · IIL — Industry Immersion Lab · Level 0 → Pre-Foundation · Sprint · 120 minutes · Edition 2026-10-31

Mission: Architecting Experiences for a Better World.

1. Your one promise

Design one workforce decision product with clear counts, traceable assumptions and a privacy-preserving human action.

Produce one Workforce Evidence Build Card. Use synthetic records and a paper prototype. Beginners explain the decision in plain language; experienced learners challenge the same decision's assumptions. Configuration and implementation research remain in the supporting case outside this sprint.

2. Read the enterprise and sources accurately

OmniWorks is aspirational: 74,000 employees, 36 countries, 61 manufacturing/engineering/assembly facilities and 320 service depots. The 61 is a mixed-facility count, not a confirmed plant-only count. Engineering, maintenance, production and field service shape the workforce problem. Case targets and victory criteria are aspirations, not achieved results. This edition adds a bounded synthetic episode; it does not assert a deployment.

Source-scoped lead: Dr. Ananya Roy, Chief Workforce Intelligence Officer. The source's unexplained attrition-risk model and request for named engineers supply the dilemma. George Mensah, Service Operations President; Emily Zhang, Data Product Manager; Mateo Alvarez, Employee Privacy Representative; and Sara Lindström, Plant Manager Persona retain their source roles. Forecasts and correlations are not causal evidence or achieved outcomes.

Existing enterprise anchors: OW-M17 — risk/security; OW-M18 — workforce/adoption; OW-M19 — delivery readiness. The workforce blueprint also inherits OW-M03, OW-M09 and OW-M11. Keep those IDs unchanged. New IDs prefixed OW-TOS-HR09 are educational additions, not previously approved client requirements.

3. Origins: five closed historical teaching windows

These windows group teaching ideas, not universal invention dates or OmniWorks corporate biography. Adoption overlaps; older practices can coexist through the present. Each generation changes the medium without automatically resolving ownership, evidence or authority.

1970–1984 — Personnel counts and supervisor estimates support staffing; people, jobs and hours can be confused.

1985–1994 — Periodic HR reports improve visibility; different site definitions still produce incompatible totals.

1995–2004 — Data warehouses and KPI dashboards standardize aggregation; joins and effective dates can still duplicate workers.

2005–2014 — Workforce planning and cross-domain analysis connect skills to demand; attractive correlations do not establish causes.

2015–2025 — Cloud analytics and predictive models extend scenarios; explanations, uncertainty, privacy and human action remain unresolved by a score alone.

Recall prompt: reconstruct the five responses as problem → process/application response → unfinished problem. Which old failure could survive in the newest system?

4. One persona-led dilemma

Scene 1

George asks Ananya whether service growth has enough skilled capacity. She starts with the decision, population and time horizon instead of promising a dashboard.

Scene 2

Emily reveals that one engineer has two assignments and a contractor appears in a separate source. Headcount, assignment count and full-time equivalent cannot be added as if they were the same unit.

Scene 3

Mateo challenges the manager's request for individual attrition-risk names. The source model cannot explain its recommendations. Ananya holds name-level use and asks whether an aggregate skills/capacity view can answer the legitimate question.

Scene 4

Sara needs an action, not a risk color. Ananya revises the product to show definitions, as-of dates, confidence and owned options such as development, workload review or recruiting. No employee is treated as destined to leave.

Decision pause: Use named predictive scores, an aggregate planning product, or improved descriptive reporting first. Which decision value and validation evidence would justify expanding scope?

5. Responsibilities, ownership and human authority

HR core (HR data owner): person/employment/assignment facts; time and learning owners provide approved hours and scoped capability evidence.

Service/EAM and Finance sources (operating/financial owners): demand, work and cost context with units and periods.

Semantic/data product (metric owner and steward): definitions, lineage, as-of treatment, joins, quality and refresh status; source facts are not overwritten by dashboard formulas.

Analytics/planning application (decision product owner): authorized aggregates and scenarios; SAP People Analytics or enterprise analytics are examples.

Business leaders own planning actions; privacy/worker representatives and qualified model reviewers govern permitted use. A model does not decide promotion, exit or retention treatment.

Design boundary: A probability is not an employee fact; small cohorts and operational joins can expose identities even when names are removed. Proposed responsibilities are learning architecture, not proof that the aspirational enterprise already operates this design. Select technology only after the business rule and accountable owner are clear. SAP examples illustrate capabilities, not mandatory product choices.

7. One build card: Workforce Evidence Build Card

Synthetic experiment: Synthetic records: person SYN-P901 has two active half-time assignments, SYN-P902 one full-time assignment, and SYN-C903 a contractor with no contracted hours supplied. Employee headcount is 2; assignment count is 3; contracted employee FTE is 2 if those fractions share the same agreed full-time basis. Contractor FTE remains unknown.

Fill these nine fields on one page: purpose and affected person; current/proposed state and source evidence; identity/object definitions; business date/time and version; application/data owners; human approval and appeal route; chosen option versus alternative with trade-off; exception/fallback and automation boundary; three test observations and revision.

Minimum design reasoning: Define population, employment scope, denominator, time basis and lineage before a number. Keep contractor counts separate from employee FTE. Record staffing demand assumptions and ranges; do not derive required people from a single productivity factor without evidence.

Do not invent country rules, safety thresholds, collective-agreement terms, live data or missing approvals. Record unknowns with an owner and discovery question. Support an accessible human route for the worker. Consider fatigue, learning opportunity, avoidable travel/rework or resource use where the dilemma provides evidence; do not infer environmental savings.

8. Exactly three tabletop tests

OW-TOS-HR09-T01 — Count reconciliation

Reveal/input: A dashboard adds three assignments and one contractor as four employees.

Expected design response: Reconcile to two employees and separate populations; preserve unknown contractor FTE and the agreed fraction basis.

OW-TOS-HR09-T02 — Privacy counterexample

Reveal/input: A two-person engineer cohort is requested with individual attrition risk.

Expected design response: Hold unsupported name-level use, assess re-identification risk and provide only an authorized safe aggregate or withhold pending review.

OW-TOS-HR09-T03 — Forecast uncertainty

Reveal/input: Service demand increases but available skills, travel and productivity assumptions are unvalidated.

Expected design response: Show a scenario range with explicit assumptions/owners, request validation and keep staffing action with accountable leaders.

For each test, record input → expected response → actual observed paper response → evidence note → pass/revise. Expected responses are proposed acceptance criteria, not executed results. Publication executes none of these tabletop tests. A bypass of the protected decision requires redesign, even if the card is complete.

9. Traceability and honest measures

OW-TOS-HR09-REQ01 — Define and reconcile workforce measures to authoritative sources and a consistent time/unit basis. Source connection: supporting HR case mandate and OW-M18. Assessed by T01; card must identify the authoritative fact and owner.

OW-TOS-HR09-REQ02 — Keep prediction uncertainty, privacy and human action rights explicit. Source connection: HR case governance/scenario injection and OW-M17/OW-M19. Assessed by T02–T03; card records exception, authority and recovery condition.

OW-TOS-HR09-ADR01 — Prefer an aggregate decision-led product with governed definitions over named opaque risk scoring; compare action usefulness and privacy cost. Compare the stated alternative and trade-off; this is a proposed educational decision that learners may reject with evidence.

OW-TOS-HR09-EXP01 — this synthetic card and T01–T03 extend OW-M19's traceable-pilot discipline. They do not complete the wider case's tests or rollout.

OW-TOS-HR09-M01 — completed mandatory card fields / 9 × 100; learning completeness only. OW-TOS-HR09-M02 — Reconcile the synthetic population/count/FTE definitions and record whether uncertain inputs stayed unknown; no forecast accuracy or retention benefit is claimed. No baseline, target attainment or operating benefit is inferred from an empty result.

OW-TOS-HR09-EV01 — initially unfilled evidence slot for observed tabletop results, revisions and reviewer questions. Existing case success metrics remain separate planned outcomes.

10. AI-driven exploration: scenarios, not predictions

These are scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps. All require approved purpose, evidence, worker participation, accountable ownership and proportionate controls.

2026–2030 — Assist: Assistance could explain metric definitions and flag join/count inconsistencies.

2031–2040 — Connect: Connected workforce and service data could support scoped capacity scenarios with lineage and permissions.

2041–2050 — Adapt: Adaptive planning could propose skills and capacity responses as work changes; leaders and workers retain meaningful participation and review.

Competing future: Clear descriptive measures and regular workforce conversations may produce better decisions than opaque prediction.

Disconfirmation question: Would the proposed forecast fail to outperform a simple planning baseline or trigger interventions without evidence of benefit? Record the evidence that would make you stop or change the hypothesis.

11. Reflection and Pre-Foundation handoff

Which number changed after defining the unit? What remains unknown? Could the supposedly anonymous view identify someone?

Pre-Foundation: Create a metric dictionary entry with owner, population, unit, as-of date, lineage, quality, access and action; carry one transparent scenario forward. Carry forward the same card, unresolved questions and proposed acceptance criteria. Deeper implementation, vendor comparisons and the wider capstone stay outside this Origin Story.
