1. What this two-hour sprint achieves

By the end you will: 1. Explain the general origins and purpose of this domain before naming a product. 2. Reconstruct its process evolution across 1975–2026, identifying what improved and what remained unresolved. 3. Compare 2030, 2035, 2040 and 2050 scenarios, with a competing future and a validation question. 4. Map application responsibilities and source objects for the OmniVerse Energy case. 5. Produce a synthetic build prototype, run three tabletop tests and defend an evidence-based next step.

The 20% to master: metric definition; as-of population; lineage; forecast uncertainty; privacy and decision value.

Deliverable: one Domain Evolution Build Card containing a timeline, persona needs, ownership map, state/control prototype, option decision and test evidence. Practical capability here means implementation preparation; it does not imply production configuration proficiency after two hours.

2. Origins and purpose — the general story

Workforce reporting began with counts, payroll costs and administrative records. Analytics broadens the task from describing people to informing a decision, but prediction and visualization cannot repair inconsistent definitions or establish causation. Trustworthy intelligence starts with purpose, population, time and ownership.

Teach the decision first: what decision is needed, whose outcome matters, what evidence supports it, which application is responsible, and who has authority to act?

Fifty years of process evolution

Dates group broad historical patterns for learning. They do not claim invention dates, uniform adoption or a source-proven worldwide chronology. Paper, legacy, cloud and assisted processes can coexist today; the table is an educational synthesis.

1985–1994 Spreadsheets and periodic MIS More flexible analysis Version drift and manual joins A vacancy count needs an agreed position definition
1995–2004 Data warehouses and standardized reporting Historical comparisons and consolidation Source defects become reusable errors Track legal entity and employment history
2005–2014 Dashboards and integrated workforce measures Broader visibility across HR processes More metrics do not ensure action A refinery manager needs a decision and owner
2015–2019 Predictive analytics and scenario planning Anticipate possible demand and retention issues Correlation and individual scoring may mislead Show uncertainty and relevant workforce constraints
2020–2022 Rapid workforce planning under disruption Shorter planning cycles Stale assumptions and small-group disclosure Reconcile planning assumptions before staffing choices

Evolution activity: cover the table. For each era, say “the process changed from ___ to ___ because ___; the unresolved problem was ___.” Use an energy example rather than a product slogan.

Historical caution: the 2020–2022 row describes possible process pressures/responses in distributed work, not proof that every energy enterprise changed in that way. Modern product documentation supports current capabilities, not the full historical chronology.

4. OmniVerse Energy — the decision and the people

Aspirational enterprise baseline: OmniVerse Energy Ltd., 68,000 employees across 42 countries. “OmniEnergy” is an alias preserved in original research. Historical eras are not a corporate biography; OmniVerse was founded in 1978.

Turn trusted workforce data into explainable decisions and measurable action—not employee surveillance. OmniEnergy must connect workforce decisions to refinery reliability, offshore staffing, renewable expansion, customer operations, safety, succession, cost, and energy-transition skills. Today, HR reports are manually assembled, definitions vary, and planning relies on historical headcount rather than future capability demand.

Scope note: The domain's 5 GW growth-plan reference has a source scope conflict; preserve it as unresolved and do not combine it with the enterprise renewable-capacity figures to manufacture a staffing baseline.

6. Key concepts and application responsibility

Concept Meaning and boundary
Metric Defined calculation, population, period and owner
Headcount/FTE People/employments count versus full-time equivalent; state counting unit
Lineage Trace from source/version through transformation to result
Forecast Conditional estimate with horizon and uncertainty
Cohort Defined group; small groups can expose individuals
Causal claim Requires stronger evidence than correlation or a model score
Application / accountable area Responsibility
HR source applications Own authoritative operational facts
Data product / metric owners Define transformations, quality and meaning
Analytics Presents governed measures and scenarios
Privacy / access authority Approves legitimate scope and granularity
Business decision owner Chooses action and evaluates results

Source information objects: metric definition; as-of date; population; lineage; forecast horizon; cohort; decision log. Record attribute owner, authoritative source, purpose, access, retention, effective date and quality rule.

Source value stream: Decision need → define metric → govern data → analyze → decide → evaluate. Define a stakeholder-valued exit criterion at each stage before detailed process modeling.

A capability is an ability the organization needs; the value stream describes progression toward stakeholder value. Neither is simply a list of applications.

AA boundary: this sprint assigns application responsibility and acceptance criteria. Reference data stewardship, security, integration, UX, infrastructure and AI concerns without assuming the application view alone implements those controls. Cross-domain consumers must not silently become owners of source facts.

8. Build experiment — a small, reviewable implementation design

Use two synthetic records, one routine and one variant drawn from the source challenges. Assign teaching identifiers HR09-SYN-001/002. Do not use real employee information, internal endpoints or live banking/access actions.

Build Card field What you must write
Purpose/population Decision, affected person/role and intended outcome; identify missing facts
Two variants Four headcounts versus Tiny cohort or opaque attrition score
Objects and time Identifiers, source, relevant validity/as-of/processing dates; distinguish their meanings
Lifecycle/gates Proposed from-state → permitted transition → prerequisite → accountable actor
Evidence Source reference, validation status, version and correction owner
Application boundary HR source applications responsibility versus Business decision owner
Global/local decision Shared principle, justified role/country variant and evidence still needed
Human and automation boundary One permitted administrative task; one protected consequential decision
Exception/recovery Visible exception, owner, fallback, recovery and confirmation
Architecture decision Compare dashboard proliferation, centralized reporting alone and governed decision products with domain-owned definitions.
Measure Definition, denominator, exclusions, as-of date, owner and interpretation limits
Test evidence Input → expected → actual paper result → correction → evidence note → pass/revise

Conceptual workflow: establish approved source evidence → validate → apply agreed rule → authorized decision/action → confirm downstream business outcome → review exceptions. These are teaching stages, not prescribed SAP object/status names. Choose actual module configuration only after validating the contract and product version.

Three tabletop tests

Test Challenge Expected result in this proposed design
T1 Four headcounts Reconcile units, dates, inclusion and concurrent-employment treatment; do not silently average
T2 Tiny cohort or opaque attrition score Restrict granular disclosure and consequential use; escalate to approved privacy/model review
T3 Missing forecast / 5 GW assumptions Show missing assumptions and scope conflict; no single precise staffing forecast

How to run: represent each record/state on paper or in a simple table. Execute the steps yourself; record whether the initial design produced the expected outcome. If it did not, revise and rerun. Designed expectations are not actual SAP test results.

Acceptance: explain the timeline, defend one 2050 hypothesis against an alternative, distinguish the concepts, assign owners and satisfy all three tabletop expectations after documented revision. An unsupported safety/pay/access/employee-rights outcome requires redesign. List any control that remains untested.

Source measure: Metric consistency = reconciled published metric instances / metric instances tested; forecast error uses defined horizon and holdout data. Owner: domain lead unless specified; cadence: proposed pilot review and monthly follow-up. Establish denominator, exclusions, as-of date and baseline before setting a numeric target. Original source “Victory Measures” are targets, not achievements.

Do not fabricate a baseline, achieved benefit or target. A successful prototype proves only the limited behaviour you actually exercised.
