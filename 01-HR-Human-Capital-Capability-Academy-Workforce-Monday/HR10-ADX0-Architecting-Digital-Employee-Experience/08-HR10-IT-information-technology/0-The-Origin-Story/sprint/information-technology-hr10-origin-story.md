## Origins: the question that survives each era

These closed windows group process evolution for teaching; they are not universal invention or adoption dates. Adoption overlaps. Paper, spreadsheets and older systems can coexist through the present.
1970–1984 — Personnel desks answer questions face to face
1985–1994 — HR help databases track service requests
1995–2004 — Intranets publish policies and employee self-service
2005–2014 — Portals connect tasks, cases and mobile experiences
2015–2025 — Personalization and conversational assistance add source and status ambiguity
A personalized portal is not the authoritative employment record or proof that an HR case is resolved.

## One story: choose, reveal, revise

Nadia Fernandes (OTS-P07), Group CHRO, asks Sofia Tan (OTS-P06), Platform Engineering Director, why remote engineers repeatedly submit the same HR help request. Mei Lin (OTS-P09), Data Governance and Knowledge Lead, sees the portal's success banner but no case receipt.
Choose before reading on: Display success after every click, ask the employee to resubmit, or confirm receipt and route the policy question to an accountable owner?
Evidence reveal: Synthetic request HELP-1000 timed out after submission. A cached answer also shows the wrong region's policy. Nadia separates submission acknowledgement from resolution, provides a safe status lookup instead of duplicate submission, and offers confidential human support with an accessible alternative channel.
Write what changed your decision and what remains unknown. An accessible explanation and a human challenge route must be available to the affected worker. An uncertain record cannot silently become an authorization.

## Application responsibility and human authority

Experience layer — Experience owner governs navigation and accessible interaction. Journey owner confirms usable status and fallback.
HR service/case system — HR service owner owns case receipt, assignment and resolution. Authorized service specialist resolves and communicates.
Policy knowledge — Policy owner maintains approved scope, version and effective date. Authorized policy expert interprets disputed guidance.
Core HR / identity — HR owns profile facts; security owns session and access. Delegated approvers authorize consequential changes.
Business owners remain accountable; applications store and process the records. Discover the actual delegated approvers before implementation. Restrict client, candidate, pay and health information to its purpose and permitted viewers. Minimize replicated data and unnecessary compute rather than assuming automation is sustainable.
SAP examples in the preserved source are discussion options, not a technology selection or a claim that configuration has been validated. Start with the business need. AI may summarize permitted evidence or draft a reminder; it cannot approve a consequential people decision, grant privilege or declare a payment complete.

## One build experiment: Decision Build Card

Synthetic inputs: Employee IT-P1000; help request HELP-1000; submission timed out; receipt unknown; cached policy region A while worker is region B; confidential concern requires human route.
Complete these fields once:
1. People and business need: who benefits, what could go wrong and why this matters in IT services or engineering.
2. Initial choice and reason: compare the choices before seeing the reveal.
3. Evidence reveal and revised choice: state what changed, and one counterexample.
4. Identity, date and source owner: identify authoritative records, effective time, provenance and uncertain facts.
5. Application responsibility: separate record keeping, orchestration, calculation, analytics and access enforcement.
6. Human approval and protected action: name the decision role, limits and worker challenge route.
7. Minimum data and permitted viewers: state purpose, retention question, client isolation and accessible communication.
8. Alternative and local variant: defend a common core with a justified regional/client variant, or explain a different design.
9. Exception owner and recovery: define hold, escalation, retry/reconciliation and confirmation conditions.
10. Measure and missing evidence: Journey states with truthful receipt/resolution status and an accessible owned fallback / states reviewed.
11. Pre-Foundation question: Map one employee task from intent through receipt to resolution; separate experience, case and policy ownership.
For each test below record input → expected → observed paper result → evidence → revision. Blank observations remain blank. A complete card can still contain a poor decision.

## Exactly three tabletop tests

OTS-HR10-T01 — Timeout after submit
Expected proposed behavior: Check the original request reference before retry; show unknown receipt honestly and provide owned recovery.
Evidence to record: Record correlation key, receipt status and accessible notice.
Observed result / evidence / revision: initially unfilled; learner completes during the exercise.

OTS-HR10-T02 — Wrong-region policy
Expected proposed behavior: Withhold unsupported guidance and route to the right policy owner; show version/scope.
Evidence to record: Record source mismatch and corrected route.
Observed result / evidence / revision: initially unfilled; learner completes during the exercise.

OTS-HR10-T03 — Confidential human request
Expected proposed behavior: Limit access and metadata, provide an accessible private handoff and explicit next status.
Evidence to record: Record permitted viewers, owner and user confirmation.
Observed result / evidence / revision: initially unfilled; learner completes during the exercise.
Acceptance: one complete card, three recorded tabletop observations after any revisions, and an explanation of source ownership and human authority. A breached authority or privacy boundary requires redesign. Publishing these tests does not execute them.

## 2026–2050: explore and challenge

2026–2030 — Assist: Find approved policy and explain case status.
2031–2040 — Connect: Connect journeys across service owners with consent and traceability.
2041–2050 — Adapt: Adapt channels to user needs without exposing confidential context.
These are scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps. Competing future: restrictive client contracts, unreliable data and fragmented regional systems favor smaller human-led workflows. Disproof question: would the proposed assistance still improve this decision if the trusted source is unavailable, stale or contested? Define evidence that would make you reject the hypothesis; preserve worker agency and an accountable stop route.
