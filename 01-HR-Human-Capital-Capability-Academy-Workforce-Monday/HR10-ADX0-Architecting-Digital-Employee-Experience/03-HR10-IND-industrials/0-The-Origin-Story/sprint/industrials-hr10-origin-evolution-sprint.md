## 1. Your one promise

Design one accessible employee-help journey that confirms status, protects privacy and reaches a human when self-service fails.

## 2. Read the enterprise and sources accurately

OmniWorks is fictional: 74,000 employees, 36 countries, 61 manufacturing/engineering/assembly facilities and 320 service depots. The 61 is a mixed-facility count, not a confirmed plant-only count. Engineering, maintenance, production and field service shape the workforce problem. Case targets and victory criteria are aspirations, not achieved results. This edition adds a bounded synthetic episode; it does not assert a deployment.

Source-scoped lead: Neha Verma, Digital Employee Experience Director. The source reports a fictional communication reach gap of 94% desk workers versus 46% shift workers and failed leave/payslip tasks. These are scenario baselines, not measured outcomes here. Carlos Mendes, Shift Operator Persona; Aisha Rahman, Mobile Field Technician Persona; Laura Stein, Global Communications Lead; and Ben Foster, HR Service Delivery Owner retain their roles.

Existing enterprise anchors: OW-M17 — risk/security; OW-M18 — workforce/adoption; OW-M19 — delivery readiness. The workforce blueprint also inherits OW-M03, OW-M09 and OW-M11. Keep those IDs unchanged. New IDs prefixed OW-TOS-HR10 are educational additions, not previously approved client requirements.

## 3. Origins: five closed historical teaching windows

These windows group teaching ideas, not universal invention dates or OmniWorks corporate biography. Adoption overlaps; older practices can coexist through the present. Each generation changes the medium without automatically resolving ownership, evidence or authority.

1970–1984 — Personnel desks and noticeboards provide local help; absent shift workers may miss information.

1985–1994 — Shared service desks and telephone support organize requests; ownership and status can remain opaque.

1995–2004 — Employee portals enable routine transactions; multiple systems and credentials fragment the experience.

2005–2014 — Mobile self-service and targeted communications improve reach; shared devices, language and weak connections still exclude workers.

2015–2025 — Unified digital front doors and guided journeys simplify navigation; a page view does not prove understanding, completed service or safe operational response.

Recall prompt: reconstruct the five responses as problem → process/application response → unfinished problem. Which old failure could survive in the newest system?

## 4. One persona-led dilemma

### Scene 1

Neha asks Carlos how he obtains a payslip explanation during a shift. A portal designed around office workers is not equitable if he lacks private access or appropriate time.

### Scene 2

Laura shows strong email reach, then reveals the source's shift-worker communication gap. Neha separates delivered message, accessible receipt, understanding and response; none follows automatically from an email count.

### Scene 3

Aisha tries a synthetic leave request on a weak mobile connection. The screen times out. Ben cannot call it resolved merely because the portal displayed a confirmation before losing the server response.

### Scene 4

Neha revises one journey with recoverable status, a discreet human handoff and content ownership. Urgent plant safety communication stays on the approved operational channel; the HR front door does not become an unverified emergency system.

Decision pause: Build around application menus, a single high-priority worker task, or an assisted service desk. Which worker evidence determines the first slice?

## 5. Responsibilities, ownership and human authority

Experience/front-door service (experience owner): navigation, accessible content and journey presentation; SAP Work Zone is an example, not the authoritative source for every task.

HR/time/payroll services (domain owners): approved leave transactions, payslip results and request outcomes; the portal displays their confirmed statuses.

Case/service management (service owner): case identity, accountable queue, response and escalation; knowledge owners approve audience, review dates and versions.

IAM/security (security owner): authentication, session privacy and shared-device logout; operations owns urgent safety communication and response.

Ben's service team owns complex human resolution; the worker can correct, reopen or escalate. Neha owns design research and equal access, not leave or pay approval.

Design boundary: Self-service must not silently close a complex or confidential case; page activity and notifications do not establish resolution or safety readiness. Proposed responsibilities are learning architecture, not proof that the fictional enterprise already operates this design. Select technology only after the business rule and accountable owner are clear. SAP examples illustrate capabilities, not mandatory product choices.

## 7. One build card: Employee Help Journey Build Card

Synthetic experiment: SYN-HELP1001 is Carlos's private pay question; SYN-LEAVE1002 is Aisha's submission with unknown backend outcome. Use a paper screen flow on shared kiosk/mobile paths, not real payslips or accounts.

Fill these nine fields on one page: purpose and affected person; current/proposed state and source evidence; identity/object definitions; business date/time and version; application/data owners; human approval and appeal route; chosen option versus alternative with trade-off; exception/fallback and automation boundary; three test observations and revision.

Minimum design reasoning: Ask for only necessary information, show a durable reference and confirmed/unknown status, and provide an accessible human route. Do not require a personal smartphone. Discover paid access arrangements, languages and assistive needs rather than assume them.

Do not invent country rules, safety thresholds, collective-agreement terms, live data or missing approvals. Record unknowns with an owner and discovery question. Support an accessible human route for the worker. Consider fatigue, learning opportunity, avoidable travel/rework or resource use where the dilemma provides evidence; do not infer environmental savings.

## 8. Exactly three tabletop tests

### OW-TOS-HR10-T01 — Unknown submission

Reveal/input: Mobile request times out after submit and the worker retries.

Expected design response: Check/reconcile the domain transaction using its reference before duplicate creation; show truthful pending/unknown status and help.

### OW-TOS-HR10-T02 — Shared-device privacy

Reveal/input: The next kiosk user opens the browser after Carlos's pay inquiry.

Expected design response: Require a private session and verified logout/timeout design, minimize displayed data and provide an assisted private route; paper testing does not prove real security.

### OW-TOS-HR10-T03 — Stale or urgent guidance

Reveal/input: Search returns an old safety instruction and a confidential issue cannot be solved by FAQ.

Expected design response: Hold stale guidance, route urgent operational matters through approved safety channels and confidential issues to the accountable human service owner.

For each test, record input → expected response → actual observed paper response → evidence note → pass/revise. Expected responses are proposed acceptance criteria, not executed results. Publication executes none of these tabletop tests. A bypass of the protected decision requires redesign, even if the card is complete.

## 10. AI-driven exploration: scenarios, not predictions

These are scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps. All require approved purpose, evidence, worker participation, accountable ownership and proportionate controls.

2026–2030 — Assist: Assistance could explain approved task steps and locate the correct human team.

2031–2040 — Connect: Connected services could show verified task/case status across channels without exposing private data.

2041–2050 — Adapt: Adaptive interfaces could respond to language and access needs with user control, preserving equivalent assisted routes.

Competing future: Reliable local human service plus a small accessible portal may serve shift workers better than a complex personalized platform.

Disconfirmation question: Would the digital journey widen the completion or privacy gap between office, shift and field workers? Record the evidence that would make you stop or change the hypothesis.

## 11. Reflection and Pre-Foundation handoff

What does a confirmation screen prove? Which worker could still be excluded? Who owns the case after the portal handoff?

Carry forward the same card, unresolved questions and proposed acceptance criteria. Deeper implementation, vendor comparisons and the wider capstone stay outside this Origin Story.
