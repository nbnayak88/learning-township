| Teaching window | Process response and persistent question |

## 4. One persona-led story

Nadia Fernandes (OCF-P07), Group CHRO, reviews a synthetic branch employee’s confidential pay question. The shared workstation shows no confirmation after submission. Sofia Chen (OCF-P06), Chief Digital and Information Officer, wants a simpler front door; Omar Haddad (OCF-P10), CISO and Resilience Lead, checks whether another worker can see the draft.

Choose before revealing: Ask the employee to resubmit repeatedly, show every case to the manager, or resolve submission state and offer a private human channel? Record your first reason on the card.

Evidence reveal: The synthetic portal timed out after creating case FIN-HELP1000. The request is owned by Payroll, while a search result points to an old policy for another legal entity. Nadia preserves the case reference; Sofia revises status visibility and guidance selection; Omar requires session privacy.

Revise your decision: One front door should expose the right next step and service owner. Portal activity, case creation and resolved employee need are different measures. Preserve an accessible human route to challenge errors. Financial service continuity matters, but an urgent cut-off does not create authority. Customer KYC and employee readiness are different processes; do not copy customer identity checks into HR without purpose and policy review.

## 5. Application responsibilities and human authority

Frame the business need first; applications implement the chosen operating model. The following is a proposed teaching architecture. Confirm named delegates, country policy, legal basis and actual product scope before implementation.

| Application responsibility | Accountable data owner | Human decision right |
|---|---|---|
| Experience portal; Work Zone example | Experience owner maintains navigation and task presentation | Service owners decide completion; a portal cannot approve payroll or restricted access. |
| Case management and knowledge | Payroll owns the case; policy owner maintains jurisdiction/version/review dates | Authorized case handler resolves or escalates; content owner approves guidance. |
| Identity and accessibility | Security owns session controls; experience owner owns accessible interaction | Employee chooses an approved private support route; manager visibility follows purpose and policy. |

Core HR owns workforce facts; domain owners own performance, learning, rewards, time or payroll facts as applicable. IAM executes access policy; integration transports and reconciles states; analytics reads authorized data and does not become its owner. AI may summarize permitted evidence or draft a reminder, but cannot approve consequential employment, regulated duties, payments or access. An interface acknowledgment is not proof of the business outcome. SAP examples illustrate responsibilities, not guaranteed features or universal localization.

## 7. One Decision Build Card

Synthetic input: Shared workstation; case FIN-HELP1000 exists but confirmation unknown; confidential pay topic; wrong-entity policy result; employee needs a keyboard-accessible alternative. No real pay or personal data.

Keep all work in this one card. Capture: business problem; initial and revised choice with reason; entity/identity/date semantics; authoritative source and accountable owner; human approval boundary; one justified local variant with purpose/minimum data/viewers; known versus unknown downstream state; exception owner and approved fallback; all three test observations and evidence references. Missing evidence stays blank or unknown, never silently becomes approval.

Compare one alternative: a manual controlled handoff may be preferable to automation when exception ownership or recovery is absent. State the evidence needed to change your mind. Avoid collecting real screening, health, payroll, customer or conduct records in this learning card.

## 8. Exactly three tabletop tests

| Test | Input / reveal | Expected proposed behavior | Evidence to record |
|---|---|---|---|
| OCF-HR10-T01 — Submission uncertainty | Portal times out after case creation. | Find the existing case with safe correlation; avoid duplicate confidential requests; show known status. | Case ID, ownership, confirmation state and recovery step. |
| OCF-HR10-T02 — Stale or wrong-entity policy | Search returns obsolete guidance from another legal entity. | Show scope/version; withhold unsupported advice; route to current policy owner. | Source scope, review date, corrected result and unresolved question. |
| OCF-HR10-T03 — Shared-device human handoff | Next worker approaches the device; employee cannot use the current control. | Protect session/draft; provide an accessible private route with minimum data. | Privacy boundary, keyboard/human alternative and handoff acknowledgment. |

For each test, record input → expected → observed → revision/evidence → Pass, Revise or Blocked. All observed results are initially unfilled. Publication does not execute any tabletop, system test, approval or operational transaction. Acceptance requires a defensible card and all three observed tabletop results with revisions; bypassing a protected human decision requires redesign.

## 10. Separate AI-driven exploration: 2026–2050

Scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps.

| Scenario window | Question to explore |
|---|---|
| 2026–2030 — Assist | Could approved-source assistance explain evidence gaps and draft next steps without approving the decision? |
| 2031–2040 — Connect | Could separately authorized services coordinate this handoff while keeping unknown states and local rights visible? |
| 2041–2050 — Adapt | Could reviewed feedback propose reversible process changes while people retain consequential authority? |

Competing future: fragmented systems, uneven access, policy restrictions and manual review persist; a simpler controlled workflow outperforms orchestration. Falsifiable hypothesis question: Would a single portal improve employee experience if confidential cases remain unowned and status is invisible? Define an observation that would disprove your claim—for example fewer steps but more unresolved exceptions or worse worker outcomes. Compare against a manual baseline before claiming value.
