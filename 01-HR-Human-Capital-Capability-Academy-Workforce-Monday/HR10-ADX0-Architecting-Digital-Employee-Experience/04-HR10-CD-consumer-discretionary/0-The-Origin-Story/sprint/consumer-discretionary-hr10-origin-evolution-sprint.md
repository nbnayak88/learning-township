## 3. Origins and process evolution

The five closed windows are educational groupings, not universal invention dates or OmniVibe's corporate biography (the source gives its founding as 1992). Adoption overlaps; older practices can coexist through the present. Ask what problem each response solved and what it left unresolved.

1970–1984 — Noticeboards and HR counters provide guidance; opening hours and privacy limit access.

1985–1994 — Intranets and service directories improve discovery; stale local copies can coexist.

1995–2004 — Employee self-service makes transactions available; completion and support ownership can remain unclear.

2005–2014 — Mobile portals and service workflows improve reach; shared-device privacy and accessibility still matter.

2015–2025 — Role-aware digital workplaces combine content and tasks; source validity, safe handoff and verified completion remain essential.

Recall: reconstruct problem → process/application response → unfinished problem. Which old failure could survive in a modern system?

## 4. One persona-led dilemma

Lead: Nikhil Das, Store Associate and Workforce Representative (OVC-P16). Counterpart: Oliver Brooks, Store Operations Director (OVC-P06). These established personas are fictional. The following incident, record IDs and reveals are new synthetic teaching additions.

Scene 1 — Nikhil needs a time-correction answer during a store shift. Several portals show conflicting instructions; the shared-device screen says submitted but supplies no reference. Oliver wants service coverage and an experience the associate can actually finish.

Scene 2 — Choose another portal, a single page that exposes everything or a role-aware front door with minimal data and clear service ownership. Reveal: the displayed article belongs to another market and is out of date. A polished screen cannot make guidance authoritative.

Scene 3 — Farah Ahmed, CISO and Privacy Lead (OVC-P13), reveals that a confidential concern could be visible on the shared device. Nikhil revises the route: private escalation, safe sign-out, accessible alternatives and a visible case reference. A help portal does not approve time, alter pay or replace urgent operational channels.

Decision pause: record your first choice before the reveal, then revise it. Name one fact that would make the alternative better.

## 5. Responsibilities and decision rights

Experience layer owns navigation and presentation, not HR facts or approval authority. Knowledge owners approve content versions and market scope; service owners own cases and resolution; core HR, Time and Payroll retain their transaction records and approvals. IAM/security governs session and role access; privacy owners govern confidential routing. Nikhil supplies feedback; the appropriate authorized service team resolves the issue. SAP Work Zone is the proposed experience example, not an assumed case-management or transaction system.

Frame the business rule and accountable owner before selecting technology. Source proposals do not authorize data sharing, sensitive transactions or autonomous decisions. Keep an accessible human exception and worker challenge route.

## 7. One build card — Employee Help Journey Build Card

Synthetic experiment (OVC-TOS-HR10-EXP01): SYN-HELP1000 asks about a missing time record from a shared store device. One stale wrong-market article and a submission with unknown outcome are supplied. Sketch one journey with source/date context, minimal case reference, private human help, accessible alternative and explicit transaction boundary.

Fill nine fields on one page: purpose/person affected; current and proposed state with source evidence; object/identity definitions; business date/time and version; application and data owners; human approval and challenge route; chosen option versus alternative and trade-off; exception/fallback and automation boundary; three test observations and your revision.

Unknown country, brand, franchise, device, policy and authority conditions need an owner and discovery question. Use no real personal data. Consider learning access, workload, service continuity and avoidable rework where evidence supports them; do not infer financial or environmental savings.

## 8. Exactly three tabletop tests

### OVC-TOS-HR10-T01 — Unknown submission result

Reveal/input: Connectivity drops after the associate presses submit.

Expected design response: Show pending/unknown state, check for an existing reference before retry and avoid duplicate cases; do not display completed without evidence.

### OVC-TOS-HR10-T02 — Wrong guidance and channel

Reveal/input: A stale wrong-market article is offered for an urgent store incident.

Expected design response: Withhold unsupported instructions, route to current authorized guidance and the correct operational channel; do not substitute an HR portal for an emergency route.

### OVC-TOS-HR10-T03 — Shared-device confidentiality

Reveal/input: A confidential concern is opened on a kiosk with another user nearby.

Expected design response: Minimize displayed data, provide private human contact and safe session closure with an accessible alternative; do not promise confidentiality that has not been verified.

For each, record input → expected response → actual observed paper response → evidence note → pass/revise. These expected responses are proposed acceptance criteria. Publication executes none of the tests; all observation slots start unfilled. A bypass of a protected decision calls for redesign, even if the card is complete.

## 9. Traceability and honest measures

OVC-TOS-HR10-REQ01 — Make authoritative guidance, service ownership and completion state visible. Source connection: HR case focus, OVC-M09–OVC-M12 and OVC-SIM-02 and OVC-SIM-07. Acceptance: T01–T02; identify authoritative evidence, owner and revision.

OVC-TOS-HR10-REQ02 — Protect privacy and accessible human escalation without crossing transaction authority. Source connection: OVC-M17–OVC-M19. Acceptance: T02–T03; identify the protected decision, exception and recovery condition.

OVC-TOS-HR10-ADR01 — Proposed educational choice: A role-aware front door over accountable services rather than a portal that duplicates all data and decisions. Learners may reject it with evidence; it is not a client-approved architecture decision.

OVC-TOS-HR10-EXP01 — The single card experiment extends the existing case's bounded-pilot discipline; T01–T03 do not complete the wider case's experiments or implementation tests.

OVC-TOS-HR10-M01 — Mandatory card fields completed / 9 × 100: learning completeness only. OVC-TOS-HR10-M02 — Record whether each proposed acceptance criterion survives its evidence reveal; record observed paper results separately from expectations. Real baselines and targets remain TBD.

OVC-TOS-HR10-EV01 — Initially unfilled evidence slot for the three observations, revised choice and reviewer questions. No achieved operational result, realized saving or executed system test is claimed.

## 10. AI-driven exploration — scenarios, not predictions

These are scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps. Each requires approved purpose, evidence, worker participation, proportionate safeguards and accountable human decisions.

2026–2030 — Assist: Find approved guidance with source, market and uncertainty.

2031–2040 — Connect: Connect help requests to authorized service status without leaking sensitive case data.

2041–2050 — Adapt: Adapt guidance presentation to worker context and preferences while preserving accessibility, privacy and human routes.

Competing future: An accessible staffed help route and simple directory may work better than personalization where devices and trusted content are lacking.

Disconfirmation question: Would the front door reduce unresolved help attempts across worker groups, or merely increase clicks and conceal failed submissions? State the observation that would make you stop, revise or prefer the competing future.
