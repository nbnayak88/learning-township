## 3. Origins: the problem survives the medium

These closed historical teaching windows are educational groupings, not universal invention dates or OmniHealth's corporate biography. Adoption overlaps; paper and older systems can coexist through the present.

1970–1984 — Supervisor notes and annual appraisals privilege what is visible; collaboration is easily missed.

1985–1994 — Rating forms and spreadsheets standardize comparison; unlike roles may still be compared unfairly.

1995–2004 — Integrated goals and review workflows connect team priorities; a single score can hide competing objectives.

2005–2014 — Continuous feedback brings more voices into coaching; permissions and opportunity to respond still matter.

2015–2025 — Cloud analytics and assisted summaries surface patterns; correlation cannot establish individual contribution or clinical causation.

Counterexample: more completed referrals may reflect easier cases or premature closure, rather than better coordinated care.

## 4. One story, one consequential choice

Dr. Amina Okafor (OHS-P07), Nursing and Care Coordination Director, reviews two synthetic teams. One closes 12 coordination tasks and another closes 9. Nina Fernandes (OHS-P06), Group CHRO, asks whether the faster team should receive the higher performance rating. Amina worries that rewarding closure alone could weaken follow-up ownership.

Pause before the reveal. Choose rank by closure count, abandon measurement, or combine meaningful goals with evidence quality and contextual review. State who benefits, who bears the risk and what evidence you need.

Evidence reveal: the 12-task team has three handoffs without receiving-owner confirmation. The 9-task team supported more complex coordination; no comparable case-mix baseline exists. These are synthetic observations, not patient outcome measures.

Revised decision: Amina pauses the ranking and asks for comparable scope, confirmed handoff criteria and staff explanation. Nina records a development conversation and a reviewable goal; neither infers clinical quality from a count or penalizes a worker for reporting an exception. A learner may defend a different option if the card names its authority, evidence, trade-off and conditions for stopping. Missing evidence remains unknown; an urgent request does not manufacture approval.

## 5. Applications serve accountable people

Proposed responsibilities, subject to local discovery:

- Performance services store goals, feedback and documented reviews; delegated managers own evaluations with a worker response and appeal route.
- Clinical/operational quality owners define appropriate service measures and contextual limitations; HR cannot redefine care quality from an activity dashboard.
- Core HR supplies role and reporting context; analytics stewards own metric definitions, lineage and permitted aggregation.
- Learning receives an agreed development need; Compensation owns any later reward proposal. A review score is not an instruction to change pay.

Across these boundaries, HR owns employment facts; designated clinical governance owns clinical privilege decisions; IAM administers approved access. Employment, learning completion, account access and permission for clinical work are distinct states. Share only the data needed for the declared purpose; keep patient records and detailed health information out of general HR learning cards. Omar Haddad (OHS-P10), CISO and Privacy Lead, governs privacy/access assurance; jurisdiction-specific applicability remains unresolved.

SAP examples illustrate responsibilities rather than prescribe a purchase: Performance & Goals linked to Employee Central, Learning and People Analytics; compensation receives only appropriately approved inputs. No feature availability, license entitlement, configuration, legal compliance or clinical conformance is established here. AI may draft a reminder or summarize permitted evidence; it cannot grant clinical privilege, make a high-impact employment decision or attest to payment or delivery.

## 7. One synthetic build experiment

Synthetic team COORD-A: 12 closures, 3 unconfirmed receivers; team COORD-B: 9 closures, complexity unknown. Review period 1–31 October 2026; no identifiable patient data or actual rating.

Complete one Team Performance Build Card with these six fields:

1. Need and scope: Support coaching and dependable coordination without rewarding unsafe shortcuts or comparing incomparable work. Explain the capability and value-stream step before naming a tool.
2. Facts and time: preserve identifiers, source, effective/as-of time, unknowns and approved policy version. Distinguish a source fact from your assumption.
3. Decision and ownership: Define one balanced goal, supporting evidence, a quality/burden guardrail and the manager's review/appeal authority. Record one alternative, its trade-off and the evidence that could favour it.
4. Handoff and recovery: Mark uncertain measures as provisional; ask the data owner for correction, notify affected workers and reopen the review if new evidence changes its basis. Name the accountable exception role, accessible human route and condition for reconciliation.
5. Local variant and data: choose one facility/language/role variation; justify minimum fields, purpose, owner, retention decision and viewers. Retain a non-digital route.
6. Evidence and learning: record T01–T03 input → expected → observed paper result → revision → reviewer question on this same card. Leave results unfilled until observed.

## 8. Exactly three tabletop tests

### OHS-HR03-T01 — Activity is not outcome

Input/reveal: Three of 12 closures lack receiving-owner confirmation.

Expected in the proposed design: Do not treat all 12 as confirmed handoffs or infer patient benefit; keep closure and confirmation definitions separate.

Record: Metric definition, numerator/denominator and revised judgment. Observed result and pass/revise remain unfilled until learners perform the tabletop.

### OHS-HR03-T02 — Unequal context

Input/reveal: Team B's complexity and staffing exposure are unknown.

Expected in the proposed design: No definitive ranking until comparability is established; record uncertainty and invite worker context.

Record: Missing denominators, contextual questions and interim coaching choice. Observed result and pass/revise remain unfilled until learners perform the tabletop.

### OHS-HR03-T03 — Late correction and appeal

Input/reveal: A corrected source changes one team's counts after the review.

Expected in the proposed design: Preserve the original review and correction history; delegated reviewer reconsiders with worker notice; no automatic pay change.

Record: Version, human reconsideration and accessible appeal route. Observed result and pass/revise remain unfilled until learners perform the tabletop.

Acceptance is a defensible completed card and an explanation of all three decisions after revision. A paper discussion proves neither a working system nor a safe clinical service. Publishing this lesson executes no tests.

## 10. AI-driven exploration: three scenarios

Scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps:
- 2026–2030 — Assist: An assistant drafts a review summary citing approved evidence.
- 2031–2040 — Connect: Governed data products link goals, team context and learning needs.
- 2041–2050 — Adapt: A bounded assistant suggests goal revisions when the service model changes, subject to manager and worker dialogue.

Competing future: facilities retain locally governed workflows and modest deterministic tools because evidence quality, cost or worker trust makes broader automation unsuitable.

Falsification question: Would AI summaries systematically under-credit coordination work or discourage exception reporting compared with facilitated reviews? State what observation would make you reduce, redesign or stop assistance. Human accountability does not disappear in any scenario.
