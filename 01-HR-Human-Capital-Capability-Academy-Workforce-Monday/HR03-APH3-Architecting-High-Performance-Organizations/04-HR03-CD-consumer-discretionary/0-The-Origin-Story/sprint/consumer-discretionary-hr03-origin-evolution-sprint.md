## 3. Origins and process evolution

The five closed windows are educational groupings, not universal invention dates or OmniVibe's corporate biography (the source gives its founding as 1992). Adoption overlaps; older practices can coexist through the present. Ask what problem each response solved and what it left unresolved.

1970–1984 — Supervisor judgments and personnel notes guide appraisal; contribution evidence can be opaque.

1985–1994 — Annual forms and objectives formalize evaluation; recency and supervisor bias can persist.

1995–2004 — Digital appraisal and goal databases improve retrieval; stored ratings still need context.

2005–2014 — Cascaded goals and feedback workflows connect strategy; goal completion can reward the wrong behavior.

2015–2025 — Continuous conversations and analytics improve visibility; fair comparisons still require role, workload and service evidence.

Recall: reconstruct problem → process/application response → unfinished problem. Which old failure could survive in a modern system?

## 4. One persona-led dilemma

Lead: Daniel Weber, Head of Rewards and Performance (OVC-P10). Counterpart: Nikhil Das, Store Associate and Workforce Representative (OVC-P16). These established personas are fictional. The following incident, record IDs and reveals are new synthetic teaching additions.

Scene 1 — Daniel is asked to align store teams with consumer experience goals. A synthetic associate leads the sales ranking; Nikhil asks whether a checkout colleague who resolves returns has been made invisible. The problem is fair contribution evidence, before a performance tool choice.

Scene 2 — Choose a sales-only ranking, identical targets for all roles or role-aware goals with common service guardrails. Reveal: the leading store deferred returns into the next review period. Sales figures alone cannot establish responsible service.

Scene 3 — Oliver Brooks, Store Operations Director (OVC-P06), reveals that shift mix differs. Daniel revises the goal and asks for observation periods, role context and employee response. A review recommendation does not authorize a pay award or adverse employment action.

Decision pause: record your first choice before the reveal, then revise it. Name one fact that would make the alternative better.

## 5. Responsibilities and decision rights

Performance services own goals, observations and review versions; managers own documented coaching and proposed ratings. Employee input and challenge routes remain visible. Commerce/returns owners attest service facts and definitions; HR core supplies approved role context; Rewards owns any later compensation decision under separate approval. SAP Performance & Goals illustrates the source proposal, not an independent truth about contribution.

Frame the business rule and accountable owner before selecting technology. Source proposals do not authorize data sharing, sensitive transactions or autonomous decisions. Keep an accessible human exception and worker challenge route.

## 7. One build card — Performance Decision Build Card

Synthetic experiment (OVC-TOS-HR03-EXP01): SYN-G300 scores a store sales associate over one synthetic review period. Include sales, return handling and assigned service duties as separate evidence fields; weights and real targets remain TBD. Record an initial rating and a revised judgment after the return deferral reveal.

Fill nine fields on one page: purpose/person affected; current and proposed state with source evidence; object/identity definitions; business date/time and version; application and data owners; human approval and challenge route; chosen option versus alternative and trade-off; exception/fallback and automation boundary; three test observations and your revision.

Unknown country, brand, franchise, device, policy and authority conditions need an owner and discovery question. Use no real personal data. Consider learning access, workload, service continuity and avoidable rework where evidence supports them; do not infer financial or environmental savings.

## 8. Exactly three tabletop tests

### OVC-TOS-HR03-T01 — Metric gaming

Reveal/input: Sales are high while unresolved returns increase.

Expected design response: Reject a sales-only inference; seek linked service evidence and revise the proposed goal or review.

### OVC-TOS-HR03-T02 — Unequal opportunity

Reveal/input: Two associates have different shifts and assigned service duties.

Expected design response: Expose comparability limits and role context; do not rank raw totals as equal opportunity.

### OVC-TOS-HR03-T03 — Contestability and boundary

Reveal/input: An associate challenges a wrong review and the assistant proposes a pay cut.

Expected design response: Preserve review versions, route the challenge to an authorized reviewer and block an unauthorized pay or employment action.

For each, record input → expected response → actual observed paper response → evidence note → pass/revise. These expected responses are proposed acceptance criteria. Publication executes none of the tests; all observation slots start unfilled. A bypass of a protected decision calls for redesign, even if the card is complete.

## 9. Traceability and honest measures

OVC-TOS-HR03-REQ01 — Connect strategy to role-aware goals and traceable contribution evidence. Source connection: HR case focus, OVC-M09–OVC-M12 and OVC-SIM-06. Acceptance: T01–T02; identify authoritative evidence, owner and revision.

OVC-TOS-HR03-REQ02 — Keep review challenge, reward approval and sensitive actions distinct. Source connection: OVC-M17–OVC-M19. Acceptance: T02–T03; identify the protected decision, exception and recovery condition.

OVC-TOS-HR03-ADR01 — Proposed educational choice: Role-aware goals with common service safeguards over sales-only ranking; compare identical targets and their fairness trade-off. Learners may reject it with evidence; it is not a client-approved architecture decision.

OVC-TOS-HR03-EXP01 — The single card experiment extends the existing case's bounded-pilot discipline; T01–T03 do not complete the wider case's experiments or implementation tests.

OVC-TOS-HR03-M01 — Mandatory card fields completed / 9 × 100: learning completeness only. OVC-TOS-HR03-M02 — Record whether each proposed acceptance criterion survives its evidence reveal; record observed paper results separately from expectations. Real baselines and targets remain TBD.

OVC-TOS-HR03-EV01 — Initially unfilled evidence slot for the three observations, revised choice and reviewer questions. No achieved operational result, realized saving or executed system test is claimed.

## 10. AI-driven exploration — scenarios, not predictions

These are scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps. Each requires approved purpose, evidence, worker participation, proportionate safeguards and accountable human decisions.

2026–2030 — Assist: Summarize submitted feedback with source and uncertainty.

2031–2040 — Connect: Connect goals with governed service evidence without conflating correlation and contribution.

2041–2050 — Adapt: Suggest revised team goals as roles change; authorized people review fairness and employee challenges.

Competing future: Frequent human coaching with a small evidence journal may outperform algorithmic ranking in context-dependent service roles.

Disconfirmation question: Would the proposed goal improve responsible service, or increase deferred returns and unfair ratings? State the observation that would make you stop, revise or prefer the competing future.
