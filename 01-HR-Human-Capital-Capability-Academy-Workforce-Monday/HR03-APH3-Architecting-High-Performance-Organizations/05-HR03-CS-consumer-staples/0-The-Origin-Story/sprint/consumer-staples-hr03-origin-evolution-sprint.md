## 3. Origins and process evolution

These closed windows are educational groupings, not universal invention dates or OmniDaily corporate biography. Adoption overlaps; older practices can coexist through the present. Reconstruct each as need → response → unresolved problem.

1970–1984 — Annual appraisals summarize supervisor judgment; vague criteria hide uneven expectations.

1985–1994 — Objectives and rating forms make expectations explicit; output counts can displace quality.

1995–2004 — HR performance systems standardize cycles; comparable forms do not guarantee comparable evidence.

2005–2014 — Cascading goals and feedback link teams; local targets can still conflict.

2015–2025 — Continuous performance and analytics improve visibility; attribution and human calibration remain unresolved by dashboards alone.

## 4. One persona-led story: The highest output, the wrong conclusion

Scene 1: Elena Petrović, Plant Operations Director (ODG-P09), sees a synthetic team leading its output target. Nandita Sen, Group CHRO (ODG-P06), asks what the output number represents.

Scene 2: Choose production volume alone or a balanced goal with quality evidence. Reveal from David Okoro, Global Quality and Product Safety Director (ODG-P07): a portion belongs to a held batch, so it is not released output.

Scene 3: A late quality result does not establish individual blame. A team also reports that equipment downtime reduced its opportunity to produce.

Scene 4: Elena revises the goal card: define released output and controllable contribution separately, invite worker evidence and route quality release to David's authority. Reviewers explain the rating rather than deriving it automatically.

Decision pause: Would a balanced evidence review or an output-only score better support trust? What evidence might show the balanced design is too complex? Record a first answer before the reveal, then revise the same card. Name a counterexample that would favor the alternative.

## 5. Responsibilities and human decision rights

Strategy/process owners define business objectives; goal/performance services own agreed objectives, feedback and reviews. Plant operations owns production evidence; quality/LIMS owns hold/release evidence. HR stewards role expectations and review governance; authorized managers/calibration panels decide ratings and appeals. Analytics assembles provenance, not employee blame. SAP Performance & Goals is an example.

These are proposed educational responsibilities. Establish the business rule, source of truth and accountable human before choosing technology. SAP examples illustrate a capability and are not verified deployments, licensing claims or product roadmaps. A worker must have an accessible human route to question incorrect data or a decision. HR status, learning completion, work authorization and quality/batch release are distinct decisions.

## 7. One build card: Performance Evidence Decision Card

Synthetic experiment: Create SYN-GOAL303 for one plant team, linked to one enterprise availability objective. Use fictional output, hold and downtime evidence with no financial benefit estimate.

Fill nine fields on one page: (1) purpose/person affected; (2) current/proposed state and evidence; (3) identifiers and definitions; (4) business time/version; (5) application/data owners; (6) human approval/appeal; (7) chosen option versus alternative and trade-off; (8) exception, fallback and automation boundary; (9) three observed test responses and revision.

Design constraint: Distinguish production, released product, availability and individual contribution. The enterprise shelf-availability target is not an employee rating or causal attribution.

All SYN identifiers, episode details and injected evidence are new teaching data. Country rules, collective agreements, qualifications, worker/device populations and local approvals remain unknown unless explicitly sourced. Record each unknown with an owner and discovery question. Do not invent legal rules or calculate unsupported operational benefits.

## 8. Exactly three tabletop tests

### ODG-TOS-HR03-T01 — Held output

Reveal/input: A high production count includes a quality-held batch.

Expected design response: Label held/released quantities and withhold unsupported achievement; do not override quality release.

### ODG-TOS-HR03-T02 — Unequal opportunity

Reveal/input: A comparison team had equipment downtime outside its control.

Expected design response: Show context and evidence; require human review rather than mechanically lowering its rating.

### ODG-TOS-HR03-T03 — Late correction

Reveal/input: Quality evidence changes after review submission.

Expected design response: Version the evidence, route a controlled review/appeal and preserve the earlier decision trail.

For each test record input → expected response → actual paper response → evidence note → pass/revise. These are proposed acceptance criteria. The evidence slot starts unfilled: publication runs none of the tests and proves no operational result. If a protected human decision can be bypassed, revise the design even if every card field is filled.

## 9. Traceability and measures

ODG-TOS-HR03-REQ01 — Trace enterprise intent to one role/team goal with defined evidence and denominator. Derived from the supporting HR source mandate and ODG-M18; assessed by T01.

ODG-TOS-HR03-REQ02 — Keep quality authority, fair context and review corrections explicit. Derived from the source governance mandate and ODG-M19; assessed by T02–T03.

ODG-TOS-HR03-ADR01 — Prefer a small balanced goal with evidence review over a single output ranking; compare clarity, effort and incentive effects. This proposed educational decision can be rejected with evidence; record the alternative and consequence.

ODG-TOS-HR03-EXP01 — The one synthetic build card extends ODG-M19's delivery-readiness discipline; T01–T03 do not execute the larger implementation case.

ODG-TOS-HR03-M01 — Mandatory card fields completed / 9 × 100: learning completeness only. ODG-TOS-HR03-M02 — Record whether T02/T03 preserve context, appeal and corrected evidence. Record actual paper observations rather than assuming success.

ODG-TOS-HR03-EV01 — Unfilled evidence slot for test observations, revisions and reviewer questions. Existing case success measures are intended outcomes; no baseline or target attainment follows from publishing this edition.

## 10. AI-driven exploration: scenarios, not predictions

These are scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps. Each needs evidence, approved purpose, accountable ownership and proportionate controls.

2026–2030 — Assist: Assistance could draft goal wording and flag ambiguous measures for manager review.

2031–2040 — Connect: Goal reviews could connect versioned plant and quality evidence without replacing ratings authority.

2041–2050 — Adapt: Adaptive goal suggestions could reflect changing work only after transparent worker/manager agreement.

Competing future: Stable goals with regular human conversations may be fairer than constantly changing algorithmic targets.

Disconfirmation question: Would the balanced goal increase gaming, unexplained ratings or review effort without improving decision quality? Name evidence that would stop or change the hypothesis.
