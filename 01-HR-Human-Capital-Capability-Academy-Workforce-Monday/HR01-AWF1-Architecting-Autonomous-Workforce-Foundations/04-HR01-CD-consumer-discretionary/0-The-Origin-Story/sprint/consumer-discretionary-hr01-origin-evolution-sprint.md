## 1. One learner promise

Explain one store reassignment without losing worker identity, history or the distinction between employment and access.

## 2. Source boundaries

These are source-stipulated baselines, not verified operations. ## 3. Origins and process evolution

The five closed windows are educational groupings, not universal invention dates or OmniVibe's corporate biography (the source gives its founding as 1992). Adoption overlaps; older practices can coexist through the present. Ask what problem each response solved and what it left unresolved.

1970–1984 — Personnel ledgers answer who works where; matching names can still hide different people.

1985–1994 — Structured staff and position registers improve organization; brand-specific meanings still diverge.

1995–2004 — HRIS/ERP connects employment transactions and payroll; a shared identifier alone does not settle employer boundaries.

2005–2014 — Self-service workflows reduce rekeying; completed HR tasks do not prove receiving-system updates.

2015–2025 — Cloud workforce cores improve shared semantics; country and franchise exceptions still need governed evidence.

Recall: reconstruct problem → process/application response → unfinished problem. Which old failure could survive in a modern system?

## 4. One persona-led dilemma

Lead: Ravi Kapoor, Group CHRO (OVC-P04). Counterpart: Jonas Lim, Integration and HR Data Lead (OVC-P12). These established personas are fictional. The following incident, record IDs and reveals are new synthetic teaching additions.

Scene 1 — Ravi sees two store records for synthetic associate SYN-P100. Oliver Brooks, Store Operations Director (OVC-P06), wants the associate ready at a receiving owned store tomorrow. The business problem is a trustworthy assignment, not choosing a new HR platform.

Scene 2 — Choose a rigid global template, independent brand files or common identity semantics with justified local extensions. Reveal: the old assignment remains valid today and the new assignment begins tomorrow. Overwriting today's state would destroy the explanation for current time and pay.

Scene 3 — Jonas reveals that the receiving row might represent a franchise worker. Employer identity and franchise authority are not resolved by a shared brand name. Ravi revises his choice: hold ambiguous employer treatment, preserve history and ask an accountable HR owner for evidence.

Decision pause: record your first choice before the reveal, then revise it. Name one fact that would make the alternative better.

## 5. Responsibilities and decision rights

Workforce core: HR owns person, employment, position, organization and assignment facts with entity-specific history rules. Time and payroll owners consume approved inputs; IAM/security owners confirm access actions. Store Operations owns local service readiness. Jonas owns interface contracts, not employment decisions. Authorized HR approvers decide the reassignment; a franchise operator's authority must be discovered. SAP Employee Central is the source's proposed core example; not every entity is technically effective-dated.

Frame the business rule and accountable owner before selecting technology. Source proposals do not authorize data sharing, sensitive transactions or autonomous decisions. Keep an accessible human exception and worker challenge route.

## 6. The 120-minute learning arc

0–10 — Read the promise and state the worker/business problem.

10–30 — Reconstruct the five historical windows and their unfinished problems.

30–50 — Play the scenes; record an initial choice, reveal evidence and revise.

50–65 — Draw application responsibilities, data ownership and human decision rights.

65–95 — Complete the one build card, compare alternatives and explore the three future scenarios; scan only a resource that answers your question.

95–115 — Perform exactly the three tabletop tests below on the paper design and record observations.

115–120 — Explain the decision in 60 seconds, reflect and hand off to Pre-Foundation. Total: 120 minutes.

## 7. One build card — Workforce Foundation Decision Card

Synthetic experiment (OVC-TOS-HR01-EXP01): SYN-P100 / SYN-E100 moves from owned store SYN-S01 to proposed SYN-S02 effective 1 November 2026. A second row has an unconfirmed employer. Use as-of views for 31 October and 1 November; do not merge by name.

Fill nine fields on one page: purpose/person affected; current and proposed state with source evidence; object/identity definitions; business date/time and version; application and data owners; human approval and challenge route; chosen option versus alternative and trade-off; exception/fallback and automation boundary; three test observations and your revision.

Unknown country, brand, franchise, device, policy and authority conditions need an owner and discovery question. Use no real personal data. Consider learning access, workload, service continuity and avoidable rework where evidence supports them; do not infer financial or environmental savings.

## 8. Exactly three tabletop tests

### OVC-TOS-HR01-T01 — As-of identity

Reveal/input: Show both dates and the ambiguous second row.

Expected design response: Preserve person and assignment history; flag employer ambiguity instead of manufacturing a second person or merging unverified people.

### OVC-TOS-HR01-T02 — Authority boundary

Reveal/input: The receiving location is revealed to be a franchise.

Expected design response: Hold unapproved cross-employer sharing; request verified employer and delegated authority without treating franchise staff as owned-store employees.

### OVC-TOS-HR01-T03 — Incomplete handoff

Reveal/input: HR approves the change; access acknowledgement is missing.

Expected design response: Keep HR approval separate from access completion, expose the exception owner and use only a specifically approved fallback.

For each, record input → expected response → actual observed paper response → evidence note → pass/revise. These expected responses are proposed acceptance criteria. Publication executes none of the tests; all observation slots start unfilled. A bypass of a protected decision calls for redesign, even if the card is complete.

## 9. Traceability and honest measures

OVC-TOS-HR01-REQ01 — Preserve identity and business-valid assignment evidence. Source connection: HR case focus, OVC-M09–OVC-M12 and OVC-SIM-04 and OVC-SIM-07. Acceptance: T01–T02; identify authoritative evidence, owner and revision.

OVC-TOS-HR01-REQ02 — Separate HR approval, franchise authority and downstream completion. Source connection: OVC-M17–OVC-M19. Acceptance: T02–T03; identify the protected decision, exception and recovery condition.

OVC-TOS-HR01-ADR01 — Proposed educational choice: Common workforce semantics with governed local exceptions rather than unbounded brand copies; compare a rigid template's local-fit cost. Learners may reject it with evidence; it is not a client-approved architecture decision.

OVC-TOS-HR01-EXP01 — The single card experiment extends the existing case's bounded-pilot discipline; T01–T03 do not complete the wider case's experiments or implementation tests.

OVC-TOS-HR01-M01 — Mandatory card fields completed / 9 × 100: learning completeness only. OVC-TOS-HR01-M02 — Record whether each proposed acceptance criterion survives its evidence reveal; record observed paper results separately from expectations. Real baselines and targets remain TBD.

OVC-TOS-HR01-EV01 — Initially unfilled evidence slot for the three observations, revised choice and reviewer questions. No achieved operational result, realized saving or executed system test is claimed.

## 10. AI-driven exploration — scenarios, not predictions

These are scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps. Each requires approved purpose, evidence, worker participation, proportionate safeguards and accountable human decisions.

2026–2030 — Assist: Draft identity-quality questions and explain missing assignment evidence.

2031–2040 — Connect: Exchange versioned assignment and access status across authorized operators.

2041–2050 — Adapt: Suggest adaptable role structures while humans approve employer and accountability changes.

Competing future: A federated register with strong local stewardship may outperform a central core when employer boundaries remain uncertain.

Disconfirmation question: Would the common core increase mistaken identity merges or delay legitimate local changes compared with a controlled federated pilot? State the observation that would make you stop, revise or prefer the competing future.
