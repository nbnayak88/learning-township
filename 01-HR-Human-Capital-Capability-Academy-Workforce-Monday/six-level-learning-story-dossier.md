# From Curiosity to Architect — Six-Level Learning Story Dossier

**SuccessLabs Academy · Experiential Learning (XL) · IIL — Industry Immersion Lab**  
**Mission:** Architecting Experiences for a Better World.  
**Edition:** 31 October 2026 · v1.0

> **What’s in it for me?** Follow one enterprise transformation from its first problem to its value realization—and develop the capability to explain, design, deliver and improve it.

**One continuing story · Six levels of growing capability · One living evidence dossier**

## At a glance: what do I gain?

| Level | My promise | I gain | I practise | I leave with |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **① Origin Story** · Explorer | **“I understand why this matters.”** | Clarity about the problem, its origins and its impact on people. | Question assumptions and explore one persona’s dilemma. | One build card and three tabletop observations, once performed and recorded. |
| **② Pre-Foundation** · Investigator | **“I can ask the right questions.”** | The language and context to join a useful discovery conversation. | Listen to stakeholders, trace the process and uncover missing facts. | A clear problem statement, stakeholder needs and discovery questions. |
| **③ Foundation** · Designer | **“I can design a sound starting point.”** | A connected understanding of process, data, applications and ownership. | Define the future state, responsibilities and acceptance criteria. | A foundation blueprint with clear boundaries and decisions. |
| **④ Essentials** · Practitioner | **“I can make the journey work.”** | Practical confidence connecting design to end-to-end delivery. | Build a prototype, connect handoffs and test the worker’s experience. | A demonstrable prototype, test evidence and feedback-driven revisions. |
| **⑤ Advanced** · Transformation Lead | **“I can handle complexity and failure.”** | Judgment under uncertainty, competing priorities and operational pressure. | Diagnose failures, defend trade-offs, rehearse recovery and assess scale. | A troubleshooting record, recovery design and scale-readiness case. |
| **⑥ Ultimate** · Architect | **“I can defend the value and the next decision.”** | The ability to judge outcomes, costs, risks and long-term value together. | Evaluate ROI assumptions, resolve remaining challenges and defend scale, redesign or stop. | A value-realization story, financial evaluation and lessons learned—the story’s conclusion. |

These are intended learning outcomes demonstrated through evidence. Stage labels describe the learner’s responsibility in the experience; they do not confer a professional job title, certification or guaranteed career result.

## How the semi-fiction progresses

Use the same enterprise, recognizable protagonists and central transformation promise throughout. Each level changes the situation and gives the learner greater responsibility.

| Act | What happens in the story? | Question I must answer | Handoff to the next act |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Origin Story — Awaken** | A person encounters a meaningful problem. Process evolution explains how it arose. | Why must something change? | “We see the problem. Do we understand the people and causes?” |
| **Pre-Foundation — Discover** | Stakeholders reveal conflicting needs, hidden dependencies and missing evidence. | What is actually happening—and to whom? | “We understand the needs. What common foundation can support them?” |
| **Foundation — Design** | The team agrees definitions, ownership, boundaries and a proposed future state. | What must we get right before building? | “The design is coherent. Can it work end to end?” |
| **Essentials — Deliver** | The first usable prototype encounters working conditions and user feedback. | Can this journey work in practice? | “The prototype works under stated conditions. What happens under pressure?” |
| **Advanced — Navigate** | Failures, scale, local variation and competing priorities challenge the original design. | Can we diagnose, recover and adapt responsibly? | “We can defend the revised design. Was the investment worthwhile?” |
| **Ultimate — Realize** | The council examines value, costs, unresolved risks and lessons, then makes its final decision. | What value does the evidence support—and what should happen next? | Conclude the original transformation promise; optionally reveal a new enterprise challenge. |

Origin Story retains its bounded **120-minute sprint: one promise, one persona-led story, one build card and exactly three tabletop tests**. Later levels use appropriate learning formats and durations; this dossier does not force every act into a sprint.

## Example: the continuing OmniWorks HR01 story

**Fictional enterprise:** OmniWorks Industrial Systems Ltd.  
**Source-scoped protagonist:** Asha Mehra, Global HR Core Transformation Director. Her source also contains Asha/Sam naming variants; the existing reconciliation issue remains open.

| Level | Continuing episode |
|---|---|
| **Origin Story** | Asha discovers that an approved workforce transfer does not prove readiness for restricted maintenance work. |
| **Pre-Foundation** | She investigates inconsistent worker definitions, country requirements and unclear ownership of access decisions. |
| **Foundation** | The team designs a workforce core, governed local extensions, identifier mappings and separate authorization responsibilities. |
| **Essentials** | Learners prototype a representative transfer across HR, learning, payroll and access services, then record what the tests reveal. |
| **Advanced** | A delayed event, disputed qualification and payroll-cutoff conflict challenge the design. Learners diagnose causes, rehearse recovery and revise decisions. |
| **Ultimate** | The council evaluates the supported worker/operational value against full costs and risks, then defends a scale, redesign, pause or stop recommendation. |

Pre-Foundation through Ultimate episodes above are **proposed story continuations**, not claims that those full learning editions, integrations or outcomes have already been delivered. [Open the Industrials HR01–HR12 publication index](industrials-origin-stories.md) for actual companion status.

## My living dossier: evidence that grows with the story

Keep one evolving case dossier. Preserve earlier thinking so the learner can explain how evidence changed the recommendation.

| Dossier section | Starts at | What I retain and develop |
|---|---|---|
| **Purpose and people** | Origin Story | Learner promise, protagonist, affected people, initial hypothesis and reflection. |
| **Discovery and sources** | Pre-Foundation | Stakeholder needs, process context, source references, assumptions and unresolved conflicts. |
| **Architecture and decisions** | Foundation | Capability/value-stream relationships, data ownership, application responsibilities, options and decision rights. |
| **Build and validation** | Essentials | Prototype versions, handoff contracts, test inputs, expected versus observed results and user feedback. |
| **Troubleshooting and resilience** | Advanced | Symptoms, competing causes, diagnosis evidence, containment, recovery, retesting and remaining risks. |
| **Value and conclusion** | Ultimate | Outcome evidence, costs, financial assumptions, sensitivities, lessons and the final council recommendation. |

Carry stable requirement, architecture-decision, experiment, test and measure IDs forward where they exist. Label new educational IDs clearly. Link revisions to earlier decisions rather than silently overwriting the learning history.

## Ultimate: the Value Realization Council finale

The learner defends one complete transformation story through six questions:

| Council question | Evidence I present |
|---|---|
| **What value changed?** | Baseline, comparison period, affected population, outcome measures, attribution limits and worker experience. |
| **Was it economically worthwhile?** | Implementation and operating costs; benefit assumptions; relevant ROI, payback or NPV analysis; sensitivity and downside scenarios. Keep cash benefits, avoided cost and released capacity distinct. |
| **What challenged the architecture?** | Constraints, dependencies, local variations, trade-offs and unresolved risks. |
| **What failed—and how did we respond?** | Troubleshooting chronology, evidence supporting the cause, containment, recovery and verification of corrective changes. |
| **What did we learn?** | Initial beliefs that changed, decisions to repeat, decisions to revise and consequences for people. |
| **What is the final recommendation?** | Scale, redesign, pause, stop or retire—with reasons, owners and any conditions for reconsideration. |

A defensible stop decision can demonstrate strong architectural judgment. The ending need not be a success celebration.

Close the human story as well: what changed for the protagonist, the worker and the people responsible for operating the solution? Resolve the original dilemma honestly, including any limitations that remain.

## Evidence and educational discipline

- Characters, dialogue and synthetic incidents may be fictional. Preserve established enterprise facts and source-scoped persona identities.
- Distinguish **source case facts, assumptions, targets, scenarios, planned tests, simulated observations and real observed outcomes**.
- An empty evidence slot is not success. A published lesson does not execute its tests.
- Fictional or simulated financial outcomes remain explicitly labelled; do not present them as client results.
- Keep historical teaching windows separate from future AI scenarios and from the enterprise’s corporate biography.
- Preserve the three classification axes: **learning level**, **HR/business theme**, and **architecture stream**; enterprise transformation streams remain separately identified.
- Educational alignment uses TOGAF for architecture decisions, BIZBOK for capabilities/value streams, BABOK for needs and acceptance, DAMA-DMBOK for governed data, COBIT for accountability, and relevant ISO/IEC principles for risk and assurance. No certification or compliance is claimed.
