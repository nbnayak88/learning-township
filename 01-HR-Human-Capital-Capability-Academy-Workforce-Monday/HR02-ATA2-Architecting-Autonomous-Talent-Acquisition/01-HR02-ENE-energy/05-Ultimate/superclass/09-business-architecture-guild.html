In brief. The Business Architecture Guild's BIZBOK Guide applied: ten domains, nine blueprints, the practice guide, alignment with IT architecture, and a worked ten-element example.

The Business Architecture Guild publishes the BIZBOK® Guide (A Guide to the Business Architecture Body of Knowledge®), the body of knowledge for business architecture. This chapter covers every part, section and appendix of the guide's contents and shows where each is applied here. The contents page supplied by the template owner is headed "BA GUILD 15.0" and lists the BIZBOK Guide v14.0 structure; match on titles if your edition numbers differently. All descriptions are written in our own words.

9.1 What business architecture is

Business architecture gives a holistic, multi-dimensional view of the business: its capabilities, its end-to-end value delivery, its information and its organisation, and how those relate to strategies, products, policies, initiatives and stakeholders. Its value is a shared, abstract picture of the enterprise and its ecosystem that turns strategy into actionable initiatives and supports transformation, complexity, risk and decision making. The one-line pitch: it enables effective, streamlined strategy execution.

Two points matter for this template: - It describes a business ecosystem, so customers, partners and outsourced capabilities are in scope, not only internal units. - It is not the operating model. The operating model shows how people, process and technology run the business; business architecture is value-driven, capability-based and ecosystem-wide, and guides operating-model investment.

Value proposition in one line. Business architecture gives leaders a shared, business-language picture so strategy, investment and change line up, and so competing or duplicate solutions are seen early. It turns "what do we do, for whom, and what must change" into views that business and IT can both use.

9.4 The framework: blueprints, scenarios, knowledgebase

The guide's framework has three components, none of them prescriptive: - Blueprints are single views of the business built from the shared domains. They give transparency, for example to expose competing solutions built for the same customers. - Scenarios apply the blueprints to a specific business question and end in a roadmap (section 9.8). - Knowledgebase stores the content once, organised in the organisation's own terms. Here it is the Git repository: registers with stable IDs.

Benefits of mapping strategy: a common language for goals and actions, visible links from strategy to capabilities and initiatives, earlier detection of conflicting objectives, and a defensible basis for prioritisation.

Strategy mapping in more detail (2.1)

Strategy mapping connects goals, objectives and courses of action to the rest of the architecture. Its principles include that a strategy belongs to a defined community of interest (the stakeholders whose value it affects) and that it responds to the external environment. Benefits are organisational alignment, fidelity of objectives as they cascade, and early detection of strategic gaps. Frameworks the guide names, and how each is used here:

Framework Use
Ansoff product and market grid Choose growth direction when defining objectives
SWOT Surface internal and external factors in the vision
Five Forces Assess competition, buyers and suppliers; risks
Kaplan-Norton strategy map Link objectives to customers, internal processes and enablers; KPI register
Business Motivation Model Structure ends, means, influencers and directives; principles and rules
Hoshin Kanri Cascade objectives into aligned actions; work packages
