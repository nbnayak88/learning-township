In brief. 9P4V is the transformation spine of this template. Four stages (Vision, Value, Victory, Venture) are delivered through nine pillars (Policy, Portfolio, Program, Platform, Product, Process, People, Practice, Project). The twelve architecture streams are the lenses, the industry is the context, and victory feeds the next vision. It is the doctoral contribution of Dr. Niladri Bihari Nayak, SuccessLabs Academy (From Vision to Venture: Enterprise Architecture in Odisha's Educational Landscape, Nayak, 2025), and is applied here to every chapter and register.

The principle. 9P4V is the spine. The 12 architecture streams are the lenses. Industry is the context. Transformation is the challenge. 21st-century skills are the capabilities. Experience is the design principle. Value and victory are the evidence. Venture is the scale.

3.1 Why a spine

TOGAF, BIZBOK, BABOK and DAMA-DMBOK each answer a part of the question. None says, in one line, where a transformation stands and what has to happen next. 9P4V does. Every artefact in this template can be placed on it, so a sponsor, an architect and a delivery lead read the same picture.

Framework Strength Gap that 9P4V closes
TOGAF Method and governance for architecture Does not frame the human and venture stages
BIZBOK Business blueprints Does not sequence vision to scale
BABOK Requirements and analysis Does not tie requirements to victory and scale
DAMA-DMBOK Data management Data is one lens, not the journey

3.4 The feedback loop

V4 Venture refines and expands into the next V1 Vision. Victory is not the end; it is the foundation of the next vision. In this template the loop is the continuous-improvement cycle (Chapter 16.9) and the reuse of the template for a new solution (Chapter 18.6).

3.5 The master journey

Case, context, vision, discover, diagnose, architect, experiment, decide, build, transform, measure, victory, venture, reflect, re-architect, next vision. The method is the SuccessLabs Applied Architecture Case Method. It should feel like a real engagement, not a textbook exercise.

3.9 Experience architecture

The mission is Architecting Experiences for a Better World. Every case states whose experience is transformed: customer, employee, partner, manager, executive or citizen. For each, ask: - What is the current experience, where are the pain points and which moments matter? - What should the future experience feel like? - Which processes, data and applications enable it, and where can AI improve it? - How will the improvement be measured?

3.10 Professional discipline

  • Big 4 transformation discipline. Diagnose (current-state, maturity, capability, process, technology, data, stakeholder and risk assessment); design (target operating model, target architecture, blueprint, business case, roadmap, governance); deliver (execution, change, implementation, adoption, training, benefits realisation); sustain (governance, continuous improvement, KPI monitoring, architecture evolution, value realisation, scaling).
  • MBB-style problem solving. Problem structuring, issue trees, hypothesis-driven thinking, MECE reasoning, 80/20 prioritisation, fact-based analysis, strategic options, trade-off analysis, executive synthesis, "so what?" thinking and recommendation under uncertainty. Evidence discipline: facts, assumptions, hypotheses, insights, decisions, kept apart.
  • Business-school pedagogy. Ambiguity, incomplete information, competing stakeholders, strategic choices, quantitative and qualitative evidence, constraints, ethical dilemmas, a decision-maker and a decision point. Final question: what would you recommend, why, and what should happen next?
  • Experimentation. Hypothesis, experiment, evidence, learning, then pivot or persevere. Define the critical assumption, experiment, MVP, success metric, data needed, risk, learning and pivot criteria.
  • Transformation shock. An unexpected event (budget cut of 20 to 25 percent, delay, failed integration, security concern, regulatory change, adoption drop, underperforming AI pilot, sponsor change, disruptive competitor) forces the plan to change. It tests adaptability, resilience, decision-making, systems thinking and leadership.
Transformation shockTypical architectural response
Budget cut of 20 to 25 percentRe-scope waves; protect highest-value capabilities
Programme delayedRe-sequence dependencies; renegotiate milestones
Critical integration failsFallback interface; contract and design review
Security concern emergesPause release; risk assessment; remediation plan
Regulatory requirement changesUpdate policy and rules; assess impact on requirements
Employee adoption dropsChange and training response; practice redesign
AI pilot underperformsPivot or persevere decision, based on evidence
Executive sponsor changesRe-engage sponsor; re-confirm vision and benefits
Competitor launches a disruptive solutionRevisit options and the value proposition

Evidence discipline. Learners separate facts, assumptions, hypotheses, insights and decisions, in that order, and never present an assumption as a fact. The closing question of every case is: What would you recommend, why, and what should happen next? There is not necessarily one perfect answer; the quality of reasoning, evidence, trade-offs, architecture and expected value is what counts.

3.11 Industry context

Work is grounded in the 11 GICS sectors: energy, materials, industrials, consumer discretionary, consumer staples, health care, financials, information technology, communication services, utilities, real estate. For each case examine industry structure, business model, customers, competitors, regulation, economics, trends, technology disruption, AI opportunities, sustainability, workforce implications and ecosystem dynamics. The learner solves a real industry problem, not a generic technology problem.

3.17 Executive Transformation Council

The final presentation simulates a real executive setting. A five-minute pitch follows the line vision, problem, insight, architecture, options, decision, roadmap, value, victory, venture, and answers: what should we do, why, how, what will it cost, what could go wrong, what value will it create, how will we know it worked, and how will we scale it?

3.19 The North Star and the signature principle

Mission, then context (11 industries), then spine (9P4V), then lenses (12 streams), then professional discipline (B-school, MBB, Big 4, innovation), then human capability (21st-century skills), then learning experience (cases, simulations, experiments, projects), then evidence (artefacts, decisions, demonstrations, outcomes), then impact: vision, value, victory, venture, next vision.

Do not teach 9P4V. Let learners experience 9P4V. The aim is people who can enter an ambiguous situation, understand the industry, frame the problem, create the vision, architect the transformation, experiment, decide, mobilise people, deliver value, create victory, build a sustainable venture and reimagine the next vision. SuccessLabs Academy develops learners into practitioners, architects, transformation leaders, enterprise builders and architects for a better world.
