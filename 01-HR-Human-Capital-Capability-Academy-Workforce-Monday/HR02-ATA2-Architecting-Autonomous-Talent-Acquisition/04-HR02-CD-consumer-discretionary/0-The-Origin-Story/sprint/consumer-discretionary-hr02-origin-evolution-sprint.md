## 3. Origins and process evolution

The five closed windows are educational groupings, not universal invention dates or OmniVibe's corporate biography (the source gives its founding as 1992). Adoption overlaps; older practices can coexist through the present. Ask what problem each response solved and what it left unresolved.

1970–1984 — Paper applications and local introductions coordinate hiring; decisions and induction evidence are hard to trace.

1985–1994 — Structured requisitions and personnel checklists improve consistency; handoffs still rely on individuals.

1995–2004 — Applicant tracking separates candidate and employee records; accepted offers still require controlled conversion.

2005–2014 — Online recruiting and onboarding workflows coordinate tasks; a green task count can hide role mismatch.

2015–2025 — Cloud talent journeys and digital preboarding improve continuity; lawful purpose, bias, authority and practical readiness still require review.

Recall: reconstruct problem → process/application response → unfinished problem. Which old failure could survive in a modern system?

## 4. One persona-led dilemma

Lead: Sofia Mendes, Head of Talent Acquisition and Onboarding (OVC-P08). Counterpart: Oliver Brooks, Store Operations Director (OVC-P06). These established personas are fictional. The following incident, record IDs and reveals are new synthetic teaching additions.

Scene 1 — Sofia faces high-volume store hiring before a fictional seasonal peak. An accepted candidate appears green in the recruiting tracker, yet Oliver cannot confirm access, induction or a supervised first shift. Faster hiring alone does not solve day-one readiness.

Scene 2 — Choose recruiting speed alone, a fully centralized onboarding checklist or a shared handoff with role- and market-specific evidence. Reveal: a digital-business candidate has been placed on a store template. A completed generic checklist can conceal missing service practice.

Scene 3 — Nikhil Das, Store Associate and Workforce Representative (OVC-P16), asks who will help the new colleague while serving customers. Reveal: the receiving operator's employer authority is unknown. Sofia revises the release gate and retains a human selection decision; no automated ranking or accepted offer authorizes work.

Decision pause: record your first choice before the reveal, then revise it. Name one fact that would make the alternative better.

## 5. Responsibilities and decision rights

Recruiting owns candidate/application/requisition records; authorized hiring managers and recruiters make selection decisions. Onboarding owns assigned readiness tasks and evidence, not universal permission to work. HR core owns the approved employment record; IAM owns approved access provisioning; Learning owns assessed learning evidence; Oliver's authorized local manager approves supervised service readiness. Sofia coordinates the handoff. SAP Recruiting and Onboarding are illustrative source proposals, with market, feature and licensing checks still open.

Frame the business rule and accountable owner before selecting technology. Source proposals do not authorize data sharing, sensitive transactions or autonomous decisions. Keep an accessible human exception and worker challenge route.

## 7. One build card — Hire-to-Readiness Build Card

Synthetic experiment (OVC-TOS-HR02-EXP01): SYN-C200 applies to SYN-REQ200; synthetic accepted offer SYN-O200 links to proposed employment SYN-E200. Build one explicit transition from selected → offer accepted → employment approved → locally ready, with missing evidence held separately. Recruiting and Onboarding remain two source cases within this single card and 120 minutes.

Fill nine fields on one page: purpose/person affected; current and proposed state with source evidence; object/identity definitions; business date/time and version; application and data owners; human approval and challenge route; chosen option versus alternative and trade-off; exception/fallback and automation boundary; three test observations and your revision.

Unknown country, brand, franchise, device, policy and authority conditions need an owner and discovery question. Use no real personal data. Consider learning access, workload, service continuity and avoidable rework where evidence supports them; do not infer financial or environmental savings.

## 8. Exactly three tabletop tests

### OVC-TOS-HR02-T01 — Candidate-to-worker mapping

Reveal/input: A returning candidate matches an existing name but not verified identity.

Expected design response: Require identity evidence and controlled conversion; preserve candidate purpose and history without blind duplication or merging.

### OVC-TOS-HR02-T02 — Readiness versus checklist

Reveal/input: All generic tasks are complete but store service practice is absent.

Expected design response: Hold unsupervised service release; name the local supervisor, required evidence and supported first-shift option.

### OVC-TOS-HR02-T03 — Withdrawn or delayed start

Reveal/input: The offer is withdrawn after an access request was sent.

Expected design response: Reconcile cancellation with each owner, revoke only approved entitlements and do not infer that a sent cancellation reached every target.

For each, record input → expected response → actual observed paper response → evidence note → pass/revise. These expected responses are proposed acceptance criteria. Publication executes none of the tests; all observation slots start unfilled. A bypass of a protected decision calls for redesign, even if the card is complete.

## 9. Traceability and honest measures

OVC-TOS-HR02-REQ01 — Preserve candidate-to-employment lineage and a human selection decision. Source connection: HR case focus, OVC-M09–OVC-M12 and OVC-SIM-02 and OVC-SIM-04. Acceptance: T01–T02; identify authoritative evidence, owner and revision.

OVC-TOS-HR02-REQ02 — Release role-specific readiness only with accountable evidence and confirmed handoffs. Source connection: OVC-M17–OVC-M19. Acceptance: T02–T03; identify the protected decision, exception and recovery condition.

OVC-TOS-HR02-ADR01 — Proposed educational choice: Use an evidence-bearing shared hire-to-readiness contract with local role tasks; compare a speed-only funnel and a universal checklist. Learners may reject it with evidence; it is not a client-approved architecture decision.

## 10. AI-driven exploration — scenarios, not predictions

These are scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps. Each requires approved purpose, evidence, worker participation, proportionate safeguards and accountable human decisions.

2026–2030 — Assist: Draft role-specific readiness questions without selecting candidates.

2031–2040 — Connect: Connect accepted-offer, employment, learning and access evidence through authorized contracts.

2041–2050 — Adapt: Suggest alternative supervised learning routes as service roles evolve; humans retain hiring and release decisions.

Competing future: Local recruiter and buddy coordination may create more trust than automated orchestration when task evidence or device access is weak.

Disconfirmation question: Would a connected checklist reduce missing readiness evidence, or merely move delays out of the recruiting metric? State the observation that would make you stop, revise or prefer the competing future.
