The hiring decision

Nandita Sen (ODG-P06), Group CHRO, and Elena Petrović (ODG-P09), Plant Operations Director, prepare a synthetic factory technician's first day. Nandita asks whether the accepted offer is enough to populate every downstream status. A corporate digital-product manager can complete remote setup, while the technician requires a local practical assessment.

Choose first: One universal checklist, or common steps with role-specific gates? Would you favour rapid automatic provisioning or a staged, evidence-based handoff?

Evidence reveal: A repeated hire message creates uncertainty about employee identity and an onboarding form is complete but the assessment evidence is absent. Nandita keeps the hiring decision, employee-creation acknowledgement and work-readiness approval separate. Elena holds the restricted assignment and offers supervised learning through an authorised local route.

Counterexample: Low-risk access could proceed while restricted work remains held; a single universal checklist would delay corporate staff or admit factory staff too early.

Hire-to-readiness architecture

SmartRecruiters carries recruiting decisions; Employee Central and Onboarding carry employment facts; the receiving owner carries readiness and access. A fact answers one question only.

Who owns each decision

Application responsibilityAccountable ownerData and human boundary
Hiring decisionGroup CHRO and accountable panelHuman selection decision
Employee creationHR steward (Employee Central)Acknowledgement is separate from readiness
Work readinessPlant operations directorRestricted assignment held until local evidence exists
Access and identityCISO and privacy leadNo shared credentials; approved assisted route for exceptions

A source-system record, a receiving-system acknowledgment and a human permission answer different questions. Provide a private, accessible correction or appeal route for workers and candidates.

Measures

  • accepted-to-ready time for factory technicians
  • competence signed off before release duties
  • local authority granted at start
  • competence complete before isolation work
  • partner onboarding complete before access
  • training complete before cold-chain duties
  • reconciliation of offline starter records
  • time to first approved planning access

Limits and next steps

  • Nothing here is tenant-tested; fit labels are proposals to confirm in fit-to-standard workshops.
  • Benefit statements and acceptance criteria are standard per stage and filled with sector facts; they need subject-matter review.
  • Recruiting roles, AI-driven roles and some case personas are synthetic.
  • Job classification, skills and competencies are a starting taxonomy; confirm against national occupation standards and the licensed SAP skills ontology.
  • Next: run the requirements and stories past an SAP recruiting lead and the sector evidence owners, then baseline effort in Explore.
