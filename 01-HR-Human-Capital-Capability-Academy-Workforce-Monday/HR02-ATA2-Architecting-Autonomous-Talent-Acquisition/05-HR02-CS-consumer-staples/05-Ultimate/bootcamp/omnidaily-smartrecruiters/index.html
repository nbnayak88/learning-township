An accepted offer is not a ready shift

Nandita Sen (ODG-P06), Group CHRO, and Elena Petrović (ODG-P09), Plant Operations Director, prepare a synthetic factory technician's first day. Nandita asks whether the accepted offer is enough to populate every downstream status. A corporate digital-product manager can complete remote setup, while the technician requires a local practical assessment.

Choose first: One universal checklist, or common steps with role-specific gates? Would you favour rapid automatic provisioning or a staged, evidence-based handoff?

Evidence reveal: A repeated hire message creates uncertainty about employee identity and an onboarding form is complete but the assessment evidence is absent. Nandita keeps the hiring decision, employee-creation acknowledgement and work-readiness approval separate. Elena holds the restricted assignment and offers supervised learning through an authorised local route.

Counterexample: Low-risk access could proceed while restricted work remains held; a single universal checklist would delay corporate staff or admit factory staff too early.

Hire-to-readiness architecture

SmartRecruiters carries recruiting decisions; Employee Central and Onboarding carry employment facts; the receiving owner carries readiness and access. A fact answers one question only.

  1. PositionApproved in Employee Central
  2. RequisitionRole-specific job
  3. Assess and selectRole-specific evidence
  4. OfferAccepted offer
  5. Hire handoffStable hire correlation key
  6. OnboardingCommon steps with role gates
  7. Restricted workLocal practical assessment and approval
