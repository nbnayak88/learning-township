## 3. Origins and process evolution

These closed windows are educational groupings, not universal invention dates or OmniDaily corporate biography. Adoption overlaps; older practices can coexist through the present. Reconstruct each as need → response → unresolved problem.

1970–1984 — Vacancy notices and personnel files fill roles; joining instructions can be lost.

1985–1994 — Structured interviews and checklists make work repeatable; local decisions remain hard to compare.

1995–2004 — Applicant tracking records selection stages; the candidate record is not yet the employee record.

2005–2014 — Career sites and onboarding workflows connect teams; completed forms do not prove job readiness.

2015–2025 — Cloud journeys link requisition, offer and onboarding; identity, accessibility and readiness evidence still need accountable review.

## 4. One persona-led story: An accepted offer is not a ready shift

Scene 1: Nandita Sen, Group CHRO (ODG-P06), and Elena Petrović, Plant Operations Director (ODG-P09), prepare a synthetic factory technician's first day. Nandita asks whether the accepted offer is enough to populate every downstream status.

Scene 2: Choose one universal checklist or common steps with role-specific gates. Reveal: a corporate digital-product manager can complete remote setup while the technician requires a local practical assessment.

Scene 4: Nandita keeps the hiring decision, employee-creation acknowledgement and work-readiness approval separate. Elena holds the restricted assignment and offers supervised learning through an authorized local route.

## 5. Responsibilities and human decision rights

Recruiting owns requisition/candidate/selection and approved offer records; the hiring panel and authorized HR approver decide selection. Onboarding coordinates forms, tasks and role-specific readiness evidence; it does not own every approval. Core HR owns accepted employee/employment facts. Site operations/quality owns restricted-work authorization; IAM owns access enforcement. SAP Recruiting, Onboarding and Employee Central are examples of the separate responsibilities.

These are proposed educational responsibilities. Establish the business rule, source of truth and accountable human before choosing technology. SAP examples illustrate a capability and are not verified deployments, licensing claims or product roadmaps. A worker must have an accessible human route to question incorrect data or a decision. HR status, learning completion, work authorization and quality/batch release are distinct decisions.

## 7. One build card: Hire-to-Readiness Decision Card

Synthetic experiment: Use SYN-REQ202, SYN-CAND202 and proposed SYN-E202 for one factory technician. Compare the same core steps with a corporate role as a counterexample; produce no second build card.

Fill nine fields on one page: (1) purpose/person affected; (2) current/proposed state and evidence; (3) identifiers and definitions; (4) business time/version; (5) application/data owners; (6) human approval/appeal; (7) chosen option versus alternative and trade-off; (8) exception, fallback and automation boundary; (9) three observed test responses and revision.

Design constraint: Offer acceptance must not imply unrestricted access, duplicate employment or practical competence. Link source identifiers and acknowledge accepted/rejected handoffs.

All SYN identifiers, episode details and injected evidence are new teaching data. Country rules, collective agreements, qualifications, worker/device populations and local approvals remain unknown unless explicitly sourced. Record each unknown with an owner and discovery question. Do not invent legal rules or calculate unsupported operational benefits.

## 8. Exactly three tabletop tests

### ODG-TOS-HR02-T01 — Role-specific gate

Reveal/input: The technician's forms are complete but practical assessment is missing.

Expected design response: Keep onboarding progress visible while holding restricted work; assign a human evidence owner.

### ODG-TOS-HR02-T02 — Duplicate hire

Reveal/input: The accepted-offer handoff is delivered twice.

Expected design response: Resolve a stable hire correlation; avoid a second employee and preserve the duplicate/reconciliation record.

### ODG-TOS-HR02-T03 — Day-one exception

Reveal/input: Identity provisioning fails before the first shift.

Expected design response: Expose owner and approved assisted route; do not call day-one readiness complete or share credentials.

For each test record input → expected response → actual paper response → evidence note → pass/revise. These are proposed acceptance criteria. The evidence slot starts unfilled: publication runs none of the tests and proves no operational result. If a protected human decision can be bypassed, revise the design even if every card field is filled.

## 9. Traceability and measures

ODG-TOS-HR02-REQ01 — Trace the approved offer to one employee-creation outcome and role-aware onboarding. Derived from the supporting HR source mandate and ODG-M18; assessed by T01.

ODG-TOS-HR02-REQ02 — Preserve separate readiness and access decisions through duplicate/failure handling. Derived from the source governance mandate and ODG-M19; assessed by T02–T03.

ODG-TOS-HR02-ADR01 — Prefer a shared journey with role-specific evidence gates over one identical global checklist; compare speed and maintenance cost. This proposed educational decision can be rejected with evidence; record the alternative and consequence.

ODG-TOS-HR02-EXP01 — The one synthetic build card extends ODG-M19's delivery-readiness discipline; T01–T03 do not execute the larger implementation case.

ODG-TOS-HR02-M01 — Mandatory card fields completed / 9 × 100: learning completeness only. ODG-TOS-HR02-M02 — Record whether duplicate prevention and unready-role holds remain visible in T02/T03. Record actual paper observations rather than assuming success.

ODG-TOS-HR02-EV01 — Unfilled evidence slot for test observations, revisions and reviewer questions. Existing case success measures are intended outcomes; no baseline or target attainment follows from publishing this edition.

## 10. AI-driven exploration: scenarios, not predictions

These are scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps. Each needs evidence, approved purpose, accountable ownership and proportionate controls.

2026–2030 — Assist: Assistance could explain missing joining evidence without deciding who is hired.

2031–2040 — Connect: Selection, onboarding, HR and site services could exchange accountable completion signals.

2041–2050 — Adapt: Journeys could adapt to role changes with explainable human review and accessible alternatives.

Competing future: Local onboarding coordinators using simple governed checklists may outperform highly personalized automation where device access is weak.

Disconfirmation question: Would added orchestration increase unresolved handoffs or disadvantage frontline joiners compared with a simpler coordinator-led pilot? Name evidence that would stop or change the hypothesis.
