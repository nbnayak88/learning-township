## 2. One persona-led story

Arjun Mehta, Group CHRO — OMF-P05, receives an urgent request to recruit a technician for recovered-feedstock operations at a cement plant.

Elena Petrova, Cement Plant Director — OMF-P07, needs the person before the next operating cycle. Nikhil Sen, Shift Supervisor and Workforce Representative — OMF-P16, needs to know what the newcomer can actually do on arrival.

A synthetic candidate, MAT-C201, has relevant maintenance experience but lacks the exact job-title keywords used in the screening template. An automated recommendation ranks the application poorly.

Arjun asks: “Are we assessing the work—or matching words?”

An authorized reviewer examines a practical assessment against the approved role criteria. The candidate progresses, receives an approved offer and accepts it.

Then the onboarding dashboard shows “initiated.” Someone proposes sending: “You are ready to start plant operations.” Nikhil challenges the message. The receiving team has not acknowledged the handoff, and task-specific readiness remains unconfirmed.

Your choice: send the message, delay every activity, or communicate the confirmed hiring status while clearly identifying outstanding readiness steps. Record your first answer.

New evidence: the candidate has no personal smartphone. Revise the journey so that completing onboarding does not depend on owning one.

These incidents and dialogue are new teaching fiction. Existing persona names, designations and IDs are preserved. No candidate assessment, hire or operational deployment has actually been executed.

## 3. Origins and evolution: 1970–present

The periods overlap; they describe teaching lenses, not a universal adoption sequence or OmniForge corporate history. Older and newer practices can coexist.

| Period | Process response | Problem that survives |
|---|---|---|
| 1970s–1990s | Referrals, personnel offices, paper applications and joining packs | Availability does not prove suitability. |
| 1980s–2000s | Personnel databases and structured selection records | Consistent forms can still contain weak criteria. |
| 1990s–2010s | Online advertisements and applicant tracking | More applicants do not guarantee better evidence. |
| 2000s–present | Connected recruiting, offers and onboarding services | An accepted offer can still reach an unprepared receiving team. |
| 2020s–present | Assisted scheduling, screening and communications | A recommendation or generated message can overstate what is known. |

Counterexample: a faster offer process can produce a worse experience if the candidate arrives without clear instructions, accessible support or an accountable receiving owner.

## 4. Application responsibilities and human decisions

This is a proposed learning architecture, not a verified OmniForge deployment. Delegated approval roles, real task criteria and receiving owners must be established before implementation.

| Responsibility | Authoritative facts and owner | Decision boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Demand and position planning | Business owner and Finance: approved need, role context and funding | Advertising does not establish approval. |
| HR02a — Recruiting | Talent Acquisition: requisition, application, assessment references and offer versions | Authorized people approve criteria, selection and offers. |
| HR02b — Onboarding | Onboarding process owner: joining tasks, responsible participants and acknowledged handoffs | Initiated onboarding does not mean ready for every task. |
| HR01 — Core HR | HR data owner: approved person and employment records | Candidate ID is not automatically an employee/person ID. |
| Learning and site authorization | Learning/assessors own competence evidence; designated site authorities own task permission | Course completion does not grant restricted-work authority. |
| IAM | Security: approved account and access provisioning | A working account or badge does not establish competence. |

SAP Recruiting and Onboarding can illustrate the handoff; actual statuses, mappings and initiation rules require configuration-specific validation. Initiating onboarding is distinct from completing subsequent activities. [R1–R2]

Automation may assist scheduling or draft a status message within approved permissions. Authorized reviewers retain hiring decisions; an assistant must not invent assessment evidence, authorize restricted work or declare unconfirmed completion. HR11 deepens AI governance; HR12 deepens transport/retry design.

## 5. One build card: Hiring-to-Readiness

Use one synthetic requisition MAT-R201, candidate MAT-C201, application MAT-A201 and approved offer version MAT-O201-v1. These are teaching identifiers. Requisition, application, offer, onboarding and employment retain their own states; do not flatten them into one universal status.

| Card field | What the learner records |
|---|---|
| Need | Required capability, business reason and demand approvers. |
| Assessment | Approved criteria, relevant evidence and accountable reviewer. |
| Identity | Candidate/application links and controlled handoff into HR01. |
| Offer | Approved version, acceptance and agreed start date. |
| Readiness | Required joining activities, receiving owner and outstanding conditions. |
| Communication | Confirmed status, next step, accessible channel and correction route. |
| Decision | Chosen approach, one alternative and the evidence that changed it. |
| Evidence | Three tabletop results, revisions and unresolved questions. |

Collect only necessary candidate information. Record who controls access and retention decisions; keep sensitive supporting records outside the general learning card. Explain how the candidate can question an error through a human-assisted route.

## 6. Three tabletop tests

| Test | Challenge | Expected behavior |
|---|---|---|
| OMF-HR02-T01 — Relevant skills, weak keyword match | The automated recommendation conflicts with a practical assessment. | Authorized review uses approved criteria, records its rationale and preserves a challenge route. |
| OMF-HR02-T02 — Accepted offer, incomplete readiness | Onboarding is initiated but the receiving owner has not acknowledged the handoff. | Communicate confirmed progress and outstanding steps; do not claim operational readiness. |
| OMF-HR02-T03 — Changed start date and duplicate handoff | An approved date correction is sent twice; the candidate lacks a smartphone. | Reconcile the current date, prevent duplicate creation and provide an accessible human-assisted route. |

Record input → expected → observed paper result → revision. These are planned tabletop tests, not executed system evidence. A proposed expectation is not an observed result.

Acceptance: complete the one card, record all three actual tabletop outcomes and revisions, and explain your decision in 60 seconds. An unauthorized hiring decision, false readiness message or restricted-work bypass requires redesign.

## 7. Exact 120-minute sprint

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–10 | Understand the promise and staffing dilemma. |
| 10–30 | Explore evolution and its persistent problems. |
| 30–45 | Make an initial decision; revise after new evidence. |
| 45–60 | Assign application ownership and human authority. |
| 60–90 | Complete the single build card. |
| 90–110 | Run three tabletop tests and revise. |
| 110–120 | Explain the decision in 60 seconds and reflect. |

Short resource lookups fit within these activities. Extensive vendor comparisons and deeper implementation learning remain outside this Origin Story.

## 8. Future exploration through 2050

Scenarios, not predictions: in 2035, verified skills evidence might help experienced workers enter circular-materials roles despite unfamiliar job titles. In 2050, portable credentials might reduce repeated assessments—or incompatible systems might increase exclusion.

What should OmniForge accept as alternative evidence? Which decisions should remain with accountable people? Better circularity does not automatically establish better working conditions; skills, inclusion and decent work require explicit attention. [R3]
