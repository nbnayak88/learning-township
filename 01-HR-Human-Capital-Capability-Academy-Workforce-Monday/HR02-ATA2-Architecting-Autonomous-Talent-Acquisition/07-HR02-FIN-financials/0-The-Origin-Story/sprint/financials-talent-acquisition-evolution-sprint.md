## 4. One persona-led story

Marcus Okoro (OCF-P08), Financial Crime Operations Director, needs a new investigator before an alert-review peak. Nadia Fernandes (OCF-P07), Group CHRO, sees an accepted offer for synthetic candidate FIN-C200. Elena Fischer (OCF-P05), Chief Compliance and Conduct Officer, finds an incomplete role check. The manager wants the account enabled today.

Choose before revealing: Treat offer acceptance as ready, delay all onboarding, or progress permitted tasks while holding restricted duties? Record your first reason on the card.

Evidence reveal: The synthetic offer was approved against funded position FIN-POS200; identity linkage is still ambiguous and the required role check is not closed. Nadia starts only non-sensitive welcome tasks. Elena requests the appropriate reviewer, while Marcus plans supervised non-restricted learning.

Revise your decision: Separate recruiting selection, employment creation, onboarding task progress and role authorization. Hiring speed cannot turn an unresolved check into clearance. Preserve an accessible human route to challenge errors. Financial service continuity matters, but an urgent cut-off does not create authority. Customer KYC and employee readiness are different processes; do not copy customer identity checks into HR without purpose and policy review.

## 5. Application responsibilities and human authority

Frame the business need first; applications implement the chosen operating model. The following is a proposed teaching architecture. Confirm named delegates, country policy, legal basis and actual product scope before implementation.

| Application responsibility | Accountable data owner | Human decision right |
|---|---|---|
| Recruiting; SAP Recruiting example | Talent Acquisition owns requisition, candidate, assessment and offer facts | Hiring manager and delegated offer approvers select people; AI does not reject candidates. |
| Onboarding and core HR | HR onboarding owner owns tasks; HR steward confirms worker identity | Authorized HR staff resolve candidate-to-worker identity and employment creation. |
| Readiness / Security | Compliance owns required check evidence; Security owns access execution | Designated role authority releases restricted duties; manager schedules permitted learning. |

Core HR owns workforce facts; domain owners own performance, learning, rewards, time or payroll facts as applicable. IAM executes access policy; integration transports and reconciles states; analytics reads authorized data and does not become its owner. AI may summarize permitted evidence or draft a reminder, but cannot approve consequential employment, regulated duties, payments or access. An interface acknowledgment is not proof of the business outcome. SAP examples illustrate responsibilities, not guaranteed features or universal localization.

## 7. One Decision Build Card

Synthetic input: FIN-C200, approved position FIN-POS200 and accepted offer OFF200; two possible worker matches; role check pending; welcome task allowed; sensitive access held. No real candidate or screening record is used.

Keep all work in this one card. Capture: business problem; initial and revised choice with reason; entity/identity/date semantics; authoritative source and accountable owner; human approval boundary; one justified local variant with purpose/minimum data/viewers; known versus unknown downstream state; exception owner and approved fallback; all three test observations and evidence references. Missing evidence stays blank or unknown, never silently becomes approval.

Compare one alternative: a manual controlled handoff may be preferable to automation when exception ownership or recovery is absent. State the evidence needed to change your mind. Avoid collecting real screening, health, payroll, customer or conduct records in this learning card.

## 8. Exactly three tabletop tests

| Test | Input / reveal | Expected proposed behavior | Evidence to record |
|---|---|---|---|
| OCF-HR02-T01 — Approved demand and fair assessment | Remove the position approval and add an unexplained AI rejection. | Hold the requisition exception for an accountable person; require role-relevant human assessment. | Position exception, criteria, human review and appeal path. |
| OCF-HR02-T02 — Ambiguous identity at handoff | Accepted offer matches two existing worker records. | Do not duplicate or merge people automatically; restrict disclosure and resolve through HR. | Candidate-to-worker mapping, unresolved match and steward decision. |
| OCF-HR02-T03 — Readiness without clearance | Welcome tasks are complete but the role check remains pending. | Show partial readiness; hold sensitive duties/access; escalate to the designated reviewer. | Task ownership, approval evidence, worker notice and permitted alternative. |

For each test, record input → expected → observed → revision/evidence → Pass, Revise or Blocked. All observed results are initially unfilled. Publication does not execute any tabletop, system test, approval or operational transaction. Acceptance requires a defensible card and all three observed tabletop results with revisions; bypassing a protected human decision requires redesign.

## 10. Separate AI-driven exploration: 2026–2050

Scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps.

| Scenario window | Question to explore |
|---|---|
| 2026–2030 — Assist | Could approved-source assistance explain evidence gaps and draft next steps without approving the decision? |
| 2031–2040 — Connect | Could separately authorized services coordinate this handoff while keeping unknown states and local rights visible? |
| 2041–2050 — Adapt | Could reviewed feedback propose reversible process changes while people retain consequential authority? |

Competing future: fragmented systems, uneven access, policy restrictions and manual review persist; a simpler controlled workflow outperforms orchestration. Falsifiable hypothesis question: Would fewer handoffs improve day-one readiness if role-check completion remains the bottleneck? Define an observation that would disprove your claim—for example fewer steps but more unresolved exceptions or worse worker outcomes. Compare against a manual baseline before claiming value.
