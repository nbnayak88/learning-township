The hiring decision

Marcus Okoro (OSP-P07), Recruiting Transformation Lead, and Rajesh Malhotra, VP and HR Business Partner, need a smart-building specialist for Maya Patel's (OSP-P03) property team. The candidate accepts an offer and the hiring dashboard turns green. Facilities has no approved site-access request and the manager has not assigned a first-day task.

Choose first: Count the hire as productive, reopen recruiting, or keep offer acceptance and readiness as separate milestones?

Evidence reveal: The start-date change has not reached onboarding and a property-specific induction remains incomplete. Marcus preserves the hiring decision and routes the dated change to onboarding. Maya agrees an accessible first-day welcome and safe supervised work. Neither an automated ranking nor an accepted offer authorises unrestricted site work.

Counterexample: A controlled manual workflow with fewer integrations can be better if it is more accessible and reliable; more automation is not automatically better.

Hire-to-readiness architecture

SmartRecruiters carries recruiting decisions; Employee Central and Onboarding carry employment facts; the receiving owner carries readiness and access. A fact answers one question only.

Who owns each decision

Application responsibilityAccountable ownerData and human boundary
SelectionRecruiting transformation lead and panelHuman review against job-relevant criteria
Start-date changeOnboarding ownerApproved change is versioned and reconciled; offer history retained
Employment factsHR steward (Employee Central)Core HR owns the employment record
Site access and inductionProperty operations director and facilitiesProperty-specific induction and safe fallback

A source-system record, a receiving-system acknowledgment and a human permission answer different questions. Provide a private, accessible correction or appeal route for workers and candidates.

Limits and next steps

  • Nothing here is tenant-tested; fit labels are proposals to confirm in fit-to-standard workshops.
  • Benefit statements and acceptance criteria are standard per stage and filled with sector facts; they need subject-matter review.
  • Recruiting roles, AI-driven roles and some case personas are synthetic.
  • Job classification, skills and competencies are a starting taxonomy; confirm against national occupation standards and the licensed SAP skills ontology.
