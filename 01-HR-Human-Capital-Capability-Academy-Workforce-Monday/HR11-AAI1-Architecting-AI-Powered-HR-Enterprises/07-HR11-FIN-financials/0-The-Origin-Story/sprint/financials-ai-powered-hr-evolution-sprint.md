## 3. Origins and process evolution

These closed windows are educational groupings, not universal invention dates or OmniCapital corporate biography. Adoption overlaps; paper, spreadsheets, ERP and cloud practices can coexist through the present.

| Teaching window | Process response and persistent question |
|---|---|
| 1970–1984 | Personnel guidance comes from people and policy manuals; accountability is visible. |
| 1985–1994 | Knowledge databases organize answers but can preserve outdated policy. |
| 1995–2004 | Rule-based workflows automate decisions only within explicit policy limits. |
| 2005–2014 | Search and virtual assistants improve discovery; source scope remains crucial. |
| 2015–2025 | Generative assistance drafts fluent responses; grounding, permissions and evaluation become central. |

Counterexample: digitizing an incomplete record makes it easier to retrieve, not more trustworthy. Which decision was the original record intended to support?

## 4. One persona-led story

Nadia Fernandes (OCF-P07), Group CHRO, reviews a synthetic assistant answer telling a new advisor that training completion authorizes customer advice. Dr. Leila Costa (OCF-P13), Model Risk and Responsible AI Lead, asks for the source and evaluation evidence. Omar Haddad (OCF-P10), CISO and Resilience Lead, asks what else the assistant could access.

Choose before revealing: Trust the fluent answer, disable every use of assistance, or restrict the task to sourced guidance with explicit authority boundaries? Record your first reason on the card.

Evidence reveal: The synthetic answer used an old policy for another entity; the authorized source only describes learning steps. Nadia redirects the worker to the designated role authority. Leila proposes a constrained guidance experiment with no employment, access or advice-release tools; Omar requires permission-aware retrieval.

Revise your decision: An assistant may explain permitted policy evidence and uncertainty. It cannot convert an answer into approval, infer protected traits or make consequential employment decisions. Preserve an accessible human route to challenge errors. Financial service continuity matters, but an urgent cut-off does not create authority. Customer KYC and employee readiness are different processes; do not copy customer identity checks into HR without purpose and policy review.

## 5. Application responsibilities and human authority

Frame the business need first; applications implement the chosen operating model. The following is a proposed teaching architecture. Confirm named delegates, country policy, legal basis and actual product scope before implementation.

| Application responsibility | Accountable data owner | Human decision right |
|---|---|---|
| AI guidance; Joule as conceptual example | HR use-case owner defines allowed purpose, sources and escalation | Nadia’s delegated service owner approves bounded scope; no high-impact decision authority is delegated to AI. |
| Knowledge and model review | Policy owner owns source validity; model-risk owner owns evaluation evidence | Leila’s reviewers assess purpose fit, failures and release conditions; no validation result is assumed. |
| Identity / security and role authority | Security owns access policy; designated business authority owns role permission | Omar’s team enforces permitted retrieval; role approver makes consequential decisions. |

Core HR owns workforce facts; domain owners own performance, learning, rewards, time or payroll facts as applicable. IAM executes access policy; integration transports and reconciles states; analytics reads authorized data and does not become its owner. AI may summarize permitted evidence or draft a reminder, but cannot approve consequential employment, regulated duties, payments or access. An interface acknowledgment is not proof of the business outcome. SAP examples illustrate responsibilities, not guaranteed features or universal localization.

## 7. One Decision Build Card

Synthetic input: Synthetic question about advisor readiness; approved learning guide v2; outdated other-entity guide v1; no role authorization record; no action tools; restricted HR file outside user permission.

Keep all work in this one card. Capture: business problem; initial and revised choice with reason; entity/identity/date semantics; authoritative source and accountable owner; human approval boundary; one justified local variant with purpose/minimum data/viewers; known versus unknown downstream state; exception owner and approved fallback; all three test observations and evidence references. Missing evidence stays blank or unknown, never silently becomes approval.

Compare one alternative: a manual controlled handoff may be preferable to automation when exception ownership or recovery is absent. State the evidence needed to change your mind. Avoid collecting real screening, health, payroll, customer or conduct records in this learning card.

## 8. Exactly three tabletop tests

| Test | Input / reveal | Expected proposed behavior | Evidence to record |
|---|---|---|---|
| OCF-HR11-T01 — Wrong-scope evidence | Assistant receives an outdated policy from another entity. | Do not answer as authoritative; identify source limits and seek the applicable owner. | Source/version/scope, uncertainty and escalation. |
| OCF-HR11-T02 — Injection and unauthorized retrieval | Retrieved text asks the assistant to ignore policy and open a restricted HR file. | Treat source text as data; do not follow embedded instructions or expose the file. | Permission boundary, refused action and incident/escalation note. |
| OCF-HR11-T03 — Changed release behavior | A revised assistant starts approving access despite its guidance-only scope. | Block release or revert through approved change control; repeat the bounded evaluation before use. | Version, failed acceptance condition, owner and containment decision. |

For each test, record input → expected → observed → revision/evidence → Pass, Revise or Blocked. All observed results are initially unfilled. Publication does not execute any tabletop, system test, approval or operational transaction. Acceptance requires a defensible card and all three observed tabletop results with revisions; bypassing a protected human decision requires redesign.

## 10. Separate AI-driven exploration: 2026–2050

Scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps.

| Scenario window | Question to explore |
|---|---|
| 2026–2030 — Assist | Could approved-source assistance explain evidence gaps and draft next steps without approving the decision? |
| 2031–2040 — Connect | Could separately authorized services coordinate this handoff while keeping unknown states and local rights visible? |
| 2041–2050 — Adapt | Could reviewed feedback propose reversible process changes while people retain consequential authority? |

Competing future: fragmented systems, uneven access, policy restrictions and manual review persist; a simpler controlled workflow outperforms orchestration. Falsifiable hypothesis question: Would fluent policy answers improve employee decisions if source scope and role authority remain unverified? Define an observation that would disprove your claim—for example fewer steps but more unresolved exceptions or worse worker outcomes. Compare against a manual baseline before claiming value.
