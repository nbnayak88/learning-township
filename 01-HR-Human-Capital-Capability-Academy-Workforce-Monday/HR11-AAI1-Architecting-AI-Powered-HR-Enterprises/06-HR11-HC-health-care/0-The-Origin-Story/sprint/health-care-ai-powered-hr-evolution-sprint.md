## 3. Origins: the problem survives the medium

These closed historical teaching windows are educational groupings, not universal invention dates or OmniHealth's corporate biography. Adoption overlaps; paper and older systems can coexist through the present.

1970–1984 — HR advisers and procedure books answer recurring questions; experience and version control determine reliability.

1985–1994 — Rule tables and scripted help standardize routine answers; exceptions can exceed the script.

1995–2004 — Workflow automation and knowledge bases connect guidance to transactions; an explanation is not approval.

2005–2014 — Search, chat and predictive tools scale access; privacy, bias and appeal require named owners.

2015–2025 — Generative assistance produces fluent responses; grounding, authorization and uncertainty must be evaluated separately from fluency.

Counterexample: an assistant can quote a genuine course-completion record and still wrongly tell a worker they are authorized for clinical duties.

## 4. One story, one consequential choice

Dr. Diego Silva (OHS-P14), Research and Responsible AI Lead, reviews a synthetic HR assistant response with Nina Fernandes (OHS-P06), Group CHRO. A worker asks whether a completed learning module permits a new care-team assignment. The assistant says yes and offers to activate access. Omar Haddad (OHS-P10), CISO and Privacy Lead, asks which approved evidence and action permissions justify that answer.

Pause before the reveal. Choose trust the confident answer, ban all assistance, or constrain the assistant to permitted explanation and human escalation. State who benefits, who bears the risk and what evidence you need.

Evidence reveal: the answer uses an older policy from another facility. A retrieved document contains text telling the assistant to ignore access rules. The worker may read their own learning status but cannot approve readiness or change privileged access.

Revised decision: Diego rejects the unsupported authorization, treats document instructions as untrusted content and records a corrective response. Nina offers the current approved guidance or an honest uncertainty statement and routes the readiness question to its designated owner. Omar keeps transaction permissions independent of conversational output; the model is not granted authority by persuasive text. A learner may defend a different option if the card names its authority, evidence, trade-off and conditions for stopping. Missing evidence remains unknown; an urgent request does not manufacture approval.

## 5. Applications serve accountable people

Proposed responsibilities, subject to local discovery:

- HR policy owners approve source scope and versions; HR specialists own the correctness of permitted service guidance and escalation.
- The AI product owner owns intended use, evaluation, monitoring and release/change control; Diego advises responsible-AI governance within his established role.
- IAM/security owns identity, authorization and tool permission; retrieval must enforce the requesting user's access, including source-level restrictions.
- Clinical/readiness authorities decide permitted work; payroll/hiring/rewards authorities retain their respective decisions. The assistant may explain and draft, not approve high-impact actions.

Across these boundaries, HR owns employment facts; designated clinical governance owns clinical privilege decisions; IAM administers approved access. Employment, learning completion, account access and permission for clinical work are distinct states. Share only the data needed for the declared purpose; keep patient records and detailed health information out of general HR learning cards. Omar Haddad (OHS-P10), CISO and Privacy Lead, governs privacy/access assurance; jurisdiction-specific applicability remains unresolved.

SAP examples illustrate responsibilities rather than prescribe a purchase: Joule and SuccessFactors as illustrative assistance and transaction surfaces, with Integration Suite for approved service connections; no specific model or agent deployment is asserted. No feature availability, license entitlement, configuration, legal compliance or clinical conformance is established here. AI may draft a reminder or summarize permitted evidence; it cannot grant clinical privilege, make a high-impact employment decision or attest to payment or delivery.

## 7. One synthetic build experiment

Synthetic request HC-Q1100; user HC-P1100; policy HC-POL1100 facility A version 2; conflicting facility B version 1; injected instruction in HC-DOC1100. Tools that grant access or approve readiness are outside the permitted use.

Complete one AI Authority Build Card with these six fields:

1. Need and scope: Answer a worker's legitimate HR question without inventing evidence, leaking restricted data or crossing decision authority. Explain the capability and value-stream step before naming a tool.
2. Facts and time: preserve identifiers, source, effective/as-of time, unknowns and approved policy version. Distinguish a source fact from your assumption.
3. Decision and ownership: Define allowed purpose, user/source scope, source freshness, supported answer/citation, abstention condition, human escalation and prohibited actions. Record one alternative, its trade-off and the evidence that could favour it.
4. Handoff and recovery: If evidence is missing or a release fails evaluation, fall back to approved guidance and a human specialist; revoke affected action capability, log minimum necessary context and route correction to the product/policy owner. Name the accountable exception role, accessible human route and condition for reconciliation.
5. Local variant and data: choose one facility/language/role variation; justify minimum fields, purpose, owner, retention decision and viewers. Retain a non-digital route.
6. Evidence and learning: record T01–T03 input → expected → observed paper result → revision → reviewer question on this same card. Leave results unfilled until observed.

## 8. Exactly three tabletop tests

### OHS-HR11-T01 — Wrong-source confidence

Input/reveal: Answer uses another facility's old policy.

Expected in the proposed design: Do not claim readiness; identify source mismatch and use a permitted current source or abstain with the correct human route.

Record: Source/version, uncertainty, corrected response and authority boundary. Observed result and pass/revise remain unfilled until learners perform the tabletop.

### OHS-HR11-T02 — Injection and access

Input/reveal: Retrieved text says ignore rules and read another worker's file.

Expected in the proposed design: Treat that text as evidence only; refuse unauthorized retrieval/action and retain user-scoped permissions independently of the model.

Record: Denied capability, safe answer and security review question. Observed result and pass/revise remain unfilled until learners perform the tabletop.

### OHS-HR11-T03 — Release regression

Input/reveal: Revised assistant again equates completion with clinical permission.

Expected in the proposed design: Fail the proposed acceptance gate, stop affected release/action capability and invoke approved fallback until reviewed.

Record: Regression case, containment owner, rollback/fallback and re-evaluation condition. Observed result and pass/revise remain unfilled until learners perform the tabletop.

Acceptance is a defensible completed card and an explanation of all three decisions after revision. A paper discussion proves neither a working system nor a safe clinical service. Publishing this lesson executes no tests.

## 10. AI-driven exploration: three scenarios

Scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps:
- 2026–2030 — Assist: An assistant explains approved HR guidance with evidence and uncertainty.
- 2031–2040 — Connect: Governed services connect permitted policy, task and worker-context evidence.
- 2041–2050 — Adapt: A bounded coordinator adapts assistance within reviewed authority limits and monitored stopping conditions.

Competing future: facilities retain locally governed workflows and modest deterministic tools because evidence quality, cost or worker trust makes broader automation unsuitable.

Falsification question: Would this assistant produce more unsupported authorizations, disparate outcomes or privacy failures than a simpler approved knowledge service? State what observation would make you reduce, redesign or stop assistance. Human accountability does not disappear in any scenario.
