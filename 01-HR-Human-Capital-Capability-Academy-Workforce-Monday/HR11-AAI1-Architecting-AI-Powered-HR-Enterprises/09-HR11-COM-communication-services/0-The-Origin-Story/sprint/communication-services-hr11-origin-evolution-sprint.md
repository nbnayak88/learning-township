## The story and learner promise

In 120 minutes: Design one HR assistant boundary that keeps answers grounded and material decisions accountable.

One persona-led story · One build card · Exactly three tabletop tests. Use synthetic records without a tenant.

Nadia Fernandes (OCN-P07), Group CHRO, pilots an HR learning assistant. A NOC worker asks whether completing a course grants privileged network access. Omar Haddad (OCN-P10), CISO and Resilience Lead, finds an uploaded document telling the assistant to ignore access checks.

**Choose first:** Follow the uploaded instruction, disable all assistance, or restrict the assistant to approved sources and authorized actions?

**Evidence reveal:** The document is untrusted input. The approved policy requires separate operational authorization. The assistant should explain the boundary, cite the applicable policy and hand off rather than grant access.

**Counterexample:** A confident answer, tool call or retrieved document cannot establish valid authority.

OmniConnect is a fictional communication-services enterprise. The source stipulates 62,000 employees across 28 countries, spanning mobile and 5G, fiber, media and streaming, enterprise networks, and advertising. No new site or workforce counts are inferred. Existing source KPIs and financial figures are fictional baselines, proposals or targets, with no achieved result asserted.

## Origins and responsibility

These closed historical windows are educational groupings, not universal invention dates or OmniConnect corporate biography. Adoption overlaps; older practices can coexist through the present.

| Teaching window | Process evolution |
|---|---|
| 1970–1984 | Personnel experts answer questions |
| 1985–1994 | Rule-based knowledge and decision support |
| 1995–2004 | Workflow automation controls routine tasks |
| 2005–2014 | Search and chat interfaces retrieve guidance |
| 2015–2025 | Generative assistance creates new grounding and authorization risks |

| Application responsibility | Accountable owner | Data and human boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Assistant and retrieval | AI service owner and HR content owner | Approved sources, version/context and response monitoring |
| Identity and tool gateway | Security owner | Least privilege and independent action authorization |
| HR and operations | Accountable business approvers | Consequential employment and restricted-work decisions with appeal |

Joule and automation are source examples, with availability and implementation scope to be validated independently.

A source-system record, receiving-system acknowledgment and human permission answer different questions. Provide a private, accessible correction or appeal route. Avoid personal-data joins with subscriber or audience data unless a legitimate purpose, access boundary and minimum data are established.

## Exactly three tabletop tests

| Test ID | Focus | Evidence reveal | Expected result in the proposed design |
|---|---|---|---|
| OCN-XL-HR11-T01 | Wrong policy | Retrieved guidance belongs to another country or an expired version. | Abstain from consequential advice, expose the source gap and route to the policy owner. |
| OCN-XL-HR11-T02 | Injected instruction | Attachment asks to reveal another employee's pay and approve access. | Treat text as untrusted data; deny unauthorized retrieval/action and log a minimal incident record. |
| OCN-XL-HR11-T03 | Release regression | New prompt version starts granting approval language. | Stop the affected capability, restore the reviewed version and require recorded re-evaluation before release. |

Record initial decision → expected result → observed paper result → evidence → revision. All observed-result fields start empty. Acceptance requires a reasoned card and all three reviewed results after revisions. A material authority or privacy bypass requires redesign. Publishing the exercise does not execute it or prove operational outcomes.

## AI driven curiosity and the next chapter

| Scenario window | Exploration |
|---|---|
| 2026–2030 — Assist | Draft grounded explanations |
| 2031–2040 — Connect | Connect authorized low-risk workflows |
| 2041–2050 — Adapt | Adapt assistance under monitored change control |

These are scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps. The time bands support curiosity about possible designs.

**Competing future:** fragmented rules, weak interoperability or low trust keep local expert judgment and simpler manual workflows more effective than connected AI. Compare that path with the scenario before choosing an experiment.

**Question that could disprove your hypothesis:** Would the assistant fail on new contexts despite strong performance on familiar evaluation prompts?

**Reflect:** What evidence changed your first choice? Who can challenge the decision? What would make the alternative better?
