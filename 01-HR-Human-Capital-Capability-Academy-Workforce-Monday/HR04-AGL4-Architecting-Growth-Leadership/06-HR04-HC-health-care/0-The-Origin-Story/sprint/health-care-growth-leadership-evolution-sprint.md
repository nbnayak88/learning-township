## 3. Origins: the problem survives the medium

These closed historical teaching windows are educational groupings, not universal invention dates or OmniHealth's corporate biography. Adoption overlaps; paper and older systems can coexist through the present.

1970–1984 — Apprenticeship and leader nomination transmit experience; access to opportunity depends heavily on sponsors.

1985–1994 — Replacement charts and confidential lists make dependencies visible; a name can conceal readiness gaps.

1995–2004 — Competency frameworks and succession workflows link roles to development; potential and current capability still differ.

2005–2014 — Talent pools, mentoring and internal mobility broaden pathways; protected time and transparent criteria matter.

2015–2025 — Skills analytics and assisted matching suggest candidates; a recommendation cannot appoint a clinical leader.

Counterexample: a role with two nominated successors can still have no authorized cover during an absence.

## 4. One story, one consequential choice

Nina Fernandes (OHS-P06), Group CHRO, reviews succession for a care-coordination leadership role. Dr. Arjun Mehta (OHS-P04), Chief Clinical Operations Officer, needs continuity during a hypothetical absence. The talent chart calls a candidate 'ready now', yet the candidate has never been assessed against the receiving unit's coordination responsibilities. Nina wants growth opportunities without pretending a talent label conveys operational authority.

Pause before the reveal. Choose appoint from the label, leave the role uncovered, or separate assessed development readiness from explicitly delegated interim cover. State who benefits, who bears the risk and what evidence you need.

Evidence reveal: the readiness label was entered before the role changed. The candidate completed a leadership course but has not accepted the proposed move; a trained deputy may provide bounded cover if an authorized local decision confirms it.

Revised decision: Nina reopens the readiness assessment and asks about aspirations and opportunity barriers. Arjun seeks the designated authority's documented interim delegation, scope and expiry; he does not treat a development nomination as professional authorization. A learner may defend a different option if the card names its authority, evidence, trade-off and conditions for stopping. Missing evidence remains unknown; an urgent request does not manufacture approval.

## 5. Applications serve accountable people

Proposed responsibilities, subject to local discovery:

- Succession services store critical-role criteria, nominated pools and readiness evidence; the accountable talent council reviews nominations with defined confidentiality.
- Learning and designated assessors own assessment evidence; employees own their stated aspirations and may challenge stale information.
- Core HR owns position and reporting structures; the delegated appointment authority owns formal role changes.
- Operational/clinical governance owns interim delegation, professional scope and continuity arrangements. A succession chart records evidence rather than granting powers.

Across these boundaries, HR owns employment facts; designated clinical governance owns clinical privilege decisions; IAM administers approved access. Employment, learning completion, account access and permission for clinical work are distinct states. Share only the data needed for the declared purpose; keep patient records and detailed health information out of general HR learning cards. Omar Haddad (OHS-P10), CISO and Privacy Lead, governs privacy/access assurance; jurisdiction-specific applicability remains unresolved.

SAP examples illustrate responsibilities rather than prescribe a purchase: Succession & Development connected to Employee Central, Performance & Goals and Learning; analytics may expose aggregate dependency risk. No feature availability, license entitlement, configuration, legal compliance or clinical conformance is established here. AI may draft a reminder or summarize permitted evidence; it cannot grant clinical privilege, make a high-impact employment decision or attest to payment or delivery.

## 7. One synthetic build experiment

Synthetic role HC-ROLE400, nominee HC-P400 and deputy HC-P401; readiness assessed under role version 1, current role version 2. Hypothetical cover 1–14 November 2026; no appointment made.

Complete one Leadership Readiness Build Card with these six fields:

1. Need and scope: Keep care coordination accountable while creating fair, consent-aware development opportunities. Explain the capability and value-stream step before naming a tool.
2. Facts and time: preserve identifiers, source, effective/as-of time, unknowns and approved policy version. Distinguish a source fact from your assumption.
3. Decision and ownership: Define critical-role criteria, dated evidence, aspiration, readiness horizon and separate temporary-cover authority. Record one alternative, its trade-off and the evidence that could favour it.
4. Handoff and recovery: If readiness is stale or no authorized cover exists, escalate the continuity gap to the operational owner; document an approved contingency and reassessment rather than silently appointing the nominee. Name the accountable exception role, accessible human route and condition for reconciliation.
5. Local variant and data: choose one facility/language/role variation; justify minimum fields, purpose, owner, retention decision and viewers. Retain a non-digital route.
6. Evidence and learning: record T01–T03 input → expected → observed paper result → revision → reviewer question on this same card. Leave results unfilled until observed.

## 8. Exactly three tabletop tests

### OHS-HR04-T01 — Changed role

Input/reveal: Ready-now label uses superseded role criteria.

Expected in the proposed design: Reassess against the current role; preserve old evidence as history; no automatic appointment.

Record: Role versions, evidence gap and assessor assignment. Observed result and pass/revise remain unfilled until learners perform the tabletop.

### OHS-HR04-T02 — Nomination without consent

Input/reveal: Candidate has not accepted mobility and works a different shift.

Expected in the proposed design: Discuss aspirations and feasible development time; do not equate nomination with agreement or penalize refusal.

Record: Worker discussion, opportunity access and revised plan. Observed result and pass/revise remain unfilled until learners perform the tabletop.

### OHS-HR04-T03 — Interim authority expiry

Input/reveal: Deputy delegation ends while leader remains absent.

Expected in the proposed design: No silent extension; accountable authority reviews cover before expiry and documents a bounded continuation or alternative.

Record: Scope, expiry, escalation and continuity decision. Observed result and pass/revise remain unfilled until learners perform the tabletop.

Acceptance is a defensible completed card and an explanation of all three decisions after revision. A paper discussion proves neither a working system nor a safe clinical service. Publishing this lesson executes no tests.

## 10. AI-driven exploration: three scenarios

Scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps:
- 2026–2030 — Assist: An assistant flags stale role-to-readiness evidence.
- 2031–2040 — Connect: Governed services connect aspirations, assessed skills and development opportunities.
- 2041–2050 — Adapt: A planner suggests succession-development options under human governance when service needs change.

Competing future: facilities retain locally governed workflows and modest deterministic tools because evidence quality, cost or worker trust makes broader automation unsuitable.

Falsification question: Would assisted nomination repeatedly exclude night-shift or part-time workers despite equivalent evidence? State what observation would make you reduce, redesign or stop assistance. Human accountability does not disappear in any scenario.
