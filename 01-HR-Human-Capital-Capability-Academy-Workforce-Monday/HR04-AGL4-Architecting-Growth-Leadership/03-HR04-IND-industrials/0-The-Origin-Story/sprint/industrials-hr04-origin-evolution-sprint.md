## 2. Read the enterprise and sources accurately

OmniWorks is fictional: 74,000 employees, 36 countries, 61 manufacturing/engineering/assembly facilities and 320 service depots. The 61 is a mixed-facility count, not a confirmed plant-only count. Engineering, maintenance, production and field service shape the workforce problem. Case targets and victory criteria are aspirations, not achieved results. This edition adds a bounded synthetic episode; it does not assert a deployment.

Dr. Samuel Reed, Retiring Principal Aerospace Engineer; Nadia Khan, Senior Systems Engineer; Peter Lang, Business Unit President; and Chinwe Eze, Talent Review Governance Lead retain their identities. Do not treat the percentages as audited workforce evidence.

## 3. Origins: five closed historical teaching windows

These windows group teaching ideas, not universal invention dates or OmniWorks corporate biography. Adoption overlaps; older practices can coexist through the present. Each generation changes the medium without automatically resolving ownership, evidence or authority.

1970–1984 — Apprenticeship and informal sponsorship transfer craft knowledge; access to sponsors can shape opportunity more than competence.

1985–1994 — Management succession charts identify replacements; technical experts may disappear from leadership-focused plans.

1995–2004 — Competency frameworks and development plans structure growth; a potential label still cannot prove readiness.

2005–2014 — Talent reviews and dual technical/management career paths widen options; calibration can retain hidden assumptions.

2015–2025 — Skills evidence and internal opportunities connect development to work; suggestions and nomination remain distinct from appointment.

Recall prompt: reconstruct the five responses as problem → process/application response → unfinished problem. Which old failure could survive in the newest system?

## 4. One persona-led dilemma

### Scene 1

Elena meets Samuel before his planned retirement. A replacement box on an organization chart cannot preserve why an aerospace design was accepted or how an anomaly was handled.

### Scene 2

Peter asks for the next manager on a talent list. Elena challenges the frame: critical expertise may need a technical pathway, shared knowledge and supervised practice rather than a management promotion.

### Scene 3

Nadia is nominated, but Chinwe reveals that the 'ready now' label lacks an observed design-review assessment. Elena holds the label, asks who could assess it, and opens a fair development opportunity instead of treating uncertainty as lack of potential.

### Scene 4

Samuel proposes a supervised review of a synthetic design exception. Elena records ownership, confidentiality and knowledge limits; a document recording Samuel's explanation is useful but does not certify Nadia or authorize her appointment.

Decision pause: Appoint the nominated successor now, develop a technical successor with staged evidence, or share continuity across an expert network. What would make the alternative safer?

## 5. Responsibilities, ownership and human authority

Succession/development service (talent owner): critical-role definitions, nominations, readiness evidence references and development plans; SAP Succession & Development is one example.

Engineering/PLM and knowledge repositories (engineering owner): approved design artifacts, versions and access restrictions; talent services must not copy all engineering IP.

Learning/assessment service (learning and qualified assessor owners): supervised practice and assessment evidence.

Authorized talent review and appointing authorities decide readiness/appointment; Chinwe governs fairness and review. Workers can discuss aspirations and challenge inaccurate labels.

Design boundary: Potential, training, observed competence and appointment are separate claims; a recommended successor is not automatically authorized for regulated engineering work. Proposed responsibilities are learning architecture, not proof that the fictional enterprise already operates this design. Select technology only after the business rule and accountable owner are clear. SAP examples illustrate capabilities, not mandatory product choices.

## 7. One build card: Technical Continuity Build Card

Synthetic experiment: SYN-ROLE401 is a critical technical role, SYN-N401 a nomination and SYN-K401 a sanitized knowledge-transfer artifact. One required review skill lacks observed evidence. Create one continuity plan with a bounded supervised exercise; no actual aircraft work is performed.

Fill these nine fields on one page: purpose and affected person; current/proposed state and source evidence; identity/object definitions; business date/time and version; application/data owners; human approval and appeal route; chosen option versus alternative with trade-off; exception/fallback and automation boundary; three test observations and revision.

Minimum design reasoning: Define the continuity need, skill/task scope, assessor, access limits and evidence currency. Preserve an alternative such as shared expertise or external support where a single successor remains unready. Do not copy confidential design material into HR.

Do not invent country rules, safety thresholds, collective-agreement terms, live data or missing approvals. Record unknowns with an owner and discovery question. Support an accessible human route for the worker. Consider fatigue, learning opportunity, avoidable travel/rework or resource use where the dilemma provides evidence; do not infer environmental savings.

## 8. Exactly three tabletop tests

### OW-TOS-HR04-T01 — Unproven readiness

Reveal/input: The nomination says 'ready now' but assessment is absent.

Expected design response: Reclassify readiness as unverified with a development/evidence action; retain the nomination without claiming validated competence.

### OW-TOS-HR04-T02 — Knowledge counterexample

Reveal/input: A signed knowledge note is available, but the successor cannot explain a novel exception.

Expected design response: Do not treat the note as competence; plan supervised practice and an authorized assessment with the specific gap visible.

### OW-TOS-HR04-T03 — Opportunity and IP boundary

Reveal/input: An overlooked engineer requests review and the plan proposes copying restricted PLM data into a talent profile.

Expected design response: Provide a fair review route, store minimal references and keep engineering access under its owner; appointment remains a human decision.

For each test, record input → expected response → actual observed paper response → evidence note → pass/revise. Expected responses are proposed acceptance criteria, not executed results. Publication executes none of these tabletop tests. A bypass of the protected decision requires redesign, even if the card is complete.

## 10. AI-driven exploration: scenarios, not predictions

These are scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps. All require approved purpose, evidence, worker participation, accountable ownership and proportionate controls.

2026–2030 — Assist: Assistance could draft knowledge-transfer prompts and find missing evidence references without rating potential.

2031–2040 — Connect: Connected technical pathways could link sanitized project experience, learning and opportunities through approved permissions.

2041–2050 — Adapt: Adaptive expert networks could rebalance continuity as engineering changes; appointments and professional authorization remain accountable.

Competing future: A resilient team of overlapping specialists may protect continuity better than one AI-ranked successor.

Disconfirmation question: Would a single-successor plan remain fragile when the nominated person is unavailable or cannot handle a new exception? Record the evidence that would make you stop or change the hypothesis.
