## 3. Origins and process evolution

The five closed windows are educational groupings, not universal invention dates or OmniVibe's corporate biography (the source gives its founding as 1992). Adoption overlaps; older practices can coexist through the present. Ask what problem each response solved and what it left unresolved.

1970–1984 — Apprenticeship and manager sponsorship grow leaders; access to opportunity can be uneven.

1985–1994 — Formal talent lists and development plans structure succession; nominations may inherit personal bias.

1995–2004 — Talent databases link roles and profiles; recorded potential is not demonstrated readiness.

2005–2014 — Career and succession workflows coordinate development; generic competency scores can hide role-specific gaps.

2015–2025 — Skills-aware talent reviews improve visibility; aspirations, fair opportunity and observed leadership evidence remain essential.

Recall: reconstruct problem → process/application response → unfinished problem. Which old failure could survive in a modern system?

## 4. One persona-led dilemma

Lead: Ravi Kapoor, Group CHRO (OVC-P04). Counterpart: Aisha Okafor, Director Learning and Skills (OVC-P09). These established personas are fictional. The following incident, record IDs and reveals are new synthetic teaching additions.

Scene 1 — A fictional store leadership vacancy prompts Ravi to choose a successor. A high-performing salesperson tops the talent list, but the role requires shift coordination, service judgment and coaching. The problem is continuity with credible readiness, not filling a talent grid.

Scene 2 — Choose immediate nomination, external search alone or a bounded development assignment alongside contingency coverage. Reveal: the candidate has never led a returns escalation or coached a mixed-experience shift.

Scene 3 — Nikhil Das, Store Associate and Workforce Representative (OVC-P16), reveals that the candidate's aspiration record is old. Ravi revises the plan to seek current interest, observable practice and a fair alternative path. Being on a succession slate is neither an appointment nor consent.

Decision pause: record your first choice before the reveal, then revise it. Name one fact that would make the alternative better.

## 5. Responsibilities and decision rights

Succession services own role risks and candidate slates; Development/Learning owns agreed practice and assessment evidence. HR core owns approved position and appointment facts; managers propose opportunities, while the authorized appointment panel decides selection. Ravi governs workforce design; Aisha owns learning evidence. Employees confirm aspirations and can challenge inaccurate profiles. SAP Succession & Development is the illustrative source proposal.

Frame the business rule and accountable owner before selecting technology. Source proposals do not authorize data sharing, sensitive transactions or autonomous decisions. Keep an accessible human exception and worker challenge route.

## 7. One build card — Leadership Readiness Build Card

Synthetic experiment (OVC-TOS-HR04-EXP01): For synthetic critical role SYN-POS400, compare candidate SYN-P400's sales evidence, outdated aspiration and missing shift-leadership practice. Propose one supervised service-lead assignment with an observable coaching criterion, a mentor and contingency cover; no appointment is performed.

Fill nine fields on one page: purpose/person affected; current and proposed state with source evidence; object/identity definitions; business date/time and version; application and data owners; human approval and challenge route; chosen option versus alternative and trade-off; exception/fallback and automation boundary; three test observations and your revision.

Unknown country, brand, franchise, device, policy and authority conditions need an owner and discovery question. Use no real personal data. Consider learning access, workload, service continuity and avoidable rework where evidence supports them; do not infer financial or environmental savings.

## 8. Exactly three tabletop tests

### OVC-TOS-HR04-T01 — Performance versus readiness

Reveal/input: The top seller lacks evidence for coaching and escalation duties.

Expected design response: Keep performance and leadership readiness separate; specify a supervised development experiment rather than assert readiness.

### OVC-TOS-HR04-T02 — Aspiration and access

Reveal/input: The nominee's interest is stale and another shift had no access to the opportunity.

Expected design response: Seek current aspirations, expose opportunity bias and preserve an alternative nomination route.

### OVC-TOS-HR04-T03 — Continuity under absence

Reveal/input: The proposed successor cannot accept the assignment.

Expected design response: Use authorized interim coverage and revise the plan; do not mark the critical role covered from nomination alone.

For each, record input → expected response → actual observed paper response → evidence note → pass/revise. These expected responses are proposed acceptance criteria. Publication executes none of the tests; all observation slots start unfilled. A bypass of a protected decision calls for redesign, even if the card is complete.

## 9. Traceability and honest measures

OVC-TOS-HR04-REQ01 — Trace leadership readiness to role-specific evidence and current aspirations. Source connection: HR case focus, OVC-M09–OVC-M12 and OVC-SIM-02 and OVC-SIM-04. Acceptance: T01–T02; identify authoritative evidence, owner and revision.

OVC-TOS-HR04-REQ02 — Preserve fair opportunity and an independently approved continuity decision. Source connection: OVC-M17–OVC-M19. Acceptance: T02–T03; identify the protected decision, exception and recovery condition.

OVC-TOS-HR04-ADR01 — Proposed educational choice: A supervised development assignment plus interim coverage rather than treating a talent label as appointment authority. Learners may reject it with evidence; it is not a client-approved architecture decision.

OVC-TOS-HR04-EXP01 — The single card experiment extends the existing case's bounded-pilot discipline; T01–T03 do not complete the wider case's experiments or implementation tests.

## 10. AI-driven exploration — scenarios, not predictions

These are scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps. Each requires approved purpose, evidence, worker participation, proportionate safeguards and accountable human decisions.

2026–2030 — Assist: Draft development questions from approved role evidence.

2031–2040 — Connect: Connect current aspirations, assessed learning and critical-role exposure.

2041–2050 — Adapt: Suggest alternative leadership pathways as business roles change; humans decide appointments and fair access.

Competing future: Mentorship and locally observed practice may be more credible than large talent-scoring systems with sparse evidence.

Disconfirmation question: Would the proposed pathway improve observable leadership and fair access, or reinforce existing nomination bias? State the observation that would make you stop, revise or prefer the competing future.
