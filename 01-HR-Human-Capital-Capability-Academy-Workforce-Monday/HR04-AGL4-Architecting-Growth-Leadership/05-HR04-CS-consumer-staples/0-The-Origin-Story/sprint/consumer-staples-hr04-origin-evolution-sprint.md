## 3. Origins and process evolution

These closed windows are educational groupings, not universal invention dates or OmniDaily corporate biography. Adoption overlaps; older practices can coexist through the present. Reconstruct each as need → response → unresolved problem.

1970–1984 — Informal replacement lists support continuity; patronage and hidden criteria limit opportunity.

1985–1994 — Structured talent reviews compare potential; labels can outlive evidence.

1995–2004 — Succession databases link people and positions; a named successor may still be unready.

2005–2014 — Development plans connect gaps to experience; attendance does not demonstrate capability.

2015–2025 — Talent pools and skills views broaden visibility; fair assessment, privacy and appointment authority remain essential.

## 4. One persona-led story: A successor on paper

Scene 1: Nandita Sen, Group CHRO (ODG-P06), reviews a fictional plant-director vacancy with Elena Petrović, Plant Operations Director (ODG-P09). A profile shows a nominated successor marked ready.

Scene 2: Choose immediate appointment, external recruitment or an interim cover with evidence-led development. Reveal: readiness evidence relates to commercial leadership, not the plant's operational context.

Scene 3: Another internal candidate has relevant operational evidence but no sponsor nomination. No evidence establishes that either person can assume every restricted authority.

Scene 4: Nandita opens a transparent review, separates interim responsibility from permanent appointment and defines the missing development evidence. Neither a talent-pool label nor a completed course becomes an appointment.

Decision pause: Would interim cover plus assessed development outperform immediate appointment? What evidence could justify a faster permanent decision? Record a first answer before the reveal, then revise the same card. Name a counterexample that would favor the alternative.

## 5. Responsibilities and human decision rights

Succession services own nominations, pools and readiness records; development/learning owns plans and assessed evidence; core HR owns positions and accepted appointments. Operations defines role-specific practical expectations. HR governs fair review and access; authorized talent panels recommend, and the delegated appointment authority decides. SAP Succession & Development is illustrative.

These are proposed educational responsibilities. Establish the business rule, source of truth and accountable human before choosing technology. SAP examples illustrate a capability and are not verified deployments, licensing claims or product roadmaps. A worker must have an accessible human route to question incorrect data or a decision. HR status, learning completion, work authorization and quality/batch release are distinct decisions.

## 7. One build card: Leadership Readiness Decision Card

Synthetic experiment: SYN-POS404 is a plant-director role with two synthetic candidates SYN-C404A/B and one temporary coverage option. Map the source's plant, regional supply-chain and country-commercial roles as context, while deciding only this plant role.

Fill nine fields on one page: (1) purpose/person affected; (2) current/proposed state and evidence; (3) identifiers and definitions; (4) business time/version; (5) application/data owners; (6) human approval/appeal; (7) chosen option versus alternative and trade-off; (8) exception, fallback and automation boundary; (9) three observed test responses and revision.

Design constraint: Do not conflate potential, performance, readiness or appointment. Record role-specific evidence date, reviewer and challenge route; restrict private talent information.

All SYN identifiers, episode details and injected evidence are new teaching data. Country rules, collective agreements, qualifications, worker/device populations and local approvals remain unknown unless explicitly sourced. Record each unknown with an owner and discovery question. Do not invent legal rules or calculate unsupported operational benefits.

## 8. Exactly three tabletop tests

### ODG-TOS-HR04-T01 — Stale readiness

Reveal/input: The ready-now label relies on old evidence from a different role.

Expected design response: Reassess against the target role and preserve uncertainty rather than confirming readiness.

### ODG-TOS-HR04-T02 — Invisible candidate

Reveal/input: A credible internal candidate lacks a sponsor nomination.

Expected design response: Offer a fair evidence-based review route; do not automatically nominate or exclude.

### ODG-TOS-HR04-T03 — Role versus authority

Reveal/input: An interim assignment is proposed without all operational delegations.

Expected design response: Limit scope and route delegations to authorized owners; do not infer every authority from the title.

For each test record input → expected response → actual paper response → evidence note → pass/revise. These are proposed acceptance criteria. The evidence slot starts unfilled: publication runs none of the tests and proves no operational result. If a protected human decision can be bypassed, revise the design even if every card field is filled.

## 9. Traceability and measures

ODG-TOS-HR04-REQ01 — Trace one critical-role succession recommendation to current role-specific evidence. Derived from the supporting HR source mandate and ODG-M18; assessed by T01.

ODG-TOS-HR04-REQ02 — Protect fair review, restricted talent data and explicit appointment/delegation authority. Derived from the source governance mandate and ODG-M19; assessed by T02–T03.

ODG-TOS-HR04-ADR01 — Prefer transparent readiness review and bounded interim coverage over automatic promotion from a pool; compare continuity risk and delay. This proposed educational decision can be rejected with evidence; record the alternative and consequence.

ODG-TOS-HR04-EXP01 — The one synthetic build card extends ODG-M19's delivery-readiness discipline; T01–T03 do not execute the larger implementation case.

ODG-TOS-HR04-M01 — Mandatory card fields completed / 9 × 100: learning completeness only. ODG-TOS-HR04-M02 — Record whether T02/T03 retain fair review and bounded delegated authority. Record actual paper observations rather than assuming success.

ODG-TOS-HR04-EV01 — Unfilled evidence slot for test observations, revisions and reviewer questions. Existing case success measures are intended outcomes; no baseline or target attainment follows from publishing this edition.

## 10. AI-driven exploration: scenarios, not predictions

These are scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps. Each needs evidence, approved purpose, accountable ownership and proportionate controls.

2026–2030 — Assist: Assistance could summarize evidence gaps while reviewers assess readiness.

2031–2040 — Connect: Development and succession services could exchange scoped evidence with worker visibility.

2041–2050 — Adapt: Development opportunities could adapt to emerging roles without promising promotion or overriding appointment authority.

Competing future: Smaller human-led talent councils with strong internal opportunity visibility may outperform algorithmic ranking.

Disconfirmation question: Would the evidence-led design systematically overlook less visible candidates or delay continuity more than a simpler fair review? Name evidence that would stop or change the hypothesis.
