## 4. One persona-led story

Arjun Mehta (OCF-P04), Chief Risk Officer, faces a synthetic critical-role vacancy. Nadia Fernandes (OCF-P07), Group CHRO, presents a ready-now successor. Daniel Weber (OCF-P12), Director Enterprise Architecture, asks whether a strong specialist must become a manager to progress.

Choose before revealing: Appoint from the talent label, freeze all mobility, or separate emergency cover, readiness evidence and longer-term development? Record your first reason on the card.

Evidence reveal: The successor has strong delivery evidence but no supervised experience of the new accountability. A second specialist prefers an expert path. Nadia revises the slate: readiness is conditional, aspirations are respected, and Arjun seeks an appropriately authorized interim arrangement.

Revise your decision: Potential is a revisable judgment, not identity or appointment approval. Specialist growth and leadership continuity deserve separate evidence. Preserve an accessible human route to challenge errors. Financial service continuity matters, but an urgent cut-off does not create authority. Customer KYC and employee readiness are different processes; do not copy customer identity checks into HR without purpose and policy review.

## 5. Application responsibilities and human authority

Frame the business need first; applications implement the chosen operating model. The following is a proposed teaching architecture. Confirm named delegates, country policy, legal basis and actual product scope before implementation.

| Application responsibility | Accountable data owner | Human decision right |
|---|---|---|
| Succession and development; SAP example | Talent owner maintains readiness evidence and restricted succession records | Calibrated talent panel recommends; authorized appointment body appoints. |
| Role and risk requirements | Role owner defines accountability and current requirements | Arjun’s delegated authority confirms interim cover and required role checks. |
| Learning and career opportunities | Learning owner records assessment; employee owns stated aspirations | Manager and employee agree development experiences; recommendations do not compel a move. |

Core HR owns workforce facts; domain owners own performance, learning, rewards, time or payroll facts as applicable. IAM executes access policy; integration transports and reconciles states; analytics reads authorized data and does not become its owner. AI may summarize permitted evidence or draft a reminder, but cannot approve consequential employment, regulated duties, payments or access. An interface acknowledgment is not proof of the business outcome. SAP examples illustrate responsibilities, not guaranteed features or universal localization.

## 7. One Decision Build Card

Synthetic input: FIN-ROLE400 critical-risk position; candidate FIN-P401 marked ready-now; practical exposure missing; candidate FIN-P402 prefers specialist progression; all synthetic.

Keep all work in this one card. Capture: business problem; initial and revised choice with reason; entity/identity/date semantics; authoritative source and accountable owner; human approval boundary; one justified local variant with purpose/minimum data/viewers; known versus unknown downstream state; exception owner and approved fallback; all three test observations and evidence references. Missing evidence stays blank or unknown, never silently becomes approval.

Compare one alternative: a manual controlled handoff may be preferable to automation when exception ownership or recovery is absent. State the evidence needed to change your mind. Avoid collecting real screening, health, payroll, customer or conduct records in this learning card.

## 8. Exactly three tabletop tests

| Test | Input / reveal | Expected proposed behavior | Evidence to record |
|---|---|---|---|
| OCF-HR04-T01 — Stale readiness | Ready-now label survives a changed role scope. | Reassess evidence against current responsibilities; keep the historical assessment. | Criteria version, missing exposure and revised readiness. |
| OCF-HR04-T02 — Aspirations and alternatives | Strong specialist declines a management track. | Offer a credible specialist path; do not lower potential automatically. | Employee preference, alternative experience and panel rationale. |
| OCF-HR04-T03 — Confidential emergency cover | Urgent vacancy prompts broad sharing of the succession slate. | Limit disclosure; route interim cover to authorized decision makers without treating the slate as approval. | Access boundary, appointment owner and continuity gap. |

For each test, record input → expected → observed → revision/evidence → Pass, Revise or Blocked. All observed results are initially unfilled. Publication does not execute any tabletop, system test, approval or operational transaction. Acceptance requires a defensible card and all three observed tabletop results with revisions; bypassing a protected human decision requires redesign.

## 10. Separate AI-driven exploration: 2026–2050

Scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps.

| Scenario window | Question to explore |
|---|---|
| 2026–2030 — Assist | Could approved-source assistance explain evidence gaps and draft next steps without approving the decision? |
| 2031–2040 — Connect | Could separately authorized services coordinate this handoff while keeping unknown states and local rights visible? |
| 2041–2050 — Adapt | Could reviewed feedback propose reversible process changes while people retain consequential authority? |

Competing future: fragmented systems, uneven access, policy restrictions and manual review persist; a simpler controlled workflow outperforms orchestration. Falsifiable hypothesis question: Would a larger succession slate improve continuity if no candidate has relevant practical exposure? Define an observation that would disprove your claim—for example fewer steps but more unresolved exceptions or worse worker outcomes. Compare against a manual baseline before claiming value.
