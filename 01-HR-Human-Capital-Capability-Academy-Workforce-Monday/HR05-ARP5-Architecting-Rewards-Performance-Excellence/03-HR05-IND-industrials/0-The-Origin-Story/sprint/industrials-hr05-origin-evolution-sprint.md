## 3. Origins: five closed historical teaching windows

These windows group teaching ideas, not universal invention dates or OmniWorks corporate biography. Adoption overlaps; older practices can coexist through the present. Each generation changes the medium without automatically resolving ownership, evidence or authority.

1970–1984 — Piece rates and local wage records connect pay to visible work; output incentives can overlook quality, safety and unequal opportunity.

1985–1994 — Salary grades and negotiated allowances create structure; local rules and scarce skills still vary.

1995–2004 — ERP compensation records and spreadsheets improve calculation; formulas can reproduce unexplained differences.

2005–2014 — Merit planning and balanced incentive frameworks connect performance to budgets; weak evidence can still distort outcomes.

2015–2025 — Cloud worksheets and equity analytics improve visibility; an anomaly is not a concluded discrimination finding or an automatic pay decision.

Recall prompt: reconstruct the five responses as problem → process/application response → unfinished problem. Which old failure could survive in the newest system?

## 4. One persona-led dilemma

### Scene 1

Rhea receives Marco's request to release a plant bonus after record production. She asks which approved plan version, population and period define the entitlement before considering a worksheet.

### Scene 2

Kenji asks whether workers understood the safety condition before the performance period. Reveal: the serious incident is recorded, but attribution and the applicable plan treatment remain under review. Rhea refuses both automatic full payout and blanket punishment.

### Scene 3

Amina reveals unexplained pay differences among scarce engineers. Rhea requests job, level, geography, experience and permitted factors with qualified review; a statistical flag does not justify inventing a cause or exposing colleagues' pay.

### Scene 4

Olivia finds a synthetic approved gross award that differs from the proposed payroll input. Rhea holds the affected transfer, retains the original proposal and records who must reconcile it. Approval, payroll calculation and bank payment remain different states.

Decision pause: Release all awards, hold the affected decision pending evidence, or apply a documented approved adjustment. Which plan evidence and worker impact support the choice?

## 5. Responsibilities, ownership and human authority

Rewards service (rewards policy owner): plan version, eligibility, proration, guideline, proposed/approved gross award and rationale; SAP Compensation/Variable Pay are possible examples.

Performance, quality and safety systems (respective owners): evidence and findings relevant to agreed rules; they do not directly determine payroll output.

HR core (HR owner): job, assignment and compensation master facts; equity analysis consumes authorized data with restricted access.

Payroll (payroll control owner): accepted inputs, calculations and resulting pay; Finance owns budget/accrual/posting reconciliation; bank/payment services own payment outcomes.

Authorized reward approvers decide under the approved plan after consultation/review; employees retain explanation and a correction/appeal route.

Design boundary: Do not infer lawful entitlement or change safety rules retroactively. Equity flags and safety incidents require accountable assessment; they are not automatic collective penalties. Proposed responsibilities are learning architecture, not proof that the fictional enterprise already operates this design. Select technology only after the business rule and accountable owner are clear. SAP examples illustrate capabilities, not mandatory product choices.

## 7. One build card: Reward Decision Build Card

Synthetic experiment: SYN-PLAN501, period P1 and employee SYN-W501 have a proposed 1,000-unit gross award. Payroll input shows 1,100 units. A safety condition's application is unresolved. Units are synthetic currency-neutral teaching values, not a pay offer or country rule.

Fill these nine fields on one page: purpose and affected person; current/proposed state and source evidence; identity/object definitions; business date/time and version; application/data owners; human approval and appeal route; chosen option versus alternative with trade-off; exception/fallback and automation boundary; three test observations and revision.

Minimum design reasoning: Keep rule version, eligibility dates, evidence status and gross-to-payroll lineage. Distinguish a held affected decision from withholding all unrelated pay. Ask qualified owners to confirm obligations and communication before any real action.

Do not invent country rules, safety thresholds, collective-agreement terms, live data or missing approvals. Record unknowns with an owner and discovery question. Support an accessible human route for the worker. Consider fatigue, learning opportunity, avoidable travel/rework or resource use where the dilemma provides evidence; do not infer environmental savings.

## 8. Exactly three tabletop tests

### OW-TOS-HR05-T01 — Plan applicability

Reveal/input: Output exceeded target but the incident treatment has no confirmed applicable rule.

Expected design response: Escalate the affected decision for authorized plan review; do not silently invent a penalty, entitlement or final award.

### OW-TOS-HR05-T02 — Equity counterexample

Reveal/input: Two engineers show different pay, but grade and assignment context are incomplete.

Expected design response: Restrict access, investigate relevant permitted factors and offer review; do not label a cause or expose named peers.

### OW-TOS-HR05-T03 — Award handoff mismatch

Reveal/input: Approved gross 1,000 units differs from payroll input 1,100 units.

Expected design response: Hold/reconcile the affected input with rewards/payroll/Finance owners, retain audit history and confirm downstream outcome before completion.

For each test, record input → expected response → actual observed paper response → evidence note → pass/revise. Expected responses are proposed acceptance criteria, not executed results. Publication executes none of these tabletop tests. A bypass of the protected decision requires redesign, even if the card is complete.

## 10. AI-driven exploration: scenarios, not predictions

These are scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps. All require approved purpose, evidence, worker participation, accountable ownership and proportionate controls.

2026–2030 — Assist: Assistance could explain approved plan terms and flag missing evidence without promising payment.

2031–2040 — Connect: Connected reward evidence could link performance, equity review and reconciled payroll inputs through minimal data.

2041–2050 — Adapt: Adaptive incentives could be revised prospectively with worker participation as service and sustainability models change; entitlement and approval remain governed.

Competing future: Simpler negotiated team rewards with fewer opaque individual metrics may create more trust than increasingly personalized formulas.

Disconfirmation question: Would added incentive signals increase unsafe shortcuts, disputed awards or unexplained variation compared with a simpler agreed plan? Record the evidence that would make you stop or change the hypothesis.
