## 3. Origins and process evolution

These closed windows are educational groupings, not universal invention dates or OmniDaily corporate biography. Adoption overlaps; older practices can coexist through the present. Reconstruct each as need → response → unresolved problem.

1970–1984 — Wage tables and supervisor awards support pay decisions; opaque criteria undermine trust.

1985–1994 — Structured salary grades and bonus rules improve consistency; local exceptions proliferate.

1995–2004 — Compensation systems calculate plans; clean arithmetic cannot fix wrong eligibility.

2005–2014 — Integrated planning links rewards and performance; an approved award is still not a paid amount.

2015–2025 — Cloud planning and analytics expose budgets; fairness, comparable evidence and local rules still need accountable decisions.

## 5. Responsibilities and human decision rights

Core HR owns dated employment/assignment facts; performance services own approved performance evidence. Rewards owns plan versions, eligibility and award proposals. Finance owns budget assurance; business/quality owners validate their metric inputs. Authorized compensation approvers decide awards; payroll owns approved inputs, calculations and reconciliation; treasury owns payment status. SAP Compensation/Variable Pay and ECP are examples.

These are proposed educational responsibilities. Establish the business rule, source of truth and accountable human before choosing technology. SAP examples illustrate a capability and are not verified deployments, licensing claims or product roadmaps. A worker must have an accessible human route to question incorrect data or a decision. HR status, learning completion, work authorization and quality/batch release are distinct decisions.

## 7. One build card: Reward Decision Card

Synthetic experiment: Use SYN-E505, two synthetic assignment periods and SYN-PLAN505A/B. Leave rates, legal terms and actual amounts unknown; test eligibility and evidence rather than inventing a statutory calculation.

Fill nine fields on one page: (1) purpose/person affected; (2) current/proposed state and evidence; (3) identifiers and definitions; (4) business time/version; (5) application/data owners; (6) human approval/appeal; (7) chosen option versus alternative and trade-off; (8) exception, fallback and automation boundary; (9) three observed test responses and revision.

Design constraint: Separate eligible service, plan version, validated result, gross award, payroll input and net payment. Unknown plan rules require discovery, not an assumed proration formula.

All SYN identifiers, episode details and injected evidence are new teaching data. Country rules, collective agreements, qualifications, worker/device populations and local approvals remain unknown unless explicitly sourced. Record each unknown with an owner and discovery question. Do not invent legal rules or calculate unsupported operational benefits.

## 8. Exactly three tabletop tests

### ODG-TOS-HR05-T01 — Transfer overlap

Reveal/input: Both assignment worksheets claim the full period.

Expected design response: Detect overlap; request approved plan/eligibility treatment and retain both source claims without double awarding.

### ODG-TOS-HR05-T02 — Provisional metric

Reveal/input: An award uses shipment data with unresolved returns/quality status.

Expected design response: Label the measure provisional and route business validation; do not substitute a different metric silently.

### ODG-TOS-HR05-T03 — Payroll mismatch

Reveal/input: Approved gross award differs from acknowledged payroll input.

Expected design response: Hold/reconcile the affected handoff with an owner; do not call the award paid.

For each test record input → expected response → actual paper response → evidence note → pass/revise. These are proposed acceptance criteria. The evidence slot starts unfilled: publication runs none of the tests and proves no operational result. If a protected human decision can be bypassed, revise the design even if every card field is filled.

## 9. Traceability and measures

ODG-TOS-HR05-REQ01 — Trace reward eligibility to dated assignment facts and the authorized plan version. Derived from the supporting HR source mandate and ODG-M18; assessed by T01.

ODG-TOS-HR05-REQ02 — Keep validated performance, approval and payroll/payment evidence distinct. Derived from the source governance mandate and ODG-M19; assessed by T02–T03.

ODG-TOS-HR05-ADR01 — Prefer evidence-backed eligibility segments where supported by plan terms over an unexamined annual snapshot; compare simplicity and exception effort. This proposed educational decision can be rejected with evidence; record the alternative and consequence.

ODG-TOS-HR05-EXP01 — The one synthetic build card extends ODG-M19's delivery-readiness discipline; T01–T03 do not execute the larger implementation case.

ODG-TOS-HR05-M01 — Mandatory card fields completed / 9 × 100: learning completeness only. ODG-TOS-HR05-M02 — Record whether T02/T03 expose provisional inputs and amount/status mismatches. Record actual paper observations rather than assuming success.

## 10. AI-driven exploration: scenarios, not predictions

These are scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps. Each needs evidence, approved purpose, accountable ownership and proportionate controls.

2026–2030 — Assist: Assistance could explain eligibility exceptions while rewards owners approve rules.

2031–2040 — Connect: Plan, business evidence and payroll services could exchange versioned approved inputs.

2041–2050 — Adapt: Reward suggestions could adapt to role changes with transparent fairness review and contestability.

Competing future: Simpler stable plans with clear human explanations may yield more trust than highly personalized incentive algorithms.

Disconfirmation question: Would segmented automation generate more unexplained exceptions or unfair outcomes than a simpler authorized plan? Name evidence that would stop or change the hypothesis.
