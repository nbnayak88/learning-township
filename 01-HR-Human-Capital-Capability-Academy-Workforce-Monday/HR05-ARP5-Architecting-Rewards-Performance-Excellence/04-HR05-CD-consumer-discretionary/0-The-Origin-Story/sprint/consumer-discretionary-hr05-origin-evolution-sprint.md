## 3. Origins and process evolution

The five closed windows are educational groupings, not universal invention dates or OmniVibe's corporate biography (the source gives its founding as 1992). Adoption overlaps; older practices can coexist through the present. Ask what problem each response solved and what it left unresolved.

1970–1984 — Wage ledgers and discretionary bonuses recognize work; reasons can be inconsistent.

1985–1994 — Grade and merit tables improve structure; local exceptions can remain opaque.

1995–2004 — Digital compensation cycles improve calculations; incorrect eligibility still produces incorrect awards.

2005–2014 — Workflow approvals and performance links improve control; incentive gaming remains possible.

2015–2025 — Cloud reward planning improves visibility; budget authority, fairness and payroll reconciliation still need evidence.

Recall: reconstruct problem → process/application response → unfinished problem. Which old failure could survive in a modern system?

## 4. One persona-led dilemma

Lead: Daniel Weber, Head of Rewards and Performance (OVC-P10). Counterpart: Nadia Rahman, Global Payroll Director (OVC-P05). These established personas are fictional. The following incident, record IDs and reveals are new synthetic teaching additions.

Scene 1 — Daniel receives a synthetic sales award proposal across two stores. Nadia asks which employment, service period, currency and approved policy the award belongs to. The business problem is a defensible reward, before selecting compensation software.

Scene 2 — Choose raw sales, a universal award rule or documented eligibility with service safeguards. Reveal: returns were delayed, and the associate transferred between stores during the award period. A high sales total does not settle eligibility or good service.

Scene 3 — Adrian Tan, Group CFO (OVC-P02), asks for the approved budget. It is absent. Daniel revises the proposal to hold release, preserve period evidence and assign policy and budget questions. A compensation planning result is not a payroll result or bank payment.

Decision pause: record your first choice before the reveal, then revise it. Name one fact that would make the alternative better.

## 5. Responsibilities and decision rights

Rewards owns eligibility policy, award proposal and approval history; Performance owns reviewed contribution evidence. HR core owns employment and assignment context; finance owns budget approval; Payroll owns accepted pay inputs and calculation/reconciliation. Treasury or payment services own payment execution under separate authority. Daniel proposes; designated reward and budget approvers authorize; Nadia accepts only reconciled inputs. SAP Compensation & Variable Pay illustrates the source proposal.

Frame the business rule and accountable owner before selecting technology. Source proposals do not authorize data sharing, sensitive transactions or autonomous decisions. Keep an accessible human exception and worker challenge route.

## 7. One build card — Reward Decision Build Card

Synthetic experiment (OVC-TOS-HR05-EXP01): Synthetic associate SYN-P500 transfers between SYN-S01/SYN-S02 mid-period. Proposed gross award is 100 synthetic currency units; payroll input shows 110. Policy, period allocation and budget approval remain unverified. Reconcile the difference without inventing a real formula or country rule.

Fill nine fields on one page: purpose/person affected; current and proposed state with source evidence; object/identity definitions; business date/time and version; application and data owners; human approval and challenge route; chosen option versus alternative and trade-off; exception/fallback and automation boundary; three test observations and your revision.

Unknown country, brand, franchise, device, policy and authority conditions need an owner and discovery question. Use no real personal data. Consider learning access, workload, service continuity and avoidable rework where evidence supports them; do not infer financial or environmental savings.

## 8. Exactly three tabletop tests

### OVC-TOS-HR05-T01 — Eligibility across a transfer

Reveal/input: A proposal covers two stores and different effective dates.

Expected design response: Trace the service period and approved employment/assignment evidence; hold ambiguous eligibility and avoid double awards.

### OVC-TOS-HR05-T02 — Harmful incentive

Reveal/input: Sales qualify but linked returns were deliberately deferred.

Expected design response: Challenge the reward design using service evidence and authorized policy review; do not silently invent weights.

### OVC-TOS-HR05-T03 — Proposal-to-pay mismatch

Reveal/input: Approved proposal would be 100 units but payroll input is 110; budget is unconfirmed.

Expected design response: Hold release, reconcile version/currency/amount and obtain budget authority; do not claim payment from approval alone.

For each, record input → expected response → actual observed paper response → evidence note → pass/revise. These expected responses are proposed acceptance criteria. Publication executes none of the tests; all observation slots start unfilled. A bypass of a protected decision calls for redesign, even if the card is complete.

## 9. Traceability and honest measures

OVC-TOS-HR05-REQ02 — Separate reward, budget, payroll and payment authority and reconcile handoffs. Source connection: OVC-M17–OVC-M19. Acceptance: T02–T03; identify the protected decision, exception and recovery condition.

## 10. AI-driven exploration — scenarios, not predictions

These are scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps. Each requires approved purpose, evidence, worker participation, proportionate safeguards and accountable human decisions.

2026–2030 — Assist: Draft eligibility explanations and highlight inconsistent inputs.

2031–2040 — Connect: Connect approved performance, reward and payroll evidence through controlled contracts.

2041–2050 — Adapt: Suggest reward design alternatives as work changes; humans retain policy, fairness and budget decisions.

Competing future: A simpler local award policy with transparent review may outperform complex optimization that employees cannot understand or challenge.

Disconfirmation question: Would the new award improve fair recognition, or increase gaming and unexplained payroll differences? State the observation that would make you stop, revise or prefer the competing future.
