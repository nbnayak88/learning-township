## 4. One persona-led story

David Morgan (OCF-P02), Group CFO, receives a synthetic bonus proposal for a wealth team. Priya Rao (OCF-P03), Chief Customer and Retail Officer, argues for retention. Elena Fischer (OCF-P05), Chief Compliance and Conduct Officer, sees an unresolved conduct review and an uncertain deferral classification.

Choose before revealing: Pay the proposal immediately, cancel every award, or distinguish approved eligibility, risk review, award decision and payroll release? Record your first reason on the card.

Evidence reveal: The worksheet amount is a recommendation, not an approved award. The synthetic policy requires an authorized remuneration review for this role and a documented deferral decision. David keeps the proposal visible without treating it as cash owed; Priya requests timely review and employee communication.

Revise your decision: Separate base pay from variable awards and proposed from approved amounts. A conduct allegation does not authorize automatic forfeiture; deferral, malus or clawback requires applicable policy and competent approval. Preserve an accessible human route to challenge errors. Financial service continuity matters, but an urgent cut-off does not create authority. Customer KYC and employee readiness are different processes; do not copy customer identity checks into HR without purpose and policy review.

## 5. Application responsibilities and human authority

Frame the business need first; applications implement the chosen operating model. The following is a proposed teaching architecture. Confirm named delegates, country policy, legal basis and actual product scope before implementation.

| Application responsibility | Accountable data owner | Human decision right |
|---|---|---|
| Compensation and Variable Pay examples | Reward owner maintains eligibility, proposal, policy version and award records | Authorized remuneration body approves awards and exceptions. |
| Risk / conduct review | Conduct owner owns findings and permitted disclosure | Elena’s delegates advise within policy; managers cannot silently override required review. |
| Payroll and finance | Payroll owns pay calculation; Finance owns funding/posting controls | Authorized payroll/payment approvers release outputs after reconciled approved inputs. |

Core HR owns workforce facts; domain owners own performance, learning, rewards, time or payroll facts as applicable. IAM executes access policy; integration transports and reconciles states; analytics reads authorized data and does not become its owner. AI may summarize permitted evidence or draft a reminder, but cannot approve consequential employment, regulated duties, payments or access. An interface acknowledgment is not proof of the business outcome. SAP examples illustrate responsibilities, not guaranteed features or universal localization.

## 7. One Decision Build Card

Synthetic input: FIN-AWARD500 proposed 100,000 fictional currency units; budget ceiling 120,000; conduct review pending; deferral classification unknown; no approved payroll output.

Keep all work in this one card. Capture: business problem; initial and revised choice with reason; entity/identity/date semantics; authoritative source and accountable owner; human approval boundary; one justified local variant with purpose/minimum data/viewers; known versus unknown downstream state; exception owner and approved fallback; all three test observations and evidence references. Missing evidence stays blank or unknown, never silently becomes approval.

Compare one alternative: a manual controlled handoff may be preferable to automation when exception ownership or recovery is absent. State the evidence needed to change your mind. Avoid collecting real screening, health, payroll, customer or conduct records in this learning card.

## 8. Exactly three tabletop tests

| Test | Input / reveal | Expected proposed behavior | Evidence to record |
|---|---|---|---|
| OCF-HR05-T01 — Proposal mistaken for award | Worksheet recommendation appears in a payroll upload. | Hold unapproved variable input and reconcile approval/version; preserve unrelated approved salary processing. | Approval state, version, exception owner and separated pay components. |
| OCF-HR05-T02 — Unknown deferral rule | Planner assumes no deferral because the field is blank. | Mark unknown; obtain role/country policy assessment; do not invent a universal remuneration rule. | Policy scope, authorized reviewer and unresolved classification. |
| OCF-HR05-T03 — Changed conduct evidence | Review status changes after award approval but before release. | Route the changed evidence under the applicable policy; no automatic confiscation or silent payment. | Versioned decision, review authority, worker communication and revised handoff. |

For each test, record input → expected → observed → revision/evidence → Pass, Revise or Blocked. All observed results are initially unfilled. Publication does not execute any tabletop, system test, approval or operational transaction. Acceptance requires a defensible card and all three observed tabletop results with revisions; bypassing a protected human decision requires redesign.

## 10. Separate AI-driven exploration: 2026–2050

Scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps.

| Scenario window | Question to explore |
|---|---|
| 2026–2030 — Assist | Could approved-source assistance explain evidence gaps and draft next steps without approving the decision? |
| 2031–2040 — Connect | Could separately authorized services coordinate this handoff while keeping unknown states and local rights visible? |
| 2041–2050 — Adapt | Could reviewed feedback propose reversible process changes while people retain consequential authority? |

Competing future: fragmented systems, uneven access, policy restrictions and manual review persist; a simpler controlled workflow outperforms orchestration. Falsifiable hypothesis question: Would automated award calculation improve reward quality if conduct review and policy interpretation remain unresolved? Define an observation that would disprove your claim—for example fewer steps but more unresolved exceptions or worse worker outcomes. Compare against a manual baseline before claiming value.
