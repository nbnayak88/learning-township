## 3. Origins and process evolution

The five closed windows are educational groupings, not universal invention dates or OmniVibe's corporate biography (the source gives its founding as 1992). Adoption overlaps; older practices can coexist through the present. Ask what problem each response solved and what it left unresolved.

1970–1984 — Buddy learning and apprenticeships transmit practical work; evidence may remain local.

1985–1994 — Course catalogs and training registers improve access; attendance need not show learning.

1995–2004 — LMS tracking makes completions visible; real task performance can remain unobserved.

2005–2014 — Digital development and internal vacancy workflows connect opportunity; unequal time and device access can persist.

2015–2025 — Skills-aware learning links practice to role needs; assessment validity and authorized mobility still require judgment.

Recall: reconstruct problem → process/application response → unfinished problem. Which old failure could survive in a modern system?

## 4. One persona-led dilemma

Lead: Aisha Okafor, Director Learning and Skills (OVC-P09). Counterpart: Oliver Brooks, Store Operations Director (OVC-P06). These established personas are fictional. The following incident, record IDs and reveals are new synthetic teaching additions.

Scene 1 — Aisha wants associates to learn across store and fulfillment roles. Oliver needs customer coverage during busy shifts. A synthetic associate has completed an online returns course and requests a service-desk opportunity. The problem is useful practice and fair opportunity, before choosing a learning platform.

Scene 2 — Choose a compulsory long course, immediate transfer after completion or short supervised practice with protected service coverage. Reveal: the completion record contains no observation of a difficult returns interaction.

Scene 3 — Nikhil Das, Store Associate and Workforce Representative (OVC-P16), reveals that the only practice slot conflicts with the associate's assigned shift. Aisha revises the experiment to offer a feasible alternative and current aspiration check. Learning completion neither proves capability nor approves mobility.

Decision pause: record your first choice before the reveal, then revise it. Name one fact that would make the alternative better.

## 5. Responsibilities and decision rights

Learning owns courses, completion records and assessment evidence; skills stewards govern capability definitions and validity. Operations owns supervised practice, coverage and local task release. HR core owns approved assignment changes; talent/mobility owners govern opportunity selection. The employee confirms aspiration and can challenge errors. Aisha verifies evidence; the authorized manager approves a placement. SAP Learning and optional mobility capabilities are source examples whose deployment is unverified.

Frame the business rule and accountable owner before selecting technology. Source proposals do not authorize data sharing, sensitive transactions or autonomous decisions. Keep an accessible human exception and worker challenge route.

## 7. One build card — Learning-to-Mobility Build Card

Synthetic experiment (OVC-TOS-HR06-EXP01): SYN-L600 shows course completion for SYN-P600 but no assessed returns skill. Build one supervised role-play, a two-part rubric (accurate policy explanation and respectful escalation), coverage plan, worker feedback and a proposed mobility decision. No real qualification or assignment is granted.

Fill nine fields on one page: purpose/person affected; current and proposed state with source evidence; object/identity definitions; business date/time and version; application and data owners; human approval and challenge route; chosen option versus alternative and trade-off; exception/fallback and automation boundary; three test observations and your revision.

Unknown country, brand, franchise, device, policy and authority conditions need an owner and discovery question. Use no real personal data. Consider learning access, workload, service continuity and avoidable rework where evidence supports them; do not infer financial or environmental savings.

## 8. Exactly three tabletop tests

### OVC-TOS-HR06-T01 — Completion versus ability

Reveal/input: The course is complete but the associate cannot explain an exception.

Expected design response: Keep completion visible, mark capability unproven and propose practice/feedback before assignment release.

### OVC-TOS-HR06-T02 — Access to practice

Reveal/input: The only training slot would leave the store uncovered or exclude a shift.

Expected design response: Revise timing or supervised cover with the accountable manager; provide an accessible alternative instead of blaming the learner.

### OVC-TOS-HR06-T03 — Stale evidence and changed role

Reveal/input: A prior assessment exists but the service policy has changed.

Expected design response: Revalidate evidence against current duties and worker aspiration; do not silently convert an old skill record into mobility approval.

For each, record input → expected response → actual observed paper response → evidence note → pass/revise. These expected responses are proposed acceptance criteria. Publication executes none of the tests; all observation slots start unfilled. A bypass of a protected decision calls for redesign, even if the card is complete.

## 9. Traceability and honest measures

OVC-TOS-HR06-REQ01 — Link claimed capability to observable current practice evidence. Source connection: HR case focus, OVC-M09–OVC-M12 and OVC-SIM-02 and OVC-SIM-04. Acceptance: T01–T02; identify authoritative evidence, owner and revision.

OVC-TOS-HR06-REQ02 — Offer feasible learning access and separate mobility selection from completion. Source connection: OVC-M17–OVC-M19. Acceptance: T02–T03; identify the protected decision, exception and recovery condition.

OVC-TOS-HR06-ADR01 — Proposed educational choice: Short supervised practice with coverage and feedback rather than a completion-only gate or an inflexible long course. Learners may reject it with evidence; it is not a client-approved architecture decision.

OVC-TOS-HR06-EXP01 — The single card experiment extends the existing case's bounded-pilot discipline; T01–T03 do not complete the wider case's experiments or implementation tests.

OVC-TOS-HR06-M01 — Mandatory card fields completed / 9 × 100: learning completeness only. OVC-TOS-HR06-M02 — Record whether each proposed acceptance criterion survives its evidence reveal; record observed paper results separately from expectations. Real baselines and targets remain TBD.

OVC-TOS-HR06-EV01 — Initially unfilled evidence slot for the three observations, revised choice and reviewer questions. No achieved operational result, realized saving or executed system test is claimed.

## 10. AI-driven exploration — scenarios, not predictions

These are scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps. Each requires approved purpose, evidence, worker participation, proportionate safeguards and accountable human decisions.

2026–2030 — Assist: Suggest relevant practice questions and explain evidence gaps.

2031–2040 — Connect: Connect assessed skills, opportunity requirements and feasible shift learning.

2041–2050 — Adapt: Suggest changing learning pathways and adjacent roles; workers and authorized managers decide participation and placement.

Competing future: Peer-led practice and local mentoring may beat recommendation engines where skill definitions and access are weak.

Disconfirmation question: Would the learning design improve observed service capability across shifts, or only increase completions? State the observation that would make you stop, revise or prefer the competing future.
