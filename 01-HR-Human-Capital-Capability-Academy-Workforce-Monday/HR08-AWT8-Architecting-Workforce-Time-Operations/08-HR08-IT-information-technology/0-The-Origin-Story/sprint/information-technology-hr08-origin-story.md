## Origins: the question that survives each era

These closed windows group process evolution for teaching; they are not universal invention or adoption dates. Adoption overlaps. Paper, spreadsheets and older systems can coexist through the present.
1970–1984 — Timesheets and rosters record attendance
1985–1994 — Time clocks and databases capture events
1995–2004 — ERP links schedules, absence and payroll inputs
2005–2014 — Web/mobile entries connect project and service work
2015–2025 — Distributed work and automated checks expose timezone and surveillance risks
Hours worked, paid hours, on-call availability and billable hours are different measures.

## One story: choose, reveal, revise

Marcus Okoro (OTS-P08), SRE and Managed Services Director, sees a synthetic incident shift span two calendar dates. Ravi Patel (OTS-P14), FinOps and Service Economics Lead, wants project costs reconciled. Nadia Fernandes (OTS-P07), Group CHRO, asks whether an approved billable entry proves overtime entitlement.
Choose before reading on: Copy all hours to billing and payroll, discard the ambiguous entry, or preserve and resolve time semantics before distinct handoffs?
Evidence reveal: The time record has no timezone and the project code changed at midnight. Marcus keeps the raw event, requests the worker's correction and manager approval, then sends separate approved inputs to payroll and project costing. Billing eligibility follows its own contract decision; attendance alone proves neither pay premium nor billability.
Write what changed your decision and what remains unknown. An accessible explanation and a human challenge route must be available to the affected worker. An uncertain record cannot silently become an authorization.

## Application responsibility and human authority

Time / absence — Time owner maintains event, schedule, absence and correction evidence. Worker submits correction; authorized manager approves.
Payroll — Payroll owns valuation of approved pay inputs. Payroll reviewer applies validated local rules.
Project costing / billing — Project owner holds cost codes; commercial owner holds billability rules. Project/commercial approvers authorize their own treatments.
Service operations — Operations owns incident coverage and service continuity. Duty manager approves coverage changes.
Business owners remain accountable; applications store and process the records. Discover the actual delegated approvers before implementation. Restrict client, candidate, pay and health information to its purpose and permitted viewers. Minimize replicated data and unnecessary compute rather than assuming automation is sustainable.
SAP examples in the preserved source are discussion options, not a technology selection or a claim that configuration has been validated. Start with the business need. AI may summarize permitted evidence or draft a reminder; it cannot approve a consequential people decision, grant privilege or declare a payment complete.

## One build experiment: Decision Build Card

Synthetic inputs: Worker IT-P800; incident INC-800; entry 23:00–02:00 with timezone initially absent; project changes at midnight; break and on-call policy unknown; payroll export already queued.
Complete these fields once:
1. People and business need: who benefits, what could go wrong and why this matters in IT services or engineering.
2. Initial choice and reason: compare the choices before seeing the reveal.
3. Evidence reveal and revised choice: state what changed, and one counterexample.
4. Identity, date and source owner: identify authoritative records, effective time, provenance and uncertain facts.
5. Application responsibility: separate record keeping, orchestration, calculation, analytics and access enforcement.
6. Human approval and protected action: name the decision role, limits and worker challenge route.
7. Minimum data and permitted viewers: state purpose, retention question, client isolation and accessible communication.
8. Alternative and local variant: defend a common core with a justified regional/client variant, or explain a different design.
9. Exception owner and recovery: define hold, escalation, retry/reconciliation and confirmation conditions.
10. Measure and missing evidence: Corrected time events with explicit timezone, approval, version and distinct downstream status / events reviewed.
11. Pre-Foundation question: Define event time, timezone, schedule, absence, paid and billable time; establish correction ownership.
For each test below record input → expected → observed paper result → evidence → revision. Blank observations remain blank. A complete card can still contain a poor decision.

## Exactly three tabletop tests

OTS-HR08-T01 — Cross-midnight, timezone absent
Expected proposed behavior: Preserve the event and hold valuation until timezone/date boundaries are confirmed; do not guess duration or split.
Evidence to record: Record original event, timezone resolution and approved correction.
Observed result / evidence / revision: initially unfilled; learner completes during the exercise.

OTS-HR08-T02 — Approved time, billing disputed
Expected proposed behavior: Send only authorized payroll inputs; hold disputed billing separately under its contract owner.
Evidence to record: Record distinct attendance, pay-input and billing states.
Observed result / evidence / revision: initially unfilled; learner completes during the exercise.

OTS-HR08-T03 — Correction after export
Expected proposed behavior: Version the correction, reconcile the original export and prevent duplicate time/pay inputs.
Evidence to record: Record event key/version, target acknowledgement and recovery owner.
Observed result / evidence / revision: initially unfilled; learner completes during the exercise.
Acceptance: one complete card, three recorded tabletop observations after any revisions, and an explanation of source ownership and human authority. A breached authority or privacy boundary requires redesign. Publishing these tests does not execute them.
