## 3. Origins and process evolution

These closed windows are educational groupings, not universal invention dates or OmniCapital corporate biography. Adoption overlaps; paper, spreadsheets, ERP and cloud practices can coexist through the present.

| Teaching window | Process response and persistent question |
|---|---|
| 1970–1984 | Local rosters and attendance registers mix planned and worked time. |
| 1985–1994 | Clocking devices improve event capture but leave missed punches. |
| 1995–2004 | Time systems connect payroll; rule interpretation remains local. |
| 2005–2014 | Web time and absence requests improve visibility; approval and coverage remain separate. |
| 2015–2025 | Connected scheduling and analytics flag exceptions; timezone, wellbeing and privacy still matter. |

Counterexample: digitizing an incomplete record makes it easier to retrieve, not more trustworthy. Which decision was the original record intended to support?

## 4. One persona-led story

Omar Haddad (OCF-P10), CISO and Resilience Lead, coordinates a synthetic overnight cyber incident. Ravi Patel (OCF-P14), Payments and Treasury Operations Lead, needs the next shift covered. Nadia Fernandes (OCF-P07), Group CHRO, sees an incomplete time record and a proposed replacement who lacks confirmed role readiness.

Choose before revealing: Assume scheduled hours were worked, erase the incomplete record, or resolve evidence and coverage as separate decisions? Record your first reason on the card.

Evidence reveal: The synthetic incident log confirms work continued past midnight, but the exit event is missing. The schedule is not proof of actual hours. Nadia seeks an authorized correction preserving the original; Omar confirms incident evidence, while Ravi escalates replacement readiness instead of bypassing it.

Revise your decision: Separate schedule, actual time, approved correction, payable input and operational assignment. More tracking is not automatically better evidence; use the least intrusive method. Preserve an accessible human route to challenge errors. Financial service continuity matters, but an urgent cut-off does not create authority. Customer KYC and employee readiness are different processes; do not copy customer identity checks into HR without purpose and policy review.

## 5. Application responsibilities and human authority

Frame the business need first; applications implement the chosen operating model. The following is a proposed teaching architecture. Confirm named delegates, country policy, legal basis and actual product scope before implementation.

| Application responsibility | Accountable data owner | Human decision right |
|---|---|---|
| Time and absence; SAP Time examples | Time owner maintains events, schedules, corrections and absence balances | Delegated manager approves evidenced corrections under applicable local rules. |
| Operations scheduling | Operations owner maintains required service coverage and role readiness | Omar/Ravi’s designated authorities assign permitted duties; schedule entry does not grant access. |
| Payroll and privacy | Payroll owns accepted payable inputs; Privacy owner governs collection purpose | Payroll approver decides controlled adjustment; local specialists validate working-time and privacy requirements. |

Core HR owns workforce facts; domain owners own performance, learning, rewards, time or payroll facts as applicable. IAM executes access policy; integration transports and reconciles states; analytics reads authorized data and does not become its owner. AI may summarize permitted evidence or draft a reminder, but cannot approve consequential employment, regulated duties, payments or access. An interface acknowledgment is not proof of the business outcome. SAP examples illustrate responsibilities, not guaranteed features or universal localization.

## 7. One Decision Build Card

Synthetic input: FIN-P800; 31 October 22:00 to 1 November 06:00 in an explicitly chosen teaching timezone Asia/Kolkata; exit event missing; incident log available; payroll input already submitted. No universal overtime or rest rule assumed.

Keep all work in this one card. Capture: business problem; initial and revised choice with reason; entity/identity/date semantics; authoritative source and accountable owner; human approval boundary; one justified local variant with purpose/minimum data/viewers; known versus unknown downstream state; exception owner and approved fallback; all three test observations and evidence references. Missing evidence stays blank or unknown, never silently becomes approval.

Compare one alternative: a manual controlled handoff may be preferable to automation when exception ownership or recovery is absent. State the evidence needed to change your mind. Avoid collecting real screening, health, payroll, customer or conduct records in this learning card.

## 8. Exactly three tabletop tests

| Test | Input / reveal | Expected proposed behavior | Evidence to record |
|---|---|---|---|
| OCF-HR08-T01 — Cross-midnight ambiguity | Exit event is missing and the date changes during the shift. | Use explicit timezone/date semantics and corroborating evidence; do not copy scheduled hours as actual. | Original events, evidence, correction reason, approver and timestamps. |
| OCF-HR08-T02 — Unavailable replacement | A replacement is available but lacks confirmed role readiness. | Escalate coverage under approved continuity procedures; availability does not grant sensitive duties. | Coverage gap, readiness owner and permitted alternative. |
| OCF-HR08-T03 — Correction after submission | Approved time changes after payroll accepted the prior input. | Preserve versions and reconcile a controlled adjustment; do not silently overwrite the accepted payroll input. | Original/corrected totals, consumer state and adjustment reference. |

For each test, record input → expected → observed → revision/evidence → Pass, Revise or Blocked. All observed results are initially unfilled. Publication does not execute any tabletop, system test, approval or operational transaction. Acceptance requires a defensible card and all three observed tabletop results with revisions; bypassing a protected human decision requires redesign.

## 10. Separate AI-driven exploration: 2026–2050

Scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps.

| Scenario window | Question to explore |
|---|---|
| 2026–2030 — Assist | Could approved-source assistance explain evidence gaps and draft next steps without approving the decision? |
| 2031–2040 — Connect | Could separately authorized services coordinate this handoff while keeping unknown states and local rights visible? |
| 2041–2050 — Adapt | Could reviewed feedback propose reversible process changes while people retain consequential authority? |

Competing future: fragmented systems, uneven access, policy restrictions and manual review persist; a simpler controlled workflow outperforms orchestration. Falsifiable hypothesis question: Would more granular time capture reduce pay errors if corrections still reach payroll after the cut-off? Define an observation that would disprove your claim—for example fewer steps but more unresolved exceptions or worse worker outcomes. Compare against a manual baseline before claiming value.
