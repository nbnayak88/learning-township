## 3. Origins: the problem survives the medium

These closed historical teaching windows are educational groupings, not universal invention dates or OmniHealth's corporate biography. Adoption overlaps; paper and older systems can coexist through the present.

1970–1984 — Duty books and attendance registers coordinate local shifts; planned duty and worked time can diverge.

1985–1994 — Clocks and scheduling spreadsheets improve capture; missing events are easily mistaken for absence.

1995–2004 — Integrated time and payroll rules value approved time; a valid time entry does not prove sufficient care-team coverage.

2005–2014 — Mobile capture and scheduling interfaces improve availability; offline workers and overnight boundaries still create exceptions.

2015–2025 — Forecasting and assisted roster suggestions expose gaps; wellbeing, professional readiness and local approval cannot be inferred from a demand score.

Counterexample: a full roster can conceal unavailable or unqualified cover, while a missing clock event can conceal genuine work.

## 4. One story, one consequential choice

Dr. Amina Okafor (OHS-P07), Nursing and Care Coordination Director, receives a synthetic overnight shift exception. The roster shows a worker covering 22:00–06:00, but only an entry event is available. Dr. Arjun Mehta (OHS-P04), Chief Clinical Operations Officer, needs accountable service coverage. A proposed replacement appears available on a screen, yet current readiness and rest-rule evidence are missing.

Pause before the reveal. Choose invent the missing exit, treat the worker as absent, or investigate time separately from the authorized coverage decision. State who benefits, who bears the risk and what evidence you need.

Evidence reveal: the facility's time feed was unavailable overnight. The employee reports completing the shift, but no delegated approver has confirmed it. The replacement's calendar availability is not evidence of professional readiness or suitability for another shift.

Revised decision: Amina requests an approved time correction with worker input and preserves the original events. Arjun routes the coverage gap to the designated operational authority using the facility's approved contingency. Neither guesses a worked duration, pay premium, staffing ratio or rest limit; local rules remain discovery items. A learner may defend a different option if the card names its authority, evidence, trade-off and conditions for stopping. Missing evidence remains unknown; an urgent request does not manufacture approval.

## 5. Applications serve accountable people

Proposed responsibilities, subject to local discovery:

- Scheduling owns planned duty and approved coverage under operational leadership; the roster is not actual attendance or clinical permission.
- Time services capture events, absences and corrections; delegated supervisors approve actual-time evidence under the time owner.
- Credential/readiness owners confirm permitted work; operational authorities decide safe assignment and contingency coverage.
- Payroll owns valuation and payment treatment of approved time. Clinical escalation follows its designated operational route, not an HR help queue.

Across these boundaries, HR owns employment facts; designated clinical governance owns clinical privilege decisions; IAM administers approved access. Employment, learning completion, account access and permission for clinical work are distinct states. Share only the data needed for the declared purpose; keep patient records and detailed health information out of general HR learning cards. Omar Haddad (OHS-P10), CISO and Privacy Lead, governs privacy/access assurance; jurisdiction-specific applicability remains unresolved.

SAP examples illustrate responsibilities rather than prescribe a purchase: Time Tracking and Employee Central connected to workforce scheduling and payroll; time capture does not by itself deliver a clinical rostering solution. No feature availability, license entitlement, configuration, legal compliance or clinical conformance is established here. AI may draft a reminder or summarize permitted evidence; it cannot grant clinical privilege, make a high-impact employment decision or attest to payment or delivery.

## 7. One synthetic build experiment

Synthetic HC-E800; facility HP-A; local shift 22:00 on 31 October → 06:00 on 1 November 2026. Entry recorded, exit missing, facility timezone unspecified. Replacement HC-P801 availability shown but readiness unknown. No actual staffing decision.

Complete one Shift-Time Decision Build Card with these six fields:

1. Need and scope: Resolve missing time and operational coverage without converting uncertain records into absence, pay or permission. Explain the capability and value-stream step before naming a tool.
2. Facts and time: preserve identifiers, source, effective/as-of time, unknowns and approved policy version. Distinguish a source fact from your assumption.
3. Decision and ownership: Distinguish schedule, captured events, attested actual time, approved correction, valued time and authorized coverage. Record local timezone and applicable rules as unresolved until discovered. Record one alternative, its trade-off and the evidence that could favour it.
4. Handoff and recovery: Use the approved offline attestation/correction route if one exists; otherwise escalate the gap. Preserve original events and link every correction to its approver, effective period and payroll receipt. Name the accountable exception role, accessible human route and condition for reconciliation.
5. Local variant and data: choose one facility/language/role variation; justify minimum fields, purpose, owner, retention decision and viewers. Retain a non-digital route.
6. Evidence and learning: record T01–T03 input → expected → observed paper result → revision → reviewer question on this same card. Leave results unfilled until observed.

## 8. Exactly three tabletop tests

### OHS-HR08-T01 — Cross-midnight gap

Input/reveal: Entry on 31 October; exit missing after midnight.

Expected in the proposed design: Flag incomplete actual time; preserve both dates and timezone uncertainty; no invented eight-hour attendance or automatic absence.

Record: Original events, worker statement, approver and dated correction. Observed result and pass/revise remain unfilled until learners perform the tabletop.

### OHS-HR08-T02 — Available but not ready

Input/reveal: Replacement calendar is clear; readiness and rest evidence missing.

Expected in the proposed design: Do not release the assignment from availability alone; designated operational authority confirms conditions or invokes its approved contingency.

Record: Readiness questions, rule gaps and accountable coverage decision. Observed result and pass/revise remain unfilled until learners perform the tabletop.

### OHS-HR08-T03 — Correction after submission

Input/reveal: Approved time changes after payroll receives the earlier input.

Expected in the proposed design: Version and reconcile the correction with payroll; avoid overwriting the audit trail or assuming payment changed.

Record: Input versions, acknowledgement, owner and worker notice. Observed result and pass/revise remain unfilled until learners perform the tabletop.

Acceptance is a defensible completed card and an explanation of all three decisions after revision. A paper discussion proves neither a working system nor a safe clinical service. Publishing this lesson executes no tests.

## 10. AI-driven exploration: three scenarios

Scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps:
- 2026–2030 — Assist: An assistant highlights missing events and explains permitted correction steps.
- 2031–2040 — Connect: Governed services connect schedule, approved time, readiness and payroll acknowledgements.
- 2041–2050 — Adapt: A planner proposes alternative coverage under locally approved constraints and human operational authority.

Competing future: facilities retain locally governed workflows and modest deterministic tools because evidence quality, cost or worker trust makes broader automation unsuitable.

Falsification question: Would assisted scheduling reduce visible gaps while increasing fatigue concerns, unfair allocation or hidden correction work? State what observation would make you reduce, redesign or stop assistance. Human accountability does not disappear in any scenario.
