## 3. Origins: the problem survives the medium

These closed historical teaching windows are educational groupings, not universal invention dates or OmniHealth's corporate biography. Adoption overlaps; paper and older systems can coexist through the present.

1970–1984 — Staff returns and manual establishment lists answer how many posts exist; missing definitions weaken comparison.

1985–1994 — Personnel statistics and spreadsheets aggregate local counts; duplicate people and different dates distort totals.

1995–2004 — Data warehouses join HR, payroll and establishment records; grain and lineage determine what a measure means.

2005–2014 — Role-based dashboards connect talent, absence and learning; aggregation can hide small-cohort privacy risk.

2015–2025 — Predictive models and assisted narratives suggest shortages; uncertainty, case mix and decision authority still constrain action.

Counterexample: a rising headcount can coexist with reduced available hours, unmet skill needs and greater administrative burden.

## 4. One story, one consequential choice

Nina Fernandes (OHS-P06), Group CHRO, sees a synthetic dashboard reporting ten workforce records for a diagnostic service. Dr. Arjun Mehta (OHS-P04), Chief Clinical Operations Officer, asks whether that proves ten people are available for care coordination. Priya Nair (OHS-P09), Patient Identity and Data Governance Lead, asks the HR steward for the count definition; her enterprise identity role does not make her the owner of employee data.

Pause before the reveal. Choose treat records as staffing capacity, discard the dashboard, or reconcile the data grain and decision purpose before using it. State who benefits, who bears the risk and what evidence you need.

Evidence reveal: the dataset has ten employment rows for nine people; one person has two concurrent employments. Summed contracted FTE is 7.5 under a supplied teaching definition. Absence, roster overlap, current skills and professional readiness are unknown, so available service capacity cannot be calculated.

Revised decision: Nina publishes separate synthetic people, employment and contracted-FTE measures with as-of dates and lineage. Arjun withholds a staffing conclusion and asks the accountable scheduling/readiness owners for missing inputs. Priya advises on lineage and ambiguous linkage while the HR steward retains workforce-data ownership. A learner may defend a different option if the card names its authority, evidence, trade-off and conditions for stopping. Missing evidence remains unknown; an urgent request does not manufacture approval.

## 5. Applications serve accountable people

Proposed responsibilities, subject to local discovery:

- Core HR owns person/employment/contract facts; the HR data steward defines deduplication and effective-time rules.
- Analytics owns semantic definitions, transformation lineage, access controls and reproducible calculations under named business metric owners.
- Scheduling, Time, Learning and professional-readiness owners supply their own approved context; no single count proves service capacity.
- Operational leaders own staffing decisions; HR owns workforce actions. Privacy/security governs disclosure, especially where small cohorts make individuals identifiable.

Across these boundaries, HR owns employment facts; designated clinical governance owns clinical privilege decisions; IAM administers approved access. Employment, learning completion, account access and permission for clinical work are distinct states. Share only the data needed for the declared purpose; keep patient records and detailed health information out of general HR learning cards. Omar Haddad (OHS-P10), CISO and Privacy Lead, governs privacy/access assurance; jurisdiction-specific applicability remains unresolved.

SAP examples illustrate responsibilities rather than prescribe a purchase: People Analytics with governed Employee Central, Time and Learning inputs; a dashboard is a decision aid rather than a care-capacity authorization. No feature availability, license entitlement, configuration, legal compliance or clinical conformance is established here. AI may draft a reminder or summarize permitted evidence; it cannot grant clinical privilege, make a high-impact employment decision or attest to payment or delivery.

## 7. One synthetic build experiment

Synthetic dataset HC-D900: ten employment rows, nine person identifiers, supplied contracted FTE sum 7.5 as of 31 October 2026. One concurrent employment; no observed availability or patient-level data. A two-person cohort is proposed for attrition reporting.

Complete one Workforce Insight Build Card with these six fields:

1. Need and scope: Answer a precise workforce question with reproducible definitions while resisting a misleading capacity inference. Explain the capability and value-stream step before naming a tool.
2. Facts and time: preserve identifiers, source, effective/as-of time, unknowns and approved policy version. Distinguish a source fact from your assumption.
3. Decision and ownership: Define each measure's grain, population, unit, as-of time, numerator/denominator and source owner; show what can and cannot be inferred. Record one alternative, its trade-off and the evidence that could favour it.
4. Handoff and recovery: Quarantine ambiguous joins and record exclusions. Correct the data product with a versioned notice; restrict or suppress small-cohort detail under an approved disclosure policy rather than inventing a universal threshold. Name the accountable exception role, accessible human route and condition for reconciliation.
5. Local variant and data: choose one facility/language/role variation; justify minimum fields, purpose, owner, retention decision and viewers. Retain a non-digital route.
6. Evidence and learning: record T01–T03 input → expected → observed paper result → revision → reviewer question on this same card. Leave results unfilled until observed.

## 8. Exactly three tabletop tests

### OHS-HR09-T01 — Count reconciliation

Input/reveal: Ten employment rows represent nine people and 7.5 contracted FTE.

Expected in the proposed design: Report the three different measures with their definitions; do not call any of them available clinical capacity.

Record: Reproducible counting logic, lineage and as-of date. Observed result and pass/revise remain unfilled until learners perform the tabletop.

### OHS-HR09-T02 — Small-cohort disclosure

Input/reveal: Viewer requests individual attrition risk for a two-person unit.

Expected in the proposed design: Do not expose individual risk from the aggregate view; apply approved purpose/access and disclosure rules, or hold for privacy review.

Record: Viewer purpose, policy gap, permitted aggregation and review decision. Observed result and pass/revise remain unfilled until learners perform the tabletop.

### OHS-HR09-T03 — Forecast uncertainty

Input/reveal: Narrative claims a staffing surplus using contracted FTE alone.

Expected in the proposed design: Reject the definitive capacity claim; list absent scheduling, skill, readiness and demand assumptions; show a conditional question instead.

Record: Assumption register, uncertainty and accountable staffing owner. Observed result and pass/revise remain unfilled until learners perform the tabletop.

Acceptance is a defensible completed card and an explanation of all three decisions after revision. A paper discussion proves neither a working system nor a safe clinical service. Publishing this lesson executes no tests.

## 10. AI-driven exploration: three scenarios

Scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps:
- 2026–2030 — Assist: An assistant explains metric definitions and flags missing denominators.
- 2031–2040 — Connect: Governed data products connect workforce facts to approved operational context.
- 2041–2050 — Adapt: A bounded scenario tool proposes staffing hypotheses with uncertainty and drift review; leaders validate before action.

Competing future: facilities retain locally governed workflows and modest deterministic tools because evidence quality, cost or worker trust makes broader automation unsuitable.

Falsification question: Would assisted insight cause more unsupported capacity claims or expose small groups more often than a simpler governed report? State what observation would make you reduce, redesign or stop assistance. Human accountability does not disappear in any scenario.
