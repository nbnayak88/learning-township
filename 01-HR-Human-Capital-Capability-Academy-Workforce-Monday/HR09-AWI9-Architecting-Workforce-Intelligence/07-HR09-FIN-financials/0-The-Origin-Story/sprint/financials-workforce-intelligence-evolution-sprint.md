## 4. One persona-led story

David Morgan (OCF-P02), Group CFO, sees 12 in one synthetic workforce report and 10 in another. Marcus Okoro (OCF-P08), Financial Crime Operations Director, requests more investigators. Nadia Fernandes (OCF-P07), Group CHRO, must explain what the figures count before discussing capacity.

Choose before revealing: Choose the larger number, average both, or reconcile people, employments and available capacity? Record your first reason on the card.

Evidence reveal: Ten synthetic people hold twelve employment records; two concurrent records do not create two extra people. Available investigation capacity is still unknown because role readiness, leave and case complexity differ. David withdraws a headcount-only conclusion and asks for a metric dictionary and bounded planning assumptions.

Revise your decision: Agree on the decision and denominator before building the dashboard. Headcount, employment records, FTE and workload capacity answer different questions. Preserve an accessible human route to challenge errors. Financial service continuity matters, but an urgent cut-off does not create authority. Customer KYC and employee readiness are different processes; do not copy customer identity checks into HR without purpose and policy review.

## 5. Application responsibilities and human authority

Frame the business need first; applications implement the chosen operating model. The following is a proposed teaching architecture. Confirm named delegates, country policy, legal basis and actual product scope before implementation.

| Application responsibility | Accountable data owner | Human decision right |
|---|---|---|
| Analytics; People Analytics example | Metric owner defines measure/cohort; HR stewards own workforce source facts | Analyst explains lineage and uncertainty; business owner decides staffing. |
| Operational workload | Marcus’s operations team owns workload, service and readiness evidence | Operations validates planning assumptions; an analytics forecast does not authorize hiring. |
| Privacy and access | Data owner and Privacy/Security roles control permitted views | Authorized reviewers approve sensitive use; do not expose tiny cohorts or individual risk scores by default. |

Core HR owns workforce facts; domain owners own performance, learning, rewards, time or payroll facts as applicable. IAM executes access policy; integration transports and reconciles states; analytics reads authorized data and does not become its owner. AI may summarize permitted evidence or draft a reminder, but cannot approve consequential employment, regulated duties, payments or access. An interface acknowledgment is not proof of the business outcome. SAP examples illustrate responsibilities, not guaranteed features or universal localization.

## 7. One Decision Build Card

Synthetic input: Synthetic extract: 10 unique person IDs and 12 employment rows; person-level contracted FTE requires reviewed allocation; tiny two-person specialist subgroup; case workload inputs incomplete. These are not OmniCapital staff counts.

Keep all work in this one card. Capture: business problem; initial and revised choice with reason; entity/identity/date semantics; authoritative source and accountable owner; human approval boundary; one justified local variant with purpose/minimum data/viewers; known versus unknown downstream state; exception owner and approved fallback; all three test observations and evidence references. Missing evidence stays blank or unknown, never silently becomes approval.

Compare one alternative: a manual controlled handoff may be preferable to automation when exception ownership or recovery is absent. State the evidence needed to change your mind. Avoid collecting real screening, health, payroll, customer or conduct records in this learning card.

## 8. Exactly three tabletop tests

| Test | Input / reveal | Expected proposed behavior | Evidence to record |
|---|---|---|---|
| OCF-HR09-T01 — People versus employments | Dashboard A counts 12 employment rows; B counts 10 people. | Reconcile by declared grain; do not average or call either a headcount without definition. | Person/employment keys, cohort, as-of date and definition. |
| OCF-HR09-T02 — Small-cohort disclosure | A two-person specialist group appears in a sensitive drill-down. | Restrict or aggregate using approved policy; totals must not reveal suppressed individuals indirectly. | Disclosure route, audience, minimum-data decision and residual inference risk. |
| OCF-HR09-T03 — Uncertain capacity forecast | Forecast assumes every listed worker is available and equally productive. | Label assumptions and missing readiness/leave/complexity; compare alternatives before requesting staffing. | Input lineage, uncertainty range and disconfirming evidence. |

For each test, record input → expected → observed → revision/evidence → Pass, Revise or Blocked. All observed results are initially unfilled. Publication does not execute any tabletop, system test, approval or operational transaction. Acceptance requires a defensible card and all three observed tabletop results with revisions; bypassing a protected human decision requires redesign.

## 10. Separate AI-driven exploration: 2026–2050

Scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps.

| Scenario window | Question to explore |
|---|---|
| 2026–2030 — Assist | Could approved-source assistance explain evidence gaps and draft next steps without approving the decision? |
| 2031–2040 — Connect | Could separately authorized services coordinate this handoff while keeping unknown states and local rights visible? |
| 2041–2050 — Adapt | Could reviewed feedback propose reversible process changes while people retain consequential authority? |

Competing future: fragmented systems, uneven access, policy restrictions and manual review persist; a simpler controlled workflow outperforms orchestration. Falsifiable hypothesis question: Would faster dashboard refresh improve staffing decisions if workload and readiness definitions remain incomplete? Define an observation that would disprove your claim—for example fewer steps but more unresolved exceptions or worse worker outcomes. Compare against a manual baseline before claiming value.
