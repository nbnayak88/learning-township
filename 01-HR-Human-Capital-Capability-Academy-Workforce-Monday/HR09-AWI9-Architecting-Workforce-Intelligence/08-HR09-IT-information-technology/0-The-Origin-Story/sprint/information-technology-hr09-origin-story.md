## One story: choose, reveal, revise

Mei Lin (OTS-P09), Data Governance and Knowledge Lead, reviews a synthetic cloud-capacity dashboard. David Chen (OTS-P02), Group CFO, asks why employee count differs from project staffing. Nadia Fernandes (OTS-P07), Group CHRO, cautions against treating a skills forecast as an individual employment decision.
Choose before reading on: Use the larger count, average all totals, or reconcile definitions and lineage before recommending a staffing action?
Evidence reveal: The dashboard counted assignments rather than distinct people: IT-P900 has two assignments. It also displayed a tiny team's attrition indicator. Mei separates person, employment, assignment and FTE measures, restricts the small-cohort view, and presents capacity assumptions as a range with missing evidence visible.
Write what changed your decision and what remains unknown. An accessible explanation and a human challenge route must be available to the affected worker. An uncertain record cannot silently become an authorization.

## Application responsibility and human authority

Core HR — HR owns person/employment and contracted work facts. HR steward validates population and effective dates.
Project / learning sources — Project owner owns allocation; learning owner owns assessed skills. Source owners validate freshness and scope.
Analytics / data product — Data owner governs metric definitions, lineage and access. Analyst explains uncertainty; privacy owner reviews disclosure.
Workforce planning — Planning owner owns demand assumptions. Accountable leader makes staffing decisions with human review.
Business owners remain accountable; applications store and process the records. Discover the actual delegated approvers before implementation. Restrict client, candidate, pay and health information to its purpose and permitted viewers. Minimize replicated data and unnecessary compute rather than assuming automation is sustainable.
SAP examples in the preserved source are discussion options, not a technology selection or a claim that configuration has been validated. Start with the business need. AI may summarize permitted evidence or draft a reminder; it cannot approve a consequential people decision, grant privilege or declare a payment complete.

## One build experiment: Decision Build Card

Synthetic inputs: Synthetic person IT-P900 has two assignments of 0.5 FTE each; person IT-P901 has one assignment of 1.0 FTE; no contractors; distinct people 2, assignments 3, contracted FTE assumed 2.0 solely for this exercise.
Complete these fields once:
1. People and business need: who benefits, what could go wrong and why this matters in IT services or engineering.
2. Initial choice and reason: compare the choices before seeing the reveal.
3. Evidence reveal and revised choice: state what changed, and one counterexample.
4. Identity, date and source owner: identify authoritative records, effective time, provenance and uncertain facts.
5. Application responsibility: separate record keeping, orchestration, calculation, analytics and access enforcement.
6. Human approval and protected action: name the decision role, limits and worker challenge route.
7. Minimum data and permitted viewers: state purpose, retention question, client isolation and accessible communication.
8. Alternative and local variant: defend a common core with a justified regional/client variant, or explain a different design.
9. Exception owner and recovery: define hold, escalation, retry/reconciliation and confirmation conditions.
10. Measure and missing evidence: Displayed metrics with explicit population, denominator, date, lineage and privacy approval / metrics reviewed.
11. Pre-Foundation question: Define person count, employment count, assignments, FTE and reporting date; write one metric contract.
For each test below record input → expected → observed paper result → evidence → revision. Blank observations remain blank. A complete card can still contain a poor decision.

## Exactly three tabletop tests

OTS-HR09-T01 — Person versus assignment count
Expected proposed behavior: Report 2 distinct people and 3 assignments; reconcile the assumed 2.0 FTE without conflating measures.
Evidence to record: Record keys, denominator and lineage.
Observed result / evidence / revision: initially unfilled; learner completes during the exercise.

OTS-HR09-T02 — Tiny cohort
Expected proposed behavior: Suppress or aggregate identifying insight under an explicit privacy rule; prevent inference from adjacent views.
Evidence to record: Record audience, disclosure risk and owner decision.
Observed result / evidence / revision: initially unfilled; learner completes during the exercise.

OTS-HR09-T03 — Stale skill forecast
Expected proposed behavior: Show uncertainty and freshness gaps; compare a competing demand assumption without individual automated action.
Evidence to record: Record sensitivity, missing inputs and decision boundary.
Observed result / evidence / revision: initially unfilled; learner completes during the exercise.
Acceptance: one complete card, three recorded tabletop observations after any revisions, and an explanation of source ownership and human authority. A breached authority or privacy boundary requires redesign. Publishing these tests does not execute them.
