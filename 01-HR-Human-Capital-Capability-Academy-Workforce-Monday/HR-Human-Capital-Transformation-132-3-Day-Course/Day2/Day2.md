### Day 2 V2: Concept 1: Workforce Core: Hire to Record
*Duration: 12-15 min*

**Hook:** One person, one record, from candidate to employee.

Did you share your notes in the previous video? [pause] If not, pause here, drop them in the comments, then come back. Sharing is how learning becomes yours.

Welcome back. Today's concept is **Workforce Core: Hire to Record**. It anchors two product academies: AWF1 Employee Central and ATA2 Recruiting (SmartRecruiters) & Onboarding. Together with the 11 GICS sectors that is 22 of the 132 courses in our meta anchor file, for example AWF1-EN, AWF1-MA, AWF1-IN ... (22 courses in this concept). [show slide: concept map]

**1. What is the concept?**
The Workforce Core is the single trusted employee record (Employee Central) fed by a clean front door (Recruiting and Onboarding). Every other human capital process reads from it. Think of it as the foundation slab of a building: nobody admires it, but everything stands on it. [pause]

**2. Why is it important?**
If the record is wrong, every downstream decision is wrong: pay, performance, planning, compliance. Most failed HR transformations did not fail on fancy modules; they failed because org structure, job data and hiring data were inconsistent from day one.
Here is a real pattern I have seen. A utilities client had four spreadsheets for headcount and none matched. After modelling the org once in Employee Central and hiring straight into it, the monthly headcount debate ended. The CHRO said, 'For the first time we argued about decisions, not numbers.' [pause]

**3. Benefits of implementing it the right way**
When you get this right, here is what changes: One source of truth across 11 sectors and every country entity; Faster, cleaner hire-to-start with no re-keying; Audit-ready data lineage and fewer payroll errors; A stable base that lets you add talent, pay and AI modules without rework. [on screen: four benefits]

**Sector lens.** Sector lens: in Energy the core must model crews, sites and certifications; in Financial Services it must model regulated roles and licences. Same architecture, different data.

**1. What is the concept?**
Compensation and Variable Pay designs fair, strategy-aligned reward. Learning and the Opportunity Marketplace builds capability and matches people to gigs, projects and roles. [pause]

**2. Why is it important?**
Reward tells people what matters; learning gives them the means to deliver it. Run separately, you pay for skills you have not built and train for skills you do not reward.
Here is a real pattern I have seen. A consumer staples company cut external hiring for analytics roles by a third after its marketplace matched warehouse planners to short analytics projects, and its pay bands recognised the new skills. [pause]

**3. Benefits of implementing it the right way**
When you get this right, here is what changes: Transparent, defensible pay decisions; Variable pay tied to real outcomes; Skills built where the business needs them; Internal mobility instead of external hiring cost. [on screen: four benefits]
