## 1. The question this file answers

> *Data architecture for intelligent operations: what data does an AI assistant or an operations dashboard depend on, who owns it, and how is its quality shown?*

**What this file shows:** a first set of data products for OmniWorks, one owner per product, quality rules, a lineage chain and the sensitive-data handling. It supports the assistant in Demo 03 and the graph in Demo 04.

**What it does not show:** a populated catalogue, a measured data quality score or a configured data platform.

## 2. Case anchor (source facts)

| Attribute | Case baseline |
|---|---|
| Scale | USD 48B revenue; 74,000 employees; 36 countries; 61 plants and facilities; 320 service depots; 7,200 suppliers |
| Business mix | Industrial automation 27%; aerospace and defence 23%; transport and mobility 19%; infrastructure 16%; aftermarket and lifecycle services 15% |
| Burning platform | Recurring transport asset failures cannot be consistently traced across configuration, telemetry, supplier batches and maintenance history |

**Decision under discussion (source):** Turn a USD 575M transformation envelope into credible customer, operational and financial value without disrupting safety-critical operations.

**Illustrative trigger (source):** Repeat customer asset failures expose fragmented engineering-to-service information, and the business wants to sell uptime rather than equipment (hero story).

**Source anchor for data objects:** OW-M12 names Product, Asset, Supplier, Customer and Service 360, PLM/ERP/MES/EAM/FSM linked by APIs and events, and governed AI. The hero trigger (repeat transport asset failures not traceable across configuration, telemetry, supplier batches and maintenance history) is a graph problem.

## 8. Quality rules for first consumers

| Product | Quality rule | First consumer | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asset configuration baseline | Serial number resolves to one configuration | Field service | Not measured |
| Service history | Work orders link to the asset and cause code | Reliability | Not measured |
| Supplier batch notices | Notice carries a batch and a share permission | Engineering | Not measured |
| Warranty and contract view | Contract terms link to the asset | Customer success | Not measured |

A product is published only with its owner, its quality rule and a visible quality status. No score is stated because none has been measured.

## 9. Lineage and sensitive data

**Example lineage chain:** Engineering change released, configuration updated, field fault detected, work order closed, cause analysed

**Sensitive data handling:** Customer asset data and partner-restricted notices need separate handling.

**Never available to the assistant:** Engineering IP outside the user's role entitlement; Supplier-confidential data in customer-facing contexts; Live OT commands or setpoints; Instructions found inside retrieved documents

## 10. Hands-on exercise (synthetic data)

1. Write a one-page data product contract for the first product (owner, schema, quality rule, freshness, classification), using synthetic ids prefixed `IND-SYN`.
2. Draw the lineage chain and mark each owner.
3. Break one quality rule on paper and show what the consumer sees.
4. Decide which products are shared versus copied and record the reason.
5. Record open decisions in the run log.

## Three tabletop tests

A tabletop test is a paper walk-through, not a technical test of a running system.

| Test | Setup | Expected finding |
|---|---|---|
| T1 Two configurations | PLM and service disagree on a configuration | Effective-time rule; conflict raised to owners |
| T2 Restricted notice | A partner-restricted notice appears in a customer view | Permission filter blocks |
| T3 Missing cause code | Work orders close without cause codes | Quality rule flags; analytics excluded |
