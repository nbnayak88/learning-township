## 1. Start with the story

A data platform for a store-and-digital change at a consumer brand business goes live, and two reports of the same measure differ.

**Transformation lead:** Mateo Silva — Supply Chain and Responsible Sourcing Lead (OVC-P14).  
**Counterpart:** Farah Ahmed — CISO and Privacy Lead (OVC-P13).  
**Business or data owner:** Priya Shah — Director Enterprise Architecture (OVC-P11).

Mateo Silva asks what must be true before the team decides. Farah Ahmed asks which evidence and owner support the decision. Priya Shah asks for the outcome that matters to the people affected.

**Evidence reveal:** In the synthetic data, the two reports define the measure differently, and ownership of the source is unclear.

**Pause:** What must be true for this decision to stand? Which evidence is missing, and who can confirm it? Ask the counterpart one discovery question before choosing a solution.

**Case boundary:** OmniVibe Consumer Ltd. is an aspirational case study (USD 28B consumer group; 72,000 employees; 48 countries). These incidents, services and dialogue are proposed learning scenarios, not corporate history, confirmed deployments or achieved results. Preserve the enterprise persona IDs above. Country-specific permissions, contracts and operating rules require validation; this sprint does not invent them.

## 2. Trace the origin: 1970–2026

These overlapping periods are teaching lenses, not invention dates or a universal adoption sequence. Earlier practices can coexist with newer ones. Pre-1978 scenes describe the wider domain, not OmniVibe Consumer Ltd. history.

- **1970s–1980s:** Reports are produced by each department.
- **1980s–1990s:** Data warehouses consolidate data; definitions differ.
- **2000s–2010s:** Data platforms and governance programmes spread; ownership is unclear.
- **2020s–2026:** Assisted analytics speeds insight; definitions, ownership and quality rules still need people.

**Mindset shift:** start with the decision, its evidence and its owner, then choose the tool or programme. Digitizing an unclear decision can accelerate the same mistake.

**Pause:** Which old problem survives in a newer interface? Who bears its cost?

## 3. Explore possible futures: 2030–2050

**2030 experiment:** allow assistance to draft options and flag gaps; retain the evidence gate, a named decision owner and a challenge route.

**2040 question:** if the enterprise and its partners share more signals, can each decision carry its evidence and owner without losing trust or choice?

**2050 scene:** Imagine an enterprise that adjusts within agreed boundaries. Providers may act faster, but people must own the purpose, the trade-offs and the right to stop.

**Competing future:** A simpler, well-owned way of working with trained people may beat a larger transformation on reliability, cost and trust.

All future passages are design hypotheses, not forecasts or product roadmaps. Test a useful part today rather than assume the future will arrive as described.

## 4. Draw the smallest useful architecture

**Capability — OVC-BTM06-OS-CAP01:** trusted shared measure. A capability is an ability; it is not an application or a sequence of steps.

**Value stages — OVC-BTM06-OS-VS01:** Define measure → assign source owner → reconcile → publish → use → review.

**Objects to distinguish:** measure, definition, source, owner and quality rule.

**Proposed responsibility:** The data owner owns the source; analytics owns the definition; the business owns the decision. BTM06 owns the measure decision, not the platform build.

**Protected boundary:** A live data platform does not make two differing numbers right.

Transformation, architecture and platform applications are optional demonstration candidates, chosen after requirements. No product is assumed installed, licensed or able to cover the whole programme. This sprint focuses on application responsibility while referencing data, integration and security owners.

## 5. Build one Decision Card

Use synthetic case **OVC-BTM06-OS-SYN001**. Synthetic measure CD-MS300, reports CD-RP300 and CD-RP301, source owner unclear. Keep everything on one card or screen.

1. **Need:** the outcome, affected people and one missing fact.
2. **Evidence:** relevant object IDs, source, validity or effective time, data purpose and permitted access.
3. **Ownership:** business decision owner, authoritative application and receiving owner.
4. **Gate:** prerequisite, allowed next state, authorized actor and the message anyone affected will see.
5. **Alternative:** Compare publishing both reports with agreeing one definition and owner.
6. **Exception:** one failure state, escalation owner and a safe recovery or assisted route.
7. **Proof:** the three results below, one measure and a stop, revise or proceed decision.

**Requirement — OVC-BTM06-OS-REQ01:** link one shared-measure experiment hypothesis to an owned intervention and observed follow-up, including negative evidence.

**Decision — OVC-BTM06-OS-ADR01:** record your chosen option and the alternative from item 5, one trade-off, and the evidence that would change your choice.

## 6. Run three tabletop tests

### T01 — Two reports define one measure differently

**Expected:** Agree a definition and a named owner.

### T02 — A source has no accountable owner

**Expected:** Assign one before the data is used for decisions.

### T03 — A quality rule blocks a needed load

**Expected:** Escalate to the owner; do not bypass the rule.

For each, record **input → expected → actual paper result → evidence note → revision → retest**. These are sprint-local tests OVC-BTM06-OS-T01/T02/T03, supporting OVC-BTM06-OS-REQ01. A completed lesson is not an executed tenant test.

**Measure — OVC-BTM06-OS-KPI01:** Share of published measures with one definition, source owner and quality rule; define window and exclusions. Assign Mateo Silva as proposed measure owner, establish the baseline and exclusions, and review after the experiment. No numeric improvement is asserted.

**Acceptance:** the card identifies source and owner, preserves the protected boundary, handles all three tests after documented revisions and explains the decision in 60 seconds. Missing evidence remains visible. Record what is still unverified.

## 7. Use your 120 minutes

- **0–20:** read the origin timeline; explain what changed and what did not.
- **20–45:** role-play the story; ask the lead, counterpart and voice one useful question each.
- **45–65:** draw responsibilities and consult one resource selectively.
- **65–100:** complete the single Decision Card and compare the alternative.
- **100–115:** run the three tests; revise from observed paper behavior.
- **115–120:** explain the decision in 60 seconds and choose the next experiment.

All reading fits within these blocks. Do not complete an entire product course during the sprint.
