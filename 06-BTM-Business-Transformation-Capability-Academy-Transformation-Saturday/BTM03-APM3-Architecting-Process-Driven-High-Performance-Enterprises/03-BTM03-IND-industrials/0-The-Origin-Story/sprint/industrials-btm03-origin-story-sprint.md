## 1. Start with the story

A redesigned process for a service-model change at an industrial business unit shows faster cycle time, while cases still stall at one handoff.

**Transformation lead:** Ravi Deshpande — VP Manufacturing, Pune Operations (OW-P10).  
**Counterpart:** Hannah Fischer — Director, Enterprise Architecture (OW-P11).  
**Business or data owner:** Dr. Leila Haddad — Head of Data Governance & AI (OW-P15).

Ravi Deshpande asks what must be true before the team decides. Hannah Fischer asks which evidence and owner support the decision. Dr. Leila Haddad asks for the outcome that matters to the people affected.

**Evidence reveal:** In the synthetic record, the cycle time excludes waiting between teams, and the handoff has no accountable receiver.

**Pause:** What must be true for this decision to stand? Which evidence is missing, and who can confirm it? Ask the counterpart one discovery question before choosing a solution.

**Case boundary:** OmniWorks Industrial Systems Ltd. is an aspirational case study (USD 48B revenue; 74,000 employees; 36 countries; 61 plants and facilities; 320 service depots; 7,200 suppliers). These incidents, services and dialogue are proposed learning scenarios, not corporate history, confirmed deployments or achieved results. Preserve the enterprise persona IDs above. Country-specific permissions, contracts and operating rules require validation; this sprint does not invent them.

## 2. Trace the origin: 1970–2026

These overlapping periods are teaching lenses, not invention dates or a universal adoption sequence. Earlier practices can coexist with newer ones. Pre-1978 scenes describe the wider domain, not OmniWorks Industrial Systems Ltd. history.

- **1970s–1980s:** Work flows by habit between departments.
- **1980s–1990s:** Process re-engineering and quality methods spread.
- **2000s–2010s:** Process mining and workflow tools expose steps; handoffs stay unowned.
- **2020s–2026:** Assisted analysis speeds discovery; handoff ownership and exceptions still need people.

**Mindset shift:** start with the decision, its evidence and its owner, then choose the tool or programme. Digitizing an unclear decision can accelerate the same mistake.

**Pause:** Which old problem survives in a newer interface? Who bears its cost?

## 3. Explore possible futures: 2030–2050

**2030 experiment:** allow assistance to draft options and flag gaps; retain the evidence gate, a named decision owner and a challenge route.

**2040 question:** if the enterprise and its partners share more signals, can each decision carry its evidence and owner without losing trust or choice?

**2050 scene:** Imagine an enterprise that adjusts within agreed boundaries. Providers may act faster, but people must own the purpose, the trade-offs and the right to stop.

**Competing future:** A simpler, well-owned way of working with trained people may beat a larger transformation on reliability, cost and trust.

All future passages are design hypotheses, not forecasts or product roadmaps. Test a useful part today rather than assume the future will arrive as described.

## 4. Draw the smallest useful architecture

**Capability — OW-BTM03-OS-CAP01:** end-to-end process performance. A capability is an ability; it is not an application or a sequence of steps.

**Value stages — OW-BTM03-OS-VS01:** Map process → measure end to end → fix handoffs → assign owners → monitor → improve.

**Objects to distinguish:** process, handoff, cycle time, exception and owner.

**Proposed responsibility:** A process owner owns the end-to-end result; each team owns its step; the receiver owns acknowledgment. BTM03 owns the process decision, not the workflow tool.

**Protected boundary:** Faster steps are not a better process if waiting and handoffs are not measured.

Transformation, architecture and platform applications are optional demonstration candidates, chosen after requirements. No product is assumed installed, licensed or able to cover the whole programme. This sprint focuses on application responsibility while referencing data, integration and security owners.

## 5. Build one Decision Card

Use synthetic case **OW-BTM03-OS-SYN001**. Synthetic process IND-PR300, handoff IND-HO300, waiting time excluded from cycle time. Keep everything on one card or screen.

1. **Need:** the outcome, affected people and one missing fact.
2. **Evidence:** relevant object IDs, source, validity or effective time, data purpose and permitted access.
3. **Ownership:** business decision owner, authoritative application and receiving owner.
4. **Gate:** prerequisite, allowed next state, authorized actor and the message anyone affected will see.
5. **Alternative:** Compare measuring step time with measuring the end-to-end case.
6. **Exception:** one failure state, escalation owner and a safe recovery or assisted route.
7. **Proof:** the three results below, one measure and a stop, revise or proceed decision.

**Requirement — OW-BTM03-OS-REQ01:** link one process-handoff experiment hypothesis to an owned intervention and observed follow-up, including negative evidence.

**Decision — OW-BTM03-OS-ADR01:** record your chosen option and the alternative from item 5, one trade-off, and the evidence that would change your choice.

## 6. Run three tabletop tests

### T01 — Cycle time excludes waiting between teams

**Expected:** Measure end-to-end including waiting.

### T02 — A handoff has no accountable receiver

**Expected:** Name the receiver and an acknowledgment step.

### T03 — An exception path is undocumented

**Expected:** Document it and name its owner.

For each, record **input → expected → actual paper result → evidence note → revision → retest**. These are sprint-local tests OW-BTM03-OS-T01/T02/T03, supporting OW-BTM03-OS-REQ01. A completed lesson is not an executed tenant test.

**Measure — OW-BTM03-OS-KPI01:** End-to-end cycle time including waiting, with defined population and exclusions. Assign Ravi Deshpande as proposed measure owner, establish the baseline and exclusions, and review after the experiment. No numeric improvement is asserted.

**Acceptance:** the card identifies source and owner, preserves the protected boundary, handles all three tests after documented revisions and explains the decision in 60 seconds. Missing evidence remains visible. Record what is still unverified.

## 7. Use your 120 minutes

- **0–20:** read the origin timeline; explain what changed and what did not.
- **20–45:** role-play the story; ask the lead, counterpart and voice one useful question each.
- **45–65:** draw responsibilities and consult one resource selectively.
- **65–100:** complete the single Decision Card and compare the alternative.
- **100–115:** run the three tests; revise from observed paper behavior.
- **115–120:** explain the decision in 60 seconds and choose the next experiment.

All reading fits within these blocks. Do not complete an entire product course during the sprint.
