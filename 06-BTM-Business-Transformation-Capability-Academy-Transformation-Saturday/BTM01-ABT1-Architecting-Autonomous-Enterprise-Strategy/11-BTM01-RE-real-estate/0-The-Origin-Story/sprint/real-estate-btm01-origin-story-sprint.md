## 1. Start with the story

Leadership of a property group approves a strategy for a portfolio-operations change, and teams ask which current initiatives it replaces.

**Transformation lead:** David Chen — Group CFO (OSP-P02).  
**Counterpart:** Daniel Weber — Enterprise Architecture Director (OSP-P10).  
**Business or data owner:** Mei Lin — Enterprise Data and ESG Lead (OSP-P08).

David Chen asks what must be true before the team decides. Daniel Weber asks which evidence and owner support the decision. Mei Lin asks for the outcome that matters to the people affected.

**Evidence reveal:** In the synthetic portfolio, every existing initiative is relabelled as strategic, and no trade-off or stop decision is recorded.

**Pause:** What must be true for this decision to stand? Which evidence is missing, and who can confirm it? Ask the counterpart one discovery question before choosing a solution.

**Case boundary:** OmniSpace Real Estate Ltd. is an aspirational case study (no enterprise numerical profile, budget or quantified benefit is supplied). These incidents, services and dialogue are proposed learning scenarios, not corporate history, confirmed deployments or achieved results. Preserve the enterprise persona IDs above. Country-specific permissions, contracts and operating rules require validation; this sprint does not invent them.

## 2. Trace the origin: 1970–2026

These overlapping periods are teaching lenses, not invention dates or a universal adoption sequence. Earlier practices can coexist with newer ones. Pre-1978 scenes describe the wider domain, not OmniSpace Real Estate Ltd. history.

- **1970s–1980s:** Strategy is set by senior judgement.
- **1980s–1990s:** Strategic planning cycles formalize goals; initiatives accumulate.
- **2000s–2010s:** Portfolio and balanced-scorecard methods spread; trade-offs stay implicit.
- **2020s–2026:** Assisted scenario work speeds analysis; trade-offs and owners still decide.

**Mindset shift:** start with the decision, its evidence and its owner, then choose the tool or programme. Digitizing an unclear decision can accelerate the same mistake.

**Pause:** Which old problem survives in a newer interface? Who bears its cost?

## 3. Explore possible futures: 2030–2050

**2030 experiment:** allow assistance to draft options and flag gaps; retain the evidence gate, a named decision owner and a challenge route.

**2040 question:** if the enterprise and its partners share more signals, can each decision carry its evidence and owner without losing trust or choice?

**2050 scene:** Imagine an enterprise that adjusts within agreed boundaries. Providers may act faster, but people must own the purpose, the trade-offs and the right to stop.

**Competing future:** A simpler, well-owned way of working with trained people may beat a larger transformation on reliability, cost and trust.

All future passages are design hypotheses, not forecasts or product roadmaps. Test a useful part today rather than assume the future will arrive as described.

## 4. Draw the smallest useful architecture

**Capability — OSP-BTM01-OS-CAP01:** explicit strategy trade-offs. A capability is an ability; it is not an application or a sequence of steps.

**Value stages — OSP-BTM01-OS-VS01:** Frame choice → compare options → decide → stop or change → fund → review.

**Objects to distinguish:** strategy, initiative, trade-off, owner and stop decision.

**Proposed responsibility:** The executive team owns the strategy; initiative owners own delivery; finance owns funding. BTM01 owns the trade-off decision, not the planning tool.

**Protected boundary:** An approved strategy is not a decision until it says what continues, changes or stops.

Transformation, architecture and platform applications are optional demonstration candidates, chosen after requirements. No product is assumed installed, licensed or able to cover the whole programme. This sprint focuses on application responsibility while referencing data, integration and security owners.

## 5. Build one Decision Card

Use synthetic case **OSP-BTM01-OS-SYN001**. Synthetic strategy RE-STR300, initiatives RE-IN300 to RE-IN303, no stop decision recorded. Keep everything on one card or screen.

1. **Need:** the outcome, affected people and one missing fact.
2. **Evidence:** relevant object IDs, source, validity or effective time, data purpose and permitted access.
3. **Ownership:** business decision owner, authoritative application and receiving owner.
4. **Gate:** prerequisite, allowed next state, authorized actor and the message anyone affected will see.
5. **Alternative:** Compare relabelling every initiative with deciding continue, change or stop.
6. **Exception:** one failure state, escalation owner and a safe recovery or assisted route.
7. **Proof:** the three results below, one measure and a stop, revise or proceed decision.

**Requirement — OSP-BTM01-OS-REQ01:** link one strategy-trade-off experiment hypothesis to an owned intervention and observed follow-up, including negative evidence.

**Decision — OSP-BTM01-OS-ADR01:** record your chosen option and the alternative from item 5, one trade-off, and the evidence that would change your choice.

## 6. Run three tabletop tests

### T01 — Every initiative is relabelled as strategic

**Expected:** Require an explicit continue, change or stop decision for each.

### T02 — Two goals compete for the same capacity

**Expected:** Name the trade-off and the decision owner.

### T03 — A goal has no measure or owner

**Expected:** Do not approve it until both exist.

For each, record **input → expected → actual paper result → evidence note → revision → retest**. These are sprint-local tests OSP-BTM01-OS-T01/T02/T03, supporting OSP-BTM01-OS-REQ01. A completed lesson is not an executed tenant test.

**Measure — OSP-BTM01-OS-KPI01:** Share of initiatives with an explicit decision, measure and owner; define window and exclusions. Assign David Chen as proposed measure owner, establish the baseline and exclusions, and review after the experiment. No numeric improvement is asserted.

**Acceptance:** the card identifies source and owner, preserves the protected boundary, handles all three tests after documented revisions and explains the decision in 60 seconds. Missing evidence remains visible. Record what is still unverified.

## 7. Use your 120 minutes

- **0–20:** read the origin timeline; explain what changed and what did not.
- **20–45:** role-play the story; ask the lead, counterpart and voice one useful question each.
- **45–65:** draw responsibilities and consult one resource selectively.
- **65–100:** complete the single Decision Card and compare the alternative.
- **100–115:** run the three tests; revise from observed paper behavior.
- **115–120:** explain the decision in 60 seconds and choose the next experiment.

All reading fits within these blocks. Do not complete an entire product course during the sprint.
