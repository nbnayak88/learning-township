## 1. Start with the story

An architecture for a supply-and-brand change at a consumer-goods business is drawn in detail, and the steering group asks which decision it supports.

**Transformation lead:** Priya Nair — Director Enterprise Architecture (ODG-P13).  
**Counterpart:** Omar Haddad — CISO and Privacy Lead (ODG-P12).  
**Business or data owner:** Diego Costa — Head of Planning Analytics and AI (ODG-P14).

Priya Nair asks what must be true before the team decides. Omar Haddad asks which evidence and owner support the decision. Diego Costa asks for the outcome that matters to the people affected.

**Evidence reveal:** In the synthetic record, the diagram shows the target state but no options, constraints or decision record.

**Pause:** What must be true for this decision to stand? Which evidence is missing, and who can confirm it? Ask the counterpart one discovery question before choosing a solution.

**Case boundary:** OmniDaily Consumer Goods Ltd. is an aspirational case study (USD 51B staples group; 84,000 employees; 57 countries; 420M households served). These incidents, services and dialogue are proposed learning scenarios, not corporate history, confirmed deployments or achieved results. Preserve the enterprise persona IDs above. Country-specific permissions, contracts and operating rules require validation; this sprint does not invent them.

## 2. Trace the origin: 1970–2026

These overlapping periods are teaching lenses, not invention dates or a universal adoption sequence. Earlier practices can coexist with newer ones. Pre-1978 scenes describe the wider domain, not OmniDaily Consumer Goods Ltd. history.

- **1970s–1980s:** Systems are documented informally.
- **1980s–1990s:** Frameworks and repositories formalize architecture; diagrams multiply.
- **2000s–2010s:** Enterprise architecture tools link applications to capabilities; decisions are rarely recorded.
- **2020s–2026:** Assisted modelling speeds views; decisions, constraints and owners still need people.

**Mindset shift:** start with the decision, its evidence and its owner, then choose the tool or programme. Digitizing an unclear decision can accelerate the same mistake.

**Pause:** Which old problem survives in a newer interface? Who bears its cost?

## 3. Explore possible futures: 2030–2050

**2030 experiment:** allow assistance to draft options and flag gaps; retain the evidence gate, a named decision owner and a challenge route.

**2040 question:** if the enterprise and its partners share more signals, can each decision carry its evidence and owner without losing trust or choice?

**2050 scene:** Imagine an enterprise that adjusts within agreed boundaries. Providers may act faster, but people must own the purpose, the trade-offs and the right to stop.

**Competing future:** A simpler, well-owned way of working with trained people may beat a larger transformation on reliability, cost and trust.

All future passages are design hypotheses, not forecasts or product roadmaps. Test a useful part today rather than assume the future will arrive as described.

## 4. Draw the smallest useful architecture

**Capability — ODG-BTM02-OS-CAP01:** decision-led enterprise architecture. A capability is an ability; it is not an application or a sequence of steps.

**Value stages — ODG-BTM02-OS-VS01:** Frame decision → describe current state → compare options → record decision → govern → review.

**Objects to distinguish:** architecture view, option, constraint, decision record and owner.

**Proposed responsibility:** Enterprise architecture owns the options; the business owns the decision; delivery owns constraints. BTM02 owns the architecture decision, not the modelling tool.

**Protected boundary:** A complete diagram is not a decision without options, constraints and a record.

Transformation, architecture and platform applications are optional demonstration candidates, chosen after requirements. No product is assumed installed, licensed or able to cover the whole programme. This sprint focuses on application responsibility while referencing data, integration and security owners.

## 5. Build one Decision Card

Use synthetic case **ODG-BTM02-OS-SYN001**. Synthetic architecture ODG-AR300, options ODG-OP300 and ODG-OP301, decision record missing. Keep everything on one card or screen.

1. **Need:** the outcome, affected people and one missing fact.
2. **Evidence:** relevant object IDs, source, validity or effective time, data purpose and permitted access.
3. **Ownership:** business decision owner, authoritative application and receiving owner.
4. **Gate:** prerequisite, allowed next state, authorized actor and the message anyone affected will see.
5. **Alternative:** Compare drawing the target state with recording options and the decision.
6. **Exception:** one failure state, escalation owner and a safe recovery or assisted route.
7. **Proof:** the three results below, one measure and a stop, revise or proceed decision.

**Requirement — ODG-BTM02-OS-REQ01:** link one architecture-decision experiment hypothesis to an owned intervention and observed follow-up, including negative evidence.

**Decision — ODG-BTM02-OS-ADR01:** record your chosen option and the alternative from item 5, one trade-off, and the evidence that would change your choice.

## 6. Run three tabletop tests

### T01 — The diagram shows no alternatives

**Expected:** Add options and the trade-offs between them.

### T02 — A constraint is discovered after approval

**Expected:** Record it and reopen the decision with the owner.

### T03 — Two teams use different definitions of one component

**Expected:** Agree a definition and a named owner.

For each, record **input → expected → actual paper result → evidence note → revision → retest**. These are sprint-local tests ODG-BTM02-OS-T01/T02/T03, supporting ODG-BTM02-OS-REQ01. A completed lesson is not an executed tenant test.

**Measure — ODG-BTM02-OS-KPI01:** Share of architecture decisions with options, constraints and a decision record; define window and exclusions. Assign Priya Nair as proposed measure owner, establish the baseline and exclusions, and review after the experiment. No numeric improvement is asserted.

**Acceptance:** the card identifies source and owner, preserves the protected boundary, handles all three tests after documented revisions and explains the decision in 60 seconds. Missing evidence remains visible. Record what is still unverified.

## 7. Use your 120 minutes

- **0–20:** read the origin timeline; explain what changed and what did not.
- **20–45:** role-play the story; ask the lead, counterpart and voice one useful question each.
- **45–65:** draw responsibilities and consult one resource selectively.
- **65–100:** complete the single Decision Card and compare the alternative.
- **100–115:** run the three tests; revise from observed paper behavior.
- **115–120:** explain the decision in 60 seconds and choose the next experiment.

All reading fits within these blocks. Do not complete an entire product course during the sprint.
