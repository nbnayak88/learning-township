# The benefit was reported; the baseline had moved
## BTM05 — Architecting Value-Driven Transformation

**IIL — Industry Immersion Lab · Version 1.0 · 10 November 2026**

**One story · One build card · Three tabletop tests**

## 1. Start with the story

A transformation benefit for a delivery-model change at a technology services firm is reported as delivered, and the sponsor asks to count it again next year.

**Transformation lead:** David Chen — Group CFO (OTS-P02).  
**Counterpart:** Mei Lin — Data Governance and Knowledge Lead (OTS-P09).  
**Business or data owner:** Daniel Weber — Director Enterprise Architecture (OTS-P12).

David Chen asks what must be true before the team decides. Mei Lin asks which evidence and owner support the decision. Daniel Weber asks for the outcome that matters to the people affected.

**Evidence reveal:** In the synthetic record, the baseline was restated mid-year, and the same benefit appears in two programmes.

**Pause:** What must be true for this decision to stand? Which evidence is missing, and who can confirm it? Ask the counterpart one discovery question before choosing a solution.

**Case boundary:** OmniTech Solutions Ltd. is an aspirational case study (USD 39B technology provider; 98,000 employees; 41 countries; 11,500 clients). These incidents, services and dialogue are proposed learning scenarios, not corporate history, confirmed deployments or achieved results. Preserve the enterprise persona IDs above. Country-specific permissions, contracts and operating rules require validation; this sprint does not invent them.

## 2. Trace the origin: 1970–2026

These overlapping periods are teaching lenses, not invention dates or a universal adoption sequence. Earlier practices can coexist with newer ones. Pre-1978 scenes describe the wider domain, not OmniTech Solutions Ltd. history.

- **1970s–1980s:** Benefits are promised in business cases.
- **1980s–1990s:** Programme offices track costs and milestones.
- **2000s–2010s:** Benefit-realization methods spread; baselines drift.
- **2020s–2026:** Assisted tracking speeds reporting; baselines, ownership and review still need people.

**Mindset shift:** start with the decision, its evidence and its owner, then choose the tool or programme. Digitizing an unclear decision can accelerate the same mistake.

**Pause:** Which old problem survives in a newer interface? Who bears its cost?

## 3. Explore possible futures: 2030–2050

**2030 experiment:** allow assistance to draft options and flag gaps; retain the evidence gate, a named decision owner and a challenge route.

**2040 question:** if the enterprise and its partners share more signals, can each decision carry its evidence and owner without losing trust or choice?

**2050 scene:** Imagine an enterprise that adjusts within agreed boundaries. Providers may act faster, but people must own the purpose, the trade-offs and the right to stop.

**Competing future:** A simpler, well-owned way of working with trained people may beat a larger transformation on reliability, cost and trust.

All future passages are design hypotheses, not forecasts or product roadmaps. Test a useful part today rather than assume the future will arrive as described.

## 4. Draw the smallest useful architecture

**Capability — OTS-BTM05-OS-CAP01:** comparable benefit evidence. A capability is an ability; it is not an application or a sequence of steps.

**Value stages — OTS-BTM05-OS-VS01:** Define benefit → fix baseline → deliver → measure → reconcile with finance → report.

**Objects to distinguish:** benefit, baseline, owner, evidence and claim.

**Proposed responsibility:** The sponsor owns the benefit; finance owns value review; programme owners own delivery. BTM05 owns the benefit decision, not the programme office.

**Protected boundary:** A reported benefit is not realized unless the baseline is comparable and counted once.

Transformation, architecture and platform applications are optional demonstration candidates, chosen after requirements. No product is assumed installed, licensed or able to cover the whole programme. This sprint focuses on application responsibility while referencing data, integration and security owners.

## 5. Build one Decision Card

Use synthetic case **OTS-BTM05-OS-SYN001**. Synthetic benefit IT-BN300, baseline versions IT-BL300 and IT-BL301, duplicate claim IT-DC300. Keep everything on one card or screen.

1. **Need:** the outcome, affected people and one missing fact.
2. **Evidence:** relevant object IDs, source, validity or effective time, data purpose and permitted access.
3. **Ownership:** business decision owner, authoritative application and receiving owner.
4. **Gate:** prerequisite, allowed next state, authorized actor and the message anyone affected will see.
5. **Alternative:** Compare reporting the latest baseline with fixing a comparable baseline.
6. **Exception:** one failure state, escalation owner and a safe recovery or assisted route.
7. **Proof:** the three results below, one measure and a stop, revise or proceed decision.

**Requirement — OTS-BTM05-OS-REQ01:** link one benefit-evidence experiment hypothesis to an owned intervention and observed follow-up, including negative evidence.

**Decision — OTS-BTM05-OS-ADR01:** record your chosen option and the alternative from item 5, one trade-off, and the evidence that would change your choice.

## 6. Run three tabletop tests

### T01 — The baseline moves mid-year

**Expected:** Restate on a comparable baseline before claiming a benefit.

### T02 — Two programmes claim one benefit

**Expected:** Count it once and name the owner.

### T03 — A forecast benefit is reported as realized

**Expected:** Label it as forecast until evidence exists.

For each, record **input → expected → actual paper result → evidence note → revision → retest**. These are sprint-local tests OTS-BTM05-OS-T01/T02/T03, supporting OTS-BTM05-OS-REQ01. A completed lesson is not an executed tenant test.

**Measure — OTS-BTM05-OS-KPI01:** Share of benefits with a comparable baseline, owner and finance-reviewed evidence; define window and exclusions. Assign David Chen as proposed measure owner, establish the baseline and exclusions, and review after the experiment. No numeric improvement is asserted.

**Acceptance:** the card identifies source and owner, preserves the protected boundary, handles all three tests after documented revisions and explains the decision in 60 seconds. Missing evidence remains visible. Record what is still unverified.

## 7. Use your 120 minutes

- **0–20:** read the origin timeline; explain what changed and what did not.
- **20–45:** role-play the story; ask the lead, counterpart and voice one useful question each.
- **45–65:** draw responsibilities and consult one resource selectively.
- **65–100:** complete the single Decision Card and compare the alternative.
- **100–115:** run the three tests; revise from observed paper behavior.
- **115–120:** explain the decision in 60 seconds and choose the next experiment.

All reading fits within these blocks. Do not complete an entire product course during the sprint.
