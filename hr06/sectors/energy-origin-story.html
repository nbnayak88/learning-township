1. What this two-hour sprint achieves

By the end you will: 1. Explain the general origins and purpose of this domain before naming a product. 2. Reconstruct its process evolution across 1975–2026, identifying what improved and what remained unresolved. 3. Compare 2030, 2035, 2040 and 2050 scenarios, with a competing future and a validation question. 4. Map application responsibilities and source objects for the OmniVerse Energy case. 5. Produce a synthetic build prototype, run three tabletop tests and defend an evidence-based next step.

The 20% to master: learning versus competence; practical evidence; credential validity; mobility versus appointment; restricted-work authorization.

2. Origins and purpose — the general story

Workplace learning emerged through apprenticeship, supervised practice and formal instruction. Training administration made attendance visible; digital learning made access scalable. Capability development asks a harder question: can the person safely and effectively perform the work, and can that capability open a credible career pathway?

Teach the decision first: what decision is needed, whose outcome matters, what evidence supports it, which application is responsible, and who has authority to act?

Fifty years of process evolution

Dates group broad historical patterns for learning. They do not claim invention dates, uniform adoption or a source-proven worldwide chronology. Paper, legacy, cloud and assisted processes can coexist today; the table is an educational synthesis.

Era Process pattern What improved Remaining gap Energy-sector example
1975–1984 Apprenticeship, classrooms and supervised practice Tacit knowledge and learning by doing Evidence varies across supervisors A technician learns beside an experienced operator
1985–1994 Training matrices and structured certification records Clearer role training requirements Attendance can stand in for ability A completed class needs assessed practice
1995–2004 Computer-based learning and electronic records Repeatable content and searchable history Content access does not prove transfer Simulation supplements hands-on evidence
2005–2014 LMS-led blended learning Assignment, delivery and tracking at scale Completion rates can dominate value An expired credential can remain hidden in a separate file
2015–2019 Digital academies and career pathways More flexible learning and visible development Opportunity access and content quality vary Map adjacent refinery skills to emerging roles
2020–2022 Remote learning and virtual collaboration Wider continuity of learning access Digital exclusion and practical-assessment gaps persist Remote study does not replace supervised hazardous-task assessment
2023–2026 Skills recommendations and generated content Potentially faster discovery and draft content Stale/incorrect material and inferred competence Expert review and qualified assessment remain distinct gates

Evolution activity: cover the table. For each era, say “the process changed from ___ to ___ because ___; the unresolved problem was ___.” Use an energy example rather than a product slogan.

Historical caution: the 2020–2022 row describes possible process pressures/responses in distributed work, not proof that every energy enterprise changed in that way. Modern product documentation supports current capabilities, not the full historical chronology.

4. OmniVerse Energy — the decision and the people

Aspirational enterprise baseline: OmniVerse Energy Ltd., 68,000 employees across 42 countries. “OmniEnergy” is an alias preserved in original research. Historical eras are not a corporate biography; OmniVerse was founded in 1978.

Convert learning activity into verified capability, safe work, and visible career opportunity. OmniEnergy must reskill its workforce for renewables, battery storage, hydrogen, digital twins, AI, OT cybersecurity, advanced maintenance, ESG, and customer energy services. Training records are fragmented, certifications expire unnoticed, and employees cannot easily see pathways from traditional energy roles into emerging careers.

Scope note: A hydrogen pathway is a aspirational learning example, not approved hazardous-work guidance. Expert-approved content and qualified practical assessment are prerequisites for a credible capability claim.

6. Key concepts and application responsibility

Concept Meaning and boundary
Learning record Evidence of an activity, completion or assessment
Competence Demonstrated ability against defined criteria
Assessor Qualified person accountable for the scoped assessment
Credential validity Scope, issuer, expiry and verification status
Opportunity A possible assignment or role, not an entitlement
Work authorization Separate approved task/site decision under HSE policy
Application / accountable area Responsibility
LMS Learning assignments, records and approved content lifecycle
Qualified assessor Validates practical competence
Marketplace / mobility service Recommends opportunities with clear prerequisites
HSE Authorizes restricted work
EC / role owners Approved role facts and appointment decisions

Source information objects: skill; proficiency evidence; assessor; credential expiry; opportunity; work authorization. Record attribute owner, authoritative source, purpose, access, retention, effective date and quality rule.

Source value stream: Skill demand → gap → explore → practice → assess → verify → deploy. Define a stakeholder-valued exit criterion at each stage before detailed process modeling.

A capability is an ability the organization needs; the value stream describes progression toward stakeholder value. Neither is simply a list of applications.

AA boundary: this sprint assigns application responsibility and acceptance criteria. Reference data stewardship, security, integration, UX, infrastructure and AI concerns without assuming the application view alone implements those controls. Cross-domain consumers must not silently become owners of source facts.

8. Build experiment — a small, reviewable implementation design

Use two synthetic records, one routine and one variant drawn from the source challenges. Assign teaching identifiers HR06-SYN-001/002. Do not use real employee information, internal endpoints or live banking/access actions.

Build Card field What you must write
Purpose/population Decision, affected person/role and intended outcome; identify missing facts
Two variants Online completion without practice versus Expired credential / delayed feed
Objects and time Identifiers, source, relevant validity/as-of/processing dates; distinguish their meanings
Lifecycle/gates Proposed from-state → permitted transition → prerequisite → accountable actor
Evidence Source reference, validation status, version and correction owner
Application boundary LMS responsibility versus EC / role owners
Global/local decision Shared principle, justified role/country variant and evidence still needed
Human and automation boundary One permitted administrative task; one protected consequential decision
Exception/recovery Visible exception, owner, fallback, recovery and confirmation
Architecture decision Compare course-completion management, locally isolated academies and shared evidence standards with role-specific practical pathways.
Measure Definition, denominator, exclusions, as-of date, owner and interpretation limits
Test evidence Input → expected → actual paper result → correction → evidence note → pass/revise

Conceptual workflow: establish approved source evidence → validate → apply agreed rule → authorized decision/action → confirm downstream business outcome → review exceptions. These are teaching stages, not prescribed SAP object/status names. Choose actual module configuration only after validating the contract and product version.

Three tabletop tests

Test Challenge Expected result in this proposed design
T1 Online completion without practice Record completion; do not assert verified competence or authorize restricted work
T2 Expired credential / delayed feed Expose validity uncertainty to owner; use approved safe fallback; no stale eligibility assumption
T3 Generated hydrogen content Hold publication for expert review; record version and accessible alternative

How to run: represent each record/state on paper or in a simple table. Execute the steps yourself; record whether the initial design produced the expected outcome. If it did not, revise and rerun. Designed expectations are not actual SAP test results.

Acceptance: explain the timeline, defend one 2050 hypothesis against an alternative, distinguish the concepts, assign owners and satisfy all three tabletop expectations after documented revision. An unsupported safety/pay/access/employee-rights outcome requires redesign. List any control that remains untested.

Source measure: Time-to-verified-competence = verification date minus pathway start; record successful practical assessment rather than completion alone. Owner: domain lead unless specified; cadence: proposed pilot review and monthly follow-up. Establish denominator, exclusions, as-of date and baseline before setting a numeric target. Original source “Victory Measures” are targets, not achievements.

Do not fabricate a baseline, achieved benefit or target. A successful prototype proves only the limited behaviour you actually exercised.
