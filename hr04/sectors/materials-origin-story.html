1. One learner promise

In 120 minutes, design and defend one evidence-backed succession decision with practical interim coverage and a clear development step.

One persona-led story · One Capability Continuity Build Card · Three tabletop tests. Keep timeline notes, ownership, decisions and observed paper results on the same card. No tenant configuration or extra deliverable is required.

2. One persona-led story

Elena Petrova, Cement Plant Director — OMF-P07, needs continuity for a technical role coordinating kiln-process decisions as recovered-feedstock use changes.

In this new synthetic scenario, the incumbent will leave in twelve weeks. A talent slide names a successor as “ready now.”

Arjun Mehta, Group CHRO — OMF-P05, asks what supports that label. The record contains a strong performance review and a completed leadership course.

Nikhil Sen, Shift Supervisor and Workforce Representative — OMF-P16, asks: “Who has observed this person handle an abnormal condition during a night shift?” Nobody can provide that evidence.

Choose first: accept the nomination, remove the candidate, or retain the nomination while reassessing readiness and arranging interim coverage. Record your reason before reading further.

New evidence: the candidate wants a technical career, not a people-management role. A supervised development opportunity exists, but previous nominations have come only from day-shift managers.

Revise the decision. Preserve the candidate's agency, widen access to consideration and address the immediate continuity gap. Nikhil can contribute evidence and challenge impractical arrangements; his title does not grant appointment or safety-authorization rights.

3. HR04 origins and evolution: 1970–2025

These are closed teaching windows. Actual adoption varied and practices overlapped; the windows do not claim invention dates, universal transitions or OmniForge corporate history.

Period Process evolution Question that remained
1970–1984 Replacement charts and manager nominations identify backups Is a named replacement actually ready?
1985–1994 Competency frameworks and assessments make expectations more explicit Are the criteria relevant and fairly applied?
1995–2004 Electronic talent inventories make succession information searchable Whose practical knowledge remains invisible?
2005–2014 Connected talent reviews link performance, succession and development Does current performance establish next-role readiness?
2015–2025 Mentoring, technical career pathways and development assignments broaden growth opportunities Who receives meaningful experience—and who is excluded?

Counterexample: an excellent current-role performance rating does not prove readiness for a different role. Business-critical roles include technical specialists who may wish to remain specialists; seniority or management grade alone does not determine criticality.

4. AI-driven Growth & Leadership: 2026–2050

The following are design scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities or an approved OmniForge roadmap. Earlier approaches continue alongside AI. Even the 2026–2030 row is a scenario for exploration, not a claim about current tenant enablement.

Period AI-driven scenario Materials experiment and human decision
2026–2030 — Assist AI helps discover candidates, summarize evidence and draft development plans Compare an assistant's proposed kiln-role successor with a manual evidence review. People validate evidence, readiness and candidate aspirations.
2031–2040 — Connect AI connects verified work experiences, mentors and development opportunities across plants Explore a supervised assignment that closes one demonstrated capability gap. Authorized assessors confirm capability; site authorities control work permissions.
2041–2050 — Adapt AI continuously helps compare development, recruitment and role-redesign options as materials processes change Reassess continuity when feedstock, technology or operating conditions change. Leaders and affected workers decide pathways, appointments and acceptable risk.

Competing 2050 scenario: opaque talent scores or fragmented records exclude workers whose experience is poorly recorded. Choose one useful experiment today and state what evidence would make you reject it.

Enduring question: Can we demonstrate readiness, provide interim coverage and support the person's chosen growth pathway?

5. Application responsibilities and human authority

This is a proposed learning architecture, not a verified deployed landscape. A nomination is not an appointment; readiness is role-specific and time-bound; interim cover and work authorization must be assessed separately.

Responsibility Accountable owner Boundary
HR01 — Core HR HR data owner: position, employment and organization facts A position record does not establish readiness.
HR04 — Succession Talent process owner: nominations, readiness evidence and review history Nomination is not appointment; delegated assessors and decision-makers require confirmation.
HR04 — Career development Employee, manager and development owner: aspirations, experiences and support A development plan does not guarantee promotion.
HR06 — Learning Learning owner and assessors: completion and assessed learning evidence Course completion alone does not establish operational readiness.
Business and site authorities Delegated role owners and relevant technical/safety assessors Validate role readiness and separately authorize restricted work; operational urgency creates no extra permission.
HR02 — Recruiting Talent Acquisition: approved external hiring option A vacancy must not silently become an approved recruitment request.

HR03 supplies relevant performance evidence; it does not own next-role readiness. HR11 covers deeper AI controls and HR12 covers detailed exchanges. An assistant may summarize permitted evidence or draft a development proposal; it cannot assign final readiness, appoint a person or grant access/work authority.

SAP examples distinguish readiness and emergency-cover information in successor nominations (R2). Validate meaning, permissions, configured features and policy for the actual implementation; an emergency-cover label alone is not evidence of competence.

6. One build card: Capability Continuity

Use synthetic position MAT-POS401, nomination MAT-NOM401 and development action MAT-DEV401. The twelve-week departure horizon is a scenario assumption, not a validated enterprise timeline.

Card field What the learner records
Critical role Business consequence of losing the capability; distinguish role from incumbent.
Readiness Required capability, evidence, assessor, assessment date and unresolved gaps.
Candidate voice Career aspiration, availability and development preferences.
Interim coverage Proposed cover, evidence of capability, authority limits, availability and escalation.
Knowledge transfer One supervised experience and an observable assessment—not just document upload.
Development One action, support owner and review point.
Decision Chosen option, alternative and evidence that would change the answer.
Evidence Three tabletop results, corrections and remaining questions.

Interim coverage must be assessed independently. If no viable cover exists, record the continuity gap and escalate to the accountable operational decision-maker; do not invent a ready successor or permit unqualified work. Relevant site controls remain in force.

Suggested progression: define critical role → assess continuity risk → consider candidates → validate readiness → agree development and interim cover → rehearse handover. These are teaching stages, not prescribed SAP status names. Appointment follows separate authorized processes.

Use synthetic records and minimum necessary data. Keep nomination discussions restricted to authorized roles, provide an appropriate correction route and avoid copying sensitive personal details into the card.

7. Three tabletop tests

Test Challenge Expected behavior
OMF-HR04-T01 — Unsupported readiness “Ready now” rests only on a rating and course completion Retain the nomination if appropriate; reassess readiness against role-specific evidence without inventing a timeframe.
OMF-HR04-T02 — Departure before development The incumbent leaves before the proposed experience can be completed Identify assessed interim coverage, explicit authority limits and an accountable continuity decision; escalate if no viable cover exists.
OMF-HR04-T03 — Narrow nominations and changed aspirations Only day-shift candidates were considered; the nominee prefers a technical pathway Broaden access, review relevant evidence and agree a suitable pathway without promising appointment.

Record input → expected → observed paper result → revision. All tests remain planned until performed. A successful paper exercise is not an executed tenant test or proof of operational continuity.

Acceptance: complete one card, record all three actual tabletop results after revision and explain the decision in 60 seconds, including interim coverage and one development step. An unsupported readiness claim or unauthorized work/appointment requires redesign.

9. Traceability and Pre-Foundation handoff

Connect the card to existing OMF-M18 — workforce/adoption, OMF-M19 — readiness/execution and OMF-M17 — risk/security. The following identifiers are new educational design anchors, not previously approved enterprise requirements.

Learning anchor Source connection and card evidence
OMF-HR04-REQ01 OMF-M18: criteria, current evidence and candidate voice support a role-specific readiness decision; tested by T01/T03.
OMF-HR04-REQ02 OMF-M17/19: independently assessed interim coverage and explicit authority limits address the continuity gap; tested by T02.
OMF-HR04-ADR01 Use common readiness principles with role-specific evidence. Compare a name-only replacement chart; state the trade-off and evidence still needed.
OMF-HR04-EXP01 / EV01 One synthetic continuity card and planned T01–T03; evidence slot initially unfilled until observed results/revisions exist.
OMF-HR04-M01 Learning measure: tests meeting expected behavior / 3, with failures/revisions recorded. No enterprise coverage baseline or achieved benefit is asserted.

Distinguish named, developing, evidence-backed ready and interim-covered positions. These statuses can overlap and must not be added together or counted as equivalent coverage. Define population, denominator, as-of date and evidence rules before reporting enterprise coverage.

Reflection: What makes readiness credible? Whose capability was overlooked? What must remain protected during the handover?

Carry the same card into Pre-Foundation to define critical position, nomination, readiness horizon, assessment, development action and handover relationships.
