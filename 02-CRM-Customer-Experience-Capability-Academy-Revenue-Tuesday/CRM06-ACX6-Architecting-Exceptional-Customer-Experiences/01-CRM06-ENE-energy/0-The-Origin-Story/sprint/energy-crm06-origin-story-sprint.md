# The loyalty score improved; the customer experience did not
## CRM06 — Architecting Exceptional Customer Experiences

**SuccessLabs Academy · OmniVerse Energy Ltd. · Level 0 → Pre-Foundation**  
**IIL — Industry Immersion Lab · Version 1.0 · 31 October 2026**  
**Planning reference:** HUB-ORIGIN-CRM06-ENE-sprint (not an LMS ID)  
**Format:** Origin Story Sprint · 120 minutes · Free — Community/Citizen Edition  
**Status:** Published learning content; not an enrolled course or certification.

> **Your promise:** In two hours, design one customer-feedback experiment that turns a real friction point into a verified improvement decision.

**One story · One build card · Three tabletop tests**

## 1. Start with the customer

A dashboard shows better survey scores, but Elena still repeats the same information on every contact. A proposed loyalty reward would compensate her without fixing the broken handoff.

**Lead:** Grace Okafor — Chief Customer and Retail Officer (OVE-P10).  
**Counterpart:** Hannah Fischer — Director of Enterprise Architecture (OVE-P12).  
**Customer voice:** Elena Brooks — VP Energy Procurement (OVE-P15).

Grace asks what changed for the customer. Hannah asks whether the survey represents customers who abandoned the journey. Elena asks for less effort, not another reward.

The team chooses one repeated-contact problem, includes customers who struggled, and compares a redesigned handoff with current practice. It treats loyalty incentives and service improvement as separate decisions.

**Pause:** What does the customer need to be true? Which fact is missing, and who can confirm it? Ask the counterpart one discovery question before choosing a solution.

**Case boundary:** OmniVerse is a fictional integrated energy enterprise with 68,000 employees in 42 countries. These new incidents, services and dialogue are proposed learning scenarios, not corporate history, confirmed deployments or achieved results. Preserve the enterprise persona IDs above. Country-specific permissions, contracts and operating rules require validation; this sprint does not invent them.

## 2. Trace the origin: 1970–2026

These overlapping periods are teaching lenses, not invention dates or a universal adoption sequence. Earlier practices can coexist with newer ones. Pre-1978 scenes describe the wider domain, not OmniVerse history.

- **1970s–1980s:** Relationship managers learn through conversations; difficult experiences may remain invisible.
- **1980s–1990s:** Surveys and loyalty programs quantify response; selection bias and incentives can distort conclusions.
- **2000s–2010s:** Journey mapping and experience platforms join feedback to touchpoints; correlation is not a proven cause.
- **2020s–2026:** Closed-loop feedback and assisted analysis identify patterns; customers still need an accountable response.

**Mindset shift:** start with the decision and its evidence, then choose the application. Digitizing an unclear process can accelerate the same mistake.

**Pause:** Which old problem survives in a newer interface? Who bears its cost: customer, frontline colleague or community?

## 3. Explore possible futures: 2030–2050

**2030 experiment:** allow assistance to prepare the low-risk part of this journey; retain the evidence gate, accountable owner and human challenge route.

**2040 question:** if providers and channels change, can the customer carry the necessary evidence without losing choice, privacy or a usable non-AI route?

**2050 scene:** Imagine a 2050 customer choosing which experience signals to share. Providers may spot friction sooner, but must demonstrate an actual improvement rather than infer emotion as fact.

**Competing future:** If customers stop answering surveys, combine accessible interviews and task observations with carefully scoped service data.

All future passages are design hypotheses, not forecasts or product roadmaps. Test a useful part today rather than assume the future will arrive as described.

## 4. Draw the smallest useful architecture

**Capability — OVE-CRM06-OS-CAP01:** closed-loop experience improvement. A capability is an ability; it is not an application or a sequence of steps.

**Value stages — OVE-CRM06-OS-VS01:** Listen → define friction → propose change → experiment → observe customer effort → decide.

**Objects to distinguish:** journey stage, feedback, respondent population, friction hypothesis, intervention and follow-up.

**Proposed responsibility:** Experience management owns feedback and improvement backlog; service owns case resolution; loyalty owns approved benefits; analytics owns measure definitions. CRM06 owns the improvement loop, not the portal build.

**Protected boundary:** Higher survey scores or reward redemption alone do not demonstrate a better journey or caused retention.

Use SAP CX and adjacent services as optional demonstration candidates, selected after requirements. No product is assumed installed, licensed or able to cover the whole journey. CRM01–CRM12 are curriculum themes; the twelve architecture streams are a separate axis. This sprint focuses application responsibility while referencing data, experience, integration and security owners.

## 5. Build one Decision Card

Use synthetic case **OVE-CRM06-OS-SYN001**. Keep everything on one card or screen.

1. **Need:** customer outcome, affected population and one missing fact.
2. **Evidence:** relevant object IDs, source, validity/effective time, data purpose and permitted access.
3. **Ownership:** business decision owner, authoritative application and receiving owner.
4. **Gate:** prerequisite, allowed next state, authorized actor and customer-visible message.
5. **Alternative:** Compare a blanket incentive with fixing the repeated-information handoff and measuring effort.
6. **Exception:** one failure state, escalation owner and safe recovery or assisted route.
7. **Proof:** the three results below, one measure and a stop/revise/proceed decision.

**Requirement — OVE-CRM06-OS-REQ01:** link one customer friction hypothesis to an owned intervention and observed follow-up, including negative feedback.

**Decision — OVE-CRM06-OS-ADR01:** record your chosen option and alternative from item 5, one trade-off, and the evidence that would change your choice.

## 6. Run three tabletop tests

### T01 — Only successful customers receive surveys

**Expected:** Expose the sampling gap and include an approved route for failed/abandoned journeys.

### T02 — Reward increases score but repeat contact persists

**Expected:** Report both outcomes; do not claim the friction is fixed.

### T03 — Feedback identifies a vulnerable or unhappy customer

**Expected:** Route with appropriate access and human follow-up; avoid exposing sensitive comments broadly.

For each, record **input → expected → actual paper result → evidence note → revision → retest**. These are sprint-local tests OVE-CRM06-OS-T01/T02/T03, supporting OVE-CRM06-OS-REQ01. A completed lesson is not an executed tenant test.

**Measure — OVE-CRM06-OS-KPI01:** Repeat-contact rate for the chosen journey, with a defined issue window and comparison population; collect effort feedback. Assign the lead as proposed measure owner, establish the baseline and exclusions, and review after the experiment. No numeric improvement is asserted.

**Acceptance:** the card identifies source/owner, preserves the protected boundary, handles all three tests after documented revisions and explains the decision in 60 seconds. Missing evidence remains visible. Record what is still unverified.

## 7. Use your 120 minutes

- **0–20:** read the origin timeline; explain what changed and what did not.
- **20–45:** role-play the story; ask the lead, counterpart and customer one useful question each.
- **45–65:** draw responsibilities and consult one resource selectively.
- **65–100:** complete the single Decision Card and compare the alternative.
- **100–115:** run the three tests; revise from observed paper behavior.
- **115–120:** explain the decision in 60 seconds and choose the next experiment.

All reading fits within these blocks. Do not complete an entire product course during the sprint.
