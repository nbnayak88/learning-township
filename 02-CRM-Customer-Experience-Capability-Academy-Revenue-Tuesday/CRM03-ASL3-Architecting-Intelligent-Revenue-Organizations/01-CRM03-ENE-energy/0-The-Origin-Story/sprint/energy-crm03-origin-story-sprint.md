# The attractive quote that promised energy nobody had confirmed
## CRM03 — Architecting Intelligent Revenue Organizations

**SuccessLabs Academy · OmniVerse Energy Ltd. · Level 0 → Pre-Foundation**  
**IIL — Industry Immersion Lab · Version 1.0 · 31 October 2026**  
**Planning reference:** HUB-ORIGIN-CRM03-ENE-sprint (not an LMS ID)  
**Format:** Origin Story Sprint · 120 minutes · Free — Community/Citizen Edition

> **Your promise:** In two hours, design one evidence-backed opportunity-to-quote decision without confusing a sales promise with confirmed supply.

**One story · One build card · Three tabletop tests**

## 1. Start with the customer

Elena requests a renewable-energy supply proposal for a new industrial site. A salesperson wants to commit a delivery date, discount and green-energy claim before the evidence is approved.

**Lead:** Grace Okafor — Chief Customer and Retail Officer (OVE-P10).  
**Counterpart:** Farah Al-Hassan — Chief Operating Officer (OVE-P04).  
**Customer voice:** Elena Brooks — VP Energy Procurement (OVE-P15).

Elena asks what is guaranteed. Farah asks which operational owner confirmed capacity. Grace notices that a probability-weighted opportunity is being described as a signed contract.

The team records quote assumptions, price validity, approval limits and evidence for environmental claims. Unconfirmed supply becomes an explicit condition, not a hidden promise.

**Pause:** What does the customer need to be true? Which fact is missing, and who can confirm it? Ask the counterpart one discovery question before choosing a solution.

**Case boundary:** OmniVerse is a fictional integrated energy enterprise with 68,000 employees in 42 countries. These new incidents, services and dialogue are proposed learning scenarios, not corporate history, confirmed deployments or achieved results. Preserve the enterprise persona IDs above. Country-specific permissions, contracts and operating rules require validation; this sprint does not invent them.

## 2. Trace the origin: 1970–2026

These overlapping periods are teaching lenses, not invention dates or a universal adoption sequence. Earlier practices can coexist with newer ones. Pre-1978 scenes describe the wider domain, not OmniVerse history.

- **1970s–1980s:** Salespeople use notebooks and personal relationships; commitments depend on individual memory.
- **1980s–1990s:** Pipeline databases structure leads and opportunities; probability can be confused with certainty.
- **2000s–2010s:** CRM and configuration/pricing tools organize offers; approved commercial terms still need operational evidence.
- **2020s–2026:** Assisted forecasting and quote drafting reduce administration; unsupported commitments can spread faster.

**Mindset shift:** start with the decision and its evidence, then choose the application. Digitizing an unclear process can accelerate the same mistake.

**Pause:** Which old problem survives in a newer interface? Who bears its cost: customer, frontline colleague or community?

## 3. Explore possible futures: 2030–2050

**2030 experiment:** allow assistance to prepare the low-risk part of this journey; retain the evidence gate, accountable owner and human challenge route.

**2040 question:** if providers and channels change, can the customer carry the necessary evidence without losing choice, privacy or a usable non-AI route?

**2050 scene:** Imagine a 2050 procurement agent requesting evidence-linked supply options. The seller's agent may draft alternatives, but it cannot invent capacity, verified attributes or contract authority.

**Competing future:** In a volatile future, long-duration assumptions fail. Keep quote validity, scenario ranges and reapproval triggers explicit.

All future passages are design hypotheses, not forecasts or product roadmaps. Test a useful part today rather than assume the future will arrive as described.

## 4. Draw the smallest useful architecture

**Capability — OVE-CRM03-OS-CAP01:** opportunity and quotation governance. A capability is an ability; it is not an application or a sequence of steps.

**Value stages — OVE-CRM03-OS-VS01:** Qualify need → assess opportunity → validate offer inputs → approve quote → communicate conditions → hand off accepted terms.

**Objects to distinguish:** lead, opportunity, quote version, price condition, supply assumption and approval.

**Proposed responsibility:** Sales owns opportunity and approved quote state; pricing owns commercial rules; operations validates supply assumptions; contract/order services own accepted commitments. CRM08 owns order execution.

**Protected boundary:** A quote is not dispatch confirmation, a guaranteed delivery or proof of renewable attributes.

Use SAP CX and adjacent services as optional demonstration candidates, selected after requirements. No product is assumed installed, licensed or able to cover the whole journey. CRM01–CRM12 are curriculum themes; the twelve architecture streams are a separate axis. This sprint focuses application responsibility while referencing data, experience, integration and security owners.

## 5. Build one Decision Card

Use synthetic case **OVE-CRM03-OS-SYN001**. Keep everything on one card or screen.

1. **Need:** customer outcome, affected population and one missing fact.
2. **Evidence:** relevant object IDs, source, validity/effective time, data purpose and permitted access.
3. **Ownership:** business decision owner, authoritative application and receiving owner.
4. **Gate:** prerequisite, allowed next state, authorized actor and customer-visible message.
5. **Alternative:** Compare immediate unconditional quoting with a conditional quote and explicit approvals.
6. **Exception:** one failure state, escalation owner and safe recovery or assisted route.
7. **Proof:** the three results below, one measure and a stop/revise/proceed decision.

**Requirement — OVE-CRM03-OS-REQ01:** retain an approved quote version with evidence for price, supply assumptions, validity and customer-visible conditions.

**Decision — OVE-CRM03-OS-ADR01:** record your chosen option and alternative from item 5, one trade-off, and the evidence that would change your choice.

## 6. Run three tabletop tests

### T01 — Price changes after quote approval

**Expected:** Preserve the offered version; apply validity and change-approval rules before reissue.

### T02 — Supply date lacks operations confirmation

**Expected:** Show the dependency and conditional status; do not claim guaranteed delivery.

### T03 — AI drafts an unsupported green claim

**Expected:** Hold publication, request verifiable evidence and obtain accountable approval.

For each, record **input → expected → actual paper result → evidence note → revision → retest**. These are sprint-local tests OVE-CRM03-OS-T01/T02/T03, supporting OVE-CRM03-OS-REQ01. A completed lesson is not an executed tenant test.

**Measure — OVE-CRM03-OS-KPI01:** Quote evidence completeness: approved quotes with all required evidence / approved quotes in scope; pair speed with rework. Assign the lead as proposed measure owner, establish the baseline and exclusions, and review after the experiment. No numeric improvement is asserted.

**Acceptance:** the card identifies source/owner, preserves the protected boundary, handles all three tests after documented revisions and explains the decision in 60 seconds. Missing evidence remains visible. Record what is still unverified.

## 7. Use your 120 minutes

- **0–20:** read the origin timeline; explain what changed and what did not.
- **20–45:** role-play the story; ask the lead, counterpart and customer one useful question each.
- **45–65:** draw responsibilities and consult one resource selectively.
- **65–100:** complete the single Decision Card and compare the alternative.
- **100–115:** run the three tests; revise from observed paper behavior.
- **115–120:** explain the decision in 60 seconds and choose the next experiment.

All reading fits within these blocks. Do not complete an entire product course during the sprint.
