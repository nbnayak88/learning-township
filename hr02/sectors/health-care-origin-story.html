The offer is accepted; who owns day-one readiness?

SuccessLabs Academy · HR02 — Talent Acquisition, Recruiting & Onboarding · GICS06 Health Care · OmniHealth Systems Ltd. IIL — Industry Immersion Lab · Level 0 — The Origin Story · Format: Sprint · 120 minutes · Edition 2026-10-31 Mission: Architecting Experiences for a Better World. Community/Citizen Edition. 1. One learner promise

In 120 minutes, design one accountable Recruiting-to-Onboarding handoff without confusing an accepted offer with permission to deliver care.

One persona-led story, one Hire-to-Readiness Build Card, exactly three tabletop tests. Use synthetic data and one card throughout; no tenant is required.

3. Origins: the problem survives the medium

These closed historical teaching windows are educational groupings, not universal invention dates or OmniHealth's corporate biography. Adoption overlaps; paper and older systems can coexist through the present.

1970–1984 — Paper requisitions, interviews and joining packs rely on personal follow-up; who owns the next action?

1985–1994 — Applicant databases and document registers improve tracking; candidate and employee identity remain different.

1995–2004 — Applicant tracking and HR workflows connect vacancy to hire; accepted offer does not prove every joining condition.

2005–2014 — Career portals and onboarding services coordinate tasks across facilities; a completed checklist may hide an unresolved dependency.

2015–2025 — Cloud recruiting and assisted matching speed administration; fair selection, credential verification and worker voice still need accountable people.

Counterexample: a fast offer-acceptance metric can improve while the receiving unit still lacks an accountable readiness owner.

4. One story, one consequential choice

Nina Fernandes (OHS-P06), Group CHRO, sees an accepted offer for a synthetic laboratory professional. Dr. Mei Lin (OHS-P11), Diagnostics and Imaging Director, wants a reliable start for the diagnostic service. The recruiter marks hiring complete, while onboarding lists credential review, learning and facility access under different owners. The candidate receives a welcoming message that sounds like unconditional readiness.

Pause before the reveal. Choose treat acceptance as readiness, postpone every joining activity, or separate selection, employment entry and role-readiness gates. State who benefits, who bears the risk and what evidence you need.

Evidence reveal: the candidate completed employment documents but the designated professional reviewer has not confirmed the scope and validity of the credential. A name match alone cannot safely link the candidate to an existing worker record.

Revised decision: Nina preserves the accepted-offer state and starts authorized administrative onboarding. Mei Lin requests the correct professional review; the joining notice states exactly which activities are permitted and who resolves the hold. Human selection and credential authority remain explicit. A learner may defend a different option if the card names its authority, evidence, trade-off and conditions for stopping. Missing evidence remains unknown; an urgent request does not manufacture approval.

5. Applications serve accountable people

Proposed responsibilities, subject to local discovery:

  • Recruiting owns requisition, candidate, assessment and offer records; delegated hiring managers and recruiters own selection decisions and fair process.
  • Onboarding owns joining-task orchestration and acknowledgements; assigned task owners attest to their own evidence, not everyone else's readiness.
  • Core HR owns employment entry after approved identity/transaction checks; a candidate ID is not automatically a person/employment ID.
  • Credentialing, Learning, IAM and the receiving facility each own their evidence and approvals. The clinical authority decides scope of work; onboarding only reports the decision.

Across these boundaries, HR owns employment facts; designated clinical governance owns clinical privilege decisions; IAM administers approved access. Employment, learning completion, account access and permission for clinical work are distinct states. Share only the data needed for the declared purpose; keep patient records and detailed health information out of general HR learning cards. Omar Haddad (OHS-P10), CISO and Privacy Lead, governs privacy/access assurance; jurisdiction-specific applicability remains unresolved.

SAP examples illustrate responsibilities rather than prescribe a purchase: Recruiting, Onboarding and Employee Central connected to Learning and approved identity/facility services. No feature availability, license entitlement, configuration, legal compliance or clinical conformance is established here. AI may draft a reminder or summarize permitted evidence; it cannot grant clinical privilege, make a high-impact employment decision or attest to payment or delivery.

  • 0–10 minutes: understand the learner promise and people problem.
  • 10–30 minutes: compare the five historical windows and identify the enduring question.
  • 30–45 minutes: choose, reveal evidence and revise the story decision.
  • 45–60 minutes: map application responsibility, data ownership and human decision rights.
  • 60–90 minutes: complete the single build card, comparing one alternative and one local variant.
  • 90–110 minutes: discuss the three tabletop tests and record observed paper decisions and revisions.
  • 110–120 minutes: explain the design in 60 seconds, reflect and name the Pre-Foundation discovery question.

Selective resource reading fits within these activities. The single card holds all notes; no extra deliverable is required.

7. One synthetic build experiment

Synthetic candidate HC-C200, offer HC-O200, future employment HC-E200 and facility LAB-A. Offer accepted; documents complete; credential review pending; one possible duplicate identity. No real credential or patient record is used.

Complete one Hire-to-Readiness Build Card with these six fields:

  1. Need and scope: Make the candidate's next permitted step and accountable owner visible across both source cases. Explain the capability and value-stream step before naming a tool.
  2. Facts and time: preserve identifiers, source, effective/as-of time, unknowns and approved policy version. Distinguish a source fact from your assumption.
  3. Decision and ownership: Record the state transitions requisition → selection → accepted offer → employment entry → permitted role start, with distinct approvals and candidate communication. Record one alternative, its trade-off and the evidence that could favour it.
  4. Handoff and recovery: Quarantine the ambiguous identity link; route it to the HR identity steward. Escalate overdue professional review to its delegated authority; do not silently merge records or promise clinical clearance. Name the accountable exception role, accessible human route and condition for reconciliation.
  5. Local variant and data: choose one facility/language/role variation; justify minimum fields, purpose, owner, retention decision and viewers. Retain a non-digital route.
  6. Evidence and learning: record T01–T03 input → expected → observed paper result → revision → reviewer question on this same card. Leave results unfilled until observed.

8. Exactly three tabletop tests

OHS-HR02-T01 — Ambiguous identity

Input/reveal: Two similar names match the candidate.

Expected in the proposed design: No automatic merge or duplicate hire; HR identity review resolves linkage using approved minimum evidence.

Record: Candidate-to-person mapping, unknowns and accountable reviewer. Observed result and pass/revise remain unfilled until learners perform the tabletop.

OHS-HR02-T02 — Accepted but not ready

Input/reveal: Offer accepted and documents complete; credential review pending.

Expected in the proposed design: Selection remains recorded; only authorized administrative onboarding proceeds; clinical work remains held for the designated decision.

Record: Separate states, task owner and clear candidate notice. Observed result and pass/revise remain unfilled until learners perform the tabletop.

OHS-HR02-T03 — Failed handoff

Input/reveal: Onboarding sends the employment request twice and receives no final confirmation.

Expected in the proposed design: Show unconfirmed status; reconcile the stable request before retry or creating another employment record; no completion claim from a sent message.

Record: Request key, receiver status, exception owner and reconciliation condition. Observed result and pass/revise remain unfilled until learners perform the tabletop.

Acceptance is a defensible completed card and an explanation of all three decisions after revision. A paper discussion proves neither a working system nor a safe clinical service. Publishing this lesson executes no tests.

10. AI-driven exploration: three scenarios

Scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps: - 2026–2030 — Assist: An assistant drafts candidate updates from approved task states. - 2031–2040 — Connect: Governed services connect recruiting, employment, credential and facility evidence. - 2041–2050 — Adapt: A bounded coordinator proposes revised onboarding sequences; humans retain selection and professional decisions.

Competing future: facilities retain locally governed workflows and modest deterministic tools because evidence quality, cost or worker trust makes broader automation unsuitable.

Falsification question: Would assisted candidate ranking or task orchestration worsen exclusion or readiness ambiguity compared with a transparent human-reviewed process? State what observation would make you reduce, redesign or stop assistance. Human accountability does not disappear in any scenario.
