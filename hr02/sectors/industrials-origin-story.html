HR02 — Talent Acquisition — Recruiting and Onboarding: The Origin Story

SuccessLabs Academy · GICS03 Industrials · OmniWorks Industrial Systems Ltd. · IIL — Industry Immersion Lab · Level 0 → Pre-Foundation · Sprint · 120 minutes · Edition 2026-10-31

Mission: Architecting Experiences for a Better World.

1. Your one promise

Design one candidate-to-ready-worker handoff that protects fair selection and prevents an accepted offer from being mistaken for permission to work.

Produce one Talent-to-Readiness Build Card. Use synthetic records and a paper prototype. Beginners explain the decision in plain language; experienced learners challenge the same decision's assumptions. Configuration and implementation research remain in the supporting case outside this sprint.

2. Read the enterprise and sources accurately

OmniWorks is aspirational: 74,000 employees, 36 countries, 61 manufacturing/engineering/assembly facilities and 320 service depots. The 61 is a mixed-facility count, not a confirmed plant-only count. Engineering, maintenance, production and field service shape the workforce problem. Case targets and victory criteria are aspirations, not achieved results. This edition adds a bounded synthetic episode; it does not assert a deployment.

Source-scoped lead: Arjun Rao, Global Talent Acquisition Product Owner; linked Onboarding lead Priya Nair, Global Workforce Readiness Lead. HR02a's 300-technician demand within 90 days and adverse-impact signal are aspirational case study triggers. HR02b supplies the separate readiness case, including missing certification, badge and payroll inputs. Its Priya Nair and Leila Haddad designations conflict with enterprise personas; retain them as source-scoped characters. Recruiting and Onboarding remain separate source cases within one learning theme, not two sprints.

Existing enterprise anchors: OW-M17 — risk/security; OW-M18 — workforce/adoption; OW-M19 — delivery readiness. The workforce blueprint also inherits OW-M03, OW-M09 and OW-M11. Keep those IDs unchanged. New IDs prefixed OW-TOS-HR02 are educational additions, not previously approved client requirements.

3. Origins: five closed historical teaching windows

These windows group teaching ideas, not universal invention dates or OmniWorks corporate biography. Adoption overlaps; older practices can coexist through the present. Each generation changes the medium without automatically resolving ownership, evidence or authority.

1970–1984 — Plant vacancy notices and personal referrals answer staffing gaps; informal selection can hide job criteria and exclude capable applicants.

1985–1994 — Personnel requisitions and structured interviews make demand visible; an approved offer still says little about day-one readiness.

1995–2004 — Applicant-tracking systems and HRIS separate applicants from employees; manual rekeying can change identity or lose approval evidence.

2005–2014 — Career sites and workflow-driven preboarding connect candidates to tasks; document completion can still mask missing PPE or qualification.

2015–2025 — Skills-based sourcing, accessible journeys and onboarding orchestration improve coordination; screening bias and false readiness remain possible.

Recall prompt: reconstruct the five responses as problem → process/application response → unfinished problem. Which old failure could survive in the newest system?

4. One persona-led dilemma

Scene 1

Arjun receives the service-growth hiring demand. Filling vacancies quickly matters to customer uptime, but skipping job-relevant assessment could increase rework and unfair exclusion. He begins with funded demand and essential skills rather than a product shortlist.

Scene 2

Reveal: the source screening pilot has unequal progression and incomplete explanations. Arjun pauses the contested automated ranking and preserves a structured human selection route. A quicker funnel is not evidence of fairer selection.

Scene 3

After a synthetic candidate accepts, Priya finds the onboarding dashboard green because documents are complete. Rafael Costa, Safety and Compliance Director in HR02b, asks for current task prerequisites. Reveal: SYN-C201 has an induction completion but no practical assessment and an unconfirmed badge.

Scene 4

Arjun and Priya revise one handoff: approved selection, accepted offer, established employment and verified work readiness are distinct states. The candidate receives one clear status and human contact rather than contradictory automated messages.

Decision pause: Choose centralized recruiting with local readiness ownership, site-owned hiring, or a shared journey team. Which control and candidate experience would you trade for speed, and which must remain?

5. Responsibilities, ownership and human authority

Demand/positions (workforce and budget owners): approved need, job criteria and funding; recruiting consumes them.

Recruiting/ATS (TA owner): candidate, application, assessment evidence, selection decision and offer. SAP Recruiting is one possible example; no platform choice is assumed.

Onboarding orchestration (readiness lead): task dependencies and journey status. Each task owner supplies evidence; an orchestration flag cannot create competence.

Workforce core (HR owner): authoritative employment after approved hire; payroll owns pay setup; IAM/plant security own access outcomes; learning owns assessed qualification.

Authorized hiring panel makes selection with job-relevant evidence and review routes; Rafael/site safety authority approves restricted work under local policy.

Design boundary: A hiring decision does not authorize plant work; automated screening cannot replace accountable selection or deny a meaningful review. Proposed responsibilities are learning architecture, not proof that the aspirational enterprise already operates this design. Select technology only after the business rule and accountable owner are clear. SAP examples illustrate capabilities, not mandatory product choices.

7. One build card: Talent-to-Readiness Build Card

Synthetic experiment: Use SYN-REQ201 funded maintenance role, SYN-C201 candidate and SYN-O201 accepted offer. Build one state chain from demand through assessment to readiness. The missing practical assessment and badge confirmation are new synthetic inputs.

Fill these nine fields on one page: purpose and affected person; current/proposed state and source evidence; identity/object definitions; business date/time and version; application/data owners; human approval and appeal route; chosen option versus alternative with trade-off; exception/fallback and automation boundary; three test observations and revision.

Minimum design reasoning: Separate candidate identity from employment identity with a governed mapping. Transfer only necessary data at the approved point; define purpose/retention and accommodation discovery. Do not import all assessment notes into operational access systems.

Do not invent country rules, safety thresholds, collective-agreement terms, live data or missing approvals. Record unknowns with an owner and discovery question. Support an accessible human route for the worker. Consider fatigue, learning opportunity, avoidable travel/rework or resource use where the dilemma provides evidence; do not infer environmental savings.

8. Exactly three tabletop tests

OW-TOS-HR02-T01 — Selection explanation

Reveal/input: A candidate contests the automated score and logs do not explain it.

Expected design response: Hold score-dependent progression/rejection, preserve evidence and use a job-relevant human review with an accessible candidate response.

OW-TOS-HR02-T02 — Readiness reveal

Reveal/input: Offer and documents are complete, but practical assessment and badge outcome are missing.

Expected design response: Keep employment/onboarding statuses distinct; block restricted release, assign evidence owners and give a clear day-one alternative.

OW-TOS-HR02-T03 — Duplicate accepted-offer event

Reveal/input: Onboarding receives SYN-O201 twice and one payroll input is absent.

Expected design response: Do not create duplicate employment/tasks; reconcile source and consumer states, expose payroll exception and retain one accountable journey owner.

For each test, record input → expected response → actual observed paper response → evidence note → pass/revise. Expected responses are proposed acceptance criteria, not executed results. Publication executes none of these tabletop tests. A bypass of the protected decision requires redesign, even if the card is complete.

9. Traceability and honest measures

OW-TOS-HR02-REQ01 — Trace approved demand through job-relevant selection to an accepted offer. Source connection: supporting HR case mandate and OW-M18. Assessed by T01; card must identify the authoritative fact and owner.

OW-TOS-HR02-REQ02 — Keep hire, document completion and verified readiness separate, with owned exceptions. Source connection: HR case governance/scenario injection and OW-M17/OW-M19. Assessed by T02–T03; card records exception, authority and recovery condition.

OW-TOS-HR02-ADR01 — Use a shared journey contract with separate ATS, onboarding and operational authorities; compare a single undifferentiated 'hired/ready' status and its false-confidence cost. Compare the stated alternative and trade-off; this is a proposed educational decision that learners may reject with evidence.

OW-TOS-HR02-EXP01 — this synthetic card and T01–T03 extend OW-M19's traceable-pilot discipline. They do not complete the wider case's tests or rollout.

OW-TOS-HR02-M01 — completed mandatory card fields / 9 × 100; learning completeness only. OW-TOS-HR02-M02 — Count explained state transitions and record whether T01 preserved review and T02 preserved the readiness hold; no hiring-quality benefit claimed. No baseline, target attainment or operating benefit is inferred from an empty result.

OW-TOS-HR02-EV01 — initially unfilled evidence slot for observed tabletop results, revisions and reviewer questions. Existing case success metrics remain separate planned outcomes.

10. AI-driven exploration: scenarios, not predictions

These are scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps. All require approved purpose, evidence, worker participation, accountable ownership and proportionate controls.

2026–2030 — Assist: Assistance could draft candidate communications and summarize authorized assessment evidence without deciding selection.

2031–2040 — Connect: Recruiting and onboarding could exchange purpose-limited readiness events while retaining separate records and owners.

2041–2050 — Adapt: Adaptive talent ecosystems could suggest apprenticeships/internal routes as skills change; applicants retain transparent review and human decisions.

Competing future: A structured human-led hiring and locally coordinated induction model may outperform automated ranking where evidence and access are uneven.

Disconfirmation question: Would the connected journey increase unexplained rejection or false-ready statuses compared with a simpler human-led route? Record the evidence that would make you stop or change the hypothesis.

11. Reflection and Pre-Foundation handoff

Which state was falsely green? What evidence makes selection defensible? What can the candidate challenge?

Pre-Foundation: Define requisition, candidate, application, offer, hire and readiness states; draft one minimal handoff contract and task dependency map, then learn Recruiting and Onboarding in their separate source cases. Carry forward the same card, unresolved questions and proposed acceptance criteria. Deeper implementation, vendor comparisons and the wider capstone stay outside this Origin Story.
