## 1. Start with the story

Ingrid Sørensen works with a close colleague in a financial-services group with high expectations and tight controls; they exchange messages all day, yet a disagreement keeps returning.

**The person facing the choice:** Ingrid Sørensen — Procurement and Third-Party Risk Lead (OCF-P16).  
**A trusted colleague:** Ravi Patel — Payments and Treasury Operations Lead (OCF-P14).  
**Someone affected:** Amelia Brooks — SME Customer Representative (OCF-P15).

Ingrid Sørensen asks what matters most before choosing. Ravi Patel asks what evidence and which support would help. Amelia Brooks asks how the choice will feel to the people around them.

**What they notice:** Retelling the conversation shows each assumed the other's view and neither asked a question.

**Pause:** What would you want to be true before you decide? Ask the colleague one curious question before choosing.

**Case boundary:** OmniCapital Financial Group Ltd. is an aspirational case study (USD 46B financial group; USD 1.4T AUM; 58M customers; 32 countries). This story, its people and its dialogue are proposed learning scenarios, not corporate history, real individuals or achieved results. Preserve the persona IDs above. Reflection is voluntary and personal; share only what you choose to.

## 2. Trace the origin: 1970–2026

These overlapping periods are teaching lenses, not invention dates or a universal sequence. Earlier practices can coexist with newer ones.

- **1970s–1980s:** Relationships are learned from family and community; workplaces avoid personal topics.
- **1980s–1990s:** Communication-skills training spreads; listening is taught as a technique.
- **2000s–2010s:** Digital messaging multiplies contact; misunderstandings multiply too.
- **2020s–2026:** Assisted writing and translation help communication; trust still depends on listening.

**Mindset shift:** Listen to understand before you try to be understood.

**Pause:** Which old habit survives in a newer tool? Who bears its cost?

## 3. Explore possible futures: 2030–2050

**2030 experiment:** Try restating the other person's view once a day before answering.

**2040 question:** Ask how relationships stay close when so much talk is written and fast.

**2050 scene:** Imagine communities where repair is a normal part of working together.

**Competing view:** Some people prefer direct, brief exchanges; clear and short may suit them better than long conversations.

All future passages are design hypotheses, not forecasts. Test a small useful part today rather than assume the future will arrive as described.

## 4. Draw the smallest useful map

**Practice — OCF-LIT08-OS-CAP01:** relationship practice. A practice is something you do repeatedly; it is not a one-time insight.

**Steps — OCF-LIT08-OS-VS01:** Listen → restate → share → repair → set boundaries → follow up.

**Things to keep distinct:** conversation, assumption, repair, boundary and follow-up.

**Five life areas:** spiritual, social, academic, professional and personal. Note which areas this practice touches and which it leaves alone.

**Protected boundary:** A communication practice does not fix an unsafe relationship; safety comes first.

## 5. Build one Practice Card

Use synthetic case **OCF-LIT08-OS-SYN001**. Synthetic person FIN-LP300, colleague FIN-LP301, conversation notes FIN-NT300. Keep everything on one card.

1. **Need:** the situation, who is affected and one missing fact.
2. **Values:** two or three values at stake, in your own words.
3. **Commitment:** one small action you can keep within a week, and who will know.
4. **Evidence:** what you will notice, record or ask to see whether it helps.
5. **Alternative:** Compare explaining your view first with restating theirs first.
6. **When to get support:** one sign that means you should talk to a trusted person or a qualified professional. 
7. **Review:** the date you will look again and what would make you stop or change course.

**Requirement — OCF-LIT08-OS-REQ01:** link one relationship practice hypothesis to a small action and an observed follow-up, including evidence that it did not help.

**Decision — OCF-LIT08-OS-ADR01:** record your chosen option and the alternative from item 5, one trade-off, and what would change your mind.

## 6. Run three real-life tests

### T01 — A disagreement becomes personal

**Expected response:** Pause, restate the other view, then share your own.

### T02 — An apology is overdue

**Expected response:** Name the harm plainly and offer repair without conditions.

### T03 — A boundary is crossed again

**Expected response:** Restate the boundary calmly and decide the consequence in advance.

For each, record **situation → what you would do → what you notice → revision → retry**. These are sprint-local tests OCF-LIT08-OS-T01/T02/T03, supporting OCF-LIT08-OS-REQ01. Completing the lesson is not a measured change in your life.

**Signal — OCF-LIT08-OS-KPI01:** Notice how often a difficult conversation included a restatement of the other view, noted privately; no outcome is promised. Choose your own baseline and review date. No improvement is promised.

**Acceptance:** the card names your values, keeps the boundary above, handles all three tests after revision and can be explained in 60 seconds. Anything unknown stays visible.

## 7. Use your 120 minutes

- **0–20:** read the origin timeline; explain what changed and what did not.
- **20–45:** role-play the story; ask the person, colleague and affected person one useful question each.
- **45–65:** map your own situation across the five life areas.
- **65–100:** complete the single Practice Card and compare the alternative.
- **100–115:** run the three tests and revise.
- **115–120:** explain your choice in 60 seconds and choose a first small action.
