# The course was finished; nothing had changed at work
## LIT06 — Architecting Continuous Learning

**SuccessLabs Academy · OmniVerse Energy Ltd. · Level 0 → Pre-Foundation**  
**IIL — Industry Immersion Lab · Version 1.0 · 10 November 2026**  
**Planning reference:** HUB-ORIGIN-LIT06-ENE-sprint (not an LMS ID)  
**Format:** Origin Story Sprint · 120 minutes · Free — Community/Citizen Edition

> **Your promise:** In two hours, design one learning plan that links a goal, a resource, real practice and review.

**One story · One practice card · Three real-life tests**

## 1. Start with the story

Rajiv Kapoor completes an online course in an energy transition programme with tight deadlines and collects the certificate, yet daily work looks the same.

**The person facing the choice:** Rajiv Kapoor — Chief Digital and Information Officer (CDIO) (OVE-P05).  
**A trusted colleague:** Hannah Fischer — Director of Enterprise Architecture (OVE-P12).  
**Someone affected:** Elena Brooks — VP Energy Procurement, fictional industrial customer (OVE-P15).

Rajiv Kapoor asks what matters most before choosing. Hannah Fischer asks what evidence and which support would help. Elena Brooks asks how the choice will feel to the people around them.

**What they notice:** Looking at the calendar, no practice time was scheduled and no one was asked to apply what was learned.

**Pause:** What would you want to be true before you decide? Ask the colleague one curious question before choosing.

**Case boundary:** OmniVerse Energy Ltd. is an aspirational case study (USD 85B revenue; 68,000 employees; 42 countries). This story, its people and its dialogue are proposed learning scenarios, not corporate history, real individuals or achieved results. Preserve the persona IDs above. Reflection is voluntary and personal; share only what you choose to.

## 2. Trace the origin: 1970–2026

These overlapping periods are teaching lenses, not invention dates or a universal sequence. Earlier practices can coexist with newer ones.

- **1970s–1980s:** Learning is mostly classroom training and apprenticeship.
- **1980s–1990s:** Corporate training expands; completion is the measure.
- **2000s–2010s:** E-learning and MOOCs widen access; completion rates stay low.
- **2020s–2026:** Assisted learning tools personalise content; practice and feedback still decide whether it sticks.

**Mindset shift:** Count what you apply, not only what you finish.

**Pause:** Which old habit survives in a newer tool? Who bears its cost?

## 3. Explore possible futures: 2030–2050

**2030 experiment:** Schedule one real use of a new skill within a week of learning it.

**2040 question:** Ask how you will keep learning when tools and roles change quickly.

**2050 scene:** Imagine learning woven into daily work, with communities teaching each other.

**Competing view:** Some people learn best by doing without formal courses; a project-first route may suit them better.

All future passages are design hypotheses, not forecasts. Test a small useful part today rather than assume the future will arrive as described.

## 4. Draw the smallest useful map

**Practice — OVE-LIT06-OS-CAP01:** learning plan. A practice is something you do repeatedly; it is not a one-time insight.

**Steps — OVE-LIT06-OS-VS01:** Set a learning goal → choose resources → practise in real work → get feedback → review.

**Things to keep distinct:** goal, resource, practice, feedback and review.

**Five life areas:** spiritual, social, academic, professional and personal. Note which areas this practice touches and which it leaves alone.

**Protected boundary:** Finishing a course is not the same as being able to use what it taught.

## 5. Build one Practice Card

Use synthetic case **OVE-LIT06-OS-SYN001**. Synthetic person ENE-LP300, course ENE-CR300, practice plan ENE-PP300 (empty). Keep everything on one card.

1. **Need:** the situation, who is affected and one missing fact.
2. **Values:** two or three values at stake, in your own words.
3. **Commitment:** one small action you can keep within a week, and who will know.
4. **Evidence:** what you will notice, record or ask to see whether it helps.
5. **Alternative:** Compare taking another course with applying what you already learned to real work.
6. **When to get support:** one sign that means you should talk to a trusted person or a qualified professional. 
7. **Review:** the date you will look again and what would make you stop or change course.

**Requirement — OVE-LIT06-OS-REQ01:** link one learning plan hypothesis to a small action and an observed follow-up, including evidence that it did not help.

**Decision — OVE-LIT06-OS-ADR01:** record your chosen option and the alternative from item 5, one trade-off, and what would change your mind.

## 6. Run three real-life tests

### T01 — Learning has no practice time

**Expected response:** Schedule a small real use within a week.

### T02 — The resource is too advanced

**Expected response:** Step back to a prerequisite and note the gap.

### T03 — A colleague could teach this

**Expected response:** Ask for a conversation and offer something in return.

For each, record **situation → what you would do → what you notice → revision → retry**. These are sprint-local tests OVE-LIT06-OS-T01/T02/T03, supporting OVE-LIT06-OS-REQ01. Completing the lesson is not a measured change in your life.

**Signal — OVE-LIT06-OS-KPI01:** Notice how many learning goals were practised in real work and reviewed each quarter; no outcome is promised. Choose your own baseline and review date. No improvement is promised.

**Acceptance:** the card names your values, keeps the boundary above, handles all three tests after revision and can be explained in 60 seconds. Anything unknown stays visible.

## 7. Use your 120 minutes

- **0–20:** read the origin timeline; explain what changed and what did not.
- **20–45:** role-play the story; ask the person, colleague and affected person one useful question each.
- **45–65:** map your own situation across the five life areas.
- **65–100:** complete the single Practice Card and compare the alternative.
- **100–115:** run the three tests and revise.
- **115–120:** explain your choice in 60 seconds and choose a first small action.
