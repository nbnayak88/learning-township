# The promotion fit the plan; it did not fit who they were becoming
## LIT01 — Architecting My Purpose & Identity

**SuccessLabs Academy · OmniVibe Consumer Ltd. · Level 0 → Pre-Foundation**  
**IIL — Industry Immersion Lab · Version 1.0 · 10 November 2026**  
**Planning reference:** HUB-ORIGIN-LIT01-CD-sprint (not an LMS ID)  
**Format:** Origin Story Sprint · 120 minutes · Free — Community/Citizen Edition  
**Status:** Published learning content; not an enrolled course or certification.

> **Your promise:** In two hours, design one purpose-and-identity practice that tests a real choice against your values.

**One story · One practice card · Three real-life tests**

## 1. Start with the story

Oliver Brooks works in a retail and brand business driven by seasons and sales targets and is offered a promotion that matches the career plan written years ago. The offer feels right on paper, yet Oliver Brooks is uneasy about what it asks them to give up.

**The person facing the choice:** Oliver Brooks — Store Operations Director (OVC-P06).  
**A trusted colleague:** Adrian Tan — Group CFO (OVC-P02).  
**Someone affected:** Emma Clarke — Loyalty Customer Representative (OVC-P15).

Oliver Brooks asks what matters most before choosing. Adrian Tan asks what evidence and which support would help. Emma Clarke asks how the choice will feel to the people around them.

**What they notice:** Looking back over a month of notes, Oliver Brooks finds the most energy comes from mentoring others, which the new role would remove. The plan was written before that became clear.

**Pause:** What would you want to be true before you decide? Ask the colleague one curious question before choosing.

**Case boundary:** OmniVibe Consumer Ltd. is an aspirational case study (USD 28B consumer group; 72,000 employees; 48 countries). This story, its people and its dialogue are proposed learning scenarios, not corporate history, real individuals or achieved results. Preserve the persona IDs above. Reflection is voluntary and personal; share only what you choose to.

## 2. Trace the origin: 1970–2026

These overlapping periods are teaching lenses, not invention dates or a universal sequence. Earlier practices can coexist with newer ones.

- **1970s–1980s:** Career is often seen as a ladder set by employers; personal purpose is a private matter.
- **1980s–1990s:** Self-help and career planning popularize goal setting; goals can borrow others' definitions of success.
- **2000s–2010s:** Coaching and strengths tools spread; identity is explored, but often as a profile rather than a practice.
- **2020s–2026:** Reflection tools and assisted prompts are everywhere; purpose still has to be tested in real choices.

**Mindset shift:** Treat purpose as something you practise and revise, not something you discover once.

**Pause:** Which old habit survives in a newer tool? Who bears its cost?

## 3. Explore possible futures: 2030–2050

**2030 experiment:** Try a short monthly values review with one commitment you can keep.

**2040 question:** Ask whether your daily choices still match your values when roles and work change faster than plans.

**2050 scene:** Imagine people redefining success many times in a life, supported by communities that expect change rather than punish it.

**Competing view:** Some people prefer a fixed plan with clear steps; a settled plan can bring calm where constant review would not.

All future passages are design hypotheses, not forecasts. Test a small useful part today rather than assume the future will arrive as described.

## 4. Draw the smallest useful map

**Practice — OVC-LIT01-OS-CAP01:** purpose-and-identity practice. A practice is something you do repeatedly; it is not a one-time insight.

**Steps — OVC-LIT01-OS-VS01:** Notice → name values → test in a small choice → reflect → commit or revise.

**Things to keep distinct:** values, roles, expectations, energy signals and commitments.

**Five life areas:** spiritual, social, academic, professional and personal. Note which areas this practice touches and which it leaves alone.

**Protected boundary:** A purpose statement does not predict success or settle a decision by itself; it is tested in real choices.

## 5. Build one Practice Card

Use synthetic case **OVC-LIT01-OS-SYN001**. Synthetic person CD-LP300, offer CD-OF300, month of reflection notes CD-NT300. Keep everything on one card.

1. **Need:** the situation, who is affected and one missing fact.
2. **Values:** two or three values at stake, in your own words.
3. **Commitment:** one small action you can keep within a week, and who will know.
4. **Evidence:** what you will notice, record or ask to see whether it helps.
5. **Alternative:** Compare following the old plan with running a small experiment first.
6. **When to get support:** one sign that means you should talk to a trusted person or a qualified professional. 
7. **Review:** the date you will look again and what would make you stop or change course.

**Requirement — OVC-LIT01-OS-REQ01:** link one purpose-and-identity practice hypothesis to a small action and an observed follow-up, including evidence that it did not help.

**Decision — OVC-LIT01-OS-ADR01:** record your chosen option and the alternative from item 5, one trade-off, and what would change your mind.

## 6. Run three real-life tests

### T01 — Values and role pull in different directions

**Expected response:** Name both values and choose a small experiment before a large decision.

### T02 — Someone else defines success for them

**Expected response:** Separate others' expectations from their own and record the difference.

### T03 — The choice can only be reversed for a short time

**Expected response:** Set a review date and a simple exit option; do not decide in a rush.

For each, record **situation → what you would do → what you notice → revision → retry**. These are sprint-local tests OVC-LIT01-OS-T01/T02/T03, supporting OVC-LIT01-OS-REQ01. Completing the lesson is not a measured change in your life.

**Signal — OVC-LIT01-OS-KPI01:** Notice how often a chosen commitment matches a stated value, noted in a weekly check; no outcome is promised. Choose your own baseline and review date. No improvement is promised.

**Acceptance:** the card names your values, keeps the boundary above, handles all three tests after revision and can be explained in 60 seconds. Anything unknown stays visible.

## 7. Use your 120 minutes

- **0–20:** read the origin timeline; explain what changed and what did not.
- **20–45:** role-play the story; ask the person, colleague and affected person one useful question each.
- **45–65:** map your own situation across the five life areas.
- **65–100:** complete the single Practice Card and compare the alternative.
- **100–115:** run the three tests and revise.
- **115–120:** explain your choice in 60 seconds and choose a first small action.
