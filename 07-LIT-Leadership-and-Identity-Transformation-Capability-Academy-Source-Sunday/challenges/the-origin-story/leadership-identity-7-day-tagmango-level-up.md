### History and future editorial note

Leadership and questions of identity existed long before 1970. One verified milestone in this pack is Robert K. Greenleaf's 1970 essay, The Servant as Leader [1]. It represents one leadership tradition, not the origin or sole model of leadership.

Other “picture 1970” scenes are illustrative teaching devices, not claims that all leaders behaved alike. Present-day scenes are exercises, and all 2050 scenes are imagined possibilities rather than predictions.

Identity here means a person's evolving understanding of who they are and how they choose to act. A short challenge explores chosen behaviours; it cannot measure someone's worth or certify a transformed identity.

## Day 1 — Choose How You Want to Show Up

🎯 **DAY 1 OF 7 — THE LEADERSHIP MINDSET SHIFT**

Welcome! Today, start with a choice rather than a title.

⏱️ **10–15 minutes: read, reflect and share.**

### 💭 A meeting needs direction

People are discussing a community session. You have an idea, but several questions remain unanswered.

Leadership could mean explaining your idea clearly. It could also mean asking what others need before proposing a solution.

You do not need to be the most senior person to make a useful contribution.

### 🕰️ A brief journey: 1970 → 2050

**1970 — A real milestone:** Robert K. Greenleaf first published The Servant as Leader [1]. It is one influential example of asking how leadership serves others.

**Today:** Consider how your choices affect people across classrooms, workplaces, communities and digital spaces.

**Imagine 2050:** AI could handle more coordination and analysis. People would still need to choose purposes, exercise judgement and take responsibility for their influence.

The future scene is a possibility, not a prediction.

### 🧠 Mindset shift

**“Leadership starts when I get the title.” → “I can practise a useful leadership behaviour now.”**

### 🌍 People and impact

A clear explanation can help someone participate. A careless interruption can make it harder to contribute.

Start with an influence you can take responsibility for.

### ✨ A fresh angle

**“I do not know yet” can be a leadership contribution.**

It makes uncertainty visible and creates room to investigate.

## Day 2 — Make a Value Visible

🧭 **DAY 2 OF 7 — FROM WORDS TO CHOICES**

Yesterday, you chose how you want to show up. Today, connect that intention to an observable behaviour.

⏱️ **10–15 minutes. One value is enough.**

### 💭 “I value respect.”

What would someone notice?

Perhaps you let a person finish. Perhaps you explain a disagreement without dismissing them. Perhaps you keep information private.

A value becomes more useful when it informs a choice.

### 🕰️ A thinking journey: 1970 → 2050

Illustrative scenes:

**Picture 1970:** Someone chooses between taking credit and acknowledging the team.

**Today:** Values show up in messages, decisions, meetings and online conduct.

**Imagine 2050:** AI can draft polished statements of values. People still need to decide whether their actions match those statements.

The question: **“What would this value look like in practice?”**

### 🧠 Mindset shift

**“I can name my values.” → “I can explain one choice they guide.”**

At SuccessLabs, three useful starting points are:

- **Community:** Make space for others to contribute.
- **Curiosity:** Ask before assuming.
- **Courage:** Raise a relevant concern respectfully.

You can choose another value that matters to you.

### 🌍 People and impact

Your choice may affect who is heard, who receives credit and who carries the work.

Consider the experience of someone with less visibility or influence in the situation.

### ✨ A fresh angle

**Values can create a real trade-off.**

Being helpful does not mean agreeing to every request. Honesty may require admitting that your capacity is limited.

### ✍️ Your 7-minute action

Reply:

- **My chosen value:** ___
- **The situation:** ___
- **A behaviour that expresses the value:** ___
- **A choice that might conflict with it:** ___
- **My practical commitment:** “When ___, I will ___.”

**Example:** Curiosity; planning a session; ask one question before suggesting a solution; jumping to an answer; “When a request is unclear, I will ask what result is needed.”

### 🧵 Follow the thread

How does this behaviour support your Day 1 intention? Keep the connection. The behaviour should be something you can attempt, not a result you require from someone else.

🎉 **Today's win:** You have turned a value into a practical choice.

## Day 3 — Notice the Moment Before Your Response

🔍 **DAY 3 OF 7 — FROM ASSUMPTION TO A DELIBERATE CHOICE**

You have a value and a behaviour. Today, explore a situation where practising it may be difficult.

⏱️ **10–15 minutes. A fictional example is enough.**

### 💭 A teammate does not reply

You think: “They are not interested.”

What do you actually know?

You know a reply has not arrived. Their reason remains unknown.

A useful leadership habit is to distinguish an observation from the meaning you have added.

### 🕰️ A thinking journey: 1970 → 2050

Illustrative scenes:

**Picture 1970:** Someone interprets silence in a conversation.

**Today:** A delayed message or brief reply can invite assumptions.

**Imagine 2050:** AI summarises communication, but a summary may still miss context. People need to question interpretations.

The question: **“What did I observe, and what am I assuming?”**

### 🧠 Mindset shift

**“My first interpretation is the truth.” → “What else might explain this?”**

You do not need to ignore a real problem. You can investigate before concluding.

### 🌍 People and impact

Assumptions can shape who is trusted or given opportunities.

A respectful question creates a chance to understand without demanding private explanations.

### ✨ A fresh angle

**You can revise a story about yourself, too.**

Instead of “I am always a poor communicator,” try describing a specific moment: “My message did not explain the next step clearly. I can revise it.”

A behaviour can be examined without making it your whole identity.

### ✍️ Your 7-minute action

Choose a low-pressure or fictional situation. Reply:

- **The situation:** ___
- **What I actually observed:** ___
- **My first interpretation:** ___
- **Another possible explanation:** ___
- **A response aligned with my chosen value:** ___

**Example:** No reply to a planning message; the person may not have seen it; I can ask whether the timing works and make the requested next step clearer.

You may draft the response without sending it.

### 🧵 Follow the thread

Does your proposed response express your Day 2 value and Day 1 purpose? Keep the reasoning, even if you later change the response.

🎉 **Today's win:** You have separated an observation, an assumption and a possible choice.

## Day 4 — Listen to Understand

👂 **DAY 4 OF 7 — MAKE ROOM FOR ANOTHER PERSPECTIVE**

Yesterday, you questioned an assumption. Today, practise an invitation to understand.

⏱️ **10–15 minutes. A draft or rehearsal counts.**

### 💭 The quiet participant

Someone says little during a planning discussion.

You could assume they have nothing to add. Or you could offer a low-pressure opportunity: “Would you like to share a thought now or in writing later?”

They can also choose not to contribute.

Leadership can create an opening without forcing participation.

### 🕰️ A thinking journey: 1970 → 2050

Illustrative scenes:

**Picture 1970:** A leader makes time to hear a different view.

**Today:** People contribute through conversations, written notes and digital channels.

**Imagine 2050:** AI helps summarise contributions. People still need to check meaning, acknowledge sources and leave room for disagreement.

The question: **“Have I understood before deciding?”**

### 🧠 Mindset shift

**“I am waiting to give my answer.” → “I am trying to understand their perspective.”**

Try a simple sequence:

1. Ask an open question.
2. Leave room for an answer.
3. Summarise what you understood.
4. Invite a correction.

### 🌍 People and impact

Not everyone prefers to speak immediately or in a large group. An alternative route can help, but ask what is useful rather than assuming.

### ✨ A fresh angle

**Listening does not require agreement.**

You can understand a view and still explain a different decision respectfully.

### ✍️ Your 7-minute action

Draft or rehearse:

- **The situation:** ___
- **My open question:** ___
- **How I would make room for a response:** ___
- **My summary:** “What I understand is ___.”
- **My check:** “Have I understood correctly, or missed something?”

For a solo exercise, invent a short response and label it fictional. You do not need to interview anyone or report their words.

### 🧵 Follow the thread

How does this invitation support your chosen value? A useful check is whether your summary reflects what was said—not whether the other person agrees with you.

🎉 **Today's win:** You have a small listening practice and a way to check your understanding.

## Day 5 — Make a Small, Honest Commitment

🤝 **DAY 5 OF 7 — BUILD TRUST THROUGH FOLLOW-THROUGH**

You have practised asking and listening. Today, choose a commitment that fits your purpose and capacity.

⏱️ **10–15 minutes. Small is useful.**

### 💭 “I will take care of everything.”

It sounds generous. But what does it mean? By when? What happens if you cannot?

Try: “I can draft the session agenda by Thursday. I cannot manage registrations this week. Can we agree who owns that?”

The second statement makes the contribution and boundary clearer.

### 🕰️ A thinking journey: 1970 → 2050

Illustrative scenes:

**Picture 1970:** A person agrees a task and reports back.

**Today:** Commitments may be scattered across messages and meetings.

**Imagine 2050:** AI tracks actions and reminders. People still need to agree realistic commitments and communicate changes.

The question: **“What can others reasonably rely on?”**

### 🧠 Mindset shift

**“Say yes to show commitment.” → “Make an honest promise and communicate when circumstances change.”**

### 🌍 People and impact

An unclear promise can create hidden work for someone else.

A realistic boundary helps a group make a better plan.

### ✨ A fresh angle

**Serving others can include a respectful “no” or “not yet.”**

Your contribution can be generous without being unlimited.

### ✍️ Your 7-minute action

Choose a low-risk task or use the fictional session example. Reply:

- **My purpose:** ___
- **One action I can commit to:** ___
- **When I would do it:** ___
- **What “done” would look like:** ___
- **One boundary or dependency:** ___
- **If circumstances change, I will:** ___

**Example:** Draft three agenda points for review; by an agreed time; a readable note shared through an agreed route; someone else confirms the session objective; communicate early if blocked.

This can remain a draft commitment. You do not need to send a message or accept a real task.

### 🧵 Follow the thread

The commitment connects your intention with an observable action. Later, you can review whether you attempted or completed it and what you learned.

🎉 **Today's win:** You have a small commitment with a clear boundary.

## Day 6 — Rehearse, Observe and Adjust

🧪 **DAY 6 OF 7 — EVIDENCE FOR LEARNING**

You have a small commitment. Today, rehearse it or review a suitable attempt.

⏱️ **10–15 minutes. Private practice is enough.**

### 💭 You planned to ask before advising

During a rehearsal, you notice that your first sentence still gives the solution.

That does not prove you cannot lead. It gives you something specific to adjust.

A small experiment asks: **“What happens when I try this behaviour in this situation?”**

### 🕰️ A thinking journey: 1970 → 2050

Illustrative scenes:

**Picture 1970:** Someone reflects after a conversation and tries a different approach.

**Today:** A short note can connect intention, action and feedback.

**Imagine 2050:** AI can help rehearse a scenario. Its feedback may be incomplete or wrong, so people still use judgement and appropriate human perspectives.

The question: **“What did I actually do, and what can I learn?”**

### 🧠 Mindset shift

**“Did I prove I am a leader?” → “What did this attempt teach me?”**

### 🌍 People and impact

If practising with another person, seek willingness and choose an appropriate situation. Do not make someone else responsible for validating your identity.

You can also practise alone using the shared fictional case.

### ✨ A fresh angle

**An uncomfortable attempt is not automatically a bad attempt—and comfort alone is not proof of usefulness.**

Look at the chosen behaviour and the available evidence.

### ✍️ Your 7-minute action

Choose one:

- Rehearse your open question and summary.
- Review whether your commitment is clear and realistic.
- Reflect on a low-pressure attempt you already made.

Reply:

- **Behaviour I intended to practise:** ___
- **Situation or rehearsal:** ___
- **What I actually tried or reviewed:** ___
- **One observation:** ___
- **What remains unknown:** ___
- **One adjustment for next time:** ___

If you have not attempted it, say **“Not tried yet”** and describe your plan. No pass/fail label is required.

### 🧵 Follow the thread

Compare the observation with your original intention. Observe the behaviour without treating one event or another person's reaction as a verdict on your identity.

🎉 **Today's win:** You have a specific learning point or a clear next attempt.

## Coach-only appendix — Personal reflection and project traceability

### Keep two uses of evidence distinct

| Personal reflection | Formal project traceability |
|---|---|
| Learner chooses a value and behaviour | Stakeholders validate a need and requirements |
| Notes support voluntary learning | Records support coverage, decisions and accountability |
| A rehearsal or observation informs a next choice | Tests verify specified requirements |
| No pass/fail judgement of identity | Test results and readiness issues need explicit status |
| Learner decides what to keep private | Project evidence follows agreed information governance |

Do not apply testing language to a person's worth, character or identity. Evidence about a behaviour in one context does not establish a fixed personality trait.

The Origin Story can guide a person's purpose and a project's purpose without making them the same thing.

### Fictional personal learning thread

**INTENT-01:** Contribute to a community planning session with curiosity and reliability.

| Chosen value | Behaviour to practise | Small experiment | Possible observation | Reflection / next choice |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Curiosity | Ask a clarifying question before advising | Rehearse a response to an unclear request | First draft gives advice before asking a question | Rewrite the opening and try another rehearsal |
| Community | Offer a choice of contribution routes | Draft an invitation to speak or respond in writing | Invitation leaves room to decline | Check whether the wording feels clear and non-pressuring |
| Courage | State a realistic commitment and boundary | Draft a task commitment with a date and dependency | Draft does not say what “done” means | Add a clear completion description and review capacity |

These are hypothetical examples, not observations about the learner. Initial status is planned / not yet attempted. Personal notes do not need to be public.

### Connection to requirements, testing and go-live

Suppose a learner later helps lead the BTM community-service improvement.

**OS-PROJ-01:** Learners need consistent session instructions and a suitable support route.

The project can maintain formal traceability while the person privately reflects on how they lead.

| Project need / requirement | Leadership action that supports the work | Formal evidence to seek | What personal reflection might ask |
|---|---|---|---|
| Validate the actual learner need | Invite relevant perspectives and distinguish observation from assumption | Agreed need statement, source references and unresolved questions | Did I make room for another view before proposing a solution? |
| Agree clear requirements and ownership | Summarise decisions and confirm responsibilities | Reviewed requirements, acceptance criteria and named owners | Did I explain my understanding and invite corrections? |
| Test relevant scenarios | Encourage honest reporting of failed or unperformed checks | Actual test results, evidence and defects | Did I respond constructively to evidence that challenged my preference? |
| Review readiness before go-live | Make unresolved issues visible and seek an authorised decision | Readiness review, support arrangements, approvals and issue ownership | Did I avoid making a promise beyond my authority or evidence? |
| Review the intended benefit after use | Ask what changed for people and where work moved | Outcome evidence, adoption feedback and limitations | Did I listen to impact as well as describe my intention? |

Project records are proposed examples, not completed work. A leadership behaviour supports sound delivery; it does not replace a test, approval or technical control.

### Facilitation notes

- Do not require vulnerability, public confessions or a dramatic identity declaration.
- Treat fictional scenarios and rehearsals as valid participation.
- Avoid ranking learners by openness, confidence, charisma or speaking frequency.
- On Day 2, ask whether the behaviour is within the learner's control.
- On Day 3, distinguish observed events from inferred motives.
- On Day 4, respect a person's option not to speak or provide feedback.
- On Day 5, value realistic boundaries rather than overcommitment.
- On Day 6, accept “Not tried yet” with a suitable plan.
- On Day 7, encourage a next experiment rather than a claim of permanent transformation.
- Community, Curiosity and Courage are guiding values; do not require a uniform identity.
- No promise of promotion, employment, certification, psychological change or automatic programme inclusion.
