# They said the team came first and scheduled over the team's holiday
## LIT04 — Architecting Authentic Leadership

**SuccessLabs Academy · OmniWorks Industrial Systems Ltd. · Level 0 → Pre-Foundation**  
**IIL — Industry Immersion Lab · Version 1.0 · 10 November 2026**

> **Your promise:** In two hours, design one leadership practice that lines up what you say, what you do and what you value.

**One story · One practice card · Three real-life tests**

## 1. Start with the story

Chen Wei tells their team in an industrial service organisation with constant fault calls that people come first, yet schedules a key review during the team's planned holiday.

**The person facing the choice:** Chen Wei — Chief Supply Chain & Procurement Officer (OW-P08).  
**A trusted colleague:** Hannah Fischer — Director, Enterprise Architecture (OW-P11).  
**Someone affected:** Elena Brooks — VP Asset Reliability, Transport Customer (OW-P13).

Chen Wei asks what matters most before choosing. Hannah Fischer asks what evidence and which support would help. Elena Brooks asks how the choice will feel to the people around them.

**What they notice:** Comparing the calendar with the stated values shows the words and the actions disagree, and the team has noticed.

**Pause:** What would you want to be true before you decide? Ask the colleague one curious question before choosing.

**Case boundary:** OmniWorks Industrial Systems Ltd. is an aspirational case study (USD 48B revenue; 74,000 employees; 36 countries; 61 plants and facilities; 320 service depots; 7,200 suppliers). This story, its people and its dialogue are proposed learning scenarios, not corporate history, real individuals or achieved results. Preserve the persona IDs above. Reflection is voluntary and personal; share only what you choose to.

## 2. Trace the origin: 1970–2026

These overlapping periods are teaching lenses, not invention dates or a universal sequence. Earlier practices can coexist with newer ones.

- **1970s–1980s:** Leadership is often equated with authority and decisiveness.
- **1980s–1990s:** Leadership development programmes spread; values statements appear on walls.
- **2000s–2010s:** Authentic and servant leadership ideas spread; gaps between words and actions stay visible.
- **2020s–2026:** Feedback tools and 360 surveys are common; leaders still choose what to do with what they hear.

**Mindset shift:** Treat feedback as data about the gap between words and actions.

**Pause:** Which old habit survives in a newer tool? Who bears its cost?

## 3. Explore possible futures: 2030–2050

**2030 experiment:** Share one commitment with the team and invite honest feedback on it.

**2040 question:** Ask how leaders will stay accountable when teams are distributed and decisions move quickly.

**2050 scene:** Imagine leaders and communities who expect honest correction and make it safe to give.

**Competing view:** Some teams prefer a clearly directive leader; a strong plan with less discussion can suit urgent work better.

All future passages are design hypotheses, not forecasts. Test a small useful part today rather than assume the future will arrive as described.

## 4. Draw the smallest useful map

**Practice — OW-LIT04-OS-CAP01:** authentic-leadership practice. A practice is something you do repeatedly; it is not a one-time insight.

**Steps — OW-LIT04-OS-VS01:** State the value → check the actions → listen → correct → follow up.

**Things to keep distinct:** stated value, action, feedback, gap and correction.

**Five life areas:** spiritual, social, academic, professional and personal. Note which areas this practice touches and which it leaves alone.

**Protected boundary:** Authenticity does not excuse harm; it means being honest about values and accountable for actions.

## 5. Build one Practice Card

Use synthetic case **OW-LIT04-OS-SYN001**. Synthetic leader IND-LP300, calendar IND-CL300, team feedback IND-FB300. Keep everything on one card.

1. **Need:** the situation, who is affected and one missing fact.
2. **Values:** two or three values at stake, in your own words.
3. **Commitment:** one small action you can keep within a week, and who will know.
4. **Evidence:** what you will notice, record or ask to see whether it helps.
5. **Alternative:** Compare explaining the decision with listening first and correcting visibly.
6. **When to get support:** one sign that means you should talk to a trusted person or a qualified professional. 
7. **Review:** the date you will look again and what would make you stop or change course.

**Requirement — OW-LIT04-OS-REQ01:** link one authentic-leadership practice hypothesis to a small action and an observed follow-up, including evidence that it did not help.

**Decision — OW-LIT04-OS-ADR01:** record your chosen option and the alternative from item 5, one trade-off, and what would change your mind.

## 6. Run three real-life tests

### T01 — Words and actions disagree

**Expected response:** Name the gap openly and agree one visible correction.

### T02 — A team member challenges the leader

**Expected response:** Listen fully before explaining, and thank the person.

### T03 — Pressure from above to drop the commitment

**Expected response:** State the cost honestly and negotiate rather than silently drop it.

For each, record **situation → what you would do → what you notice → revision → retry**. These are sprint-local tests OW-LIT04-OS-T01/T02/T03, supporting OW-LIT04-OS-REQ01. Completing the lesson is not a measured change in your life.

**Signal — OW-LIT04-OS-KPI01:** Notice how often a stated commitment is kept or openly revised, noted with team feedback; no outcome is promised. Choose your own baseline and review date. No improvement is promised.

**Acceptance:** the card names your values, keeps the boundary above, handles all three tests after revision and can be explained in 60 seconds. Anything unknown stays visible.

## 7. Use your 120 minutes

- **0–20:** read the origin timeline; explain what changed and what did not.
- **20–45:** role-play the story; ask the person, colleague and affected person one useful question each.
- **45–65:** map your own situation across the five life areas.
- **65–100:** complete the single Practice Card and compare the alternative.
- **100–115:** run the three tests and revise.
- **115–120:** explain your choice in 60 seconds and choose a first small action.
