# The phone was always on; attention was never there
## LIT07 — Architecting a Balanced Digital Life

**SuccessLabs Academy · OmniConnect Media Networks Ltd. · Level 0 → Pre-Foundation**  
**IIL — Industry Immersion Lab · Version 1.0 · 10 November 2026**  
**Planning reference:** HUB-ORIGIN-LIT07-COM-sprint (not an LMS ID)  
**Format:** Origin Story Sprint · 120 minutes · Free — Community/Citizen Edition  
**Status:** Published learning content; not an enrolled course or certification.

> **Your promise:** In two hours, design one digital-balance practice that protects attention, sleep and relationships.

**One story · One practice card · Three real-life tests**

## 1. Start with the story

Amelia Jones in a media and networks group with a constant news cycle answers messages late at night and finds it hard to be present with family or focused at work.

**The person facing the choice:** Amelia Jones — Subscriber and Enterprise Client Representative (OCN-P15).  
**A trusted colleague:** Sofia Tan — Chief Digital and Information Officer (OCN-P06).  
**Someone affected:** Mei Lin — Subscriber Data and Consent Lead (OCN-P09).

Amelia Jones asks what matters most before choosing. Sofia Tan asks what evidence and which support would help. Mei Lin asks how the choice will feel to the people around them.

**What they notice:** A simple log of notifications and sleep shows late-night alerts and broken focus blocks.

**Pause:** What would you want to be true before you decide? Ask the colleague one curious question before choosing.

**Case boundary:** OmniConnect Media Networks Ltd. is an aspirational case study (USD 37B communications group; 62,000 employees; 28 countries; 210M subscriptions). This story, its people and its dialogue are proposed learning scenarios, not corporate history, real individuals or achieved results. Preserve the persona IDs above. Reflection is voluntary and personal; share only what you choose to.

## 2. Trace the origin: 1970–2026

These overlapping periods are teaching lenses, not invention dates or a universal sequence. Earlier practices can coexist with newer ones.

- **1970s–1980s:** Work ends when you leave the office.
- **1980s–1990s:** Pagers and early email blur the edge of the workday.
- **2000s–2010s:** Smartphones and social media make connection constant.
- **2020s–2026:** Assisted notifications and remote work stretch availability; people still choose their boundaries.

**Mindset shift:** Decide where your attention goes before the device does.

**Pause:** Which old habit survives in a newer tool? Who bears its cost?

## 3. Explore possible futures: 2030–2050

**2030 experiment:** Try one phone-free hour a day and one agreed response window.

**2040 question:** Ask how families and teams will agree norms as tools get more persistent.

**2050 scene:** Imagine communities where switching off is respected and technology adapts to people.

**Competing view:** Some roles genuinely need constant availability; an on-call rota may fit better than a personal boundary.

All future passages are design hypotheses, not forecasts. Test a small useful part today rather than assume the future will arrive as described.

## 4. Draw the smallest useful map

**Practice — OCN-LIT07-OS-CAP01:** digital-balance practice. A practice is something you do repeatedly; it is not a one-time insight.

**Steps — OCN-LIT07-OS-VS01:** Notice habits → choose boundaries → agree norms → try → review.

**Things to keep distinct:** notification, attention block, boundary, norm and review.

**Five life areas:** spiritual, social, academic, professional and personal. Note which areas this practice touches and which it leaves alone.

**Protected boundary:** A digital boundary is a personal choice; it does not claim to cure any condition.

## 5. Build one Practice Card

Use synthetic case **OCN-LIT07-OS-SYN001**. Synthetic person COM-LP300, notification log COM-LG300, sleep notes COM-SL300. Keep everything on one card.

1. **Need:** the situation, who is affected and one missing fact.
2. **Values:** two or three values at stake, in your own words.
3. **Commitment:** one small action you can keep within a week, and who will know.
4. **Evidence:** what you will notice, record or ask to see whether it helps.
5. **Alternative:** Compare constant availability with agreed response windows.
6. **When to get support:** one sign that means you should talk to a trusted person or a qualified professional. 
7. **Review:** the date you will look again and what would make you stop or change course.

**Requirement — OCN-LIT07-OS-REQ01:** link one digital-balance practice hypothesis to a small action and an observed follow-up, including evidence that it did not help.

**Decision — OCN-LIT07-OS-ADR01:** record your chosen option and the alternative from item 5, one trade-off, and what would change your mind.

## 6. Run three real-life tests

### T01 — Notifications interrupt focused work

**Expected response:** Choose which alerts matter and silence the rest.

### T02 — The team expects instant replies

**Expected response:** Agree response norms openly.

### T03 — A habit slips after a stressful week

**Expected response:** Restart with one small boundary rather than a full reset.

For each, record **situation → what you would do → what you notice → revision → retry**. These are sprint-local tests OCN-LIT07-OS-T01/T02/T03, supporting OCN-LIT07-OS-REQ01. Completing the lesson is not a measured change in your life.

**Signal — OCN-LIT07-OS-KPI01:** Notice how many days a chosen boundary was kept, noted weekly; no outcome is promised. Choose your own baseline and review date. No improvement is promised.

**Acceptance:** the card names your values, keeps the boundary above, handles all three tests after revision and can be explained in 60 seconds. Anything unknown stays visible.

## 7. Use your 120 minutes

- **0–20:** read the origin timeline; explain what changed and what did not.
- **20–45:** role-play the story; ask the person, colleague and affected person one useful question each.
- **45–65:** map your own situation across the five life areas.
- **65–100:** complete the single Practice Card and compare the alternative.
- **100–115:** run the three tests and revise.
- **115–120:** explain your choice in 60 seconds and choose a first small action.
