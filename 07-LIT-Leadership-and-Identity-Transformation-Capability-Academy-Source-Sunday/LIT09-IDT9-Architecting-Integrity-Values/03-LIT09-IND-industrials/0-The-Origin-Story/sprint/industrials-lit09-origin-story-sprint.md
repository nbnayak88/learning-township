# The shortcut was allowed and still did not sit right
## LIT09 — Architecting Integrity & Values

**SuccessLabs Academy · OmniWorks Industrial Systems Ltd. · Level 0 → Pre-Foundation**  
**IIL — Industry Immersion Lab · Version 1.0 · 10 November 2026**  
**Planning reference:** HUB-ORIGIN-LIT09-IND-sprint (not an LMS ID)  
**Format:** Origin Story Sprint · 120 minutes · Free — Community/Citizen Edition  
**Status:** Published learning content; not an enrolled course or certification.

> **Your promise:** In two hours, design one values-in-action practice for hard choices, with a record of the reasons.

**One story · One practice card · Three real-life tests**

## 1. Start with the story

Sofia Laurent in an industrial service organisation with constant fault calls is offered a shortcut that is within the rules and would help the team, yet it feels unfair to someone not in the room.

**The person facing the choice:** Sofia Laurent — Group CFO (OW-P03).  
**A trusted colleague:** Marcus Vogel — CISO (OW-P06).  
**Someone affected:** Elena Brooks — VP Asset Reliability, Transport Customer (OW-P13).

Sofia Laurent asks what matters most before choosing. Marcus Vogel asks what evidence and which support would help. Elena Brooks asks how the choice will feel to the people around them.

**What they notice:** Writing down their values, Sofia Laurent sees that the shortcut conflicts with fairness and that someone else would carry the cost.

**Pause:** What would you want to be true before you decide? Ask the colleague one curious question before choosing.

**Case boundary:** OmniWorks Industrial Systems Ltd. is an aspirational case study (USD 48B revenue; 74,000 employees; 36 countries; 61 plants and facilities; 320 service depots; 7,200 suppliers). This story, its people and its dialogue are proposed learning scenarios, not corporate history, real individuals or achieved results. Preserve the persona IDs above. Reflection is voluntary and personal; share only what you choose to.

## 2. Trace the origin: 1970–2026

These overlapping periods are teaching lenses, not invention dates or a universal sequence. Earlier practices can coexist with newer ones.

- **1970s–1980s:** Ethics is taught as rules and codes.
- **1980s–1990s:** Corporate codes and compliance programmes spread.
- **2000s–2010s:** Scandals prompt integrity and whistleblowing frameworks.
- **2020s–2026:** Assisted decisions and monitoring help check rules; integrity is still a personal and shared choice.

**Mindset shift:** Check who carries the cost of a choice before you take it.

**Pause:** Which old habit survives in a newer tool? Who bears its cost?

## 3. Explore possible futures: 2030–2050

**2030 experiment:** Record one hard decision a month with your reasons.

**2040 question:** Ask how you will keep your values when rules, tools and pressures change quickly.

**2050 scene:** Imagine communities where speaking up is safe and expected.

**Competing view:** In some settings clear rules and independent checks protect people better than personal judgement alone.

All future passages are design hypotheses, not forecasts. Test a small useful part today rather than assume the future will arrive as described.

## 4. Draw the smallest useful map

**Practice — OW-LIT09-OS-CAP01:** values-in-action practice. A practice is something you do repeatedly; it is not a one-time insight.

**Steps — OW-LIT09-OS-VS01:** Notice the dilemma → name values → weigh who is affected → decide → record → review.

**Things to keep distinct:** dilemma, value, affected person, decision and reason.

**Five life areas:** spiritual, social, academic, professional and personal. Note which areas this practice touches and which it leaves alone.

**Protected boundary:** A personal values practice does not replace rules, policies or professional obligations.

## 5. Build one Practice Card

Use synthetic case **OW-LIT09-OS-SYN001**. Synthetic person IND-LP300, shortcut IND-SC300, affected person IND-LP301. Keep everything on one card.

1. **Need:** the situation, who is affected and one missing fact.
2. **Values:** two or three values at stake, in your own words.
3. **Commitment:** one small action you can keep within a week, and who will know.
4. **Evidence:** what you will notice, record or ask to see whether it helps.
5. **Alternative:** Compare taking the shortcut quietly with raising the cost openly.
6. **When to get support:** one sign that means you should talk to a trusted person or a qualified professional. 
7. **Review:** the date you will look again and what would make you stop or change course.

**Requirement — OW-LIT09-OS-REQ01:** link one values-in-action practice hypothesis to a small action and an observed follow-up, including evidence that it did not help.

**Decision — OW-LIT09-OS-ADR01:** record your chosen option and the alternative from item 5, one trade-off, and what would change your mind.

## 6. Run three real-life tests

### T01 — A shortcut helps the team but hides a cost

**Expected response:** Disclose the cost and ask who bears it.

### T02 — A senior person asks them to look away

**Expected response:** Raise it through the proper route and record the conversation.

### T03 — The right choice costs them something

**Expected response:** Acknowledge the cost and decide with open eyes.

For each, record **situation → what you would do → what you notice → revision → retry**. These are sprint-local tests OW-LIT09-OS-T01/T02/T03, supporting OW-LIT09-OS-REQ01. Completing the lesson is not a measured change in your life.

**Signal — OW-LIT09-OS-KPI01:** Notice how often a hard decision was recorded with its reasons and reviewed, noted privately; no outcome is promised. Choose your own baseline and review date. No improvement is promised.

**Acceptance:** the card names your values, keeps the boundary above, handles all three tests after revision and can be explained in 60 seconds. Anything unknown stays visible.

## 7. Use your 120 minutes

- **0–20:** read the origin timeline; explain what changed and what did not.
- **20–45:** role-play the story; ask the person, colleague and affected person one useful question each.
- **45–65:** map your own situation across the five life areas.
- **65–100:** complete the single Practice Card and compare the alternative.
- **100–115:** run the three tests and revise.
- **115–120:** explain your choice in 60 seconds and choose a first small action.
