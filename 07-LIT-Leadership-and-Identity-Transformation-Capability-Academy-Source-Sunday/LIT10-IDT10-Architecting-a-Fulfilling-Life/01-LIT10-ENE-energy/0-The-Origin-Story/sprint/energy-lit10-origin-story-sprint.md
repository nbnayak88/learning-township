# Every area looked fine on paper; the week felt empty
## LIT10 — Architecting a Fulfilling Life

**SuccessLabs Academy · OmniVerse Energy Ltd. · Level 0 → Pre-Foundation**  
**IIL — Industry Immersion Lab · Version 1.0 · 10 November 2026**  
**Planning reference:** HUB-ORIGIN-LIT10-ENE-sprint (not an LMS ID)  
**Format:** Origin Story Sprint · 120 minutes · Free — Community/Citizen Edition

> **Your promise:** In two hours, design one five-area life review that leads to one small, kept commitment.

**One story · One practice card · Three real-life tests**

## 1. Start with the story

Hannah Fischer in an energy transition programme with tight deadlines meets work and family goals, yet feels something is missing.

**The person facing the choice:** Hannah Fischer — Director of Enterprise Architecture (OVE-P12).  
**A trusted colleague:** Michael Reed — Group Chief Financial Officer (CFO) (OVE-P03).  
**Someone affected:** Elena Brooks — VP Energy Procurement, fictional industrial customer (OVE-P15).

Hannah Fischer asks what matters most before choosing. Michael Reed asks what evidence and which support would help. Elena Brooks asks how the choice will feel to the people around them.

**What they notice:** A review across the five life areas (spiritual, social, academic, professional, personal) shows two areas have been empty for months.

**Pause:** What would you want to be true before you decide? Ask the colleague one curious question before choosing.

**Case boundary:** OmniVerse Energy Ltd. is an aspirational case study (USD 85B revenue; 68,000 employees; 42 countries). This story, its people and its dialogue are proposed learning scenarios, not corporate history, real individuals or achieved results. Preserve the persona IDs above. Reflection is voluntary and personal; share only what you choose to.

## 2. Trace the origin: 1970–2026

These overlapping periods are teaching lenses, not invention dates or a universal sequence. Earlier practices can coexist with newer ones.

- **1970s–1980s:** Success is often defined as career and income.
- **1980s–1990s:** Work-life balance enters the conversation.
- **2000s–2010s:** Wellbeing and purpose programmes spread; measures multiply.
- **2020s–2026:** Assisted tracking is common; what counts as fulfilment is still personal.

**Mindset shift:** Review all five areas, not only the loudest one.

**Pause:** Which old habit survives in a newer tool? Who bears its cost?

## 3. Explore possible futures: 2030–2050

**2030 experiment:** Do a short review of the five areas each quarter.

**2040 question:** Ask how fulfilment is judged when work and life blur.

**2050 scene:** Imagine communities that value many kinds of fulfilment and support people through changes.

**Competing view:** Some people find fulfilment in depth in one area; a focused life can be as meaningful as a balanced one.

All future passages are design hypotheses, not forecasts. Test a small useful part today rather than assume the future will arrive as described.

## 4. Draw the smallest useful map

**Practice — OVE-LIT10-OS-CAP01:** five-area review. A practice is something you do repeatedly; it is not a one-time insight.

**Steps — OVE-LIT10-OS-VS01:** Review the five areas → notice gaps → choose one commitment → try → review.

**Things to keep distinct:** life area, gap, commitment, evidence and review.

**Five life areas:** spiritual, social, academic, professional and personal. Note which areas this practice touches and which it leaves alone.

**Protected boundary:** Fulfilment is personal; there is no standard score or target to reach.

## 5. Build one Practice Card

Use synthetic case **OVE-LIT10-OS-SYN001**. Synthetic person ENE-LP300, life-area review ENE-RV300, commitments ENE-CM300. Keep everything on one card.

1. **Need:** the situation, who is affected and one missing fact.
2. **Values:** two or three values at stake, in your own words.
3. **Commitment:** one small action you can keep within a week, and who will know.
4. **Evidence:** what you will notice, record or ask to see whether it helps.
5. **Alternative:** Compare adding more work to the strongest area with a small commitment in the emptiest.
6. **When to get support:** one sign that means you should talk to a trusted person or a qualified professional. 
7. **Review:** the date you will look again and what would make you stop or change course.

**Requirement — OVE-LIT10-OS-REQ01:** link one five-area review hypothesis to a small action and an observed follow-up, including evidence that it did not help.

**Decision — OVE-LIT10-OS-ADR01:** record your chosen option and the alternative from item 5, one trade-off, and what would change your mind.

## 6. Run three real-life tests

### T01 — One area absorbs all the time

**Expected response:** Rebalance one small commitment in the neglected area.

### T02 — Success is measured by others' standards

**Expected response:** Define fulfilment in their own words.

### T03 — A good season ends

**Expected response:** Review commitments and let go of what no longer fits.

For each, record **situation → what you would do → what you notice → revision → retry**. These are sprint-local tests OVE-LIT10-OS-T01/T02/T03, supporting OVE-LIT10-OS-REQ01. Completing the lesson is not a measured change in your life.

**Signal — OVE-LIT10-OS-KPI01:** Notice how many commitments were kept in each life area over a month, noted privately; no outcome is promised. Choose your own baseline and review date. No improvement is promised.

**Acceptance:** the card names your values, keeps the boundary above, handles all three tests after revision and can be explained in 60 seconds. Anything unknown stays visible.

## 7. Use your 120 minutes

- **0–20:** read the origin timeline; explain what changed and what did not.
- **20–45:** role-play the story; ask the person, colleague and affected person one useful question each.
- **45–65:** map your own situation across the five life areas.
- **65–100:** complete the single Practice Card and compare the alternative.
- **100–115:** run the three tests and revise.
- **115–120:** explain your choice in 60 seconds and choose a first small action.
