# The volunteer day ended; the need did not
## LIT12 — Architecting a Better World

**SuccessLabs Academy · OmniHealth Systems Ltd. · Level 0 → Pre-Foundation**  
**IIL — Industry Immersion Lab · Version 1.0 · 10 November 2026**

**Format:** Origin Story Sprint · 120 minutes · Free — Community/Citizen Edition

> **Your promise:** In two hours, design one contribution project that starts from a real community need and lasts beyond one event.

**One story · One practice card · Three real-life tests**

## 1. Start with the story

Ravi Menon organises a volunteer day for colleagues in a health-care network where care and paperwork compete. The event goes well, but the community's need remains.

**The person facing the choice:** Ravi Menon — Procurement and Clinical Asset Director (OHS-P15).  
**A trusted colleague:** Elena Brooks — Patient and Family Experience Director (OHS-P08).  
**Someone affected:** Amelia Jones — Patient and Family Representative (OHS-P16).

Ravi Menon asks what matters most before choosing. Elena Brooks asks what evidence and which support would help. Amelia Jones asks how the choice will feel to the people around them.

**What they notice:** Talking with the community shows the need was ongoing, and the event answered the organiser's idea rather than the need.

**Pause:** What would you want to be true before you decide? Ask the colleague one curious question before choosing.

**Case boundary:** OmniHealth Systems Ltd. is an aspirational case study (USD 34B healthcare group; 126,000 staff; 19 countries). This story, its people and its dialogue are proposed learning scenarios, not corporate history, real individuals or achieved results. Preserve the persona IDs above. Reflection is voluntary and personal; share only what you choose to.

## 2. Trace the origin: 1970–2026

These overlapping periods are teaching lenses, not invention dates or a universal sequence. Earlier practices can coexist with newer ones.

- **1970s–1980s:** Service is led by charities and institutions.
- **1980s–1990s:** Corporate giving and volunteering programmes spread.
- **2000s–2010s:** Social enterprise and impact measurement grow; local voice can be missed.
- **2020s–2026:** Assisted matching connects helpers and needs; listening to the community still comes first.

**Mindset shift:** Listen to the need before choosing the response.

**Pause:** Which old habit survives in a newer tool? Who bears its cost?

## 3. Explore possible futures: 2030–2050

**2030 experiment:** Ask one community partner what is needed before planning anything.

**2040 question:** Ask how contribution stays useful and respectful as tools make it easier to start things.

**2050 scene:** Imagine communities that direct their own help and invite others in.

**Competing view:** Sometimes giving money to a trusted local organisation does more than giving time.

All future passages are design hypotheses, not forecasts. Test a small useful part today rather than assume the future will arrive as described.

## 4. Draw the smallest useful map

**Practice — OHS-LIT12-OS-CAP01:** contribution project. A practice is something you do repeatedly; it is not a one-time insight.

**Steps — OHS-LIT12-OS-VS01:** Listen → name the need → agree a small commitment → act → review with the community.

**Things to keep distinct:** need, partner, commitment, action and review.

**Five life areas:** spiritual, social, academic, professional and personal. Note which areas this practice touches and which it leaves alone.

**Protected boundary:** Contribution is voluntary and must respect consent and local context; it does not claim a measured impact.

## 5. Build one Practice Card

Use synthetic case **OHS-LIT12-OS-SYN001**. Synthetic person HC-LP300, event HC-EV300, community need HC-ND300. Keep everything on one card.

1. **Need:** the situation, who is affected and one missing fact.
2. **Values:** two or three values at stake, in your own words.
3. **Commitment:** one small action you can keep within a week, and who will know.
4. **Evidence:** what you will notice, record or ask to see whether it helps.
5. **Alternative:** Compare a one-off event with a small ongoing commitment agreed with the community.
6. **When to get support:** one sign that means you should talk to a trusted person or a qualified professional. 
7. **Review:** the date you will look again and what would make you stop or change course.

**Requirement — OHS-LIT12-OS-REQ01:** link one contribution project hypothesis to a small action and an observed follow-up, including evidence that it did not help.

**Decision — OHS-LIT12-OS-ADR01:** record your chosen option and the alternative from item 5, one trade-off, and what would change your mind.

## 6. Run three real-life tests

### T01 — The project responds to the helper's idea, not the need

**Expected response:** Ask the community what is needed first.

### T02 — Participants have unequal ability to join

**Expected response:** Make taking part accessible and voluntary.

### T03 — Enthusiasm fades after the event

**Expected response:** Agree a small ongoing commitment with partners.

For each, record **situation → what you would do → what you notice → revision → retry**. These are sprint-local tests OHS-LIT12-OS-T01/T02/T03, supporting OHS-LIT12-OS-REQ01. Completing the lesson is not a measured change in your life.

**Signal — OHS-LIT12-OS-KPI01:** Notice how many agreed contribution commitments were kept over a quarter, reviewed with partners; no outcome is promised. Choose your own baseline and review date. No improvement is promised.

**Acceptance:** the card names your values, keeps the boundary above, handles all three tests after revision and can be explained in 60 seconds. Anything unknown stays visible.

## 7. Use your 120 minutes

- **0–20:** read the origin timeline; explain what changed and what did not.
- **20–45:** role-play the story; ask the person, colleague and affected person one useful question each.
- **45–65:** map your own situation across the five life areas.
- **65–100:** complete the single Practice Card and compare the alternative.
- **100–115:** run the three tests and revise.
- **115–120:** explain your choice in 60 seconds and choose a first small action.
