# The missed pilot became a verdict instead of a lesson
## LIT02 — Architecting a Growth Mindset Life

**SuccessLabs Academy · OmniConnect Media Networks Ltd. · Level 0 → Pre-Foundation**  
**IIL — Industry Immersion Lab · Version 1.0 · 10 November 2026**  
**Planning reference:** HUB-ORIGIN-LIT02-COM-sprint (not an LMS ID)  
**Format:** Origin Story Sprint · 120 minutes · Free — Community/Citizen Edition

> **Your promise:** In two hours, design one growth practice that turns a setback into a small, safe experiment.

**One story · One practice card · Three real-life tests**

## 1. Start with the story

After a pilot in a media and networks group with a constant news cycle misses its target, Priya Shah tells the team to avoid new ideas for now. People stop proposing experiments.

**The person facing the choice:** Priya Shah — Service Catalog and Fulfillment Lead (OCN-P13).  
**A trusted colleague:** Mei Lin — Subscriber Data and Consent Lead (OCN-P09).  
**Someone affected:** Amelia Jones — Subscriber and Enterprise Client Representative (OCN-P15).

Priya Shah asks what matters most before choosing. Mei Lin asks what evidence and which support would help. Amelia Jones asks how the choice will feel to the people around them.

**What they notice:** Reviewing the pilot, Priya Shah sees that a key input arrived two weeks late and nobody asked what the team had learned.

**Pause:** What would you want to be true before you decide? Ask the colleague one curious question before choosing.

**Case boundary:** OmniConnect Media Networks Ltd. is an aspirational case study (USD 37B communications group; 62,000 employees; 28 countries; 210M subscriptions). This story, its people and its dialogue are proposed learning scenarios, not corporate history, real individuals or achieved results. Preserve the persona IDs above. Reflection is voluntary and personal; share only what you choose to.

## 2. Trace the origin: 1970–2026

These overlapping periods are teaching lenses, not invention dates or a universal sequence. Earlier practices can coexist with newer ones.

- **1970s–1980s:** Success is often explained by talent; failure is hidden.
- **1980s–1990s:** Quality and learning-organisation ideas encourage reviewing mistakes; blame still lingers.
- **2000s–2010s:** Growth-mindset ideas spread widely; they are sometimes reduced to slogans.
- **2020s–2026:** Assisted feedback and learning tools are common; people still choose how to treat a setback.

**Mindset shift:** Treat a setback as information about the approach, not a verdict on the person.

**Pause:** Which old habit survives in a newer tool? Who bears its cost?

## 3. Explore possible futures: 2030–2050

**2030 experiment:** Run one small experiment a month and review it with a trusted colleague.

**2040 question:** Ask how teams will keep learning from mistakes when work changes quickly.

**2050 scene:** Imagine communities where trying and learning are expected, and where people can stop an experiment safely.

**Competing view:** Some settings reward steady, careful execution; deliberate caution may suit high-risk work better than frequent experiments.

All future passages are design hypotheses, not forecasts. Test a small useful part today rather than assume the future will arrive as described.

## 4. Draw the smallest useful map

**Practice — OCN-LIT02-OS-CAP01:** growth experiment. A practice is something you do repeatedly; it is not a one-time insight.

**Steps — OCN-LIT02-OS-VS01:** Describe the setback → separate facts from verdicts → learn → design a small experiment → try → review.

**Things to keep distinct:** setback, facts, feedback, lesson and experiment.

**Five life areas:** spiritual, social, academic, professional and personal. Note which areas this practice touches and which it leaves alone.

**Protected boundary:** A positive mindset does not remove real constraints or make every setback pleasant.

## 5. Build one Practice Card

Use synthetic case **OCN-LIT02-OS-SYN001**. Synthetic person COM-LP300, pilot COM-PL300, team review notes COM-NT300. Keep everything on one card.

1. **Need:** the situation, who is affected and one missing fact.
2. **Values:** two or three values at stake, in your own words.
3. **Commitment:** one small action you can keep within a week, and who will know.
4. **Evidence:** what you will notice, record or ask to see whether it helps.
5. **Alternative:** Compare avoiding new ideas with running a smaller, safer experiment.
6. **When to get support:** one sign that means you should talk to a trusted person or a qualified professional. 
7. **Review:** the date you will look again and what would make you stop or change course.

**Requirement — OCN-LIT02-OS-REQ01:** link one growth experiment hypothesis to a small action and an observed follow-up, including evidence that it did not help.

**Decision — OCN-LIT02-OS-ADR01:** record your chosen option and the alternative from item 5, one trade-off, and what would change your mind.

## 6. Run three real-life tests

### T01 — A setback is treated as proof of low ability

**Expected response:** Reframe to what was tried, what was learned and what changes next.

### T02 — Feedback arrives sharply

**Expected response:** Listen, ask one clarifying question and decide what to test.

### T03 — Fear of repeating the failure

**Expected response:** Design a smaller experiment with a clear stop rule.

For each, record **situation → what you would do → what you notice → revision → retry**. These are sprint-local tests OCN-LIT02-OS-T01/T02/T03, supporting OCN-LIT02-OS-REQ01. Completing the lesson is not a measured change in your life.

**Signal — OCN-LIT02-OS-KPI01:** Notice the number of small experiments tried and reviewed each month, with what was learned; no result is promised. Choose your own baseline and review date. No improvement is promised.

**Acceptance:** the card names your values, keeps the boundary above, handles all three tests after revision and can be explained in 60 seconds. Anything unknown stays visible.

## 7. Use your 120 minutes

- **0–20:** read the origin timeline; explain what changed and what did not.
- **20–45:** role-play the story; ask the person, colleague and affected person one useful question each.
- **45–65:** map your own situation across the five life areas.
- **65–100:** complete the single Practice Card and compare the alternative.
- **100–115:** run the three tests and revise.
- **115–120:** explain your choice in 60 seconds and choose a first small action.
