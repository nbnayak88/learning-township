## 1. The decision behind the numbers

A refinery business reports higher margin after shared maintenance and energy costs are allocated elsewhere. A renewable unit appears less profitable although operating performance has not changed.

**Workshop lead:** Aditi Sen — Group Financial Controller (OVE-FI-OS-P01, new fictional learning persona).  
**Counterpart:** Farah Al-Hassan — Chief Operating Officer (OVE-P04).

Farah asks whether the business improved or the allocation changed. Aditi asks which cost pool, driver and quantity explain the movement and how the total reconciles.

They separate direct costs, shared allocation and business measures. The card compares two allocation drivers and records the decision's limitations rather than claiming that one margin proves operational value.

**Pause:** Which fact would change your decision? Ask the counterpart one question before proposing a product.

OmniVerse is a fictional energy enterprise with 68,000 employees in 42 countries. Existing OVE-P identities come from the enterprise register. Aditi, Rohan and Amira are explicitly new fictional operational personas with OVE-FI-OS-P identifiers. All new dialogue and incidents are learning designs. Country, entity, accounting basis and approved policy must be established rather than assumed.

## 2. Origins and evolution, 1970–2025

These closed, non-overlapping periods are teaching lenses, not invention dates or universal adoption deadlines. Practices coexist. Before OmniVerse's fictional 1978 founding, examples describe the wider domain.

- **1970–1989:** Factory cost sheets and periodic allocations explain spending locally.
- **1990–1999:** Cost-center spreadsheets make drivers calculable; assumptions may be hidden.
- **2000–2009:** ERP management accounting links costs to objects; reconciled totals do not prove causal allocation.
- **2010–2019:** Profitability models connect product/customer dimensions; driver choices alter interpretation.
- **2020–2025:** AI-assisted driver analysis proposes patterns; business validity must be tested.

**Mindset:** faster processing is useful when the evidence and decision remain trustworthy. Who bears the cost of an incorrect result: a supplier, worker, customer, investor or community?

## 3. AI-driven horizon, 2026–2050

**2026 starting experiment:** let assistance prepare a low-risk draft, match suggestion or evidence summary. Test its limits before permitting consequential actions.

**2030 checkpoint:** compare the assisted process with a human-led baseline. Require source evidence, approved authority and a usable challenge route.

**2035–2050 scenario:** Imagine a 2050 cost model linking financial and resource-use evidence. More granular data can improve explanation, but allocation remains a governed model with purpose and trade-offs.

**Alternative:** If metering coverage is incomplete, disclose estimated allocations and sensitivity rather than invent precise usage.

These are possibilities and test questions, not forecasts, accounting interpretations or product commitments. Autonomy means bounded assistance under approved authority.

## 4. Smallest useful architecture

**Capability — OVE-FI07-OS-CAP01:** transparent cost and profitability analysis.

**Value stages — OVE-FI07-OS-VS01:** Define purpose → classify cost → select driver → allocate → reconcile → compare alternatives → explain.

**Objects:** cost pool, direct cost, allocation driver, quantity, cost object, period and reconciliation.

**Application responsibility:** Source ledgers own recorded expense; management accounting owns allocation models; operations validates physical drivers; profitability analytics owns the approved view. FI07 does not overwrite statutory source entries to improve management margin.

**Boundary:** An allocation is not automatically an avoidable cost, a cash saving or proof of causation.

The twelve FI themes are a curriculum axis, separate from the twelve architecture streams. SAP Finance is an optional demonstration context; edition, licensing and local requirements remain to validate. Live configuration, posting and payment execution are outside this paper sprint.

## 5. One Decision Card

Use synthetic case **OVE-FI07-OS-SYN001**. Keep the response to one card or screen.

1. **Need:** one business decision, affected population and missing fact.
2. **Evidence:** source IDs, entity, period/effective date, currency or other relevant unit, and approved policy owner.
3. **Ownership:** source application, accountable decision maker and receiving owner.
4. **Gate:** prerequisite, authorized next state and failure/hold condition.
5. **Alternative:** Compare allocation by revenue with an operationally relevant driver; explain the fit and missing evidence without declaring a universal winner.
6. **Recovery:** exception owner, escalation and safe correction/retry.
7. **Proof:** the three actual paper results, measure and stop/revise/proceed decision.

**Requirement — OVE-FI07-OS-REQ01:** retain allocation purpose, driver evidence and reconciled totals with a sensitivity to an alternative.

**Decision — OVE-FI07-OS-ADR01:** record your choice, trade-off and the evidence that would make you change it.

## 6. Three tabletop tests

### T01 — Allocated total differs from the approved cost pool

**Expected:** Reconcile scope, currency, period and rounding before publishing margin.

### T02 — Driver changes midway without disclosure

**Expected:** Version the model and show comparable results or disclose the break.

### T03 — Lower allocated cost is claimed as cash saving

**Expected:** Require evidence of actual cost avoidance/cash impact; report allocation effect separately.

Record **input → expected → actual paper result → evidence note → revision → retest**. Tests OVE-FI07-OS-T01/T02/T03 support REQ01; expected results are not observed tenant evidence.

**Measure — OVE-FI07-OS-KPI01:** allocation runs with source reconciliation and approved driver evidence / runs reviewed. Name the accountable operational owner, baseline, exclusions and as-of date before setting a target.

**Acceptance:** your card preserves the boundary, names accountable owners, handles all three tests after revisions and supports a 60-second explanation. Unresolved facts remain visible; no achieved financial benefit is claimed.

## 7. Your 120 minutes

- **0–20:** explore the history and explain the enduring problem.
- **20–45:** role-play the lead and counterpart; ask for evidence.
- **45–65:** map responsibilities and consult one resource selectively.
- **65–100:** complete the card and compare the alternative.
- **100–115:** run the three tests and revise.
- **115–120:** explain the decision and choose the next experiment.
