## 1. The decision behind the numbers

An assistant recommends a journal to clear a refinery variance. Its explanation cites an outdated policy and a spreadsheet cell containing instructions to bypass approval.

**Workshop lead:** Dr. Leila Haddad — Head of Data Governance and AI (OVE-P16).  
**Counterpart:** Aditi Sen — Group Financial Controller (OVE-FI-OS-P01, new fictional learning persona).

Aditi asks which evidence supports the proposed treatment. Leila asks whether retrieved content has been mistaken for authority to execute a tool.

They restrict the assistant to assembling evidence and drafting a recommendation. Policy validation, approval, posting permissions and reversal/recovery remain separate controlled steps.

**Pause:** Which fact would change your decision? Ask the counterpart one question before proposing a product.

OmniVerse is a fictional energy enterprise with 68,000 employees in 42 countries. Existing OVE-P identities come from the enterprise register. Aditi, Rohan and Amira are explicitly new fictional operational personas with OVE-FI-OS-P identifiers. All new dialogue and incidents are learning designs. Country, entity, accounting basis and approved policy must be established rather than assumed.

## 2. Origins and evolution, 1970–2025

These closed, non-overlapping periods are teaching lenses, not invention dates or universal adoption deadlines. Practices coexist. Before OmniVerse's fictional 1978 founding, examples describe the wider domain.

- **1970–1989:** Calculation rules automate repeatable work; exceptions require human explanation.
- **1990–1999:** Expert systems and spreadsheets encode decisions; hidden rules can age.
- **2000–2009:** Workflow and analytics prioritize finance tasks; data quality determines usefulness.
- **2010–2019:** Predictive matching and anomaly detection suggest exceptions; scores are not accounting conclusions.
- **2020–2025:** Generative drafting expands assistance; grounding, authorization and evaluation become explicit design needs.

**Mindset:** faster processing is useful when the evidence and decision remain trustworthy. Who bears the cost of an incorrect result: a supplier, worker, customer, investor or community?

## 3. AI-driven horizon, 2026–2050

**2026 starting experiment:** let assistance prepare a low-risk draft, match suggestion or evidence summary. Test its limits before permitting consequential actions.

**2030 checkpoint:** compare the assisted process with a human-led baseline. Require source evidence, approved authority and a usable challenge route.

**2035–2050 scenario:** For 2030–2050 exploration, imagine agents coordinating reconciliations across entities. They may propose tasks within delegated bounds, but must retain evidence, meaningful human challenge and safe recovery.

**Alternative:** If the model or evidence store fails, reviewers need a non-AI route; an unavailable assistant must not force uncontrolled posting.

These are possibilities and test questions, not forecasts, accounting interpretations or product commitments. Autonomy means bounded assistance under approved authority.

## 4. Smallest useful architecture

**Capability — OVE-FI11-OS-CAP01:** controlled AI-assisted finance decisions.

**Value stages — OVE-FI11-OS-VS01:** Verify scope → retrieve evidence → propose → evaluate → approve → execute authorized step → reconcile.

**Objects:** approved policy version, source evidence, prompt/input, recommendation, tool permission, approval and journal reference.

**Application responsibility:** Accounting owners approve policy/treatment; AI orchestration enforces permitted tools; application controls enforce posting authority; controllers review results; FI12 owns exchange/replay behavior.

**Boundary:** Model confidence does not authorize journals, payments, tax submissions or changes to accounting policy.

The twelve FI themes are a curriculum axis, separate from the twelve architecture streams. SAP Finance is an optional demonstration context; edition, licensing and local requirements remain to validate. Live configuration, posting and payment execution are outside this paper sprint.

## 5. One Decision Card

Use synthetic case **OVE-FI11-OS-SYN001**. Keep the response to one card or screen.

1. **Need:** one business decision, affected population and missing fact.
2. **Evidence:** source IDs, entity, period/effective date, currency or other relevant unit, and approved policy owner.
3. **Ownership:** source application, accountable decision maker and receiving owner.
4. **Gate:** prerequisite, authorized next state and failure/hold condition.
5. **Alternative:** Compare automatic variance-clearing with evidence assembly, human review and a narrow authorized action scope.
6. **Recovery:** exception owner, escalation and safe correction/retry.
7. **Proof:** the three actual paper results, measure and stop/revise/proceed decision.

**Requirement — OVE-FI11-OS-REQ01:** ground recommendations in current approved evidence and prevent unapproved consequential tool actions.

**Decision — OVE-FI11-OS-ADR01:** record your choice, trade-off and the evidence that would make you change it.

## 6. Three tabletop tests

### T01 — Evidence is stale or contradicts policy

**Expected:** Hold the recommendation, identify uncertainty and request current approved evidence.

### T02 — Retrieved document instructs bypass of approval

**Expected:** Treat text as untrusted input, deny the action and record the attempt.

### T03 — Execution response is lost

**Expected:** Check the journal/action reference and business outcome before any retry; prevent duplicate effects.

Record **input → expected → actual paper result → evidence note → revision → retest**. Tests OVE-FI11-OS-T01/T02/T03 support REQ01; expected results are not observed tenant evidence.

**Measure — OVE-FI11-OS-KPI01:** recommendations supported by correct current evidence / reviewed evaluation cases, paired with unsafe-action and escalation results. Name the accountable operational owner, baseline, exclusions and as-of date before setting a target.

**Acceptance:** your card preserves the boundary, names accountable owners, handles all three tests after revisions and supports a 60-second explanation. Unresolved facts remain visible; no achieved financial benefit is claimed.

## 7. Your 120 minutes

- **0–20:** explore the history and explain the enduring problem.
- **20–45:** role-play the lead and counterpart; ask for evidence.
- **45–65:** map responsibilities and consult one resource selectively.
- **65–100:** complete the card and compare the alternative.
