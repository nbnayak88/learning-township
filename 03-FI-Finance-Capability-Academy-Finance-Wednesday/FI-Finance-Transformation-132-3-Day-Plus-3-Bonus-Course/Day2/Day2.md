### Day 2 V2: Concept 1: Record and Measure: Ledger and Controlling
*Duration: 12-15 min*

**Hook:** If the books are wrong, everything built on them is wrong.

Did you share your notes in the previous video? [pause] If not, pause here, drop them in the comments, then come back. Sharing is how learning becomes yours.

Welcome back. Today's concept is **Record and Measure: Ledger and Controlling**. It anchors two product academies: AFR1 S/4HANA Finance and ACC7 S/4HANA Controlling. Together with the 11 GICS sectors that is 22 of the 132 courses in our meta anchor file, for example AFR1-EN, AFR1-MA, AFR1-IN ... (22 courses in this concept). [show slide: concept map]

**1. What is the concept?**
S/4HANA Finance is the single ledger that records every financial transaction once. Controlling (management accounting) adds cost centres, profitability and internal reporting so you can see not only what happened but where value is made or lost. [pause]

**2. Why is it important?**
Every finance process, from payables to consolidation, reads from the ledger. When the chart of accounts, cost structures and master data are inconsistent, you get long closes, reconciliations and numbers nobody trusts.
Here is a real pattern I have seen. A materials group ran its close in 14 working days because three ledgers had to be reconciled by hand. Moving to one ledger with a clean chart of accounts and controlling structure cut the close sharply, and the CFO started the month-end review with answers, not questions. [pause]

**3. Benefits of implementing it the right way**
When you get this right, here is what changes: One source of financial truth in real time; Faster, cleaner period close; Margin and cost visibility by product, customer and unit; A stable core that planning, compliance and AI can rely on. [on screen: four benefits]

**Sector lens.** Sector lens: Energy and Materials need asset-heavy cost tracking and joint-venture accounting; Financial Services needs regulatory ledgers and product profitability.

### Day 2 V3: Concept 2: Pay and Collect: Payables and Receivables
*Duration: 12-15 min*

**Hook:** Cash is the oxygen of the enterprise.

Did you share your notes in the previous video? [pause] If not, pause here, drop them in the comments, then come back. Sharing is how learning becomes yours.

Welcome back. Today's concept is **Pay and Collect: Payables and Receivables**. It anchors two product academies: APT2 FI-AP (Accounts Payable) and AOT3 FI-AR (Accounts Receivable). Together with the 11 GICS sectors that is 22 of the 132 courses in our meta anchor file, for example APT2-EN, APT2-MA, APT2-IN ... (22 courses in this concept). [show slide: concept map]

**1. What is the concept?**
FI-AP manages what the company owes: invoices, approvals, payments and supplier relationships. FI-AR manages what customers owe: invoicing, collections, credit and dispute handling. [pause]

**2. Why is it important?**
Payables and receivables set the rhythm of working capital. Slow invoice processing, late collections and unmanaged disputes lock cash inside the business and strain supplier and customer relationships.
Here is a real pattern I have seen. A consumer staples company cut its collection cycle once disputes were tracked in one queue rather than inboxes. Cash arrived weeks earlier, and treasury finally trusted the forecast. [pause]

**3. Benefits of implementing it the right way**
When you get this right, here is what changes: Faster invoice-to-pay and fewer duplicate or late payments; Shorter days sales outstanding; Cleaner supplier and customer master data; Predictable cash flow for treasury and planning. [on screen: four benefits]

**Sector lens.** Sector lens: Retail-heavy Consumer Discretionary handles huge volumes of small payments; Industrials handles milestone and project-based billing.

### Day 2 V4: Concept 3: Comply and Control: Compliance and Governance
*Duration: 12-15 min*

**Hook:** Compliance is cheaper when it is designed in, not bolted on.

Did you share your notes in the previous video? [pause] If not, pause here, drop them in the comments, then come back. Sharing is how learning becomes yours.

Welcome back. Today's concept is **Comply and Control: Compliance and Governance**. It anchors two product academies: ATX4 Document and Reporting Compliance and AGR9 GRC for Finance. Together with the 11 GICS sectors that is 22 of the 132 courses in our meta anchor file, for example ATX4-EN, ATX4-MA, ATX4-IN ... (22 courses in this concept). [show slide: concept map]

**1. What is the concept?**
Document and Reporting Compliance (DRC) produces statutory e-documents and country reports in the right format at the right time. GRC for Finance defines who can do what, monitors risk and proves control to auditors. [pause]

**2. Why is it important?**
Tax authorities and auditors do not accept 'the system was down'. Manual compliance work is expensive and fragile, and weak segregation of duties is how fraud and audit findings begin.
Here is a real pattern I have seen. A group operating in 20 countries filed statutory reports from spreadsheets. Automating them through a single compliance layer meant a new country went live in weeks, not quarters, and audit requests were answered from the system. [pause]

**3. Benefits of implementing it the right way**
When you get this right, here is what changes: On-time, correct statutory filings across countries; Segregation of duties designed into processes; Audit evidence produced as a by-product of work; Lower compliance cost and less firefighting. [on screen: four benefits]

**Sector lens.** Sector lens: Financial Services and Health Care face heavy regulation and audit scrutiny; Utilities and Energy face tariff, tax and environmental reporting.
