### Day 2 V2: Concept 1: Record and Measure: Ledger and Controlling
*Duration: 12-15 min*

**Hook:** If the books are wrong, everything built on them is wrong.

Did you share your notes in the previous video? [pause] If not, pause here, drop them in the comments, then come back. Sharing is how learning becomes yours.

Welcome back. Today's concept is **Record and Measure: Ledger and Controlling**. It anchors two product academies: AFR1 S/4HANA Finance and ACC7 S/4HANA Controlling. Together with the 11 GICS sectors that is 22 of the 132 courses in our meta anchor file, for example AFR1-EN, AFR1-MA, AFR1-IN ... (22 courses in this concept). [show slide: concept map]

**1. What is the concept?**
S/4HANA Finance is the single ledger that records every financial transaction once. Controlling (management accounting) adds cost centres, profitability and internal reporting so you can see not only what happened but where value is made or lost. [pause]

**2. Why is it important?**
Every finance process, from payables to consolidation, reads from the ledger. When the chart of accounts, cost structures and master data are inconsistent, you get long closes, reconciliations and numbers nobody trusts.
Here is a real pattern I have seen. A materials group ran its close in 14 working days because three ledgers had to be reconciled by hand. Moving to one ledger with a clean chart of accounts and controlling structure cut the close sharply, and the CFO started the month-end review with answers, not questions. [pause]

**3. Benefits of implementing it the right way**
When you get this right, here is what changes: One source of financial truth in real time; Faster, cleaner period close; Margin and cost visibility by product, customer and unit; A stable core that planning, compliance and AI can rely on. [on screen: four benefits]

**Sector lens.** Sector lens: Energy and Materials need asset-heavy cost tracking and joint-venture accounting; Financial Services needs regulatory ledgers and product profitability.

When you get this right, here is what changes: On-time, correct statutory filings across countries; Segregation of duties designed into processes; Audit evidence produced as a by-product of work; Lower compliance cost and less firefighting. [on screen: four benefits]

### B1 V2: In-Depth Concept: Build the Finance Transformation Business Case
*Duration: 20-30 min*

**Hook:** Business Case is where theory becomes income and impact.

**Take notes.** Keep a page open. Write three things as we go and share them towards the end. [pause]

**1. What is the concept?** (about 3 min)
A business case is a one-page, CFO and CEO-ready argument that links a finance change to money, risk and growth. It names the problem, the cost of doing nothing, the benefit, the investment and the proof. [show slide]

**2. Why is it important?** (about 4 min)
Good designs die in budget meetings. Leaders fund outcomes, not modules. If you can express a finance transformation idea in their language (cost, risk, revenue, speed), your design gets built. Here is what I have seen. A mid-size firm had a sound finance transformation design that stalled for a year. One page with three numbers and one owner per benefit got it approved in a single meeting.

**3. Benefits of implementing it the right way** (about 3 min)
When this is done well: Faster funding and sponsor commitment; Clear success measures from day one; Fewer scope arguments because value is agreed; A career-defining skill that moves you from consultant to advisor. [on screen: benefits]

**4. How to do it? Tactical** (about 10-12 min)
Follow these steps in order. Pause the video at each step and do it. [screen share]
1. Pick one of the 132 courses and sector, for example Energy (AFR1-EN) or Financial Services (AOT3-FS).
2. Write the problem in one sentence with one number.
3. Quantify the cost of doing nothing (cost, risk, lost revenue).
4. List 3 benefits and attach a KPI and an owner to each.
5. Estimate the investment (people, licences, change) and a range, not a point.
6. Add risks and the simplest mitigation for each.
7. Write the 60-second story for the CFO and CEO: problem, impact, option, ask.

**5. Resources and tool** (about 3 min)
Your tool for this bonus: **Finance Value Case Canvas (Excel) from the Codex**. Open it from the Codex linked below. [on screen: tool walkthrough] Assignment: write a one-page finance value case for one course ID of your choice. Submit the canvas and your 60-second story.

### B2 V2: In-Depth Concept: Deliver with Fit-to-Standard and Clean Core (SAP Activate)
*Duration: 20-30 min*

**Hook:** Fit-to-Standard Delivery is where theory becomes income and impact.

**Take notes.** Keep a page open. Write three things as we go and share them towards the end. [pause]

**1. What is the concept?** (about 3 min)
Fit-to-standard means testing business requirements against the standard solution first and only building where there is a real gap. SAP Activate organises delivery into six phases: Discover, Prepare, Explore, Realize, Deploy, Run. Clean core keeps extensions upgrade-safe. [show slide]

**2. Why is it important?** (about 4 min)
Customisation is the quiet reason projects run late and over budget. A disciplined fit-gap keeps scope honest, protects upgrades and lets you reuse one design across many sectors. Here is what I have seen. A project arrived at Realize with 140 'gaps'. A structured fit-gap showed 90 were standard behaviour that nobody had demonstrated. The build shrank by two thirds and went live on time.

**3. Benefits of implementing it the right way** (about 3 min)
When this is done well: Shorter, more predictable delivery; Lower cost of ownership and safer upgrades; Clear decisions on what to configure, extend or defer; Reusable designs across the 11 sectors. [on screen: benefits]

**4. How to do it? Tactical** (about 10-12 min)
Follow these steps in order. Pause the video at each step and do it. [screen share]
1. List requirements as short, testable statements.
2. Run each against standard: Fit, Configure, Gap.
3. For every Gap choose: adopt standard, extend in a clean-core way, or defer.
4. Record the decision, owner and effort in the Fit-Gap Matrix.
5. Map decisions to the six Activate phases and their quality gates.
6. Review the matrix with the business before Explore ends.
7. Keep the matrix alive in Run as the change log.

**5. Resources and tool** (about 3 min)
Your tool for this bonus: **Fit-Gap Matrix and the SAP Activate six-phase checklist (Codex)**. Open it from the Codex linked below. [on screen: tool walkthrough] Assignment: take 10 requirements from any course in the catalog, classify each as Fit / Configure / Gap, and decide the clean-core option for every gap.

### B3 V2: In-Depth Concept: Land Adoption and Your Capstone Portfolio
*Duration: 20-30 min*

**Hook:** Adoption and Portfolio is where theory becomes income and impact.

**Take notes.** Keep a page open. Write three things as we go and share them towards the end. [pause]

**1. What is the concept?** (about 3 min)
Adoption is the share of people who actually use the new way of working. A capstone portfolio is your proof: three or four artifacts (a map, a case, a fit-gap, a plan) that show you can do the work in a real sector. [show slide]

**2. Why is it important?** (about 4 min)
A finance transformation that nobody uses delivers nothing, and a learner without proof stays invisible. Adoption turns design into value; the portfolio turns learning into opportunity. Here is what I have seen. A learner posted a three-artifact portfolio for one sector and was invited to a client workshop within a week. The artifacts, not the certificate, opened the door.

**3. Benefits of implementing it the right way** (about 3 min)
When this is done well: Higher usage and faster time to value; Visible, shareable proof of skill; Stronger interviews, proposals and promotions; A natural path to Pro and Star stages. [on screen: benefits]

**4. How to do it? Tactical** (about 10-12 min)
Follow these steps in order. Pause the video at each step and do it. [screen share]
1. Define who must change and what exactly changes for them.
2. Pick 3 adoption measures (usage, quality, sentiment) and a target.
3. Plan communication and training per group, in that order.
4. Create a Champion network of one person per team.
5. Assemble your portfolio: value case, fit-gap, process or journey map, adoption plan.
6. Publish it (one page per artifact) and share the link in the community.
7. Book a review with your accountability partner.
