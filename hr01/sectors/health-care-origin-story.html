1. One learner promise

In 120 minutes, design one facility transfer that preserves workforce identity while keeping clinical readiness and access decisions explicit.

One persona-led story, one Workforce Transfer Build Card, exactly three tabletop tests. Use synthetic data and one card throughout; no tenant is required.

3. Origins: the problem survives the medium

These closed historical teaching windows are educational groupings, not universal invention dates or OmniHealth's corporate biography. Adoption overlaps; paper and older systems can coexist through the present.

1970–1984 — Personnel files and local duty lists record who belongs to a facility; identity is difficult to reconcile.

1985–1994 — Personnel databases and payroll link employment to pay; a paid worker is not necessarily ready for every role.

1995–2004 — Integrated HR and position structures coordinate transfers; effective dates and delegated approval matter.

2005–2014 — Self-service and connected learning improve visibility; credential and access owners still differ.

2015–2025 — Cloud workforce records and assisted checks reveal missing evidence; a green HR status still cannot grant clinical privilege.

Counterexample: digitizing a staff list makes it easier to find, but does not make its facility assignment current.

4. One story, one consequential choice

Nina Fernandes (OHS-P06), Group CHRO, reviews a synthetic same-employer transfer from a clinic to a hospital care-coordination unit. Dr. Amina Okafor (OHS-P07), Nursing and Care Coordination Director, sees the worker on tomorrow's list and asks who will receive the follow-up tasks. The HR transfer is approved, but the receiving facility's role-readiness record has no accountable reviewer. Nina wants mobility without turning an employment transaction into clinical permission.

Pause before the reveal. Choose a blanket transfer hold, automatic release of every entitlement, or an approved employment change with separate readiness gates. State who benefits, who bears the risk and what evidence you need.

Evidence reveal: the same legal employer is assumed for this exercise; the transfer takes effect on 1 November 2026. A course is completed, but no receiving-facility readiness decision is recorded. The employment history is intact.

Revised decision: Nina retains the approved workforce change and marks clinical/task readiness as unresolved. Amina seeks the designated facility authority's review and an authorized alternative duty. Neither substitutes for a professional credentialing decision. A learner may defend a different option if the card names its authority, evidence, trade-off and conditions for stopping. Missing evidence remains unknown; an urgent request does not manufacture approval.

5. Applications serve accountable people

Proposed responsibilities, subject to local discovery:

  • Core HR stores person, employment, organization and position facts; delegated HR approvers own the employment change under CHRO-sponsored governance.
  • Credential/readiness services hold verified professional and facility evidence; designated credentialing and clinical authorities decide permitted work.
  • Learning stores course and assessment evidence under the learning owner; completion is one input, not clinical authorization.
  • IAM provisions policy-approved entitlements; security owns access evidence. Scheduling owns the roster; the receiving unit confirms actual readiness.

Across these boundaries, HR owns employment facts; designated clinical governance owns clinical privilege decisions; IAM administers approved access. Employment, learning completion, account access and permission for clinical work are distinct states. Share only the data needed for the declared purpose; keep patient records and detailed health information out of general HR learning cards. Omar Haddad (OHS-P10), CISO and Privacy Lead, governs privacy/access assurance; jurisdiction-specific applicability remains unresolved.

SAP examples illustrate responsibilities rather than prescribe a purchase: Employee Central for workforce master data, Learning for learning evidence, and an integration layer for bounded handoffs. No feature availability, license entitlement, configuration, legal compliance or clinical conformance is established here. AI may draft a reminder or summarize permitted evidence; it cannot grant clinical privilege, make a high-impact employment decision or attest to payment or delivery.

6. The exact 120-minute sprint

  • 0–10 minutes: understand the learner promise and people problem.
  • 10–30 minutes: compare the five historical windows and identify the enduring question.
  • 30–45 minutes: choose, reveal evidence and revise the story decision.
  • 45–60 minutes: map application responsibility, data ownership and human decision rights.
  • 60–90 minutes: complete the single build card, comparing one alternative and one local variant.
  • 90–110 minutes: discuss the three tabletop tests and record observed paper decisions and revisions.
  • 110–120 minutes: explain the design in 60 seconds, reflect and name the Pre-Foundation discovery question.

Selective resource reading fits within these activities. The single card holds all notes; no extra deliverable is required.

7. One synthetic build experiment

Synthetic HC-P100 / HC-E100; clinic CL-A → hospital HP-A; same employer assumed; entry 31 October 2026, effective 1 November 2026. Course complete, receiving readiness unknown. Labels are educational, not prescribed SAP fields.

Complete one Workforce Transfer Build Card with these six fields:

  1. Need and scope: Enable a transparent transfer into coordinated care without losing employment history or implying readiness. Explain the capability and value-stream step before naming a tool.
  2. Facts and time: preserve identifiers, source, effective/as-of time, unknowns and approved policy version. Distinguish a source fact from your assumption.
  3. Decision and ownership: Separate person, employment, position assignment, course completion, clinical readiness and system access; identify the delegated approver for each. Record one alternative, its trade-off and the evidence that could favour it.
  4. Handoff and recovery: Track HR approval and receiving-system confirmation separately. If the facility feed is unavailable, escalate to its designated owner and hold affected permissions under the approved local procedure. Name the accountable exception role, accessible human route and condition for reconciliation.
  5. Local variant and data: choose one facility/language/role variation; justify minimum fields, purpose, owner, retention decision and viewers. Retain a non-digital route.
  6. Evidence and learning: record T01–T03 input → expected → observed paper result → revision → reviewer question on this same card. Leave results unfilled until observed.

8. Exactly three tabletop tests

OHS-HR01-T01 — Before and on the date

Input/reveal: Compare 31 October and 1 November views.

Expected in the proposed design: The old assignment precedes the effective change; person/employment identity and history remain. Readiness is separate.

Record: Two dated views and approval reference. Observed result and pass/revise remain unfilled until learners perform the tabletop.

OHS-HR01-T02 — Completion without readiness

Input/reveal: Learning is complete; facility review is missing.

Expected in the proposed design: No clinical privilege or task release follows automatically; an authorized role resolves the exception.

Record: Hold, authority, alternative-duty decision and worker notice. Observed result and pass/revise remain unfilled until learners perform the tabletop.

OHS-HR01-T03 — Receiving-system outage

Input/reveal: HR records the change but the roster service gives no acknowledgement.

Expected in the proposed design: Downstream state stays unconfirmed; no full-completion claim or access expansion; reconcile with the facility owner.

Record: Per-system state, fallback gap and reconciliation condition. Observed result and pass/revise remain unfilled until learners perform the tabletop.

Acceptance is a defensible completed card and an explanation of all three decisions after revision. A paper discussion proves neither a working system nor a safe clinical service. Publishing this lesson executes no tests.

10. AI-driven exploration: three scenarios

Scenarios, not predictions, guaranteed capabilities, deployments or product roadmaps: - 2026–2030 — Assist: An assistant flags missing receiving-facility evidence. - 2031–2040 — Connect: Governed services exchange current readiness evidence across facilities. - 2041–2050 — Adapt: A bounded planner proposes alternative assignments under accountable human approval.

Competing future: facilities retain locally governed workflows and modest deterministic tools because evidence quality, cost or worker trust makes broader automation unsuitable.

Falsification question: Would assisted transfers create more unresolved permissions or worker delays than a simple reviewed checklist? State what observation would make you reduce, redesign or stop assistance. Human accountability does not disappear in any scenario.
