## 1. Start with the story

A spend initiative for a spare assembly for a service depot reports a larger saving each month after the baseline is updated.

**Procurement lead:** Chen Wei — Chief Supply Chain & Procurement Officer (OW-P08).  
**Counterpart:** Sofia Laurent — Group CFO (OW-P03).  
**Data or business owner:** Dr. Leila Haddad — Head of Data Governance & AI (OW-P15).

Chen Wei asks what must be true before the team decides. Sofia Laurent asks which evidence and owner support the decision. Dr. Leila Haddad asks for the outcome that matters to the people affected.

**Evidence reveal:** In the synthetic record, volume dropped while unit price stayed flat, and two teams claim the same saving.

**Pause:** What must be true for this decision to stand? Which evidence is missing, and who can confirm it? Ask the counterpart one discovery question before choosing a solution.

**Case boundary:** OmniWorks Industrial Systems Ltd. is an aspirational case study (USD 48B revenue; 74,000 employees; 36 countries; 61 plants and facilities; 320 service depots; 7,200 suppliers). These incidents, services and dialogue are proposed learning scenarios, not corporate history, confirmed deployments or achieved results. Preserve the enterprise persona IDs above. Country-specific permissions, contracts and operating rules require validation; this sprint does not invent them.

## 2. Trace the origin: 1970–2026

These overlapping periods are teaching lenses, not invention dates or a universal adoption sequence. Earlier practices can coexist with newer ones. Pre-1978 scenes describe the wider domain, not OmniWorks Industrial Systems Ltd. history.

- **1970s–1980s:** Savings are negotiated and reported by buyers.
- **1980s–1990s:** Spend analysis begins; baselines vary.
- **2000s–2010s:** Savings tracking tools spread; double counting is common.
- **2020s–2026:** Assisted analysis speeds insight; baselines and finance review still decide.

**Mindset shift:** start with what was needed, what evidence exists and who owns the decision, then choose the automation. Automating a weak control can repeat the same mistake faster.

**Pause:** Which old problem survives in a newer interface? Who bears its cost?

## 3. Explore possible futures: 2030–2050

**2030 experiment:** allow assistance to propose routine work and flag unusual items; retain the evidence gate, a named approver and a way to reverse.

**2040 question:** if buyers and suppliers exchange evidence directly, can it travel with the transaction without losing verification or fairness?

**2050 scene:** Imagine sourcing and supply decisions that explain themselves on request. Providers may show evidence sooner, but a person must own award, commitment and exceptions.

**Competing future:** A simpler, rules-based process with trained buyers may beat an adaptive service on reliability, fairness and cost.

All future passages are design hypotheses, not forecasts or product roadmaps. Test a useful part today rather than assume the future will arrive as described.

## 4. Draw the smallest useful architecture

**Capability — OW-PROC08-OS-CAP01:** comparable spend-improvement evidence. A capability is an ability; it is not an application or a sequence of steps.

**Value stages — OW-PROC08-OS-VS01:** Baseline → initiate → measure → reconcile with finance → report → review.

**Objects to distinguish:** baseline, initiative, volume, unit price, total cost and saving claim.

**Proposed responsibility:** Procurement owns initiatives; finance owns value review; category owners own scope. PROC08 owns the improvement flow, not the budget.

**Protected boundary:** A saving is not valid if the baseline moved or the cost shifted elsewhere.

Procurement applications are optional demonstration candidates, chosen after requirements. No product is assumed installed, licensed or able to cover the whole process. This sprint focuses on application responsibility while referencing data, integration and security owners.

## 5. Build one Decision Card

Use synthetic case **OW-PROC08-OS-SYN001**. Synthetic initiative IND-IN300, baseline versions IND-BL300 and IND-BL301, finance review pending. Keep everything on one card or screen.

1. **Need:** the outcome, affected people and one missing fact.
2. **Evidence:** relevant object IDs, source, validity or effective time, data purpose and permitted access.
3. **Ownership:** business decision owner, authoritative application and receiving owner.
4. **Gate:** prerequisite, allowed next state, authorized actor and the message anyone affected will see.
5. **Alternative:** Compare reporting the latest baseline with fixing a comparable baseline.
6. **Exception:** one failure state, escalation owner and a safe recovery or assisted route.
7. **Proof:** the three results below, one measure and a stop, revise or proceed decision.

**Requirement — OW-PROC08-OS-REQ01:** link one spend-evidence experiment hypothesis to an owned intervention and observed follow-up, including negative evidence.

**Decision — OW-PROC08-OS-ADR01:** record your chosen option and the alternative from item 5, one trade-off, and the evidence that would change your choice.

## 6. Run three tabletop tests

### T01 — Volume drops while unit price is unchanged

**Expected:** Do not report a price saving; show the volume effect separately.

### T02 — Two teams claim one saving

**Expected:** Count it once and name the owner.

### T03 — A cheaper option adds freight or inspection cost

**Expected:** Include total cost before claiming the saving.

For each, record **input → expected → actual paper result → evidence note → revision → retest**. These are sprint-local tests OW-PROC08-OS-T01/T02/T03, supporting OW-PROC08-OS-REQ01. A completed lesson is not an executed tenant test.

**Measure — OW-PROC08-OS-KPI01:** Share of savings with a comparable baseline and finance-reviewed evidence; define window and exclusions. Assign Chen Wei as proposed measure owner, establish the baseline and exclusions, and review after the experiment. No numeric improvement is asserted.

**Acceptance:** the card identifies source and owner, preserves the protected boundary, handles all three tests after documented revisions and explains the decision in 60 seconds. Missing evidence remains visible. Record what is still unverified.

## 7. Use your 120 minutes

- **0–20:** read the origin timeline; explain what changed and what did not.
- **20–45:** role-play the story; ask the lead, counterpart and voice one useful question each.
- **45–65:** draw responsibilities and consult one resource selectively.
- **65–100:** complete the single Decision Card and compare the alternative.
- **100–115:** run the three tests; revise from observed paper behavior.
- **115–120:** explain the decision in 60 seconds and choose the next experiment.

All reading fits within these blocks. Do not complete an entire product course during the sprint.
