## 1. The purchase behind the process

A category initiative claims lower spend after refinery demand fell and exchange rates moved. Another team claims the same saving, while a lower-priced option introduces higher inspection and freight costs.

**Lead:** Wei Zhang — Chief Supply Chain & Procurement Officer (OVE-P11).  
**Counterpart:** Michael Reed — Group Chief Financial Officer (OVE-P03).

Michael asks which reduction came from procurement action. Wei asks which quantities, scope and total-cost assumptions the comparison holds constant.

The team chooses a comparable baseline, separates price, volume and currency effects, and tracks one initiative with a benefit owner. Negotiated opportunity remains distinct from realized net benefit.

**Pause:** What business need and evidence must be clear before the next commitment? Ask both people one useful question.

OmniVerse is a fictional integrated energy group with 68,000 employees in 42 countries. Existing OVE-P personas retain their source identities. Dialogue, incidents, supplier records and proposed outcomes here are new learning fiction. No real supplier, award, transaction or implementation result is represented.

## 2. Origins and evolution, 1970–2025

These closed, non-overlapping periods are teaching lenses, not invention dates or universal adoption deadlines. Practices coexist. Pre-1978 examples describe the wider domain rather than OmniVerse corporate history.

- **1970–1989:** Purchase ledgers compare unit prices locally.
- **1990–1999:** Spend spreadsheets reveal variation but change baselines easily.
- **2000–2009:** Spend analytics links categories and suppliers across ERP sources.
- **2010–2019:** Category initiatives track opportunities and implementation.
- **2020–2025:** Assisted opportunity detection suggests leverage; attribution and realized value still require evidence.

**Mindset:** optimize the business outcome and relationship, with evidence for each consequential decision. A faster purchase can still create a larger downstream problem.

**Social impact question:** whose safety, livelihood, access to opportunity or environment could this choice affect?

## 3. AI-driven horizon, 2026–2050

**2026 experiment:** use assistance for a narrow evidence summary, draft or exception suggestion. Test what it misses before delegating any action.

**2030 checkpoint:** compare assisted work with a human-led baseline, including contrary evidence and a challenge route.

**2035–2050 scenario:** Imagine a 2050 optimization assistant comparing full-lifecycle options. It must expose service, quality, environmental and continuity assumptions rather than maximize a price headline.

**Competing future:** If demand or scope changes materially, reset the comparison transparently or report an incomparable period.

These are design hypotheses, not forecasts, legal interpretations or vendor roadmaps. Autonomy remains bounded by approved authority.

## 4. Smallest useful architecture

**Capability — OVE-PROC08-OS-CAP01:** spend optimization excellence.

**Value stages — OVE-PROC08-OS-VS01:** Baseline → identify opportunity → compare total cost → approve initiative → implement → validate value.

**Objects to distinguish:** baseline quantity/price, scope, currency/date, initiative, enabling cost, owner and realized observation.

**Application responsibility:** Category owners own initiatives; analytics supplies reconciled spend; operations validates effects; Finance validates realized value. PROC08 chooses improvement actions; PROC10 governs information definitions.

**Protected boundary:** Spend reduction, negotiated price, cost avoidance and realized cash benefit are different measures.

Start with the business need before choosing SAP Ariba, SAP Business Network, ERP or another platform. Product scope, editions, local obligations and licensing require validation. The twelve PROC themes and twelve architecture streams are separate axes.

## 5. One Decision Card

Use synthetic case **OVE-PROC08-OS-SYN001**. The Google workbook supplies editable fields; the deck supports discussion. Together they decode this same sprint rather than adding separate required deliverables.

1. **Need:** one decision, affected population and missing fact.
2. **Evidence:** source/business IDs, specification or scope, validity, unit/currency where relevant.
3. **Owner:** accountable business decision maker, source application and receiving owner.
4. **Gate:** prerequisite, allowed next state and exception condition.
5. **Alternative:** Compare headline price reduction with a total-cost initiative and explicit attribution.
6. **Recovery:** escalation owner and safe correction/assisted route.
7. **Proof:** three actual paper results, a measure and stop/revise/proceed decision.

**Requirement — OVE-PROC08-OS-REQ01:** retain comparable scope/baseline and separate attribution, enabling costs and overlap before claiming value.

**Decision — OVE-PROC08-OS-ADR01:** record the chosen option, alternative, trade-off and evidence that would change your choice.

## 6. Three tabletop tests

### T01 — Volume drops while unit price is unchanged

**Expected:** Separate demand effect from procurement-driven improvement.

### T02 — Two teams claim one saving

**Expected:** Resolve attribution and avoid double counting.

### T03 — Cheaper option adds freight/inspection cost

**Expected:** Include relevant costs and risk assumptions before selecting the initiative.

Record **input → expected → actual paper result → evidence → reviewer decision → revision/retest**. Sprint-local tests OVE-PROC08-OS-T01/T02/T03 support REQ01. Publishing expected results does not prove execution.

**Measure — OVE-PROC08-OS-KPI01:** material initiative claims with agreed baseline/owner/cost/attribution evidence / claims reviewed. Confirm the owner, baseline, period and exclusions before setting targets.

**Acceptance:** the card names owners and sources, preserves the protected boundary, handles all three tests after documented revisions and explains the decision in 60 seconds. Keep unverified facts visible.

## 7. Your 120 minutes

- **0–20:** read the evolution; explain the problem that persists.
- **20–45:** role-play the lead and counterpart; request evidence.
- **45–65:** map application responsibilities and inspect one resource.
- **65–100:** complete the card and compare the alternative.
- **100–115:** run the three tests and revise from observed paper behavior.
- **115–120:** explain your decision and choose the next experiment.
