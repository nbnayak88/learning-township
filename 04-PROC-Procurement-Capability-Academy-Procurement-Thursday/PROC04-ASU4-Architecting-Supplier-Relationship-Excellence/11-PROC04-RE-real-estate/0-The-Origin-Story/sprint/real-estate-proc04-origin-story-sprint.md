## 1. Start with the story

A facilities contractor registers once and a buyer for a property portfolio assumes it is approved for a new safety-critical category.

**Procurement lead:** Amelia Jones — Procurement and Supplier Services Lead (OSP-P15).  
**Counterpart:** Omar Haddad — CISO and Resilience Lead (OSP-P09).  
**Data or business owner:** Mei Lin — Enterprise Data and ESG Lead (OSP-P08).

Amelia Jones asks what must be true before the team decides. Omar Haddad asks which evidence and owner support the decision. Mei Lin asks for the outcome that matters to the people affected.

**Evidence reveal:** In the synthetic record, registration confirms identity only; qualification for the new category was never assessed, and a similarly named entity exists.

**Pause:** What must be true for this decision to stand? Which evidence is missing, and who can confirm it? Ask the counterpart one discovery question before choosing a solution.

## 2. Trace the origin: 1970–2026

These overlapping periods are teaching lenses, not invention dates or a universal adoption sequence. Earlier practices can coexist with newer ones. Pre-1978 scenes describe the wider domain, not OmniSpace Real Estate Ltd. history.

- **1970s–1980s:** Suppliers are known personally.
- **1980s–1990s:** Approved-vendor lists formalize relationships; scope is vague.
- **2000s–2010s:** Portals centralize registration; approval spreads beyond scope.
- **2020s–2026:** Assisted matching helps identity checks; scope and judgement still need owners.

**Mindset shift:** start with what was needed, what evidence exists and who owns the decision, then choose the automation. Automating a weak control can repeat the same mistake faster.

**Pause:** Which old problem survives in a newer interface? Who bears its cost?

## 3. Explore possible futures: 2030–2050

**2030 experiment:** allow assistance to propose routine work and flag unusual items; retain the evidence gate, a named approver and a way to reverse.

**2040 question:** if buyers and suppliers exchange evidence directly, can it travel with the transaction without losing verification or fairness?

**2050 scene:** Imagine sourcing and supply decisions that explain themselves on request. Providers may show evidence sooner, but a person must own award, commitment and exceptions.

**Competing future:** A simpler, rules-based process with trained buyers may beat an adaptive service on reliability, fairness and cost.

All future passages are design hypotheses, not forecasts or product roadmaps. Test a useful part today rather than assume the future will arrive as described.

## 4. Draw the smallest useful architecture

**Capability — OSP-PROC04-OS-CAP01:** scoped supplier qualification. A capability is an ability; it is not an application or a sequence of steps.

**Value stages — OSP-PROC04-OS-VS01:** Register → verify identity → qualify by scope → improve → review.

**Objects to distinguish:** supplier identity, qualification scope, category and improvement action.

**Proposed responsibility:** Supplier management owns identity; quality owns qualification; category owners own use. PROC04 owns the relationship decision, not the portal build.

**Protected boundary:** Registration confirms identity, not qualification for every category.

Procurement applications are optional demonstration candidates, chosen after requirements. No product is assumed installed, licensed or able to cover the whole process. This sprint focuses on application responsibility while referencing data, integration and security owners.

## 5. Build one Decision Card

Use synthetic case **OSP-PROC04-OS-SYN001**. Synthetic suppliers RE-SUP300 and RE-SUP301 (similar names), category RE-CAT300, qualification scope limited. Keep everything on one card or screen.

1. **Need:** the outcome, affected people and one missing fact.
2. **Evidence:** relevant object IDs, source, validity or effective time, data purpose and permitted access.
3. **Ownership:** business decision owner, authoritative application and receiving owner.
4. **Gate:** prerequisite, allowed next state, authorized actor and the message anyone affected will see.
5. **Alternative:** Compare treating registration as approval with qualifying per category.
6. **Exception:** one failure state, escalation owner and a safe recovery or assisted route.
7. **Proof:** the three results below, one measure and a stop, revise or proceed decision.

**Requirement — OSP-PROC04-OS-REQ01:** link one supplier-scope experiment hypothesis to an owned intervention and observed follow-up, including negative evidence.

**Decision — OSP-PROC04-OS-ADR01:** record your chosen option and the alternative from item 5, one trade-off, and the evidence that would change your choice.

## 6. Run three tabletop tests

### T01 — A registered supplier enters a new safety-critical category

**Expected:** Require category-specific qualification before use.

### T02 — Similar names refer to different entities

**Expected:** Do not merge; confirm identity with evidence.

### T03 — A relationship action passes its due date

**Expected:** Escalate to the owner and record the reason for delay.

For each, record **input → expected → actual paper result → evidence note → revision → retest**. These are sprint-local tests OSP-PROC04-OS-T01/T02/T03, supporting OSP-PROC04-OS-REQ01. A completed lesson is not an executed tenant test.

**Measure — OSP-PROC04-OS-KPI01:** Share of supplier uses covered by in-scope qualification; define window and exclusions. Assign Amelia Jones as proposed measure owner, establish the baseline and exclusions, and review after the experiment. No numeric improvement is asserted.

**Acceptance:** the card identifies source and owner, preserves the protected boundary, handles all three tests after documented revisions and explains the decision in 60 seconds. Missing evidence remains visible. Record what is still unverified.
