## 1. The purchase behind the process

A supplier registered for office services is shown as qualified to maintain refinery control equipment. Two legal entities share a similar trading name and one site's collaboration contact receives broader data than required.

**Lead:** Wei Zhang — Chief Supply Chain & Procurement Officer (OVE-P11).  
**Counterpart:** Omar Haddad — Chief Information Security Officer (OVE-P13).

Wei asks what approval actually covers. Omar asks which organization and person are authenticated, qualified and authorized for each collaboration.

The team separates supplier identity from category/site qualification and contact access. It assigns relationship reviews and visible correction actions instead of a universal approved flag.

**Pause:** What business need and evidence must be clear before the next commitment? Ask both people one useful question.

OmniVerse is a fictional integrated energy group with 68,000 employees in 42 countries. Existing OVE-P personas retain their source identities. Dialogue, incidents, supplier records and proposed outcomes here are new learning fiction. No real supplier, award, transaction or implementation result is represented.

## 2. Origins and evolution, 1970–2025

These closed, non-overlapping periods are teaching lenses, not invention dates or universal adoption deadlines. Practices coexist. Pre-1978 examples describe the wider domain rather than OmniVerse corporate history.

- **1970–1989:** Local supplier cards preserve relationships but offer little shared visibility.
- **1990–1999:** Vendor databases centralize records and duplicate names.
- **2000–2009:** Supplier lifecycle platforms formalize registration and qualification.
- **2010–2019:** Networks connect collaboration and relationship reviews.
- **2020–2025:** Assisted profiling proposes segments; identity and qualification still need evidence.

**Mindset:** optimize the business outcome and relationship, with evidence for each consequential decision. A faster purchase can still create a larger downstream problem.

**Social impact question:** whose safety, livelihood, access to opportunity or environment could this choice affect?

## 3. AI-driven horizon, 2026–2050

**2026 experiment:** use assistance for a narrow evidence summary, draft or exception suggestion. Test what it misses before delegating any action.

**2030 checkpoint:** compare assisted work with a human-led baseline, including contrary evidence and a challenge route.

**2035–2050 scenario:** Imagine a 2050 supplier sharing a portable qualification profile. OmniVerse must still check current evidence for its own category, site and engagement.

**Competing future:** If credentials are fragmented, retain transparent manual qualification and correction without excluding capable smaller suppliers.

These are design hypotheses, not forecasts, legal interpretations or vendor roadmaps. Autonomy remains bounded by approved authority.

## 4. Smallest useful architecture

**Capability — OVE-PROC04-OS-CAP01:** supplier relationship excellence.

**Value stages — OVE-PROC04-OS-VS01:** Identify → register → qualify scope → authorize collaboration → review relationship → improve or restrict.

**Objects to distinguish:** supplier legal identity, contact, category/site scope, qualification validity, relationship owner and action.

**Application responsibility:** Supplier stewardship owns identity; category/site owners approve qualification; IAM controls collaboration access; relationship owners own business reviews. PROC07 owns quality evidence and PROC09 owns risk assessment.

**Protected boundary:** Registration, qualification, award, purchasing permission and site work authorization are different states.

Start with the business need before choosing SAP Ariba, SAP Business Network, ERP or another platform. Product scope, editions, local obligations and licensing require validation. The twelve PROC themes and twelve architecture streams are separate axes.

## 5. One Decision Card

Use synthetic case **OVE-PROC04-OS-SYN001**. The Google workbook supplies editable fields; the deck supports discussion. Together they decode this same sprint rather than adding separate required deliverables.

1. **Need:** one decision, affected population and missing fact.
2. **Evidence:** source/business IDs, specification or scope, validity, unit/currency where relevant.
3. **Owner:** accountable business decision maker, source application and receiving owner.
4. **Gate:** prerequisite, allowed next state and exception condition.
5. **Alternative:** Compare one universal approved flag with category/site-specific evidence and owned reviews.
6. **Recovery:** escalation owner and safe correction/assisted route.
7. **Proof:** three actual paper results, a measure and stop/revise/proceed decision.

**Requirement — OVE-PROC04-OS-REQ01:** retain scoped, current qualification and relationship authority for each proposed engagement.

**Decision — OVE-PROC04-OS-ADR01:** record the chosen option, alternative, trade-off and evidence that would change your choice.

## 6. Three tabletop tests

### T01 — Registered supplier enters a new safety-critical category

**Expected:** Require applicable qualification instead of inheriting office-services approval.

### T02 — Similar names refer to different entities

**Expected:** Preserve identities and validate before merge or shared access.

### T03 — Relationship action passes its due date

**Expected:** Keep the action visible, assign escalation and reassess the applicable scope.

Record **input → expected → actual paper result → evidence → reviewer decision → revision/retest**. Sprint-local tests OVE-PROC04-OS-T01/T02/T03 support REQ01. Publishing expected results does not prove execution.

**Measure — OVE-PROC04-OS-KPI01:** engagements with valid scope-specific qualification / engagements reviewed. Confirm the owner, baseline, period and exclusions before setting targets.

**Acceptance:** the card names owners and sources, preserves the protected boundary, handles all three tests after documented revisions and explains the decision in 60 seconds. Keep unverified facts visible.

## 7. Your 120 minutes

- **0–20:** read the evolution; explain the problem that persists.
- **20–45:** role-play the lead and counterpart; request evidence.
- **45–65:** map application responsibilities and inspect one resource.
- **65–100:** complete the card and compare the alternative.
- **100–115:** run the three tests and revise from observed paper behavior.
- **115–120:** explain your decision and choose the next experiment.
