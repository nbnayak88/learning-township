## 1. The purchase behind the process

A supplier delivers turbine components on time, but inspection evidence reveals a nonconformity. The supplier scorecard remains green because delivery performance outweighs quality concerns.

**Lead:** Carlos Mendes — VP Asset Reliability and Maintenance (OVE-P09).  
**Counterpart:** Wei Zhang — Chief Supply Chain & Procurement Officer (OVE-P11).

Carlos asks who may release the material for use. Wei asks whether a strong aggregate score is hiding a critical defect and whether the corrective action was verified.

The team separates delivery receipt from quality disposition, identifies the affected batch and requires evidence before closing corrective action. The scorecard reflects each measure's limits.

**Pause:** What business need and evidence must be clear before the next commitment? Ask both people one useful question.

OmniVerse is a fictional integrated energy group with 68,000 employees in 42 countries. Existing OVE-P personas retain their source identities. Dialogue, incidents, supplier records and proposed outcomes here are new learning fiction. No real supplier, award, transaction or implementation result is represented.

## 2. Origins and evolution, 1970–2025

These closed, non-overlapping periods are teaching lenses, not invention dates or universal adoption deadlines. Practices coexist. Pre-1978 examples describe the wider domain rather than OmniVerse corporate history.

- **1970–1989:** Receiving inspection records issues on paper.
- **1990–1999:** Quality spreadsheets collect defect counts without consistent scope.
- **2000–2009:** ERP quality and procurement link batches and supplier records.
- **2010–2019:** Supplier scorecards connect business reviews to improvement actions.
- **2020–2025:** Assisted anomaly detection flags patterns; authorized disposition and effectiveness evidence remain essential.

**Mindset:** optimize the business outcome and relationship, with evidence for each consequential decision. A faster purchase can still create a larger downstream problem.

**Social impact question:** whose safety, livelihood, access to opportunity or environment could this choice affect?

## 3. AI-driven horizon, 2026–2050

**2026 experiment:** use assistance for a narrow evidence summary, draft or exception suggestion. Test what it misses before delegating any action.

**2030 checkpoint:** compare assisted work with a human-led baseline, including contrary evidence and a challenge route.

**2035–2050 scenario:** Imagine a 2050 supplier providing verifiable digital inspection evidence. OmniVerse still needs its applicable acceptance rules and an accountable disposition.

**Competing future:** If supplier evidence is incomplete, quarantine/hold and an approved inspection route must remain available.

These are design hypotheses, not forecasts, legal interpretations or vendor roadmaps. Autonomy remains bounded by approved authority.

## 4. Smallest useful architecture

**Capability — OVE-PROC07-OS-CAP01:** supplier quality performance excellence.

**Value stages — OVE-PROC07-OS-VS01:** Receive → inspect → assess conformity → authorize disposition → correct → verify effectiveness.

**Objects to distinguish:** batch/item, receipt, inspection result, nonconformity, disposition, corrective action and effectiveness check.

**Application responsibility:** Quality/engineering owners decide conformity and release; procurement coordinates supplier improvement; inventory records holds; supplier relationship owners review performance.

**Protected boundary:** On-time delivery and an aggregate supplier score cannot authorize release of nonconforming safety-critical material.

Start with the business need before choosing SAP Ariba, SAP Business Network, ERP or another platform. Product scope, editions, local obligations and licensing require validation. The twelve PROC themes and twelve architecture streams are separate axes.

## 5. One Decision Card

Use synthetic case **OVE-PROC07-OS-SYN001**. The Google workbook supplies editable fields; the deck supports discussion. Together they decode this same sprint rather than adding separate required deliverables.

1. **Need:** one decision, affected population and missing fact.
2. **Evidence:** source/business IDs, specification or scope, validity, unit/currency where relevant.
3. **Owner:** accountable business decision maker, source application and receiving owner.
4. **Gate:** prerequisite, allowed next state and exception condition.
5. **Alternative:** Compare score-based closure with defect-specific disposition and an evidence-backed improvement action.
6. **Recovery:** escalation owner and safe correction/assisted route.
7. **Proof:** three actual paper results, a measure and stop/revise/proceed decision.

**Requirement — OVE-PROC07-OS-REQ01:** link the affected item/batch to authorized disposition and verified corrective-action effectiveness.

**Decision — OVE-PROC07-OS-ADR01:** record the chosen option, alternative, trade-off and evidence that would change your choice.

## 6. Three tabletop tests

### T01 — Critical defect exists despite high overall score

**Expected:** Hold applicable release and show the defect independently of aggregate performance.

### T02 — Supplier marks corrective action complete

**Expected:** Require agreed evidence and verify effectiveness before closure.

### T03 — Batch identity is missing

**Expected:** Trace scope and containment; avoid claiming unaffected material is safe without evidence.

Record **input → expected → actual paper result → evidence → reviewer decision → revision/retest**. Sprint-local tests OVE-PROC07-OS-T01/T02/T03 support REQ01. Publishing expected results does not prove execution.

**Measure — OVE-PROC07-OS-KPI01:** quality actions closed with effectiveness evidence / actions reported closed. Confirm the owner, baseline, period and exclusions before setting targets.

**Acceptance:** the card names owners and sources, preserves the protected boundary, handles all three tests after documented revisions and explains the decision in 60 seconds. Keep unverified facts visible.

## 7. Your 120 minutes

- **0–20:** read the evolution; explain the problem that persists.
- **20–45:** role-play the lead and counterpart; request evidence.
- **45–65:** map application responsibilities and inspect one resource.
- **65–100:** complete the card and compare the alternative.
- **100–115:** run the three tests and revise from observed paper behavior.
- **115–120:** explain your decision and choose the next experiment.
