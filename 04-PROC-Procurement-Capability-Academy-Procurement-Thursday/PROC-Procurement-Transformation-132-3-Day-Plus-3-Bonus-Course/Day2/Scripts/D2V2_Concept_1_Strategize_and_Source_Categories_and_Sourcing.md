**1. What is the concept?**
Category management groups spend into manageable categories and sets a strategy for each: what to buy, from whom, at what risk and value. Sourcing then runs the events (RFx, auctions, negotiations) that turn the strategy into awarded business. [pause]

**2. Why is it important?**
Buying by habit or by department leaves money on the table and risk unmanaged. Without a category strategy, sourcing becomes a price chase; without sourcing discipline, a good strategy never reaches the contract.
Here is a real pattern I have seen. A materials company bought packaging through nine business units with nine supplier lists. One category strategy and one sourcing event consolidated the volume, improved terms and cut the supplier base in half, with no loss of service. [pause]

**3. Benefits of implementing it the right way**
When you get this right, here is what changes: Clear category strategies tied to business needs; Competitive, transparent sourcing events; Measurable savings and better supplier terms; A repeatable rhythm the whole function can follow. [on screen: four benefits]

**Sector lens.** Sector lens: Energy and Industrials run large, project-based sourcing events; Consumer Staples runs high-volume, repeat category cycles.
