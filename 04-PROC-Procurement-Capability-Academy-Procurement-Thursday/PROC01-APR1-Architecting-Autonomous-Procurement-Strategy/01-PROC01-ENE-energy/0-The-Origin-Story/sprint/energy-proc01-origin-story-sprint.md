## 1. The purchase behind the process

A refinery critical-spares category offers a low-price single-source option. A buyer wants to aggregate every site into one contract before confirming interchangeability, lead times and outage exposure.

**Lead:** Wei Zhang — Chief Supply Chain & Procurement Officer (OVE-P11).  
**Counterpart:** Carlos Mendes — VP Asset Reliability and Maintenance (OVE-P09).

Carlos asks what happens if the source fails during a turnaround. Wei asks which purchases genuinely belong to one category and what evidence supports concentration.

The team separates category strategy from supplier award and compares consolidation with a qualified alternative-source arrangement. Each proposed saving keeps its baseline, risk and business owner.

**Pause:** What business need and evidence must be clear before the next commitment? Ask both people one useful question.

OmniVerse is a fictional integrated energy group with 68,000 employees in 42 countries. Existing OVE-P personas retain their source identities. Dialogue, incidents, supplier records and proposed outcomes here are new learning fiction. No real supplier, award, transaction or implementation result is represented.

## 2. Origins and evolution, 1970–2025

These closed, non-overlapping periods are teaching lenses, not invention dates or universal adoption deadlines. Practices coexist. Pre-1978 examples describe the wider domain rather than OmniVerse corporate history.

- **1970–1989:** Local purchase books emphasize availability and relationships.
- **1990–1999:** Spend spreadsheets expose price variation but hide category differences.
- **2000–2009:** ERP purchasing standardizes transactions while strategy remains fragmented.
- **2010–2019:** Category platforms connect spend and market evidence.
- **2020–2025:** Analytics suggests opportunities; technical criticality and dependency still need review.

**Mindset:** optimize the business outcome and relationship, with evidence for each consequential decision. A faster purchase can still create a larger downstream problem.

**Social impact question:** whose safety, livelihood, access to opportunity or environment could this choice affect?

## 3. AI-driven horizon, 2026–2050

**2026 experiment:** use assistance for a narrow evidence summary, draft or exception suggestion. Test what it misses before delegating any action.

**2030 checkpoint:** compare assisted work with a human-led baseline, including contrary evidence and a challenge route.

**2035–2050 scenario:** Imagine a 2050 category assistant proposing adaptive sourcing portfolios. It must show where cost gains increase supply dependency and leave consequential strategy choices accountable.

**Competing future:** In a fragmented supply market, deliberate redundancy may outperform maximum consolidation.

These are design hypotheses, not forecasts, legal interpretations or vendor roadmaps. Autonomy remains bounded by approved authority.

## 4. Smallest useful architecture

**Capability — OVE-PROC01-OS-CAP01:** procurement strategy.

**Value stages — OVE-PROC01-OS-VS01:** Frame need → segment category → validate evidence → compare options → approve → track outcomes.

**Objects to distinguish:** category, demand, specification, spend baseline, criticality, dependency and option.

**Application responsibility:** Category owners govern strategy; engineering validates interchangeability/criticality; Finance validates value; sourcing owns award execution. PROC01 does not automatically select a supplier.

**Protected boundary:** Purchase-price reduction is not automatically total-cost saving or acceptable operational risk.

Start with the business need before choosing SAP Ariba, SAP Business Network, ERP or another platform. Product scope, editions, local obligations and licensing require validation. The twelve PROC themes and twelve architecture streams are separate axes.

## 5. One Decision Card

Use synthetic case **OVE-PROC01-OS-SYN001**. The Google workbook supplies editable fields; the deck supports discussion. Together they decode this same sprint rather than adding separate required deliverables.

1. **Need:** one decision, affected population and missing fact.
2. **Evidence:** source/business IDs, specification or scope, validity, unit/currency where relevant.
3. **Owner:** accountable business decision maker, source application and receiving owner.
4. **Gate:** prerequisite, allowed next state and exception condition.
5. **Alternative:** Compare maximum consolidation with a scoped dual-source or contingency option.
6. **Recovery:** escalation owner and safe correction/assisted route.
7. **Proof:** three actual paper results, a measure and stop/revise/proceed decision.

**Requirement — OVE-PROC01-OS-REQ01:** record one scoped category choice with approved demand/specification evidence, cost baseline and continuity trade-off.

**Decision — OVE-PROC01-OS-ADR01:** record the chosen option, alternative, trade-off and evidence that would change your choice.

## 6. Three tabletop tests

### T01 — Two parts look similar but are not interchangeable

**Expected:** Keep specifications separate until engineering validates substitution.

### T02 — Supplier concentration increases

**Expected:** Show disruption dependency and obtain an accountable strategy decision.

### T03 — Savings baseline mixes scope and currency

**Expected:** Normalize the comparison and hold unsupported saving claims.

Record **input → expected → actual paper result → evidence → reviewer decision → revision/retest**. Sprint-local tests OVE-PROC01-OS-T01/T02/T03 support REQ01. Publishing expected results does not prove execution.

**Measure — OVE-PROC01-OS-KPI01:** category decisions with validated baseline/specification/risk evidence / decisions reviewed. Confirm the owner, baseline, period and exclusions before setting targets.

**Acceptance:** the card names owners and sources, preserves the protected boundary, handles all three tests after documented revisions and explains the decision in 60 seconds. Keep unverified facts visible.

## 7. Your 120 minutes

- **0–20:** read the evolution; explain the problem that persists.
- **20–45:** role-play the lead and counterpart; request evidence.
- **45–65:** map application responsibilities and inspect one resource.
- **65–100:** complete the card and compare the alternative.
- **100–115:** run the three tests and revise from observed paper behavior.
- **115–120:** explain your decision and choose the next experiment.
