## 1. The purchase behind the process

A supplier offers a low-carbon critical component, but the claim's boundary and verification are unclear. A risk service also reports a possible issue without confirming it refers to the same legal entity.

**Lead:** Leena Fernandes — Chief Sustainability and Transition Officer (OVE-P06).  
**Counterpart:** Wei Zhang — Chief Supply Chain & Procurement Officer (OVE-P11).

Leena asks what the claim includes and which evidence supports it. Wei asks how the team can protect operations and treat the supplier fairly while investigating.

They verify supplier identity, scope the risk/claim, identify missing evidence and agree proportionate due diligence. A score triggers inquiry rather than automatic exoneration or exclusion.

**Pause:** What business need and evidence must be clear before the next commitment? Ask both people one useful question.

OmniVerse is a fictional integrated energy group with 68,000 employees in 42 countries. Existing OVE-P personas retain their source identities. Dialogue, incidents, supplier records and proposed outcomes here are new learning fiction. No real supplier, award, transaction or implementation result is represented.

## 2. Origins and evolution, 1970–2025

These closed, non-overlapping periods are teaching lenses, not invention dates or universal adoption deadlines. Practices coexist. Pre-1978 examples describe the wider domain rather than OmniVerse corporate history.

- **1970–1989:** Purchasing relies on local reputation and supplier declarations.
- **1990–1999:** Risk registers and audits formalize concerns but remain disconnected.
- **2000–2009:** Supplier-risk systems aggregate external information.
- **2010–2019:** Sustainability due diligence extends beyond price and delivery.
- **2020–2025:** AI-assisted signals broaden detection; provenance and fair review still matter.

**Mindset:** optimize the business outcome and relationship, with evidence for each consequential decision. A faster purchase can still create a larger downstream problem.

**Social impact question:** whose safety, livelihood, access to opportunity or environment could this choice affect?

## 3. AI-driven horizon, 2026–2050

**2026 experiment:** use assistance for a narrow evidence summary, draft or exception suggestion. Test what it misses before delegating any action.

**2030 checkpoint:** compare assisted work with a human-led baseline, including contrary evidence and a challenge route.

**2035–2050 scenario:** Imagine a 2050 evidence network tracing environmental and labor claims through supply tiers. Incomplete coverage must remain visible, with correction and supplier participation.

**Competing future:** If smaller suppliers lack digital credentials, provide proportionate alternative evidence routes rather than assume low capability or compliance.

These are design hypotheses, not forecasts, legal interpretations or vendor roadmaps. Autonomy remains bounded by approved authority.

## 4. Smallest useful architecture

**Capability — OVE-PROC09-OS-CAP01:** risk sustainable procurement.

**Value stages — OVE-PROC09-OS-VS01:** Identify exposure → verify identity → assess evidence → investigate → decide proportionate action → monitor.

**Objects to distinguish:** supplier identity, risk/claim scope, evidence source, validity, due-diligence action and decision.

**Application responsibility:** Risk/sustainability owners define assessment requirements; procurement owns engagement decisions; legal/compliance advise applicable obligations; suppliers can correct factual evidence.

**Protected boundary:** ISO 20400 is guidance, not proof that a supplier or this sprint is certified. This scenario does not prescribe sanctions or legal conclusions.

Start with the business need before choosing SAP Ariba, SAP Business Network, ERP or another platform. Product scope, editions, local obligations and licensing require validation. The twelve PROC themes and twelve architecture streams are separate axes.

## 5. One Decision Card

Use synthetic case **OVE-PROC09-OS-SYN001**. The Google workbook supplies editable fields; the deck supports discussion. Together they decode this same sprint rather than adding separate required deliverables.

1. **Need:** one decision, affected population and missing fact.
2. **Evidence:** source/business IDs, specification or scope, validity, unit/currency where relevant.
3. **Owner:** accountable business decision maker, source application and receiving owner.
4. **Gate:** prerequisite, allowed next state and exception condition.
5. **Alternative:** Compare an opaque score-based exclusion with evidence-led investigation and bounded mitigation.
6. **Recovery:** escalation owner and safe correction/assisted route.
7. **Proof:** three actual paper results, a measure and stop/revise/proceed decision.

**Requirement — OVE-PROC09-OS-REQ01:** retain verified identity and claim/risk evidence with an accountable, proportionate review decision.

**Decision — OVE-PROC09-OS-ADR01:** record the chosen option, alternative, trade-off and evidence that would change your choice.

## 6. Three tabletop tests

### T01 — Risk hit concerns a similarly named supplier

**Expected:** Validate identity before consequential action.

### T02 — Environmental claim omits lifecycle boundary

**Expected:** Request scope/method/source and withhold unsupported promotional or sourcing conclusions.

### T03 — Evidence expires during the engagement

**Expected:** Trigger review and appropriate mitigation; do not preserve a stale clear status.

Record **input → expected → actual paper result → evidence → reviewer decision → revision/retest**. Sprint-local tests OVE-PROC09-OS-T01/T02/T03 support REQ01. Publishing expected results does not prove execution.

**Measure — OVE-PROC09-OS-KPI01:** material risk/claim decisions with current scoped evidence and owner / decisions reviewed. Confirm the owner, baseline, period and exclusions before setting targets.

**Acceptance:** the card names owners and sources, preserves the protected boundary, handles all three tests after documented revisions and explains the decision in 60 seconds. Keep unverified facts visible.

## 7. Your 120 minutes

- **0–20:** read the evolution; explain the problem that persists.
- **20–45:** role-play the lead and counterpart; request evidence.
- **45–65:** map application responsibilities and inspect one resource.
- **65–100:** complete the card and compare the alternative.
- **100–115:** run the three tests and revise from observed paper behavior.
- **115–120:** explain your decision and choose the next experiment.
